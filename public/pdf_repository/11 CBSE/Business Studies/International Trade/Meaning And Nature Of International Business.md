# 10.1 MEANING AND NATURE OF INTERNATIONAL BUSINESS

International business refers to those business activities that take place beyond the geographical limits of a country. It involves not only the international movements of goods and services, but also of capital, personnel, technology and intellectual property like patents, trademarks, knowhow and copyrights.
In the words of Roger Bennett, "International business involves commercial activities that cross national frontiers".

In the words of Michael R. Czinkota, "International business consists of transactions that are devised and carried out across national borders to satisfy the objectives of the individuals, companies and organisations. These transactions take on various forms which are often interrelated".

## Features of International Business

The main features of international business are as follows:

1. Involves Two Countries: International business is possible only when there are transactions across different countries.
2. Use of Foreign Exchange: Every country has its own different currency. This gives rise to the problem of exchange of currencies as foreign currency is used in making transactions.
3. Legal Obligations: Each country has its own laws regarding foreign trade, which have to be complied with. Further, there is more government intervention in case of international transactions.

4. High degree of risk: International business faces huge risk due to long distances, risk of fluctuations in two currencies, fear of obsolescence, etc.
5. Heavy Documentation: It is subject to number of formalities. Many documents have to be filled in and despatched to the other party.
6. Time Consuming: The time gap between sending and receiving of goods and payment is wider as compared to inland trade.
7. Lack of Personal Contact: It lacks direct and personal contact between importer \& exporter.
8. Language Differences: Every country has its own different language. While doing business with a foreign country, knowledge of language of that country plays a significant role in the success of business.

## International Business Vs International Trade 

It is generally believed that International Business and International Trade are one and the same thing. But this is not true.

- International Trade is the exchange (export and import) of goods and services between different countries of the world, while International Business involves not only the international movements of goods and services, but also of capital, personnel, technology and intellectual property.
- International Business also includes:
(i) Services such as international travel and tourism, transportation, communication, banking, warehousing, distribution and advertising.
(ii) Foreign Investments by companies and production of goods \& services in foreign countries.

So, international business is a much broader term and includes both trade and production of goods and services across frontiers.

## Reasons for International Business

The international business is needed due to the following reasons:

1. Uneven Distribution of Natural Resources: All the countries cannot produce equally well or cheaply due to unequal distribution of natural resources among them or differences in their productivity levels. As a result, they exchange their surplus production with goods that they are in short supply in their country.
2. Availability of Factors of Production: The various factors of production such as labour, capital and raw materials, needed for producing different goods and services differ among nations.
3. Specialisation: Some countries specialise in the production of goods and services for which they have some advantages like advanced technical know-how, high labour productivity, suitable climatic conditions, etc. For example, West Bengal specialises in jute products.
4. Cost Benefits: Production costs differ in different countries due to difference in socioeconomic, geographical and political conditions. Some countries are in a better position

to produce some goods more economically than other countries. As a result, firms engage in international business to import what is available at lower prices in other countries and export goods on which they can fetch better prices.

## Domestic Business Vs International Business 

The major points of difference between these two are:

| Basis | Domestic Business | International Business |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Nationality of <br> Buyers and Sellers | Both buyers and sellers belong to same country. It makes easier for both the parties to understand each other and enter into business deals. | Buyers and sellers come from different countries. It makes business dealings relatively difficult due to differences in their languages, attitudes, customs, etc. |
| Nationality of other Stakeholders | The stakeholders like employees, suppliers, shareholders, etc. belong to one country, which leads to easy decision making process due to more consistency in their value systems and behaviour. | The stakeholders belong to different countries, which makes decision making process complex due to different value systems and aspirations. |
| Mobility of Factors of Production | The degree of mobility of factors of production (like labour and capital) is relatively more within a country as they can move freely within the country. | The factors are less mobile across nations due to legal restrictions or variations in social \& economic environment. Moreover, labour finds it difficult to adjust to the climatic, economic and socio-cultural conditions that differ from country to country. |
| Nature of Customers | Customers are more homogeneous in their taste, preferences, consumption patterns and buying behaviour. | Customers lack homogeneity due to different socio-cultural background, tastes, fashions, languages, beliefs and customs, attitudes and product preferences in different countries. It causes variation in not only their demand for different products, but also in their communication patterns and purchase behaviours. |
| Business Systems and Practices | They are relatively more homogeneous within a country. | They are less homogeneous due to difference in development level, infrastructure facilities and market support services. |

| Political System and Risks | Domestic business firms are familiar with political system of their country. As a result, they are in better position to understand and predict its impact on business. | International business faces difficulties in understanding and coping up with different political systems of every country. Many nations favour products originating in their own countries as compared to those coming from other countries. It quite often becomes a severe problem in case of international business. |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Business Regulations and Policies | It is subject to rules, laws or taxation policies of a single country. | It is subject to rules, laws or taxation policies of various countries. Many nations often discriminate (through Tariff and taxation policies) against foreign products, services and capital. |
| Currency Used | Currency of domestic country is used. | It involves use of currencies of more than one country. As exchange rate keeps on fluctuating, it becomes difficult for firms to fix prices of their products. |
| Risk | It involves comparatively less degree of risk. | It involves high degree of risk. |
| Order processing time | There is a less time gap in order and supply of goods. | There is a wide time gap between order and supply of goods. |
| Effect on Foreign Reserve | Domestic business has no effect on foreign reserves of a country. | International business has direct impact on foreign reserves of a country. |
