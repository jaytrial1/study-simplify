# 10.6 WORLD TRADE ORGANIZATION (WTO) 

The World Trade Organization (WTO) was established on January 1, 1995 to replace General Agreement on Trade and Tariff (GATT), which remained in force from 1948 to 1994. WTO's headquarter is situated at Geneva, Switzerland and was set up to have a permanent institution to promote free and fair trade amongst nations. India is a founding member of WTO. There are 164 members in WTO and all the members are required to adopt laws and policies framed under WTO rules.
WTO is the only international organisation, which deals with the global rules of trade between nations. Its main function is to ensure that trade flows as smoothly, predictably and freely as possible.

## GATT Vs WTO

1. Nature: GATT was a set of rules, with no institutional foundation, whereas, WTO is a permanent institution with a permanent framework and its own secretariat.
2. Scope: GATT dealt only with trade in goods, while WTO covers services and intellectual property rights as well.
3. Dispute settlement:WTO dispute settlement system is faster, more automatic and less subject to blockages, than the GATT system.
Though, WTO is a successor to GATT, it is a much more powerful body than GATT.

## Objectives of WTO

The major objectives of WTO are as follows:

1. To ensure reduction of tariffs and other trade barriers imposed by different countries.
2. To improve standards of living, create employment, increase income and effective demand and facilitate higher production and trade.
3. To facilitate optimum use of world's resources for sustainable development.
4. To promote an integrated, more viable and durable trading system.

## Role or Functions of WTO

WTO plays an important role by performing the following functions:

1. Encouraging member countries to come forward to WTO for mitigating their grievances.
2. Laying down a commonly accepted code of conduct in order to reduce trade barriers.
3. Acting as a dispute settlement body.
4. Ensuring that all rules and regulations prescribed in the Act are duly followed by the member countries for the settlement of their disputes.
5. Holding consultations with IMF and IBRD and its affiliated agencies to bring better understanding and cooperation in global economic policy making.
6. Regularly supervising the operations of the revised Agreements and Ministerial declarations relating to goods, services and Trade Related Intellectual Property Rights (TRIPS).

## Benefits of WTO 

Some of the major benefits of WTO are as follows:

1. Promotes international peace and facilitates international business.
2. Settles disputes between member nations.
3. To make international trade and relations smooth and predictable by framing rules and regulations.
4. Promotes standard of living of people by increasing their income level due to free trade.
5. Provides more choice of products and qualities (due to free trade).
6. Stimulates economic growth of developing countries by providing them special and preferential treatment in trade related matters.
7. Encourages good government.
