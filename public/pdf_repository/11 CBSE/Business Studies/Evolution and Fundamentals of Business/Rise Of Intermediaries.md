# RISE OF INTERMEDIARIES 

An intermediary is a firm or person who acts as a link between parties while carrying out a business transaction. Intermediaries included commission agents, brokers and distributors both for wholesale and retail goods. Intermediaries played an important role in the promotion of trade. They provided considerable financial security to the manufacturers by assuming responsibility for the risks involved, especially in foreign trade.

- The institution of Jagat Seths (rich business, banking and money lender families) also developed and exercised great influence during the Mughal period and the days of the East India Company. Bankers began to act as trustees and executors of endowments.
- Most of the foreign trade was financed by loans. However, interest rate for longer voyages was kept high due to huge risk involved.
- The emergence of credit transactions and availability of loans and advances resulted in increase of commercial operations.
- Indian subcontinent enjoyed favourable balance of trade as exports exceeded imports with large margins. As a result, indigenous banking system benefitted the manufacturers, traders and merchants with additional capital funds for expansion and development.
- In the later stage, Commercial and Industrial banks evolved in order to finance trade and commerce and agricultural banks to provide both short-and long-term loans to agriculturists.
