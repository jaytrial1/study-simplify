# Difference between Business, Profession and Employment
| Basis | Business | Profession | Employment |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Mode of <br> Establishment or <br> Commencement | Commencement of business <br> depends on entrepreneur's <br> decision and other legal <br> formalities, if necessary. | It commences on getting <br> membership of a professional <br> body and certificate of practice. | It commences on getting <br> an appointment letter <br> and entering into service <br> agreement. |
| Nature of Work | It involves providing goods <br> and services to the public. | It involves rendering of <br> personalised expert services. | It involves performing work as <br> per service contract or rules <br> of service. |
| Qualification | It does not require any <br> minimum qualification. | It requires expertise and <br> training in a specific field. | It requires qualification and <br> training as prescribed by the <br> employer. |
| Reward or Return | Reward for business is profit. | Reward for profession is <br> professional fees. | Reward for employment is <br> salary. |
| Capital Investment | It requires capital investment <br> as per size and nature of <br> business. | It requires limited capital for <br> establishment. | No capital is required. |
| Risk | It involves high degree of risk <br> as profits are uncertain and <br> irregular. | It involves moderate risk as fee <br> is generally regular and certain. | It does not involve risk as <br> employee gets fixed and <br> regular pay. |
| Transfer of Interest | Transfer of interest with some <br> formalities. | It is not possible to transfer the <br> interest. | It is not possible to transfer <br> the interest. |
| Code of Conduct | No code of conduct is <br> prescribed. | Professional code of conduct is <br> to be followed. | Norms of behaviour as laid <br> down by the employer have <br> to be followed. |
| Motive | The basic motive of business <br> is to earn profit. | The basic motive of profession <br> is to render service besides <br> earning income. | The basic motive of employ- <br> ment is to earn livelihood in <br> the form of salary. |
| Examples | Shop, Factory | Legal, Medical Profession, <br> Chartered Accountancy | Jobs in Banks, Insurance <br> Companies, Government <br> Departments |

## Business Functions at Enterprise Level
Business includes a wide variety of functions, which are performed by different kinds of organisations. The four major functions performed by business enterprises are:

1. Financing: It is concerned with mobilising and utilising funds for running a business enterprise.
2. Production: It involves conversion of raw materials into finished products or generation of services.
3. Marketing: It refers to all those activities which facilitate exchange of goods and services from producers to the users or consumers, at a place they want, at a time they require and at a price they are prepared to pay.
4. Human Resource Management: It aims at ensuring the availability of working people who have necessary skills to perform various tasks in enterprises.
