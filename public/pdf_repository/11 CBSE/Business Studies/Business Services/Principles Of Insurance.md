# Principles of Insurance

The contract of insurance is based on the following principles:

1. Utmost Good Faith: A contract of insurance is a contract of 'uberrimae fidei', i.e. a contract found on utmost good faith.

* Both the parties, the insurer and the insured, should display good faith towards each other in regard to the contract.
* It is obligatory on the part of the insured to disclose all material facts about the subject to be insured.
* In case of failure to disclose any material fact, the contract of insurance is voidable at the option of the insurer.
* Similarly, the insurer must also clear all the terms and conditions in the insurance contract.
For example, Ramesh took a life insurance policy from LIC. However, while taking the
policy, he did not disclose about his heart disease. Later on, he died because of a heart

attack. Now, LIC is not liable to pay the compensation as a material fact was hidden by Ramesh.

## What do you mean by 'Material Fact'? 

A material fact is one which goes to the root of the insurance contract, i.e. a fact is material if it can influence the insurer in accepting or declining the risk or in fixing the conditions of insurance or rate of premium.
2. Insurable Interest: The insured must have an insurable interest in the subject matter of insurance. Insurable interest means some pecuniary (economic) interest in the subject matter of insurance contract.

- A person is said to have an insurable interest in the property if he will be adversely affected by its destruction.
- The insured must either own a part or whole of the subject-matter of insurance or he must be in such a position that injury to it would affect him adversely. For example, a person has an insurable interest in his wife and children, but not in strangers.
- In case of insurance of property, insurable interest of the insured must exist at the time of entering into an insurance contract. However, it is not necessary that the insured must own that property at the time of insurance.
- The concept of 'Insurable Interest' is different in different contract of insurance.
(i) Life Insurance: Insurable Interest must be present at the time of contracti Suppose, a person took life insurance policy of his wife from LIC. After one year, the couple got divorced and after two years, his wife met with an accident and died on the spot. In the given case, the given person is entitled to the compensation from LIC.
(ii) Fire Insurance: Insurable interest must be present at the time of taking the policy is well as at the time of loss by subject matter. Suppose, a person (say, Ramesh) took a fire insurance policy of his factory premises. In case of loss to the factory due to fire, Ramesh can claim compensation. However, if before the fire, Ramesh sells his factory to another person (say, Ankit), then Ankit cannot claim compensation.
(iii) Marine Insurance: Insurable Interest must be there at the time of occurrence of loss.

3. Indemnity: The term 'indemnity' means security against loss. The purpose of this principle is to put the insured, in the event of loss, in the same position that he occupied immediately before the happening of the event.

- It means, the insured is entitled to recover the extent of the loss suffered by him, up to the limit of the amount covered by the policy.
- The insured is not allowed to make any profit on the happening of an event. The compensation payable and the loss suffered are to be measured in terms of money.
- All insurance contracts of fire or marine insurance are contracts of indemnity.

- For example, if a person has insured his house against fire for ₹ 1,50,000 and he suffers a loss of $₹ 1,00,000$, then the insurer will pay him $₹ 1,00,000$ and not the policy amount of ₹ 1,50,000.
It must be noted that 'Principle of Indemnity' is not applicable to life insurance as loss arising on death of the assured cannot be measured in terms of money.

4. Proximate Cause: Causes of loss may be of two kinds: (a) Insured Perils; and (b) Uninsured Perils. According to this principle, the insurer is liable for the loss only when such loss is proximately caused by the perils, which are stated in the policy.

- When a loss occurs due to two or more causes, then proximate cause is the one which is the most dominant and most effective cause. Insurer is not liable for any uninsured causes or perils.
- For example, Rohit took a marine policy to protect his goods from perils of sea. On the way, the goods were spoiled by rats. In such case, no compensation will be given as loss caused due to rats is a remote cause and not the proximate cause. However, if the rats make a hole in the ship and water enters from the hole and spoils the goods, then Rohit is entitled to the compensation as loss caused due to water is a proximate cause.

5. Subrogation: According to this principle, the insurer gets all the rights against the third party with respect to the subject-matter insured, after compensating the loss of the insured.
For example, if an insured car has been stolen, then the insurer will compensate the loss as the car was covered by insurance. However, if the car is recovered later, then the insurer will have all the rights on that car. This is because the insured should not be allowed to make any profit, by selling the lost car being recovered.
It must be noted that 'Principle of Subrogation' applies to all insurance contracts, which are contracts of indemnity.
6. Contribution: According to this principle, insurer who has paid the claim amount under an insurance policy has the right to recover the proportionate contribution from the other insurer. The insured can take more than one policy on the same property.

- In case there is a loss on such property, then the insured has no right to recover more than the full amount of his actual loss.
- If he recovers the full amount of his actual loss from one insurer, he will cease to have any right to obtain further payment from the other insurer.
- In such a case, the principle of contribution will apply, i.e. the insurer who has paid the compensation will recover the proportionate contribution from the other insurer.
- For example, Harish insures his house for ₹ 2,00,000 with insurer X and for ₹ 4,00,000 with insurer Y. Harish incurs a loss for ₹ 2,40,000. Now, liability of X and Y will be calculated as follows:
$\overline{\text { Liability of } X}=\frac{\text { Sum insured with } X}{\text { Total Sum insured }} \times \text { Actual Loss }$

$$
=\frac{2,00,000}{6,00,000} \times 2,40,000=\$ 80,000
$$

Liability of $Y=\frac{4,00,000}{6,00,000} \times 2,40,000=\$ 1,60,000$
'Principle of Contribution' ensures equitable distribution of losses between insurers.
7. Mitigation: According to this principle, it is the duty of the insured to take reasonable steps to minimise the loss or damage to the insured property.

- This principle aims at making sure that the insured behaves as a prudent person and does not become careless after taking a policy to cover any risk.
- If reasonable care is not taken then the claim from the insurance company may be lost.
- For example, if goods kept in a warehouse are covered by a Fire Policy and the warehouse catches fire, then the insured must try to recover the goods and save them from fire to minimise the loss or damage.
