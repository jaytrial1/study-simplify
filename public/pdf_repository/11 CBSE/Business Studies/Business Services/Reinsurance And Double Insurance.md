# Reinsurance and Double Insurance 

## Reinsurance

It is a contract of insurance, in which an insurer enters into a contract with another insurer to insure the whole or a part of risk covered by the first insurer. It happens when an insurance company feels that it cannot bear the entire risk alone by itself. In such case, it transfers a part of the risk to other insurance companies.
Example: A insures his house for ₹ 50 lakhs with insurer $X$ at a premium of ₹ 10,000. $X$ feels that the risk of ₹ 50 lakhs is beyond its capacity. As a result, $X$ gets the same house insured from $Y$. Such a contract between $X$ (original insurance company) and $Y$ is known as reinsurance.
Reinsurance contracts are also governed by the same principles which apply to the original contract of insurance. Since contracts of reinsurance are based on principle of indemnity, they deal with reinsurance in fire, marine and general insurance cases.

## Double Insurance

When more than one insurance policy is taken to cover the same risk, then it is known as Double Insurance.

Impact of Double Insurance
- Life Insurance: Double insurance may be profitable in case of life insurance as insured can claim full policy money under all the polices. A person can take up any number of life polices for any amount and at the time of maturity, he shall be entitled to claim money under all the policies.
- Fire and Marine Insurance: When the same subject matter is insured with more than one insurer, then insured can claim his loss from each of the insurer. However, the total claim cannot exceed the amount of actual loss or the total insured value in aggregate, whichever is less. The insurers will bear the claim in their respective proportions (refer example given in Principle of Contribution).

## Other Insurance 

1. Motor Vehicle Insurance: This policy is taken to cover risk of theft or damage of car, injuries to or death of any passenger or injuries to or death of third parties. The owner of a motor car may take a comprehensive policy which will cover all these risks. The rate of premium is standardised.
2. Burglary Insurance: This type of policy protects the insured from loss caused to him on account of burglary, house-breaking or theft. The insurable interest must exist at the time of loss but not necessarily at the time when policy was taken and principle of causa proxima will also apply.
3. Cattle Insurance: In this contract, a sum of money is secured to the assured in the event of death of animals like bulls, buffaloes, cows, etc.
4. Crop Insurance: This contract is taken to provide financial support to farmers in the event of a crop failure due to drought or flood.
5. Sports Insurance: This policy assures a comprehensive cover available to amateur sportsmen covering their sporting equipment, personal effects, legal liability and personal accident risks. This cover is not available to professional sportsmen.
6. Amartya Sen Siksha Yojana: This policy secures the education of dependent children in case the insured parent or legal guardian sustains any bodily injury due to an accident and also such injury results in the death of the insured within twelve months. In such a case, the insurer shall indemnify the insured student, in respect of all covered expenses to be incurred from the date of occurrenceof such accident till the expiry date of policy or completion of the duration of covered course, whichever occurs first. Such indemnity should not exceed the amount stated in the policy.
7. Rajeswari Mahila Kalyan Bima Yojana: This policy provides relief to the family members of insured women in case of their death or disablement due to problems incidental to women only.
