# Life Insurance 

Life of all the individuals is uncertain as it is always exposed to some kind of risks. There may be risk of dying too early or dying too late. As a result, there is a need for life insurance policy to provide financial support to the family in case of death or during old age.
Life Insurance can be defined as a contract between an insurance policy holder and an insurance company, where the insurer promises to pay a sum of money (known as 'Sum Assured' or 'Cover Amount') in exchange for a premium, upon the death of an insured person (i.e. assured) or on the expiry of a certain fixed period (i.e. maturity time), whichever is earlier.

- In case of life insurance, the insured has to pay fixed amount of premium (which can be paid monthly, quarterly, half-yearly or annually) and in return, the sum assured is paid at the death or on maturity period, whichever comes earlier.
- If the person (i.e. assured) dies early, then his family gets compensation amount.
- If the assured survives, then he himself gets the compensation amount on the maturity period.
- When people have the fear of dying too early, then they take life insurance policy to provide financial support to their family after their death.
- When people have the fear of dying too late, then they take life insurance policy to get financial support and economic independence during their old age.
- Life insurance is not only a protection but is a sort of 'investment' also because a certain sum is returnable to the insured at the time of death or at the expiry of a certain period. It encourages savings as premium has to be paid regularly. Thus, it provides a sense of security to the insured and his dependents.
You Must Know this: Life Insurance should more properly be called 'Life Assurance' because the risk insured here is certain and natural. Only the time of occurrence is not certain.


## Essential Elements of Life Insurance Contract

The main elements of a life insurance contract are:

1. It must have all the essentials of a valid contract: Elements like existence of an agreement, free consent of the parties to that agreement, competence of the parties to contract and lawful consideration are necessary for a life insurance contract.
2. It is a contract of utmost good faith: The assured must disclose all material facts about his health to the insurer, even if the insurer does not ask him. He should be honest and truthful.
3. The insured must have insurable interest at the time of making the contract: Without the insurable interest, the contract is void. However, presence of insurable interest is not necessary at the time of maturity.
4. It is not a contract of indemnity: As loss arising on death of the assured cannot be measured in terms of money. That is why the amount payable is fixed, at the time of entering into the contract.
