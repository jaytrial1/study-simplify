# 3.2 PRIVATE SECTOR AND PUBLIC SECTOR

India is a Mixed Economy, where both private and public (government) enterprises are allowed to operate. Therefore, Indian economy may be classified into two sectors:

1. Private Sector: Private Sector includes all those enterprises which are managed and owned by individuals or group of individuals. As discussed in the previous chapter, the various forms of organisation under private sector are Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, Joint Hindu Family Business, Cooperative Society and Company.
2. Public Sector: Public Sector includes all those enterprises which are managed and owned partly or wholly by the Central or State Government. Such enterprises may also be a part of the ministry or come into existence by a Special Act of the Parliament. The government participates in the economic activities of the country through these enterprises.


The Industrial Policy Resolutions of 1948 and 1956 emphasised a dominant role for the public sector in order to achieve rapid industrialisation for the growth of the economy.

- A number of industries were exclusively reserved for the public sector. Entry of private sector was restricted through licensing.
- However, dominance of Public Sector for about 4 decades hampered the process of growth and development.
- As a result, India decided for a comprehensive reform of the economy in 1991. In the '1991 Industrial Policy', government opted for disinvestment of public sector, enhancing role of private sector and encouraging foreign direct investment.
- So, after the reforms of 1991, Indian Economy consists of public sector units, private sector enterprises and global enterprises.
