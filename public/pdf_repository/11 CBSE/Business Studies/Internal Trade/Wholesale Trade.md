# 9.3 WHOLESALE TRADE

Wholesale trade refers to buying and selling of goods and services in large quantities for the purpose of resale or intermediate use. The traders who deal in wholesale trade are known as wholesale traders. Wholesalers buy the goods directly from manufacturers in bulk and sell them in relative smaller quantities to the large number of retailers. Therefore, they serve as an important link between manufacturers and retailers.

## Features of Wholesale Trade

1. This trade provides link between the manufacturer and the retailer as there is usually no direct dealing with the ultimate consumers.
2. Wholesaler is the first intermediary in the distribution chain.
3. The wholesaler buys goods in bigger lots and sells in smaller lots.
4. It requires large capital to maintain large stock and to provide credit facilities to the retailers.
5. The wholesaler usually specialises in one particular line of goods for example, foodgrains, stationery, etc.
6. The margin of profit is generally low in order to take advantage of high turnover.
7. The wholesaler performs a number of functions and services such as:
(i) Grading of Products
(ii) Packing into smaller lots
(iii) Storage and Transportation
(iv) Promotion of Goods
(v) Collection of market information
8. The wholesaler bears the business risks by buying and selling goods in his/her own name.
9. Wholesaler store the goods in the godown and keeps only the samples in the shop.

## Services of Wholesalers 

Wholesalers provide various services to the 'manufacturers' as well as the 'retailers'.

## Services to Manufacturers

The wholesalers provide following services to manufacturers:

1. Facilitating Large-scale Production: As the wholesalers purchase goods from the manufacturers in bulk quantities, producers are able to undertake production on a largescale and take advantage of the economies of scale.
2. Bearing Risk: The wholesalers take delivery of goods from manufacturers and keep them in their own warehouses. In the process, they bear risk arising out of fall in prices, theft, spoilage, etc. and relieve the manufacturers from such risks.
3. Financial Assistance: Wholesalers generally purchase goods from manufacturers on cash basis and sometimes even give advance money for bulk orders. In this way, wholesalers provide financial help to the manufacturers as they are not required to block the capital in stock.
4. Expert Advice: As the wholesalers are in direct contact with the retailers, they keep informing the manufacturers about the changes in customers' tastes and preferences, market conditions, competitive activities, etc. Such market information helps the manufacturers to regulate the production as per the changes.
5. Help in the Marketing Function: The wholesaler collects order from large number of retailers and supply goods to them, who in turn sell the goods to consumers. As a result, wholesaler allows the manufacturer to concentrate more on production activity and relieves him from marketing of goods.
6. Facilitate Continuity: The wholesalers purchase the goods from producers as and when these are produced. It helps the manufacturers in maintaining continuity of production activity.
7. Storage: The wholesaler takes delivery of goods when these are produced in the factory and store them in their own warehouses. It relieves the manufacturer of the storing problems.

## Services to Retailers

The important services offered by wholesalers to the retailers are as follows:

1. Availability of Goods: The retailer has to maintain stock of large variety of products to meet demand of the customers. The wholesaler provides the products of various manufacturers to the retailers and saves them from keeping large quantity of each product.
2. Marketing Support: The wholesalers undertake advertisements and other marketing functions to induce customers to purchase the goods. Such advertising benefits the retailers in increasing the demand of various new products.
3. Grant of Credit: Wholesalers provide financial help to retailers by selling them goods on credit. It enables the retailers to manage their business with less working capital. The retailers generally pay to the wholesalers after receiving the payment from the customers.

4. Specialised Knowledge: Wholesalers specialise in a particular line of products and have expert knowledge of the market conditions relating to such products. They pass on the benefit of their specialised knowledge to the retailers and inform them about new products, their uses, quality, prices, etc. They may also advise on set up of the retail outlet.
Retailer cannot possess such knowledge as he deals in large number of products of different varieties.
5. Risk Sharing: Retailers purchase goods from the wholesalers in smaller quantities and that too on credit. It saves the retailers from assuming risk of spoilage, obsolescence, price and demand fluctuations and other damages.
6. Economies of Scale: Wholesaler enjoys the economies of bulk purchase. They purchase at lower prices and store goods during the period of low demand. Wholesaler shares a part of the benefits with the retailer.
7. Transportation Facility: Many wholesalers maintain their own transport for movement of goods from warehouses to the place of retailers. It makes retailers free from the problem of transportation of goods purchased by them.
