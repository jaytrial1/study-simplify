# Chain Stores or Multiple Shops

Chain stores or multiple shops are networks of retail shops, which are owned by the single business enterprise. These chain stores may be established by manufactures or traders. Its management is centralised. These multiple shops are established with similar appearance over different localities of the city or in different cities.
All the retail shops deal in similar line of standardised and branded consumer products. They all have identical merchandising strategies, with identical products and displays. Chain stores are very effective in handling high-volume merchandise, whose sales are relatively constant throughout the year. Bata, Pizza Hut, Cafe Coffee Day, Nirula's and Raymond's Showrooms are examples of multiple shops.

## Features of Chain Stores 

The important features of chain stores are as follows:

1. Location: These shops are located in populated localities, where sufficient number of customers can be approached. The object is to have shops near to the customers, rather than attracting them to a central place.
2. Centralised Purchases: The head office makes all the purchases for all these multiple shops. Thereafter, goods are dispatched to different shops as per their requirements. It saves the cost of operation of these shops.
3. Centralised Control: All stores are controlled by the head office, which formulates policies and get them implemented in different shops. Branch Manager is responsible for each retail shop and sends daily reports to the head office in respect of sales, cash deposits and the requirements of the stock.
4. Cash Sales: All the sales at the multiple shops are made on cash basis and cash is deposited daily into the bank account on behalf of the head office and a report is sent to the head office in this regard.
5. Regular Inspection: The head office normally appoints inspectors, who inspect the retails shops with respect to quality of customer service provided, adherence to policies of head office, and so on.
6. Prices: Goods are sold at all the stores at a centrally fixed price.
7. Window Dressing: All the stores are decorated in the same setting and style.

### Advantages of Chain Stores

1. Economies of Scale: The chain stores enjoy economies of scale as goods are purchased or produced in bulk. It results in availing the benefits of large scale operations.
2. Elimination of Middlemen: It enables the manufacturer to eliminate the profits of middlemen as goods are brought directly from the manufacturers and sold to the final consumers.
3. No Bad Debts: All the sales in these shops are made on cash basis. So, there is no fear of bad debts.

4. Transfer of Goods: The shortage of goods at one branch can be met through transfer of goods from other branches operating in the same city. Moreover, goods which are not in demand at one branch can be easily transferred to other branches with ready demand for them.
5. Diffusion of Risk: The loss incurred by one shop can easily be absorbed by profits made in other shops. It reduces the total risk of an organisation.
6. Low Cost: Multiple shops generally operate on lower costs because of centralised purchasing, elimination of middlemen, centralised promotion of sales and no risk of bad debts due to cash sales.
7. Flexibility: The multiple shop system has high degree of flexibility as the loss making retail shops can be closed down or shifted to other locations, without having much impact on the profitability of the organisation.

### Limitations of Chain Stores 

1. Limited Selection of Goods: Consumers get only a limited choice of goods as multiple shops deal in a limited line of products of uniform quality, which are mostly produced by the marketers. They do not sell products of other manufacturers.
2. Lack of Initiative: The branch managers do not enjoy any discretion and have to obey the instructions received from the head office. It leads to lack of initiative, imagination and determination in increasing the sales.
3. Lack of Personal Touch: There is lack of personal touch in case of multiple shops as they are managed by hired employees, who do not care much for the customers' satisfaction.
4. Difficult to Change Demand: The purchases are centralised and are made on a large scale at the head office. In case, there is a sudden change in the fashion or taste of the consumers, there is the risk of dead stock resulting in heavy losses.
5. No Credit Facility: The multiple shops sell goods on cash basis only. Lack of credit facilities discourages certain class of customers.
6. Huge Capital Requirement: Establishment of chain stores requires huge investment both in fixed assets as well as in current assets. So, chain stores may be run only by a company which has considerable financial resources.


Difference between Departmental Stores and Multiple Shops

| Basis | Departmental Stores | Multiple Shops or Chain Stores |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Location | Departmental stores are centrally located in the city. | Multiple shops are located in every important locality of the city. |
| Range of Products | Departmental stores deal in a large variety of products as they aim to satisfy all needs of the customers under one roof. | Multiple shops are specialised in only one line of product. |
| Services Offered | They provide numerous services to the customers, like liberal credit, free home delivery, etc. | Multiple shops do not offer such services. They provide limited services with respect to guarantee and repairs of the product. |
| Pricing | They do not have uniform pricing policy for all the departments. Discount is offered on certain products to clear the stock. | They sell goods at fixed prices and maintain uniform pricing policies for all the shops. |
| Class of Customers | They generally attract customers belonging to higher income group. | They attract customers from all income groups. |
| Credit Facilities | They provide credit facilities to regular and reputed customers. | No credit facility is provided as all sales are made in cash only. |
| Flexibility | There is more flexibility as policies can be changed as per the requirements. | There is less flexibility as branch managers have to operate as per instructions received from the head office. |
| Risk | Risk is more as it centered on one shop only. | The risk is divided over all the shops. |
| Advertising | They advertise locally as they are usually located at one place. | They advertise to cover a larger area as multiple shops are spread throughout the state or country. |
