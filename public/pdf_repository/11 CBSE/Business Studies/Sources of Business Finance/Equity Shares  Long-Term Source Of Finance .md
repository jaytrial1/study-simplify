# Equity Shares (Long-term Source of Finance)

Equity shares are the most important source of raising long-term capital by a company. Equity shares are those shares which do not carry any special or preferential rights in respect of payment of annual dividend and repayment of capital. The money raised by issue of equity shares is termed as "Equity Share Capital"
It represents the owners' capital as equity shareholders are the real owners of the company. Thus, the capital raised by the issue of such shares is known as ownership capital or owners' funds.

## Features of Equity Shares

1. Risk bearer capital: The equity shareholders are the primary risk bearers. They enjoy the reward as well as bear the risk of ownership.
2-Maturity: Equity shares provide permanent capital to the company and cannot be redeemed during the life time of the company.
3. Return: The return earned by equity shareholders is known as 'dividend', which varies with the earnings of the company.
4. Claim over residual income: Equity shareholders are referred to as 'residual owners' as they get dividend, only after all other claims on the company's income and assets have been settled.
5. Voting rights: Equity shareholders have voting rights in proportion to the shares held by shareholders. They have a right to participate in the company's management.
6. Limited Liability: The liability of equity shareholders is limited to the extent of capital contributed by them in the company.

## Merits of Equity Shares

The merits of raising funds through issuing equity shares are as follows:

1. Ideal for adventurous investors: Equity shares are suitable for investors who are willing to assume risk for higher returns.
2. No obligation as to dividend: There is no burden or obligation on the company to pay dividend as it depends on the profits available and the decision of directors and members.
3. Source of fixed capital: Equity capital serves as permanent capital as it is to be repaid only at the time of liquidation of a company.
4. Provides credit standing: Equity capital provides credit worthiness to the company and confidence to prospective loan providers.
5. No charge on assets: Equity shares do not carry any charge against the assets of the company. As a result, assets can be freely mortgaged for the purpose of borrowings.
6. Democratic management: Equity shareholders enjoy voting rights, which ensures democratic management of the company.

## Limitations of Equity Shares

Equity shares as a source of business finance suffer from the following limitations:

1. Risk of fluctuating returns: Equity shares are not suitable for investors who desire to earn stable return on their investments as equity shares get fluctuating returns.
2. High cost of capital: The cost of equity shares is generally more as compared to the cost of raising funds through other sources.
3. Dilution of control: The additional issue of equity shares dilutes the control and earnings of existing equity shareholders.
4. Legal formalities: A company has to comply with number of legal formalities before issue of equity shares to the general public.
5. Danger of over-capitalisation: Equity share capital is a permanent source of capital. If the company has raised excess equity capital due to faulty financial planning, then company may get into a state of over-capitalisation. In such a situation, equity capital may remain idle and underutilised.
