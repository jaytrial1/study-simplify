# Features of Debentures 

1. Borrowed Fund: Debentures constitute the borrowed funds of a company. Therefore, debenture holders are termed as creditors of the company.
2. Periodic Interest Payment: Debenture holders are paid fixed rate of interest at specified intervals, say six months or one year.
3. Compulsory Payment of Interest: Payment of interest is a legal compulsion on the company. It has to be paid whether the profits are earned or not.
4. No Voting Rights: Debenture holders do not have voting rights, i.e. they cannot participate in the management of the company. So, debentures do not dilute the control of equity shareholders.
5. Redemption of debentures: Debentures have to be redeemed or paid back after the expiry of a fixed period or when the company is in the process of winding up.
6. Economical: Raising finance through debentures is less costly as compared to preference or equity capital as interest payment on debentures is a tax deductible expense.
7. Security: Generally, debentures are secured by charge on or mortgage of the assets of the company, which can be realised in the event of default of the company.
Public issue of debentures requires that the issue be rated by a credit rating agency like CRISIL (Credit Rating and Information Services of India Ltd.) on aspects like track record of the company, profitability, debt servicing capacity, credit worthiness and perceived risk of lending.

## Types of Debentures

1. Secured Debentures: The debentures, which are secured by a charge on the assets or property of the company, are known as secured debentures. If the company fails to pay the interest or principal amount, then debenture holders can sell the assets in order to satisfy their claims. They are also known as 'Mortgage Debentures'.
2. Unsecured Debentures: The debentures, which do not carry any charge or security on the assets of the company, are known as unsecured debentures. In case of default in payment by the company, they can only file a suit for recovery of money. They are also known as 'Simple Debentures' or 'Naked Debentures'.
3. Registered Debentures: The debentures, in which the names of debenture holders are duly recorded in the register maintained by the company, are known as registered debentures. Such debentures can be transferred only by a transfer deed and not by mere delivery.
4. Bearer Debentures: The debentures, which can be transferred by mere delivery, are known as bearer debentures. The company does not keep any record of such debenture holders. Interest due is paid on showing coupons attached to the debentures.
5. Convertible Debentures: The debentures, which can be converted into equity shares after the expiry of a specified period, are known as convertible debentures.
6. Non-Convertible Debentures: The debentures, which cannot be converted into equity shares, are known as non-convertible debentures.
7. First Debentures: The debentures, which are repaid before other debentures are repaid, are known as first debentures.
8. Second Debentures: The debentures, which are paid after the first debentures have been paid back, are known as second debentures.
9. Zero Interest Debentures (ZID): The debentures, which do not carry any explicit rate of interest, are known as zero interest debentures. The difference between the face value of the debenture and its purchase price is the return to the investor.

## Merits of Debentures 

The merits of raising funds through issue of debentures are as follows:

1. Ideal for safe investors: It is preferred by investors who want fixed income at lesser risk.
2. No loss of control: Debentures do not dilute the control of equity shareholders on management as they do not carry voting rights.
3. Flexibility: Debentures provide financial flexibility as they can be redeemed when the company has surplus funds. It minimises the risk of over-capitalisation.
4. Economical: Raising finance through debentures is less costly as compared to preference or equity capital as interest payment on debentures is a tax deductible expense.
5. Suitable during stable earnings: The issue of debentures is suitable in the situation when the sales and earnings are relatively stable.
6. Tax benefits: Interest paid on debentures is allowed as deduction in computation of taxable income. This results in tax relief for the company.
7. Trading on equity: Debenture holders are paid fixed rate of interest. After payment of interest, the remaining profits are available to shareholders. When the earnings of the company increase, the company can take advantage of Trading on equity by increasing the dividend rate on equity shares.

## Demerits of Debentures

Debentures as source of finance suffer from following limitations:

1. Permanent burden of interest: The debentures put a permanent obligation on the company to pay interest irrespective of profits. There is a greater risk during fluctuating earnings.
2. Adverse impact on borrowing capacity: The issue of debentures reduces the capacity of the company to further borrow funds. Large amount of debentures also reduces the credit standing of the company.
3. Repayment obligation: The redeemable debentures have to be paid back on the specified date, even if the company is facing difficulties.
4. Charge on assets: Generally, debentures are issued against security of assets of the company. In the event of default, the debenture holders may attach the pledged assets.

Difference between Shares and Debentures

| Basis | Shares | Debentures |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Nature of fund | Shares are considered as the part of owners' funds of the company. | Debentures are considered as the part of borrowed fund of the company. |
| Status | Shareholders are the owners of the company. | Debenture holders are the creditors of the company. |
| Nature of return | Shares get return in the form of dividend. | Debentures get return in the form of interest. |
| Rate of return | Dividend fluctuates depending on residual profits. | Interest rate is fixed irrespective of profits. |
| Security of assets | Shares do not create any charge on the assets of the company. | Debentures usually have a charge on the assets of the company |
| Control | Shareholders enjoy voting rights and can exercise control over the management of the company. | Debentures do not carry any voting rights. So, debenture holders do not have any control over the company. |
| Degree of risk | Shareholders bear a high degree of risk. | Debenture holders bear minimum degree of risk. |
| Redemption | Shares are not redeemable during the existence of a company (except for redeemable preference shares). | Debentures are generally redeemed after a specified period. |
| Effect on borrowing capacity | Issue of equity shares increases the borrowing capacity of a company. | Issue of debentures reduces the borrowing capacity of a company. |
