# Indian Depository Receipts (IDRs) (Long-term Source of Finance)

IDR is a financial instrument denominated in Indian Rupees in the form of a Depository Receipt created by an Indian Depository to enable a foreign company to raise funds from the Indian securities market. IDR is a specific Indian version of the similar global depository receipts.

## Important Facts about IDR

- Issuer: In an IDR, issuer is a foreign company, which wishes to raise funds from the Indian market. Foreign companies issue shares to an Indian Depository (custodian of securities registered with the Securities and Exchange Board of India), which would in turn issue depository receipts to investors in India. The issuer company intending to issue IDRs should have paid up capital and free reserve of atleast $\$ 100$ million.
- Benefits of IDR: The benefits of the underlying shares (like bonus, dividends, etc.) accrue to the IDR holders in India.
- Method of Issue: According to SEBI guidelines, IDRs are issued to Indian residents in the same way as domestic shares are issued. The issuer company makes a public offer in India and residents can bid in exactly the same format and method as they bid for Indian shares.
- Approvals for issue of IDRs: IDR issue will require approval from SEBI and application can be made for this purpose 90 days before the issue opening date.
- Listing: These IDRs would be listed on stock exchanges in India and would be freely transferable.
- Denomination: IDRs would be denominated in Indian rupees, irrespective of the denomination of underlying shares.
- Example: 'Standard Chartered PLC' was the first company that issued Indian Depository Receipt in Indian securities market in June, 2010.
