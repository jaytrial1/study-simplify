# Difference between Owners' Funds and Borrowed Funds

| Basis | Owners' Funds | Borrowed Funds |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| Meaning | It refers to the funds provided by the owner <br> of the business enterprise. | It refers to the funds raised through loans <br> or borrowings. |
| Period of Investment | It is a permanent source of capital for business | Borrowed fund is for a fixed period of time. |
| Requirement of security | It does not require any security. | Borrowed funds are generally provided on the security of some fixed assets. |
| Control | The providers of Owners' Funds have a control over the management of business. | The providers of borrowed funds do not get the right to control. |
| Risk of Refund | There is a risk involved in the refund of Owners' Funds as they are the primary risk bearer. | There is negligible risk involved in the refund of Borrowed Funds. |
| Return | Return on Owners' Funds (like equity and preference shares) is known as dividend, which varies with earnings of the company. | The providers of borrowed funds get interest at a fixed rate at regular intervals. |
| Sources | Equity Shares, Preference Shares, Retained Earnings, etc. | Debentures \& Bonds, Loans, Inter-Corporate Deposits, Trade Credit, Public Deposits, etc. |
