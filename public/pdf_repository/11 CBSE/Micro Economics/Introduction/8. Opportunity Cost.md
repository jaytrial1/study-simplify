# 8. Opportunity Cost

## 1.8 OPPORTUNITY COST

As resources are scarce, society is always forced to make choices. To produce more of one good, a certain amount of other goods has to be sacrificed. The true cost of using economic resources in any given project is the loss of the alternative output that they might have produced.

For example, if we use a certain amount of land, labor, and capital to build a factory, then the economic cost (or opportunity cost) of the factory might be the houses that these resources could have produced.

Hence, Opportunity Cost is the cost of the next best alternative foregone. For example, suppose you are working in a bank at a salary of ₹ 40,000 per month. Further suppose, you receive two more job offers:

- To work as an executive at ₹ 30,000 per month; or
- To become a journalist at ₹ 35,000 per month.

In the given case, the opportunity cost of working in the bank is the cost of the next best alternative foregone, i.e., ₹ 35,000. The amount of other goods and services that must be sacrificed to obtain more of any one good is called the opportunity cost of that good.

### One More Example

Suppose you have ₹ 40,000 and you want to purchase one laptop and an LED TV. With only ₹ 40,000 in hand, you cannot have both. You can either buy a laptop or an LED TV. If you decide to purchase a laptop, then the opportunity cost of choosing the laptop is the cost of the foregone satisfaction (from the LED TV). This example clearly demonstrates a fundamental economic condition: "As our resources are limited, we are always forced to make choices between alternate commodities."
