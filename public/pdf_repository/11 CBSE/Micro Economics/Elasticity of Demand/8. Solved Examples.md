# Formulae at a Glance

## 1. Percentage Method / Proportionate Method

$$
\text { Elasticity of Demand }\left(E_{d}\right)=\frac{\text { Percentage Change in Quantity Demanded }}{\text { Percentage Change in Price }}
$$

Elasticity of Demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q} \quad$ OR $\frac{1}{\text { Slope of Demand Curve }} \times \frac{P}{Q}$
(Slope of Demand Curve $=\Delta P / \Delta Q$ )

## 2. Total Expenditure Method (Or Total Outlay Method or Total Revenue Method)
(i) $E_{d}<1$ : When TE varies directly with price.
(ii) $E_{d}>1$ : When TE varies inversely with price.
(iii) $E_{d}=1$ : When TE does not change with change in price.

## Calculation of Elasticity of Demand (When both price and quantity are given)

### Example 1
When price is ₹ 10 per unit, demand for a commodity is 100 units. As the price falls to ₹ 8 per unit, demand expands to 150 units. Calculate elasticity of demand.

**Solution:**

| Original Quantity (Q) | $=100$ units | Original Price $(P)$ | $=$ | ₹ 10 |
| :---------------------- | :------------- | :--------------------- | :-: | :----- |
| New Quantity $\left(Q_{1}\right)$ | $=150$ units | New Price $\left(P_{1}\right)$ | $=$ | ₹ 8 |
| Change in Quantity $(\Delta Q)$ | $=50$ units | Change in Price $(\Delta P)$ | $=$ | -₹ 2 |
| Elasticity of Demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=$ ? |  |  |  |  |

Price Elasticity of demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}=\frac{50}{-2} \times \frac{10}{100}=(-) 2.5$

Ans. $E_{d}=(-) 2.5 \quad$ (Demand is highly elastic as $E_{d}>1$ )

Negative sign of $E_{d}$ indicates the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

Negative sign of $E_{d}$ indicates the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

## Calculation of Price or Quantity (When Elasticity of demand is given)

### Example 4
The market demand for a good at ₹ 4 per unit is 100 units. Due to increase in price, the market demand falls to 75 units. Find out the new price, if the price elasticity of demand is $(-)$1.

**Solution:**

| Original Quantity $(Q)$ | $=100$ units | Original Price $(P)$ | $=$ | ₹ 4 |
| :---------------------- | :------------- | :--------------------- | :-: | :---- |
| New Quantity $\left(Q_{1}\right)$ | $=75$ units | New Price $\left(P_{1}\right)$ | $=$ | $?$ |
| Change in Quantity $(\Delta Q)$ | $=-25$ units | Change in Price $(\Delta P)$ | $=$ | $\Delta P$ |
| Elasticity of Demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=(-) 1$ |  |  |  |  |

Price Elasticity of demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$

$-1=\frac{-25}{\Delta P} \times \frac{4}{100} \Rightarrow \Delta P=₹ 1$

As the quantity demanded is decreasing, price will increase. It means,
New Price $=$ Original Price $(P)+$ Change in Price $(\Delta P)=4+1=₹ 5$

Ans. New Price $=₹ 5$

| :---------------------- | :------------- | :--------------------- | :-: | :---- |

| Elasticity of Demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=2$ |  |  |  |  |

Price Elasticity of demand $\left(E_{d}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$

$2=\frac{40}{\Delta P} \times \frac{5}{100} \Rightarrow \Delta P=₹ 1$

As the quantity demanded is increasing, price will decrease. It means,
New Price $=$ Original Price $(P)$ - Change in Price $(\Delta P)=₹ 5-₹ 1=₹ 4$

Ans. New Price $=₹ 4$
