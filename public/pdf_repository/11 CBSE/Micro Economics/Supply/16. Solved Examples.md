## Example 1

The supply schedule for three firms ($\mathrm{X}, \mathrm{Y}$ and Z) are given below. Derive the market supply schedule:

| Price (₹) | Firm $X$ | Firm $Y$ | Firm $Z$ |
| :---------: | :-------: | :-------: | :-------: |
|      1      |     5     |     7     |     10     |
|      2      |     7     |     9     |     12     |
|      3      |    10     |    15     |    18     |
|      4      |    15     |    20     |    25     |
|      5      |    20     |    30     |    27     |

### Solution:

| Price (₹) | Firm $X$ (units) | Firm $Y$ (units) | Firm $Z$ (units) | Market Supply (units) |
| :---------: | :---------------: | :---------------: | :---------------: | :-------------------: |
|      1      |         5         |         7         |         10        |           22          |
|      2      |         7         |         9         |         12        |           28          |

|      4      |        15         |        20         |        25        |           60          |
|      5      |        20         |        30         |        27        |           77          |

Market Supply = Firm X's supply + Firm Y's supply + Firm Z's supply

## Example 4

With the help of supply function: $Q_{x}=-10+2 p$, answer the following questions: (i) Calculate supply at price of ₹ 7 ; (ii) At what price, supply will be 0 ; (iii) Calculate the price at which firm will be willing to supply 50 units.

### Solution:

(i) $Q_{x}=-10+2 p$. Putting the value of price (i.e. ₹ 7), we get: $Q_{x}=-10+2 \times 7=4$ units

(ii) To get the price at which supply will be zero, put 0 as value of supply: $0=-10+2 p$. It means, $p=5$.

(iii) To get the price, when supply is 50 units, put 50 as value of supply: $50=-10+2 p$. It means, $p=30$.

## Example 5

Suppose there are two producers in the market for a commodity and their supply functions are as follows:

- $Q_{1}=-30+3 p$ for any price more than or equal to 10 , and $Q_{1}=0$ at any price less than 10 .
- $Q_{2}=-20+2 p$ for any price more than or equal to 10 , and $Q_{2}=0$ at any price less than 10 .

Find out the market supply function.

### Solution:

From the given supply functions, it can be seen that both the producers do not want to supply the commodity for any price less than ₹ 10 . Hence, the market supply will be:

$Q_{\text {Market }}=Q_{1}+Q_{2}$

$Q_{\text {Market }}=(-30+3 p)+(-20+2 p)$

$Q_{\text {Market }}=-50+5 p$ for any price more than or equal to 10 and $Q_{\text {Market }}=0$ at any price less than 10.

# Practicals on Elasticity of Supply

## Formula of Elasticity of Supply

Elasticity of Supply $\left(E_{s}\right)=\frac{\text { Percentage Change in Quantity Supplied }}{\text { Percentage Change in Price }}$

Elasticity of Supply $\left(E_{s}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q} \quad$ OR $\frac{1}{\text { Slope of Supply Curve* }} \times \frac{P}{Q}$

(Slope of Supply Curve* $=\Delta P / \Delta Q$ )

## Calculation of Elasticity of Supply (When both price and quantity are given)

### Example 6

If price of a commodity falls from ₹ 60 per unit to ₹ 58 per unit, its supply falls from 400 units to 300 units. Find out its elasticity of supply.

#### Solution:

| Original Quantity (Q) | = | 400 units | Original Price (P) | = | ₹ 60 |
| :-------------------: | :-: | :--------: | :----------------: | :-: | :----: |
| New Quantity $\left(Q_{1}\right)$ | = | 300 units | New Price $\left(P_{1}\right)$ | = | ₹ 58 |
| Change in Quantity $(\Delta Q)$ | = | 100 units | Change in Price $(\Delta P)$ | = | ₹ 2 |

Elasticity of Supply $\left(E_{s}\right)=$ ?

Price Elasticity of Supply $\left(E_{s}\right)=\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}=\frac{100}{2} \times \frac{60}{400}=7.5$

Ans. $E_{s}=7.5$ (Supply is highly elastic as $E_{s}>1$ )

$E_{s}$ is always positive due to direct relationship between price and quantity supplied

# Example 28

A producer received $₹ 6,000$ when the price of a commodity was $₹ 60$ per unit. The receipts increased to $₹ 8,400$ when price increased by $₹ 10$. Calculate the elasticity of supply?

**Solution:**

| Price $(\boldsymbol{\ell})$ | Total Receipts ( $\boldsymbol{\ell}$ ) | Quantity in units (Total Receipts + Price) |
| :-----------------------: | :-----------------------------------: | :--------------------------------------------: |
| 60 | 6,000 | 100 |

$E_{s}$ is always positive due to direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.
