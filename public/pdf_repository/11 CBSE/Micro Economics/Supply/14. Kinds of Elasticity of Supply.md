# Price Elasticities of Supply

Different commodities respond differently to a given change in price. Depending upon the degree of responsiveness of the quantity supplied to the price change, there are five kinds of price elasticities of supply.

## 1. Perfectly Elastic Supply

When there is an infinite supply at a particular price and the supply becomes zero with a slight fall in price, then the supply of such a commodity is said to be perfectly elastic. In such a case $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=\infty$ and the supply curve is a horizontal straight line parallel to the X -axis, as shown in Fig. 9.20:

**Interpretation:**

A perfectly elastic supply curve indicates that producers are willing to supply an unlimited quantity of a good or service at a specific price (P). Any increase in price above "P" would result in no quantity being supplied, while any decrease in price would result in an infinite quantity being supplied (theoretically). In reality, perfectly elastic supply is a theoretical concept but useful for illustrating extreme sensitivity to price changes.*

To illustrate the percentage / proportionate method, let us consider an example:

**Example:** Suppose, at the price of ₹ 10 per unit, a firm supplies 50 units of a commodity. When the price rises to ₹ 12 per unit, the firm increases the supply to 70 units.
The price elasticity of supply will be calculated as:
Price Elasticity of Supply $\left(\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\right)=\frac{\text { Percentage Change in Quantity Supplied }}{\text { Percentage Change in Price }}$
Now,
Percentage change in Quantity Supplied $=\frac{\text { Change in Quantity Supplied }(\Delta \mathrm{Q})}{\text { Initial Quantity Supplied }(\mathrm{Q})} \times 100$

$$
=\frac{(70-50)}{50} \times 100=40 \%
$$

Percentage change in Price $=\frac{\text { Change in Price }(\Delta \mathrm{P})}{\text { Initial Price }(\mathrm{P})} \times 100$

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{(12-10)}{10} \times 100=20 \% \\
\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}} & =\frac{40 \%}{20 \%}=2
\end{aligned}
$$

## Elasticity is a 'Unit Free' Measure

The coefficient of price elasticity of supply is a pure number and is independent of price and quantity units. It happens because elasticity considers percentage change in price and quantity supplied.

## Price Elasticity of Supply is Positive

So far, we have seen that the concept of elasticity of supply is similar to the concept of elasticity of demand. However, there is one difference. Elasticity of supply will always have a positive sign as against the negative sign of elasticity of demand. It happens because of the direct relationship between price and quantity supplied.
