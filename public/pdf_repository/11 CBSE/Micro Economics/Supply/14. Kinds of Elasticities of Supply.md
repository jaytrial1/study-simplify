# Price Elasticity of Supply

Different commodities respond differently to a given change in price. Depending upon the degree of responsiveness of the quantity supplied to the price change, there are five kinds of price elasticities of supply.

## 1. Perfectly Elastic Supply

When there is an infinite supply at a particular price and the supply becomes zero with a slight fall in price, then the supply of such a commodity is said to be perfectly elastic. In such a case $\mathbf{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=\infty$ and the supply curve is a horizontal straight line parallel to the $X$-axis, as shown in Fig. 9.20:

## 2. Perfectly Inelastic Supply

When the supply does not change with change in price, then supply for such a commodity is said to be perfectly inelastic.
In such a case, $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=0$ and the supply curve (SS) is a vertical straight line parallel to the Y-axis as shown in Fig. 9.21.

Table 9.9: Perfectly Inelastic Supply

| Price <br> (in ₹) | Supply <br> (in units) |
| :----------------: | :---------------------: |
|         20         |           20            |
|         30         |           20            |
|         40         |           20            |

Quantity supplied remains same at 20 units, whether the price is ₹ 20, ₹ 30 or ₹ 40. As seen in the diagram, quantity supplied remains the same at OQ, with change in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$ or $\mathrm{OP}_{2}$. It must be noted that perfectly inelastic supply is an imaginary situation.

## 3. Highly Elastic Supply

When percentage change in quantity supplied is more than the percentage change in price, then supply for such a commodity is said to be highly elastic. In such a case, $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}>1$ and the supply curve has an intercept on the Y -axis as shown in Fig. 9.22.

Table 9.10: Highly Elastic Supply

| Price <br> (in ₹) | Supply <br> (in units) |
| :----------------: | :---------------------: |
|         10         |           100           |
|         15         |           200           |

As seen in the schedule, the quantity supplied rises by $100 \%$ due to a $50 \%$ rise in price. In Fig. 9.22, the quantity supplied rises from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ with rise in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$. As $\mathrm{QQ}_{1}$ is proportionately more than $\mathrm{PP}_{1}$, elasticity of supply is more than 1.

## 4. Less Elastic Supply

When percentage change in quantity supplied is less than the percentage change in price, then supply for such a commodity is said to be less elastic.

In such a case, $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}<1$ and the supply curve has an intercept on the X -axis as shown in Fig. 9.23.

Table 9.11: Less Elastic Supply

| Price <br> (in ₹) | Supply <br> (in units) |
| :----------------: | :---------------------: |
|         10         |           100           |
|         15         |           120           |

In Table 9.11, the quantity supplied rises by $20 \%$ due to $50 \%$ rise in price. In Fig. 9.23, the quantity supplied rises from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ with rise in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$. As $\mathrm{QQ}_{1}$ is proportionately less than $\mathrm{PP}_{1}$, elasticity of supply is less than 1.

## 5. Unitary Elastic Supply

When percentage change in quantity supplied is equal to percentage change in price, then supply for such a commodity is said to be unitary elastic. In such a case, $\mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=1$ and supply curve is a straight line passing through the origin as shown in Fig. 9.24.

Table 9.12: Unitary Elastic Supply

| Price <br> (in ₹) | Supply <br> (in units) |
| :----------------: | :---------------------: |
|         10         |           100           |
|         15         |           150           |

In Table 9.12, the quantity supplied also rises by $50 \%$ due to $50 \%$ rise in price. In Fig. 9.24, the quantity supplied rises from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ with rise in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$. As $\mathrm{QQ}_{1}$ is proportionately equal to $\mathrm{PP}_{1}, \mathrm{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=1$.

# Important Observations

1.  All the supply curves, which pass through the origin are unitary elastic: In Fig. 9.25, A, B and C are the supply curves of three different commodities. The price elasticity of supply for all 3 curves is equal to one. Although $A$ is steeper and $C$ is flatter, but elasticity will be equal to one. It means, any straight line supply curve, which passes through the origin has unitary elastic supply, irrespective of the angle it makes with the origin.

# 2. Flatter the Curve, More is the Elasticity at the Point of Intersection

In Fig. 9.26, supply curves SS (flatter curve) and $\mathrm{S}_{1} \mathrm{~S}_{1}$ (steeper curve) intersect each other at point E .

*   At point $\mathrm{E}, \mathrm{OQ}$ quantity is supplied at the price of OP.
*   When price falls from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$, quantity supplied falls from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{2}$ for supply curve SS and from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ for supply curve $\mathrm{S}_{1} \mathrm{~S}_{1}$.
*   Now, with the same change in price $\left(\mathrm{PP}_{1}\right)$, change in supply $\left(\mathrm{QQ}_{2}\right)$ in case of supply curve SS is more than change in supply $\left(\mathrm{QQ}_{1}\right)$ under supply curve $\mathrm{S}_{1} \mathrm{~S}_{1}$.

It means supply is more elastic in case of SS (flatter curve) as compared to $\mathrm{S}_{1} \mathrm{~S}_{1}$ (steeper curve).

## Quick Recap - Coefficients of $E_{s}$

| Type              | Value                 | Description                         |
| :---------------- | :-------------------- | :---------------------------------- |
| Perfectly Elastic | $\left(E_{s}=\infty\right)$ | Infinite supply at same price      |
| Perfectly Inelastic | $\left(E_{s}=0\right)$    | Same supply at all prices           |
| Highly Elastic    | $\left(E_{s}>1\right)$    | $\% \Delta$ in Supply $>\% \Delta$ in Price |
| Less Elastic      | $\left(E_{s}<1\right)$    | $\% \Delta$ in Supply $<\% \Delta$ in Price |
| Unitary Elastic   | $\left(E_{s}=1\right)$    | $\% \Delta$ in Supply $=\% \Delta$ in Price |
