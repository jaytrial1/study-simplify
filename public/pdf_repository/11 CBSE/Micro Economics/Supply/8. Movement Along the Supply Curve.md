# MOVEMENT ALONG THE SUPPLY CURVE (CHANGE IN QUANTITY SUPPLIED)

When the quantity supplied of a commodity changes due to a change in its own price, keeping other factors constant, it is known as a 'change in quantity supplied'. It is graphically expressed as a movement along the same supply curve.

There can be either a downward movement (Contraction in supply) or an upward movement (Expansion in supply) along the same supply curve. Let us understand the movement along the supply curve with the help of Fig. 9.4:

**Title:** Movement Along the Supply Curve

**Axes:**

*   **X-axis:** Labeled "Quantity Supplied (in units)." It represents the quantity of a good or service that producers are willing and able to sell at a given price.
*   **Y-axis:** Labeled "Price (in ₹)," indicating the price of the good or service, measured in Rupees (₹).

**Curve:**

*   A positively sloped curve labeled "S-S" represents the supply curve. This indicates a direct relationship between price and quantity supplied; as the price increases, the quantity supplied also increases, and vice-versa.

**Points:**

*   **Point A:** This point lies on the supply curve "S-S". At point A, the price is P and the quantity supplied is Q.
*   **Point B:** This point also lies on the supply curve. Point B is above and to the right of point A, representing an increase in both price and quantity supplied. At Point B, the price is P1 and the quantity supplied is Q1.
*   **Point C:** This point also lies on the supply curve. Point C is below and to the left of point A, representing a decrease in both price and quantity supplied. At Point C, the price is P2 and the quantity supplied is Q2.

**Arrows:**

*   An arrow points from Point A to Point B. This arrow visually represents the movement along the supply curve due to an increase in price (from P to P1) and a corresponding increase in quantity supplied (from Q to Q1). This is referred to as an "extension of supply".
*   An arrow points from Point A to Point C. This arrow visually represents the movement along the supply curve due to a decrease in price (from P to P2) and a corresponding decrease in quantity supplied (from Q to Q2). This is referred to as a "contraction of supply".

**Overall Interpretation:**

The graph illustrates the concept of "movement along the supply curve." This movement occurs solely due to changes in the good's own price. When the price increases (from P to P1), the quantity supplied increases, and the movement is from A to B. Conversely, when the price decreases (from P to P2), the quantity supplied decreases, and the movement is from A to C. The supply curve itself (S-S) remains unchanged because factors other than price are held constant.*

*   Expansion in Supply is shown by an upward movement from A to B. Quantity supplied rises from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ due to a rise in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$
*   Contraction in Supply is shown by a downward movement from A to C. Quantity supplied falls from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{2}$ due to a fall in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{2}$

Fig. 9.4

In Fig. 9.4, OQ quantity is supplied at the price of OP. A change in price leads to an upward or downward movement along the same supply curve:

*   Upward Movement: When the price rises to $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$, the quantity supplied also rises to $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ (known as expansion in supply), leading to an upward movement from A to B along the same supply curve SS.
*   Downward Movement: On the other hand, a fall in price from OP to $\mathrm{OP}_{2}$ leads to a decrease in quantity supplied from OQ to $\mathrm{OQ}_{2}$ (known as contraction in supply), resulting in a downward movement from A to C along the same supply curve SS.

Let us now understand the meaning of Expansion and Contraction in Supply.

# Expansion in Supply

Expansion in Supply refers to a rise in the quantity supplied due to an increase in the price of the commodity, other factors remaining constant.

- It leads to an upward movement along the same supply curve.
- It is also known as 'Extension in Supply' or 'Increase in Quantity Supplied'.
- It can be better understood from Table 9.4 and Fig. 9.5.

## Table 9.4: Expansion in Supply

| Price (in ₹) | Quantity (in units) |
| :----------: | :-----------------: |
|      20      |         100         |
|      25      |         150         |

As seen in the given schedule and diagram, the quantity supplied rises from 100 units to 150 units, with an increase in price from ₹ 20 to ₹ 25, resulting in an upward movement from A to B, along the same supply curve SS.

# Contraction in Supply

Contraction in supply refers to a fall in the quantity supplied due to a decrease in the price of the commodity, other factors remaining constant.

- It leads to a downward movement along the same supply curve.
- It is also known as 'Decrease in Quantity Supplied'. It can be better understood from Table 9.5 and Fig. 9.6.

## Table 9.5: Contraction in Supply

| Price (in ₹) | Quantity (in units) |
| :----------: | :-------------------: |
|      20      |          100          |
|      15      |          70           |

As seen in the given schedule and diagram, quantity supplied falls from 100 units to 70 units, with a decrease in price from ₹ 20 to ₹ 15, resulting in a downward movement from A to B, along the same supply curve SS.
