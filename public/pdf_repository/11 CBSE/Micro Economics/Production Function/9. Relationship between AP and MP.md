# 5.9 RELATIONSHIP BETWEEN AP AND MP

The relationship between AP and MP is discussed through Table 5.4 and Fig. 5.4:

## Table 5.4: Relationship between AP and MP

| Fixed factor (Land) <br> (in acres) | Units of Variable factor <br> (labour) | AP <br> (units) | MP <br> (units) |
| :----------------------------------: | :---------------------------------------: | :-------------: | :-------------: |
|                  1                   |                     0                     |       -       |       -       |
|                  1                   |                     1                     |       10       |       10       |
|                  1                   |                     2                     |       15       |       20       |
|                  1                   |                     3                     |       15       |       15       |
|                  1                   |                     4                     |       13       |       7       |
|                  1                   |                     5                     |      10.40      |       0       |
|                  1                   |                     6                     |       8       |       -4       |

The relationship can be summarised as under:

1.  As long as MP is more than AP, AP rises, i.e. up to $2^{\text {nd }}$ unit of variable factor.
2.  When MP is equal to AP, AP is at its maximum, i.e. at $3^{\text {rd }}$ unit of variable factor.
3.  When MP is less than AP, AP falls (from $4^{\text {th }}$ unit of variable factor).
4.  Thereafter, both AP and MP fall, but MP becomes negative, whereas, AP remains positive. MP falls at a faster rate in comparison to fall in AP.

### Fig. 5.4

**Title:** Relationship between AP and MP

**Type:** Graph with two curves representing Average Product (AP) and Marginal Product (MP).

**Axes:**

*   **X-axis:** Labeled "Units of Variable Factor." The x-axis is numbered from 0 to 6.
*   **Y-axis:** Labeled "AP/MP (in units)." The y-axis is numbered from 0 to 25 in increments of 5.

**Curves:**

*   **AP (Average Product):** Represented by a blue line. It initially increases, reaches a peak around 2 units of variable factor, and then gradually declines. It is labeled as AP next to the curve at x=6.
*   **MP (Marginal Product):** Represented by an orange/red line. It increases sharply initially, reaching its peak before the AP curve peaks around the 2nd unit of variable factor. The MP curve then declines more steeply than the AP curve, crossing the x-axis around 5 units of variable factor, meaning it becomes negative after that point. The MP is labeled as MP next to the curve at x=6.

**Key Features/Observations:**

*   The MP curve reaches its maximum point *before* the AP curve does.
*   The MP curve intersects with the AP curve at the AP curve's maximum. Both curves cross at the y value equal to 15 and at x=3
*   After the intersection, the MP curve declines more rapidly than the AP curve.
*   At some point (around x=5), the MP curve crosses the x-axis, indicating that the marginal product becomes zero and then negative.

**Overall Interpretation:**

The graph illustrates the relationship between average product and marginal product. It shows how the addition of variable factors impacts the average output and the change in total output. The shapes of the curves reflect the law of diminishing returns, where adding more of a variable input eventually leads to smaller increases in output. The marginal product becoming negative suggests that adding even more of the variable factor beyond a certain point actually decreases total output.

It must be noted that both AP and MP are derived from TP. AP is calculated on the basis of all the units, whereas, MP is based on the additional unit only. So, it is the MP which pulls the AP up or down.
