# Law of Diminishing Returns

Law of diminishing returns states that when more and more units of a variable factor are employed with a fixed factor, then the marginal product of the variable factor must fall. It means that marginal returns diminish when the proportion between variable and fixed factors increases. This law is also known as the Law of Diminishing Marginal Product.

Let us understand this with Table 5.2 and Fig 5.2.

As seen in Table 5.2 and Fig. 5.2, MP falls when more and more units of the variable factor (labour) are employed with the fixed factor (land). This law considers only the falling phases.

**Interpretation:**

The graph illustrates the law of diminishing returns, which states that as more units of a variable factor (such as labor) are added to a fixed amount of other resources (such as capital), the marginal product of the variable factor will eventually decrease. In other words, each additional unit of the variable factor contributes less to the overall output than the previous unit. The downward-sloping line of the "MP" demonstrates this diminishing return as units of the variable factor increase.

LVP is an extension to the Law of Diminishing Returns as it also considers the phase of rising MP in addition to falling MP. The common element between both the laws is that MP is bound to decrease sooner or later with the increase in units of the variable factor.

In the short run, a firm can change the output by changing the quantity of variable factors only. However, in the long run, all the factors become variable, i.e., the distinction between fixed and variable factors disappears. The resultant change in output due to change in all factor inputs is studied under 'Law of Returns to Scale', discussed in the Power Booster Section.
