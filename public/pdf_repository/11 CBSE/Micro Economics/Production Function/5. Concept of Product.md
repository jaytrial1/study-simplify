# Product or Output

Product or output refers to the volume of goods produced by a firm or an industry during a specified period of time.
The concept of product can be looked at from three different angles:

(i) Total Product (TP)
(ii) Marginal Product (MP)
(iii) Average Product (AP)

## Total Product (TP)

Total product refers to the total quantity of goods produced by a firm during a given period of time with a given number of inputs.
For example, if 10 laborers produce 60 kg of rice, then the total product is 60 kg.
In the short run, a firm can expand TP by increasing only the variable factors. However, in the long run, TP can be raised by increasing both fixed and variable factors.

Total Product is also known as 'Total Physical Product (TPP)' or 'Total Return' or 'Total Output'.

## Average Product (AP)

Average product refers to output per unit of variable input.
For example, if total product (TP) is 60 kg of rice, produced by 10 laborers (variable input), then the average product will be $60 \div 10=6 \mathrm{~kg}$.
AP is obtained by dividing TP by units of variable factor.

$$
\text { Average Product }(\mathrm{AP})=\frac{\text { Total Product }(\mathrm{TP})}{\text { Units of Variable Factor }(\mathrm{n})}
$$

TP in terms of AP will be:

$$
\mathrm{TP}=\mathrm{AP} \times \text { Units of Variable Factor }
$$

Average Product is also known as 'Average Physical Product (APP)' or 'Average Return'.

## Marginal Product (MP)

Marginal Product refers to the addition to total product when one more unit of variable factor is employed. It measures extra output per extra unit of input holding all other inputs fixed.
Where,

$$
\mathrm{MP}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{n}}-\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{n}-1}
$$

$\mathrm{MP}_{\mathrm{n}}=$ Marginal product of $\mathrm{n}^{\text {th }}$ unit of variable factor;
$\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{n}}=$ Total product of n units of variable factor;
$\mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{n}-1}=$ Total product of $(\mathrm{n}-1)$ units of variable factor;
$\mathrm{n} \quad=$ number of units of variable factor.

For example, If 10 laborers make 60 kg of rice and 11 laborers make 67 kg of rice, then $\mathrm{MP}_{11}$ of the $11^{\text {th }}$ labor will be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{MP}_{11}=\mathrm{TP}_{11}-\mathrm{TP}_{10} \\
& \mathrm{MP}_{11}=67-60=7 \mathrm{~kg}
\end{aligned}
$$

Marginal Product (MP) is also known as 'Marginal Physical Product (MPP)' or 'Marginal Return'.

### One More Way to Calculate MP

We know, MP is the change in TP when one more unit of variable factor is employed. However, when the change in the variable factor is greater than one unit, then MP can be calculated as:

$$
\mathrm{MP}=\frac{\text { Change in Total Product }}{\text { Change in units of Variable Factor }}=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{TP}}{\Delta \mathrm{n}}
$$

Suppose 2 laborers produce 60 units and 5 laborers produce 90 units, then MP will be:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{MP}=\frac{\text { TP of } 5 \text { labourers }- \text { TP of } 2 \text { labourers }}{5 \text { labourers }-2 \text { labourers }} \\
& \text { or } \quad \mathrm{MP}=\frac{90-60}{5-2}=\frac{30}{3}=10 \text { units }
\end{aligned}
$$

### TP is Summation of MP

Total Product can also be calculated as the sum of the marginal product.
It means, $\quad \mathrm{TP}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{MP}_{1}+\mathrm{MP}_{2}+\mathrm{MP}_{3}+\ldots \ldots \ldots . . \mathrm{MP}_{\mathrm{n}}$
or, $\quad \mathrm{TP}=\Sigma \mathrm{MP}$
