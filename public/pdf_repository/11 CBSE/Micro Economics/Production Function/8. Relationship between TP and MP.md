The relationship between TP and MP can be summarised as under:

1.  As long as TP increases at an increasing rate (till point 'P'), MP also increases.
2.  When TP increases at a diminishing rate, MP decreases. It starts happening when 3 units of labour are employed and continues till 5 units of the variable factor.
3.  When TP reaches its maximum point (point M), MP becomes zero (point N ), i.e. at $5^{\text {th }}$ unit of the variable factor.
4.  When TP starts decreasing, MP becomes negative, i.e. from $6^{\text {th }}$ unit of the variable factor.

### Detailed Description of the Image

**Title:** Relationship between TP and MP

**Type:** Economic Graph showing the Total Product (TP) and Marginal Product (MP) relationship.

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis:** Labeled as "TP / MP (in units)". This axis represents the units of total product and marginal product. The scale ranges from 0 to 60.
*   **X-axis:** Labeled as "Units of Variable factor". This axis represents the quantity of a variable input (e.g., labor). The scale ranges from 0 to 6.

**Curves/Lines:**

*   **TP (Total Product) Curve:** A blue curve that originates from the origin (0,0), initially increases at an increasing rate, then increases at a decreasing rate, reaches a maximum point (labeled as "M (TP is Maximum)"), and then starts to decline.
*   **MP (Marginal Product) Curve:** A red curve that originates from the origin, rises to a peak at around x=2, then declines, crosses the X-axis (MP=0) at around x=5, labelled as "N (MP is Zero)".
*   **P (Point):** Indicated in line with 2 units of the variable factor, where TP and MP intersects.

**Key Features and Observations:**

*   **Origin:** Both curves start from the origin, implying that with zero units of the variable factor, both total and marginal product are zero.
*   **Increasing Returns:** Initially, as the variable factor increases, both TP and MP increase. MP reaches its maximum before TP.
*   **Diminishing Returns:** After MP reaches its maximum (around 2 units of the variable factor), it starts to decline. TP continues to increase but at a decreasing rate. This represents the stage of diminishing returns.
*   **Negative Returns:** After TP reaches its maximum (point M), it starts to decrease. At this point, MP becomes negative (goes below the X-axis at Point N)
*   **Relationship at Maximum TP:** At the point where TP is maximum (point M), MP is zero (point N)
*   **Intersection of TP and MP:** Both the TP and MP intersects each other at point P, when two units of the variable factor are used.
