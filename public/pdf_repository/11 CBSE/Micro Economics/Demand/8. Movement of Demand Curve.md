## MOVEMENT ALONG THE DEMAND CURVE (CHANGE IN QUANTITY DEMANDED)

When the quantity demanded of a commodity changes due to a change in its price, keeping other factors constant, it is known as a change in quantity demanded. It is graphically expressed as a movement along the same demand curve.

There can be either a downward movement (Expansion in demand) or an upward movement (Contraction in demand) along the same demand curve. Let us understand the movement along the demand curve with the help of Fig. 3.4:

In Fig. 3.4, OQ quantity is demanded at a price of OP. Change in price leads to an upward or downward movement along the same demand curve:

*   Upward Movement: When price rises to $\mathrm{OP}_{2}$, quantity demanded falls to $\mathrm{OQ}_{2}$ (known as contraction in demand) leading to an upward movement from A to C along the same demand curve DD.

### **Title:** Movement Along the Demand Curve

**Description:**

This image presents a graphical representation of the concept of movement along a demand curve in economics. It illustrates how changes in price affect the quantity demanded, leading to expansions and contractions in demand, as opposed to shifts of the entire curve.

**Elements:**

*   **Axes:**
    *   X-axis: Represents the quantity demanded (in units).
    *   Y-axis: Represents the price (in ₹ - Indian Rupees).

*   **Demand Curve:**

    *   Downward-sloping curve (labeled as 'D') depicting the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. This means as price increases, the quantity demanded decreases and vice-versa. The curve is labeled 'DD.'

*   **Points of Significance:**

    *   Point A: Represents an initial equilibrium with a price of 'P' and a quantity demanded of 'Q'.
    *   Point B: Represents a movement downward along the demand curve from point A. At point B, the price has fallen to 'P1', and the quantity demanded has increased to 'Q1'. This exemplifies an expansion in demand.
    *   Point C: Represents a movement upward along the demand curve from point A. At point C, the price has risen to 'P2', and the quantity demanded has decreased to 'Q2'. This illustrates a contraction in demand.

*   **Arrows:**
    *   An arrow pointing from A to B indicates the direction of movement when the demand expands (downward movement along the curve).
    *   An arrow pointing from A to C indicates the direction of movement when the demand contracts (upward movement along the curve).

*   **Textual Annotations:**

    *   "Movement along the Demand Curve": Title of the graph.
    *   "Expansion in Demand":
        *   Shown by a downward movement from A to B.
        *   Quantity demanded rises from OQ to OQ1 due to a fall in price from OP to OP1.
    *   "Contraction in Demand":
        *   Shown by an upward movement from A to C.
        *   Quantity demanded falls from OQ to OQ2 due to a rise in price from OP to OP2.

**Interpretation:**

The graph demonstrates that *movements along* the demand curve (i.e., expansions and contractions) occur when the *price* of the good or service changes.

*   **Expansion in Demand:**  A fall in price (from P to P1) leads to an increase in the quantity demanded (from Q to Q1). The consumer moves *downward* along the existing demand curve.

*   **Contraction in Demand:** A rise in price (from P to P2) leads to a decrease in the quantity demanded (from Q to Q2). The consumer moves *upward* along the existing demand curve.

**Important Note:**  Movements *along* the demand curve differ from *shifts* of the entire demand curve. Shifts occur when factors other than price (e.g., income, tastes, prices of related goods) change, causing the entire demand curve to move to the left or right.

## Expansion in Demand

Expansion in demand refers to a rise in the quantity demanded due to a fall in the price of commodity, other factors remaining constant.

*   It leads to a downward movement along the same demand curve.
*   It is also known as Extension in Demand or Increase in Quantity Demanded.

It can be better understood from Table 3.4 and Fig. 3.5.

| Table 3.4 | Expansion in Demand |
| :--------: | :-----------------: |
| Price ($₹$) |   Demand (units)   |
|     20     |         100        |
|     15     |         150        |

As seen in the given schedule and diagram, the quantity demanded rises from 100 units to 150 units with a fall in the price from ₹ 20 to ₹ 15, resulting in a downward movement from A to B along the same demand curve DD.

# Contraction in Demand

Contraction in demand refers to a fall in the quantity demanded due to a rise in the price of a commodity, other factors remaining constant.

- It leads to an upward movement along the same demand curve.
- It is also known as 'Decrease in Quantity Demanded'. It can be better understood from Table 3.5 and Fig. 3.6.

## Table 3.5: Contraction in Demand

| Price ($₹$) | Demand (units) |
| :----------: | :------------: |
|      20      |       100      |
|      25      |       70       |

As seen in the given schedule and diagram, the quantity demanded falls from 100 units to 70 units with a rise in the price from ₹ 20 to ₹ 25, resulting in an upward movement from A to B along the same demand curve DD.
