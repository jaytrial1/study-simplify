## Example 3

The following table shows the expenditure, which Amit is willing to spend on commodity ' $x$ ' at various levels of price. Prepare demand schedule of Amit.

| Price ($\boldsymbol{\ell}$) | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| :--------------------------: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: | :-: |
| Expenditure ($\boldsymbol{\ell}$) | 100 | 96 | 84 | 80 | 72 |

### Solution:

| Price                     | Expenditure                 |
| :------------------------: | :--------------------------: |
| ($\boldsymbol{\ell}$)      | ($\boldsymbol{\ell}$)      |
|             5              |             100              |
|             6              |              96              |
|             7              |              84              |
|             8              |              80              |
|             9              |              72              |

Demand in units

(Expenditure + Price)

$$
\begin{aligned}
& 100 \div 5=20 \\
& 96 \div 6=16 \\
& 84 \div 7=12 \\
& 80 \div 8=10 \\
& 72 \div 9=8
\end{aligned}
$$

## Example 5

There are only 3 consumers ( $X, Y$ and $Z$ ) in a market and their demand functions are given as: $Q_{X}=30-2 P ; Q_{Y}=40-3 P ; Q_{Z}=50-4 P$.
From the given individual demand functions, determine the market demand function. Also. calculate the market demand at a price of $\boldsymbol{\ell} 10$ per unit.

### Solution:

Market demand function can be determined through summation of individual demand function, i.e.

$$
Q_{M D}=Q_{X}+Q_{Y}+Q_{Z}=(30-2 P)+(40-3 P)+(50-4 P)=120-9 P
$$

Market Demand at a price of $\boldsymbol{\ell} 10$ per unit: $Q_{M D}=120-9 P=120-9 \times 10=30$ units.

## Example 6

With the help of demand function: $Q_{d}=40-5 p$, answer the following questions: (i) Calculate demand at price of $\boldsymbol{\ell}2$; (ii) Calculate price, when demand will be 0 ; (iii) Calculate demand, when price will be 0 .

### Solution:

(i) $Q_{d}=40-5 p$. Putting the value of price (i.e. $\boldsymbol{\ell} 2$ ), we get: $Q_{d}=40-5 \times 2=\mathbf{3 0}$ units
(ii) Putting the value of demand ( 0 ), we get: $0=40-5 p$. It means, $p=\boldsymbol{\ell} 8$. At price of $\boldsymbol{\ell} 8$, demand will be zero.
(iii) Putting the value of price ( 0 ), we get:
$Q_{d}=40-5 \times 0=\mathbf{4 0}$ units. At demand of 40 units, price will be zero.
