# 7. Budget Line

So far, we have discussed different combinations of two goods that provide the same level of satisfaction. However, which combination a consumer will actually purchase depends upon his income ('consumer budget') and the prices of the two commodities. A Consumer Budget states the real income or purchasing power of the consumer from which he can purchase certain quantitative bundles of two goods at a given price. It means a consumer can purchase only those combinations (bundles) of goods which cost less than or equal to his income.

A Budget line is a graphical representation of all possible combinations of two goods which can be purchased with a given income and prices, such that the cost of each of these combinations is equal to the money income of the consumer. Alternately, a Budget Line is the locus of different combinations of the two goods which the consumer consumes and which cost exactly his income. A Budget Line is also known as:

(a) Price Line
(b) Price Opportunity Line
(c) Price-Income Line
(d) Budget Constraint Line

Let us understand the concept of Budget line with the help of an example:

Suppose, a consumer has an income of ₹ 20. He wants to spend it on two commodities: X and Y and both are priced at ₹ 10 each. Now, the consumer has three options to spend his entire income:

(i) Buy 2 units of X
(ii) Buy 2 units of Y
(iii) Buy 1 unit of X and 1 unit of Y

It means, possible bundles can be: $(2,0) ;(0,2)$ or $(1,1)$.

When all these three bundles are represented graphically, we get a downward sloping straight line, known as 'Budget Line'. It is also known as the price line.

## Budget Set

A Budget set is the set of all possible combinations of the two goods which a consumer can afford, given his income and prices in the market.

In addition to the three options, there are some more options available to the consumer within his income, even if the entire income is not spent. The Budget set includes all the bundles with the total income of ₹ 20, i.e. possible bundles or a Consumer's bundles are: $(0,0) ;(0,1) ;(0,2) ;(1,0) ;(2,0) ;(1,1)$. A Consumer's Bundle is a quantitative combination of two goods which can be purchased by a consumer from his given income.

## 7.1 Budget Line

### Important Points About Budget Line (Refer Fig. 2.8)

1.  Budget line $AB$ slopes downwards as more of one good can be bought by decreasing some units of the other good.

2.  Bundles which cost exactly equal to consumer's money income (like combinations E to J) lie on the budget line.

3.  Bundles which cost less than consumer's money income (like combination D) shows under spending. They lie inside the budget line.

4.  Bundles which cost more than consumer's money income (like combination C) are not available to the consumer. They lie outside the budget line.

5.  Budget Line is relevant (as compared to Budget Set) to determine Consumer's Equilibrium as it is assumed that consumer spends the entire income.

### Algebraic Expression of Budget Line

The budget line can be expressed as an equation:

$$
M=\left(P_{A} \times Q_{A}\right)+\left(P_{B} \times Q_{B}\right)
$$

Where:

$M=$ Money income;

$Q_{A}=$ Quantity of apples $(A)$;

$Q_{B}=$ Quantity of bananas $(B)$;

$P_{A}=$ Price of each apple;

$P_{B}=$ Price of each banana.

All points on the budget line 'AB' indicate those bundles, which cost exactly equal to '$M$'.

### Algebraic Expression for Budget Set or Budget Constraint

The consumer can buy any bundle $(A, B)$, such that: $M \geq\left(P_{A} \times Q_{A}\right)+\left(P_{B} \times Q_{B}\right)$. Equation of Budget Set is often referred to as 'Budget Constraint'. This inequality of expenditure on two goods and income is also referred to as 'Budget Constraints'. It tells that the total expenditure on the two goods must be less than or equal to the income. It sets the limit what the consumer can buy at the prevailing prices with his/her given income.

### Difference between Budget Line and Budget Set

| S. No. | Budget Line                                                                                                                                        | Budget Set                                                                                                                                               |
| :----: | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
|   1.   | Budget Line includes all the possible bundles that the consumer can purchase by spending his entire income at the given prices.                   | Budget Set includes all the possible bundles which cost less than or equal to consumer's money income at the given prices.                                |

### Slope of the Budget Line

We know, the slope of a curve is calculated as a change in variable on the vertical or Y-axis divided by change in variable on the horizontal or X-axis. In the example of apples and bananas, slope of the budget line will be number of units of bananas, that the consumer is willing to sacrifice for an additional unit of apple.

$$
\text { Slope of Budget Line }=\frac{\text { Units of Bananas (B) willing to Sacrifice }}{\text { Units of Apples (A) willing to Gain }}=\frac{\Delta B}{\Delta A}
$$

As seen in Fig. 2.8, as we move downwards, 2 bananas need to be sacrificed each time to gain 1 apple. So Slope of Budget Line = $\frac{-2}{1}=-2$

**Numerator will always have a negative value as it shows the number of units to be sacrificed. However, for analysis, the absolute value is always considered.**

Slope of Budget Line is denoted by Market Rate of Exchange (MRE). So, MRE is the rate at which one good is sacrificed in the market to obtain an extra unit of other good. MRE or slope of budget line is also equal to 'Price Ratio' of two goods.

### Price Ratio

Price Ratio is the price of the good on the horizontal or $X$-axis divided by the price of the good on the vertical or Y-axis. For instance, if good X is plotted on the horizontal axis and good Y on the vertical axis, then:

$\frac{\text { Price Ratio }}{}=\frac{\text { Price of } X\left(P_{X}\right)}{\text { Price of } Y\left(P_{Y}\right)}=\frac{P_{X}}{P_{Y}}$

#### Why Slope of Budget Line is Represented by Price Ratio?

A point on the budget line indicates a bundle which the consumer can purchase by spending his entire income. So, if the consumer wants to have one more unit of good 1 (say, Apples or A), then he will have to give up some amount of good 2 (say, Bananas or B). The number of bananas needed to be given up to gain 1 apple depends on the prices of apples and bananas.

As per Table 2.7, Apple (A) is priced at ₹ $4\left(P_{A}\right)$ and Bananas (B) at ₹ $2\left(P_{B}\right)$. It means, to gain 1 apple, consumer will have to reduce his expenditure on bananas by ₹ 4, i.e., the consumer will have to sacrifice 2 bananas to gain 1 apple. It means, the consumer will have to give $\frac{P_{A}}{P_{B}}$ units of Bananas to gain one apple. $\frac{P_{A}}{P_{B}}$ is nothing but the price ratio between Apples and Bananas.

So, it is rightly said that Price Ratio indicates the slope of Budget Line.

Moreover, the Price ratio remains constant throughout because $P_{X}$ and $P_{Y}$ on the basis of which $\Delta X$ and $\Delta Y$ are calculated are constant throughout.

## 7.2 Properties of Budget Line

The two main properties of the Budget Line are:

1.  **Budget Line is Downward Sloping:** The budget line has a negative slope, i.e., it slopes downwards as more of one good can be bought by decreasing some units of the other good.

2.  **Budget Line is a Straight Line:** The slope of the Budget Line is represented by the Price Ratio. As the Price Ratio is constant throughout, the budget line is a straight line.

### Shift in Budget Line

The budget line is drawn with the assumptions of constant income of the consumer and constant prices of the commodities. A new budget line would have to be drawn if either:

(a) Income of the consumer changes, or

(b) Price of the commodity changes.

Let us understand this with the example of apples and bananas:

1.  **Effect of a Change in the Income of Consumer:** If there is any change in the income, assuming no change in prices of apples and bananas, then the budget line will shift. When income increases, the consumer will be able to buy more bundles of goods, which were previously not possible. It will shift the budget line to the right from 'AB' to '$\mathrm{A}_{1} \mathrm{~B}_{1}$', as seen in Fig. 2.9. The new budget line '$\mathrm{A}_{1} \mathrm{~B}_{1}$' will be parallel to the original budget line 'AB'.
