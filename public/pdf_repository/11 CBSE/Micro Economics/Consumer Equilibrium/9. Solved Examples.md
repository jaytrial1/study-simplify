# 9. Solved Examples

## Important Formulas at a Glance

1.  $\mathrm{TU}_{n}=\mathrm{U}_{1}+\mathrm{U}_{2}+\mathrm{U}_{3}+\ldots \ldots\ldots\ldots+\mathrm{U}_{\mathrm{n}}=\sum \mathrm{MU}$
2.  $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{n}}=\mathrm{TU}_{\mathrm{n}}-\mathrm{TU}_{\mathrm{n}-1}$
3.  Consumer's Equilibrium in case of Single Commodity is attained when MU = Price.
4.  Consumer's Equilibrium in case of Two Commodities: (i) MU of last rupee spent on each commodity is same, i.e. $\frac{M U_{x}}{P_{x}}=\frac{M U_{y}}{P_{y}} \quad$ Or $\frac{M U_{x}}{M U_{y}} \geq \frac{P_{x}}{P_{y}}$; and (ii) MU falls as consumption increases.

## Practicals on TU and MU

### Example 1

A person's total utility (TU) schedule is given below. Derive marginal utility (MU).

| Units | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| :---- | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| TU | 0 | 10 | 25 | 38 | 48 | 55 |

**Solution:**

| Units | TU | MU <br> MU $_{n}=T U_{n}-T U_{n-1}$ |
| :----: | :----: | :----: |
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 10 | $10-0=10$ |
| 2 | 25 | $25-10=15$ |
| 3 | 38 | $38-25=13$ |
| 4 | 48 | $48-38=10$ |
| 5 | 55 | $55-48=7$ |

### Example 2

A person's MU schedule is given below. Derive TU:

| Units consumed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
| :---- | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| MU | 9 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 0 | -2 |

**Solution:**

| Units Consumed | MU | TU <br> TU $=\sum M U$ |
| :----: | :----: | :----: |
| 1 | 9 | 9 |
| 2 | 6 | $9+6=15$ |
| 3 | 4 | $15+4=19$ |
| 4 | 2 | $19+2=21$ |
| 5 | 0 | $21+0=21$ |
| 6 | $-2$ | $21-2=19$ |

## Practicals on Consumer's Equilibrium (Single Commodity)

### Example 4

Suppose the price of a commodity ' $x$ ' is given as ₹ 8 and the MU (in terms of money) for 4 units is given as:

| Units | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| :---- | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| $M U_{n}(\Á)$ | 12 | 10 | 8 | 6 |

How many units should a consumer purchase so that his satisfaction is maximum?

**Solution:**

| Units <br> Consumed | $M U_{n}$ <br> (in $\text{\AA}$) | Price $\left(P_{n}\right)$ <br> (in $\text{\AA}$) |
| :----: | :----: | :----: |
| 1 | 12 | 8 |
| 2 | 10 | 8 |
| 3 | 8 | 8 |
| 4 | 6 | 8 |

Consumer will purchase 3 units because at $3^{\text {rd }}$ unit, $\mathrm{MU}=$ Price.

*   The consumer will not purchase less than 3 units as MU > Price and there is scope for increasing the total satisfaction by purchasing more units.
*   Similarly, consumer will not buy more than 3 units as MU < Price and total satisfaction can be increased by purchasing less quantity.

### Example 6

Following is the utility schedule of a person:

| Units of $x$ consumed | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| :----: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| MU (in utils) | 50 | 40 | 30 | 20 | 10 |

Suppose that the commodity is sold for ₹ 4 and MU of one rupee is 5 utils. How many units of the commodity will the person purchase to maximise his satisfaction?

**Solution:**

Let us first calculate MU in terms of money $\left(\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{x}}\right)$.
Given: Value of 5 utils $=₹ 1$, i.e. $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{M}}=5$. We know: $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{x}}=\frac{\mathrm{MU} \text { in Utils }}{\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{M}}}$

| Units of $x$ consumed | Price of $x$ (₹) | Marginal Utility (in utils) | Marginal Utility in (MU) 5 utils = ₹ 1 |
| :----: | :----: | :----: | :----: |
| 1 | 4 | 50 | $50 \div 5=10$ |
| 2 | 4 | 40 | $40 \div 5=8$ |
| 3 | 4 | 30 | $30 \div 5=6$ |
| 4 | 4 | 20 | $20 \div 5=4$ |
| 5 | 4 | 10 | $10 \div 5=2$ |

Consumer will purchase 4 units because at $4^{\text {th }}$ unit, $\mathrm{MU}=$ Price.

*   The consumer will not purchase less than 4 units as MU > Price and there is scope for increasing the total satisfaction by purchasing more units.
*   Similarly, consumer will not buy more than 4 units as MU < Price and total satisfaction can be increased by purchasing less quantity.

### Example 7

Suppose that an ice-cream is sold for ₹ 30. Laxmi, who loves ice-cream, has already eaten 3. Her marginal utility from eating the $3^{\text {rd }}$ ice-cream is 90 utils. If MU of ₹ 1 is 3 utils, should she eat more ice-creams or should she stop?

**Solution:**

Given: Price $\left(P_{I C}\right)=₹ 30$; Marginal utility from eating $3^{\text {rd }}$ ice-cream $=90$ utils. Value of 3 utils $=₹ 1$, i.e. $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{M}}=3$.
MU in terms of Money $\left(\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{IC}}\right)=\frac{\mathrm{MU} \text { in Utils }}{\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{M}}}=\frac{90}{3}=30$

Laxmi should not consume any more ice-creams as the condition for consumer equilibrium "MU ${ }_{\mathrm{IC}}=$ $P_{I C}$ " is satisfied at $3^{\text {rd }}$ ice-cream.

## Practicals on Consumer's Equilibrium (Two Commodities)

### Example 8

The marginal utility schedule for goods $X$ and $Y$ are given below. Both the goods are priced at ₹ 1 each and income of Rakesh (an individual) is assumed to be ₹ 8 . Determine how many units of both the commodities should be purchased by Rakesh to maximize his total utility?

| Quantity <br> (units) | Marginal Utility of $X\left(M U_{X}\right)$ <br> (Utils) | Marginal Utility of $Y\left(M U_{Y}\right)$ <br> (Utils) |
| :----: | :----: | :----: |
| 1 | 11 | 19 |
| 2 | 10 | 17 |
| 3 | 9 | 15 |
| 4 | 8 | 13 |
| 5 | 7 | 12 |
| 6 | 6 | 10 |
| 7 | 5 | 8 |
| 8 | 4 | 6 |

**Solution:**

To maximise the total utility, Rakesh should purchase that combination of both the goods, when:
(i) MU of last rupee spent on each commodity is same, i.e. $\frac{M U_{X}}{P_{X}}=\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}$; and
(ii) MU falls as consumption increases.

It happens when Rakesh purchases 6 units of $Y$ and 2 units of $X$. At this combination:

*   MU from last rupee (i.e. $8^{\text {th }}$ rupee) spent on commodity $X$ gives the same satisfaction of 10 utils as given by last rupee (i.e. $7^{\text {th }}$ rupee) spent on commodity $Y$; and
*   MU of each commodity falls as consumption increases.

The total satisfaction of 107 utils $(=19+17+15+13+12+11+10+10)$ will be obtained.

### Example 9

A consumer consumes only two goods $X$ and $Y$ whose prices are ₹ 5 and ₹ 4 per unit respectively. If $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{Y}}=16$ at the point of consumer's equilibrium, calculate $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{X}}$.

**Solution:**

A consumer in consumption of two commodities is at equilibrium when: $\frac{M U_{X}}{P_{X}}=\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}$
Putting the given values in the equilibrium condition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{M U_{X}}{5}=\frac{16}{4} \\
& \frac{M U_{X}}{5}=4 \\
& \mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{X}}=20
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 10

A consumer consumes only two goods $X$ and $Y$. The consumer chooses a combination of the two goods with marginal utility of $X$ equal to 30 and that of $Y$ equal to 20. The price of $X$ is ₹ 6 per unit. What must be the price of $Y$ to be in equilibrium?

**Solution:**

A consumer in consumption of two commodities is at equilibrium when: $\frac{M U_{X}}{P_{X}}=\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}$
Putting the given values in the equilibrium condition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \frac{30}{6}=\frac{20}{P_{Y}} \\
& P_{Y}=4
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 11

A consumer consumes only two goods $X$ and $Y$ whose prices are ₹ 5 and ₹ 6 per unit respectively. If the consumer chooses a combination of the two goods with marginal utility of $X$ equal to 35 and that of $Y$ equal to 30 , is the consumer in equilibrium? Give reasons. What will a rational consumer do in this situation? Use utility analysis.

**Solution:**

Given $P_{X}=5, P_{Y}=6$ and $M U_{X}=35, M U_{Y}=30$. A consumer will be in equilibrium when $\frac{M U_{X}}{P_{X}}=\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}$
Substituting values, we find that:

$$
\frac{35}{5}>\frac{30}{6} \text { Or } \frac{M U_{X}}{P_{Y}}>\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}
$$

Since per rupee $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{x}}$ is higher than per rupee $\mathrm{MU}_{\mathrm{y}}$, consumer is not in equilibrium. The consumer will buy more of $X$ and less of $Y$. As a result $M U_{x}$ will fall and $M U_{y}$ will rise. The reaction will continue till $\frac{M U_{X}}{P_{X}}$ and $\frac{M U_{Y}}{P_{Y}}$ are equal and consumer is in equilibrium.

### Example 14

Suppose there are four bundles containing goods $x$ and goods $y$ : Bundle (5, 5); Bundle (4, 4); Bundle (5, 4); Bundle (4,5). If a consumer's preferences are monotonic, then which bundle will be preferred by the consumer? Give reason for your answer.

**Solution:**

Consumer will prefer the bundle $(5,5)$ as compared to all the three bundles if he has monotonic preferences.

*   The consumer will prefer the bundle $(5,5)$ as compared to bundle $(4,4)$ because the former has more of both goods.
*   Similarly, he will prefer the bundle $(5,5)$ as compared to bundle $(5,4)$ because the former has same amount of good $x$, but more of good $y$.
*   Similarly, he will prefer the bundle $(5,5)$ as compared to bundle $(4,5)$ because the former has same amount of good $y$, but more of good $x$.

So, the consumer will prefer the bundle $(5,5)$.

### Example 15

A consumer consumes only two goods $X$ and $Y$ whose prices are ₹ 6 and ₹ 3 per unit respectively. What will be the $\mathrm{MRS}_{\mathrm{XY}}$, when the consumer is in equilibrium?

**Solution:**

A consumer in consumption of two commodities is at equilibrium when: $\mathrm{MRS}_{\mathrm{XY}}=\frac{\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{X}}}{\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{Y}}}$
Putting the given values in the equilibrium condition:

$$
\begin{aligned}
& \mathrm{MRS}_{\mathrm{XY}}=\frac{6}{3} \\
& \mathrm{MRS}_{\mathrm{XY}}=2
\end{aligned}
$$

### Example 16

If a consumer faces the Budget line equation: $20 \mathrm{X}+10 \mathrm{Y}=500$. Answer the following questions:
(i) What will be the slope of budget line?
(ii) How many units would he be able to buy if the entire sum of ₹ 500 is to be spent on Good $X$ only? Show calculations.

(iii) Construct a new budget line equation if the price of Good $Y$ falls by $50 \%$. Also, write the slope of the new budget line equation.

**Solution:**

(i) The slope of a budget line measures the amount of change in good $Y$ required per unit of change in good $X$ along the budget line. It is measured as follows:
Slope $=\frac{-P_{X}}{P_{Y}}=\frac{-20}{10}=-2$
(ii) If entire sum of ₹ 500 is to be spent on Good $X$, then: $20 X+10(0)=500$
or, $20 X=500$
or, $X=500 \div 20=25$ units.
(iii) If price of Good $Y$ falls by $50 \%$, then new price of Good $Y$ will be ₹ $10-50 \%$ of $₹ 10=₹ 5$. New Budget Line Equation will be: $20 X+5 Y=500$.
Slope $=\frac{-P_{X}}{P_{Y}}=\frac{-20}{5}=-4$
