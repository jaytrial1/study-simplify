The concepts of demand and supply help us to understand the motivations and actions of people in their roles as consumers and producers. However, these concepts are of limited use when they are taken separately. They become more useful when they are used together to explain the behaviour of equilibrium in the market.

So, a market consists of three elements:
(i) Demand, describing the behaviour of consumers in the market.
(ii) Supply, describing the behaviour of firms in the market.
(iii) Market Equilibrium, connecting demand and supply and describing how consumers and producers interact in the market.

This chapter deals with the study of the determination of 'Market Equilibrium' and the effects of changes in demand and/or supply on the 'Market Equilibrium'.
