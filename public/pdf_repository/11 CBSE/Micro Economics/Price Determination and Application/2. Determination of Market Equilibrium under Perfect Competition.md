As discussed before, perfect competition is a market structure where each firm is a price-taker and price is determined by the market forces of demand and supply. We know, equilibrium refers to a state of balance. It means, under perfect competition, market equilibrium is determined when market demand is equal to market supply.

Let us recall the concepts of market demand and market supply:

(i) Market demand is the sum total of demand for a commodity by all the buyers in the market. Its curve slopes downwards due to operation of law of demand.

(ii) Market supply is the sum total of supplies of a commodity by all the producers in the market. Its curve slopes upwards due to operation of law of supply.

Both market demand and market supply act as the counteracting forces, which move in the opposite directions. Market Equilibrium is determined when the quantity demanded of a commodity becomes equal to the quantity supplied. The price determined corresponding to market equilibrium is known as equilibrium price and the corresponding quantity is known as equilibrium quantity. # Important Points about Market Equilibrium under Perfect Competition

- Each firm is a price-taker and the industry is the price-maker.
- Each firm earns only normal profits in the long run (as discussed in the previous chapter).
- Decisions of consumers and producers in the market are coordinated through a free flow of prices known as the price mechanism.
- It is assumed that both the law of demand and the law of supply operate.
- Equilibrium Price is the price at which the quantity demanded of a commodity is equal to the quantity supplied.
- At the equilibrium price, there is neither a shortage nor an excess of demand and supply.
- Equilibrium Quantity is the quantity demanded and supplied at the equilibrium price.

## Excess Supply

Excess supply refers to a situation when the quantity supplied is more than the quantity demanded at the prevailing market price. Under this situation, the market price is more than the equilibrium price. In Table 11.1, excess supply occurs at prices of ₹8 and ₹10, when market supply is more than market demand. In Fig. 11.3, if the market price is $\mathrm{OP}_{1}$ (more than the equilibrium price of OP), then the market supply of $\mathrm{OQ}_{1}$ is more than the market demand of $\mathrm{OQ}_{2}$. This situation is termed excess supply.

- Excess supply of $\mathrm{Q}_{1} \mathrm{Q}_{2}$ will lead to competition amongst sellers as each seller wants to sell his product.
- Sellers would be ready to charge a lower price to sell the excess stock, which will lead to a fall in price.
- With a decrease in price, market supply will fall due to the law of supply, and market demand will rise due to the law of demand.
- The price will continue to fall till excess supply is wiped out. This is shown by arrows in the diagram. Eventually, the price will decrease to a level where market demand becomes equal to market supply at $O Q$ and an equilibrium price of $O P$ is attained.
