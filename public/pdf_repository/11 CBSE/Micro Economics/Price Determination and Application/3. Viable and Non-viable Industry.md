# Viable Industry

Viable industry refers to an industry for which the supply curve and demand curve intersect each other in positive axes.
In a viable industry, supply and demand curves must intersect at some positive point as shown in Fig. 11.4. As seen in the given diagram, both demand and supply curves intersect each other in the positive range of the X-axis and Y-axis.

# Non-Viable Industry

Non-viable industry refers to an industry for which the supply curve and demand curve never intersect each other in the positive axes.
In a non-viable industry, the supply curve lies above the demand curve as the price is too high for the consumers. It happens when the price, at which producers are ready to produce, is so high that consumers are not willing to buy even a single unit. As a result, the product is not produced.
As seen in Fig. 11.5, the demand and supply curves never intersect each other in the positive range of both the axes.

## Image Description

The image depicts a supply and demand graph, a fundamental concept in economics.

**Axes:**

*   **Y-Axis:** Represents the "Price (in ₹)" which indicates the cost of a product or service.
*   **X-Axis:** Represents the "Quantity Demanded and Supplied (in units)," showing the number of goods or services that consumers want to buy and producers are willing to sell.

**Curves:**

*   **Demand Curve (D):** A downward-sloping line, labeled "D," illustrates the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded. As the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases, and vice versa.
*   **Supply Curve (S):** An upward-sloping line, labeled "S," illustrates the direct relationship between price and quantity supplied. As the price increases, the quantity supplied increases, and vice versa.

**Equilibrium:**

*   **Intersection Point:** The point where the supply curve (S) and demand curve (D) intersect represents the equilibrium. At this point, the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied, establishing the market-clearing price and quantity. A note of "The axis" has also been written above this intersection point

**Title:**

*   **"Viable Industry"**: This indicates that the graph represents a market for a product or service where there is both demand and supply, leading to a stable equilibrium.

**Overall Impression:**

The graph is a standard representation of market dynamics, illustrating how the forces of supply and demand interact to determine prices and quantities in a viable industry.

## Title: Supply and Demand Curves for a Non-Viable Industry

**Description:**

This image presents a supply and demand graph typical for a non-viable industry. It shows the relationship between price (in rupees) and the quantity demanded and supplied (in units).

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis (Vertical):** Represents the price in rupees.
    *   Labeled as "Price (in ₹)".
*   **X-axis (Horizontal):** Represents the quantity demanded and supplied in units.
    *   Labeled as "Quantity Demanded and Supplied (in units)".

**Curves:**

*   **Supply Curve:**
    *   A line that begins at point S on the Y-axis (price axis) and slopes upwards to the right.
    *   Labeled as "Supply curve".
    *   Indicates that as the price increases, the quantity supplied also increases.

*   **Demand Curve:**
    *   A line that begins at point D on the Y-axis (price axis) and slopes downwards to the right.
    *   Labeled as "Demand curve".
    *   Indicates that as the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases.

**Other Markings:**

*   **Origin:** The intersection of the X and Y axes is labeled as "O".
*   The point on the y-axis at which the supply and demand curves begin from is labeled "S" and "D" respectively.
*   **Label:** "Non-Viable Industry" indicates the context of the graph.

**Interpretation:**

In a non-viable industry, the supply and demand curves start at different price points on the y-axis, where S and D are the start positions for the supply and demand curves respectively. As the price rises, the quantity supplied increases, and as the price drops, the quantity demanded rises.
