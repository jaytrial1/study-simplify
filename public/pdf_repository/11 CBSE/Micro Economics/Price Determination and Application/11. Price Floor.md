# PRICE FLOOR OR MINIMUM SUPPORT PRICE (MSP)

Government also intervenes in the process of price determination through Price Floor. Price Floor refers to the minimum price (above the equilibrium price), fixed by the government, which the producers must be paid for their produce. Price Floor or Minimum Price Ceiling refers to imposition of lower limit on the price that may be charged for a particular good or service. When government feels that the price fixed by the forces of demand and supply is not remunerative from the producers' point of view, then it fixes a price (known as Price Floor), which is more than the equilibrium price.

## Need for Price Floor

The need for price floor arises when government finds that the equilibrium price is too low for the producers.

- Most well-known examples of imposition of Price Floor are agricultural price support programmes and minimum wage legislation.
- Indian Government maintains a variety of minimum support price programmes for various agricultural products like wheat, sugarcane etc. and the floor is normally set at a level higher than the market determined price for these goods.

*   **Price Floor:** A horizontal dashed line labeled "PRICE FLOOR" at a price level above the equilibrium price (labeled "P1" on the Y-axis). A price floor is a minimum legal price set by the government or another authority, below which the price cannot fall.

*   **Points A and B:**
    *   Point A: The intersection of the demand curve (D) and the price floor line.
        *   Q1: Quantity demanded at the price floor.
    *   Point B: The intersection of the supply curve (S) and the price floor line.

**Interpretation**

*   **Market Equilibrium:** Without intervention, the market naturally reaches an equilibrium point (E) where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at the equilibrium price (P).

*   **Effect of Price Floor:** The introduction of a price floor above the equilibrium price has the following effects:
    *   **Reduces Quantity Demanded:** Consumers are less willing to buy the good at the higher price (quantity demanded decreases to Q1).
    *   **Increases Quantity Supplied:** Producers are incentivized to supply more of the good at the higher price (quantity supplied increases to Q2).
    *   **Creates a Surplus:** The quantity supplied (Q2) exceeds the quantity demanded (Q1), resulting in a surplus of the good or service.

*   **Graphical Representation of Surplus:** The "Surplus" area on the graph visually represents the difference between the quantity supplied and the quantity demanded at the price floor. This indicates that there is an excess of the good or service that is not being purchased by consumers at the mandated price.*

*Price Floor is also known as 'Support Price' as it normally fixed above the equilibrium price to protect the interest of producers like farmers. At this support price, government buys the entire produce of farmers which they fail to sell in the open market.
- The excess supply may be purchased by the government either to increase its buffer stocks or for exports.

## Implications of Price Floor or Minimum Price Ceiling

Price Floor is normally set at a level higher than the equilibrium price. This leads to excess supply. Since producers are not able to sell all they want to sell, they illegally sell the good or service below the minimum price.

## Buffer Stock acts as a Tool of Price Floor

Buffer Stock is an important tool in the hands of government to ensure price floor. When market price is lower than what the government feels should be given to the farmers / producers, it purchases the commodity at higher price from the farmers / producers so as to maintain stock of the commodity with itself, to be released in case of shortage of the commodity in future.

## Minimum Wage Legislation

Under minimum wage legislation, the government aims to ensure that wage rate of labour does not fall below a particular level and minimum wages are set above the equilibrium wage level (as discussed in case of Price Floor).

## Difference between Price Ceiling and Price Floor

| Basis                         | Price Ceiling                                                                                                 | Price Floor                                                                                                |
| :---------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning                       | It means imposition of upper limit on the <br> price of a good by the government.                           | It means imposition of lower limit on the <br> price that may be charged for a particular <br> good or service. |
| Relation with <br> Equilibrium Price | It is fixed at a level lower than the <br> equilibrium price.                                          | It is fixed above the equilibrium price.                                                                    |
| Purpose/Objective             | It is generally imposed on essential items <br> so that common people can afford them,                       | It is generally imposed to protect <br> the interests of a certain category of <br> producers.              |
| Impact                        | This leads to excess demand, i.e. shortage <br> is created in the market, which may <br> further lead to Black Marketing. | This leads to excess supply, i.e. surplus <br> is created, which is either bought by <br> government or sold by producers below <br> the minimum price. |
