# Change in Demand or Shift in the Demand Curve

Change in demand or shift in the demand curve occurs due to change in any of the factors that were assumed constant under the law of demand. The change may be either an 'Increase in Demand' or 'Decrease in Demand'. Original market equilibrium is determined at point $E$, when the original demand curve $D D$ and supply curve SS intersect each other. OQ is the equilibrium quantity and $O P$ is the equilibrium price.

## Increase in Demand

(i) Increase in Demand: An increase in demand (assuming no change in supply) leads to a rightward shift in demand curve from DD to $\mathrm{D}_{1} \mathrm{D}_{1}$ (Fig. 11.6).

**Title:** Demand and Supply Graph Illustrating Equilibrium Shift

**Description:**

This graph illustrates the basic economic principles of supply and demand, and how shifts in demand affect market equilibrium.

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis:** Labeled as "Price (in ₹)". This axis represents the price of a good or service.
*   **X-axis:** Labeled as "Quantity Demanded and Supplied (in units)". This axis represents the quantity of the good or service being traded.

**Curves:**

*   **Initial Demand Curve (D):** A downward-sloping line representing the initial demand for the product. This curve indicates that as the price decreases, the quantity demanded increases, and vice versa.
*   **Shifted Demand Curve (D1):** A dashed downward-sloping line, located to the right of the initial demand curve. This represents an increase in demand.
*   **Supply Curve (S):** An upward-sloping line representing the supply of the product. This curve indicates that as the price increases, the quantity supplied increases, and vice versa.

**Points of Equilibrium:**

*   **Initial Equilibrium (E):** The point where the initial demand curve (D) intersects with the supply curve (S). This point represents the initial market equilibrium, where the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at a specific price.
*   **New Equilibrium (E1):** The point where the new demand curve (D1) intersects with the supply curve (S). This represents the new market equilibrium after the increase in demand.

**Key Values:**

*   **OP:** The initial equilibrium price level.
*   **OP1:** The new equilibrium price level.
*   **OQ:** The initial equilibrium quantity.
*   **OQ1:** The new equilibrium quantity.

**Additional Annotations:**

*   An arrow points from the initial demand curve (D) to the new demand curve (D1) to illustrate the increase in demand.
*   Text labels explain the changes:
    *   "New Equilibrium is determined at E1"
    *   "Equilibrium Price rises from OP to OP1"
    *   "Equilibrium Quantity rises from OQ to OQ1"

**Summary:**

The graph demonstrates that an increase in demand (shift from D to D1) leads to a new equilibrium at E1, resulting in both an increase in the equilibrium price (from OP to OP1) and an increase in the equilibrium quantity (from OQ to OQ1). This visually represents the fundamental relationship between supply, demand, and market equilibrium.

Fig. 11.6

When demand increases to $\mathrm{D}_{1} \mathrm{D}_{1}$, it creates an excess demand at the old equilibrium price of OP. This leads to competition among buyers, which raises the price. Increase in price leads to rise in supply and fall in demand. These changes continue till the new equilibrium is established at point $E_{1}$. As there is an increase in demand only, equilibrium price rises from $O P$ to $O P_{1}$ and equilibrium quantity rises from $O Q$ to $O Q_{1}$.

## Decrease in Demand

(ii) Decrease in Demand: In case of decrease in demand (supply remaining unchanged), demand curve shifts to the left from DD to $\mathrm{D}_{2} \mathrm{D}_{2}$ (Fig. 11.7).
When demand decreases to $\mathrm{D}_{2} \mathrm{D}_{2}$, it creates an excess supply at the old equilibrium price of OP. This leads to competition among sellers, which reduces the price. Decrease in price leads to rise in demand and fall in supply. These changes continue till the new equilibrium is established at point $E_{2}$. Equilibrium price falls from $O P$ to $O P_{2}$ and equilibrium quantity falls from $O Q$ to $O Q_{2}$.
