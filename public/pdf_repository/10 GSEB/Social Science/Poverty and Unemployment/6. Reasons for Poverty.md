This section explores the various factors contributing to the prevalence of poverty, particularly highlighting issues related to agriculture, lack of alternative employment, social factors, economic policies, and demographic changes.

# Reasons for Poverty

Poverty is often more deeply rooted in rural areas compared to urban areas. The following are key reasons for its existence:

*   **Agricultural Sector Issues:** Decreased income from agriculture due to insufficient development in the field and inadequate irrigation facilities.
*   **Lack of Alternative Employment:** Limited job opportunities outside of farming in rural areas.
*   **Deficiency in Knowledge and Skills:** Lack of awareness regarding other employment avenues, education, skill development, or training in rural areas.
*   **Increased Debt:** Excessive expenditure influenced by the caste system, orthodoxy, traditions, and customs leads to increased debt. This is often due to unproductive expenditure.
*   **Illiteracy and Exploitation:** Illiteracy makes the poor vulnerable to exploitation and injustice. They also miss out on the benefits of government schemes due to lack of information.
*   **Policy Neglect:** Ignoring the necessities and economic welfare of the poorest sections of society during the formulation of economic policies.
*   **Shift in Crop Production:** Encouragement of cash crops led to reduced production of edible crops (foodgrains, pulses), causing scarcity and price rise, making it difficult for people to afford two meals a day.
*   **Impact of Economic Reforms:** Economic reforms sometimes led to the collapse of the rural economy, the breakdown of cottage and small-scale industries, increased migration, and decreased agricultural income.
*   **Health Issues and Costs:** Poor people suffer from malnutrition and diseases. While income remains static, expenditure on health and medicines increases.
*   **Technological Change:** Changes in technology caused the deterioration of traditional businesses and cottage industries, leading to increased unemployment.
*   **Population Growth:** Increased population growth, decreased death rates, and increased average lifespan led to a labor supply exceeding demand, increasing unemployment. Simultaneously, the production of basic necessities decreased, prices rose, purchasing power declined, and the standard of living deteriorated, thus increasing poverty.
