This section defines the concept of the world labor market, discusses the international movement of labor including 'brain drain', and highlights the increasing demand for skilled professionals in the globalized economy.

# The World Labour Market

The *world labor market* refers to the exchange or movement of laborers among different nations. When laborers migrate from one country to another for employment, business, training, or higher education, it is called the *movement of labor at the international level*.

The departure of highly educated individuals with technical knowledge and skills in search of better income, facilities, and job opportunities is known as *brain drain* or migration at the international level. Due to increasing social status consciousness, country-wise labor mobility has recently become significantly popular. The migration or permanent settlement of intelligent and talented people, on whom considerable resources have been spent, results in a loss of personalities possessing talent, intelligence, technical knowledge, and scientific attitude.

Globalization and liberalization have created new situations in economies. Currently, there is increased demand for people with knowledge and skills in information technology, communication technology, biotechnology, computer science, and medicine. Many countries are recruiting individuals with special qualifications and skills as trained expert laborers and professionals, often using attractive tactics and high perks. Industrial firms send employees abroad for required qualifications, knowledge, and skills to remain competitive. This is part of international migration.

Countries receive foreign exchange as income due to the migration of people working abroad. This inflow of foreign money helps to some extent in solving foreign exchange problems.
