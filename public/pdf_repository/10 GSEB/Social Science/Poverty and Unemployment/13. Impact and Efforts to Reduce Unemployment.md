This section discusses the negative social and economic effects of unemployment and outlines the various steps taken by the government to mitigate this problem through policy changes, skill development, and employment generation programs.

# Impact and Efforts to Reduce Unemployment

Problem of unemployment is the weakest link in our planning. Poverty and unemployment are two sides of the same coin and are interconnected. Unemployment is a major cause of poverty. This challenging problem primarily affects educated youth, leading to a loss of interest in studies and negative social and mental effects. They can become psychologically disappointed. Prolonged unemployment can lead to involvement in antisocial or immoral activities such as drug trafficking, illegal activities, robberies, and extortion. It increases social and economic inequality and class discrimination, causing a fall in living standards. When unemployment is coupled with price rise, the condition of the poor and unemployed becomes even more difficult and pathetic, sometimes leading them towards substance abuse and other addictions. Thus, unemployment is detrimental to the individual, the family, and the economy.

We have previously discussed government plans to reduce unemployment within the context of poverty alleviation programs. Let's recall them. In addition to those, many effective steps have been taken by the government:

*   **(1) High Economic Growth and Investment:** Aiming for a high economic development goal (e.g., 10% per year) acceptable to all. Increasing capital investment creates more employment opportunities. New sectors should be opened for fast and balanced economic development alongside agriculture, small/household industries, and cottage industries. To increase employment, the government provides economic help, education, and training centers through various schemes.
*   **(2) Promoting Labour-Intensive Industries:** The government should emphasize the development of units using labor-intensive methods for consumer goods production, small and cottage industries, village industries, handlooms, and handicrafts. Encouraging policies should be implemented within schemes for this purpose.
*   **(3) Rural Employment Generation:** More people can be employed in rural areas with less investment by developing new methods. This includes enabling multi-cropping to reduce seasonal unemployment, bringing new land under cultivation, arranging water and electricity for every field, implementing small and large irrigation schemes (dams, check dams, ponds, canals, tubewells), road construction, agricultural activities, poultry, fishery, cattle rearing, dairy industry, and afforestation programs. Planning should be employment-oriented to increase opportunities in villages, allowing villagers to earn sufficient income while working locally. This reduces migration to cities and eases pressure on urban employment demand. Encouraging plantation agriculture, organic farming, dry farming, multi-cropping, and growing vegetables and fruits is also important. Emphasis should be placed on improving the quality of education in villages.
*   **(4) Human Development in Rural Areas:** To sustain human development, focus is placed on health, education, pure drinking water, nutritious food, electricity, roads, banking, insurance, internet, communication, entertainment, water harvesting, constructing permanent public property, developing and encouraging local industries, and adopting employment-oriented programs. The main objective is the qualitative life of rural people and result-oriented improvement.
*   **(5) Skill Development and Vocational Education:** To decrease educated unemployment and youth unemployment, skills should be developed, and employment should be provided based on qualifications. Business-oriented or technical education policies should be adopted to produce skilled artisans. School and college curricula should align with the requirements of local industries. Efforts are made to provide education and training to youth to develop special skills, improve quality along with production, increase employment, income, and living standards, and ensure continued employment.
*   **(6) National Initiatives and Technical Education:** The Labour Ministry of the Indian government and State governments have implemented ambitious programs like "*Make in India*", "*Skill India*", and "*Digital India*" to enhance youth's knowledge, understanding, enthusiasm, and work capacity alongside industrial development. Educational and training programs have been started to develop skills. Technical colleges and universities have been set up nationwide, offering education according to vocational curricula and modern technology. Efforts are being made to meet employment needs through relevant curricula in schools and colleges, and vocational and technical education.

Currently, Higher Institutions like IITs and IIMs are being established in most states.

*   **(7) Exploring New Employment Areas and Entrepreneurship:** The government has opened new employment areas to plan manpower requirements. Significant opportunities exist in computer technology, information technology, pharmaceuticals, business management, packing and processing, outsourcing, marketing, catering, event management, office management, hotel management, share-stock marketing, etc. New university curricula have been designed to fulfill local requirements, and education, training, and teaching methods have been changed accordingly to meet future job needs. Short-term diploma or certificate courses (e.g., spinning, weaving, tanning, plumbing, repairing electronics like radio, TV, fridge, mobile, AC) have been started to meet manpower needs after completing regular education. Revolutions in automobile, electronics, computer science, genetic science, aero-space, and robotics have led to new courses to produce skilled artisans, engineers, and technicians. Under '*Start up India*', efforts are made to provide low-interest loans to entrepreneurs to promote new businesses. Coordination between local industries and training institutes helps ensure the supply of labor as per local requirements, promoting self-employment with government economic help instead of solely focusing on 'white-collar' jobs. Creating an environment and structure where educational courses are cheaper, expenses are reduced, and admission is easy is necessary.
*   **(8) Promoting Entrepreneurship and Financial Support:** Setting up new businesses and industries is essential for industrial growth and generating new employment opportunities. Investment, skill development, and cooperation are vital to increasing entrepreneurship among youth. To promote self-employment, the government has started many plans providing financial help at low interest rates for small investments, purchasing equipment, raw materials, or office furniture initially, and help with selling produced goods. Efforts provide technical, commercial, and managerial knowledge and support. Banks, financial institutions, local businessmen, and welfare organizations offer easy economic loan facilities, helping women establish self-employment through household industries. This encourages moving beyond traditional businesses and expands the horizon of new business and industrial sectors.
*   **(9) Role of Employment Exchange Centers:** Employment exchange centers act as a link between the unemployed and employers seeking laborers, workers, or educated/skilled/unskilled individuals. These organizations provide authentic information regarding the registration of educated unemployed, available job locations, and types of work. They offer career guidance and provide information through magazines like '*Rozgar Karkidi*'. Free services are provided through model career centers and a helpline number (1800-425-1514) offering important information, skill programs, and employment fair details. By 2015, there were 947 employment exchange centers in India. By 2013, 468.23 lakh unemployed people were registered nationwide, while in Gujarat, 8.30 lakh unemployed were registered.
