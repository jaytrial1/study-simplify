# Key Poverty Alleviation Schemes

Major objectives of the following schemes are to provide employment in agriculture, irrigation, road construction, food security, organic farming, and agricultural product sales to generate direct income and improve the financial condition of the poor.

*   **(i) Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchai Yojna:** Under the National Agriculture Plan, this scheme aims to increase the agriculture growth rate and related sectors by improving irrigation facilities. It seeks to bring irrigation to every part of agricultural land, promote drip irrigation, and construct small, medium, and large check dams to address water problems, ensuring every field receives water. These steps aim to protect farmers from agricultural risks and debt, providing them employment and income to escape the cycle of poverty.
*   **(ii) Pradhan Mantri Fasal Bima Yojna:** This scheme improves farm safety insurance plans to provide economic support to farmers during natural calamities. The government also provides bonuses on cotton purchases and concessions for crop loss/damage. '*Kshtimukt Krushi bhav panch*' has been set up to stabilize prices.
*   **(iii) Rastriya Payjal Karykam:** Programs include providing water to every field, improving existing canal networks, stopping soil erosion, digging new tubewells for tribal areas, and preventing salinity. Other initiatives involve digging ponds, watershed development, tank construction, rainwater harvesting, afforestation, canal lining, planting trees/shrubs, renovation of check dams, and increasing water storage capacity. These efforts aim to generate employment in rural areas and help families escape poverty.

State Governments also provide low-interest bank finance for Kharif crops, cattle rearing, and fertilizer storage, and support Central Government irrigation schemes.

The Gujarat Government has implemented poverty elimination programs, including organizing poverty welfare fairs for the past ten years to help farmers become self-reliant.

*   **(iv) E-Nam Yojna:** This program establishes an agricultural market where farmers can list their products online for traders to bid from anywhere. Its objective is to protect farmers from losses caused by mediators and brokers, ensuring they receive better remuneration and economic profit through competition.

Steps should be taken to develop agriculture and maximize returns, encouraging farmers to remain in the agricultural business.

*   **2. 'Gramoday se Bharat Uday':** This program focuses on farmer-oriented schemes. It provides economic help to build fences against wild animal crop damage, support for growing cattle fodder during crises, assistance for cattle shelters, rain forecasting, and mineral region discovery using satellite/drone technology. It includes land survey records, low-interest finance for farming mechanization (tractors, mini-tractors), subsidies, help for water tank construction, improving plantation crops, computerization in agriculture and finance societies, setting up testing labs (cotton, pulses, spices), ensuring suitable prices for farmers, deepening ponds, constructing farm ponds, cleaning/extending canals/reservoirs, and repairing check dams to increase water storage. These initiatives provide employment during non-farming periods and create permanent rural assets, helping farmers escape debt.
*   **3. Dindayal Upadhyay Gramjyoti Yojna:** This scheme aims to provide uninterrupted 24x7 electricity supply to rural areas at concessional rates for homes and farms. It involves laying new lines to connect 18,000 villages without electricity, setting up new electricity sub-stations, and providing subsidies for agricultural equipment. Subsidies are also provided for solar energy systems and equipment.
*   **4. Subsidy under the scheme 'Vanbandhu Kalyan Yojana for all-round development of Adivasis':** This scheme provides training and guidance to Adivasi women for livestock rearing ('Comprehensive Dairy Development Employment Scheme'), cultivation of creeper crops, construction of pavilions, organic farming, grading, and packaging. 'Samras hostels' and 'Smart Ashrams' are also established.
*   **5. Encouragement to Organic Farming:** This scheme supports farmers with registration, fee payment, purchasing farm products, training and education, low-interest finance, and market arrangements. It also promotes environmental protection and reduces agricultural expenditure.
*   **6. Mukhy Mantri Gram Sadak Yojna:** This program plans and implements road construction projects, helping village panchayats connect villages with roads and highways. It also includes programs like toilet construction.
*   **7. "MAA Annapurna Yojna":** Under this program, the Gujarat Government provides 35 kg of food grains per family per month *free of cost* to absolutely poor families and BPL individuals in cities and villages. Middle-class poor receive 5 kg of food grains at low rates (wheat at ₹2/kg, rice at ₹3/kg) from fair price shops. This scheme benefits 3.82 crore people, ensuring food security and saving a large sum of money that can be used to purchase other goods and improve living standards.

*   **8. Sansad Adarsh Gram Yojna:** Objectives include preventing migration from villages to cities. Members of Parliament adopt villages in their constituencies to improve education, health, and employment facilities, creating 'ideal villages'. Other goals are creating permanent public assets, developing opportunities for a good life, rural upliftment, preserving cultural heritage, creating employment through social harmony, and increasing human development.
*   **9. Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MANREGA):** This popular employment-oriented program, with the slogan "*Our village, Our work, along with reasonable price*", aims to enhance the livelihood of rural families or adults willing to do unskilled physical labor. It guarantees wage employment for at least 100 days (7 hours per day) per financial year for one person per family. The government pays fixed daily wages. If employment is not provided upon request, the government must pay an unemployment allowance. Works include village development, construction of personal toilets/wells, land leveling, horticulture, labor in Indra Awas Yojna, cattle sheds, organic manure pits, poultry/goat sheds, fish drying yards, canal cleaning, water storage works, and roadside tree plantation. By guaranteeing wage employment, the government aims to improve the living standards of the poor.
*   **10. Mission Mangalam:** Through this program, the state government organizes women from BPL families into '*sakhi mandal*' or '*self-help groups*'. They receive skill-based training to generate self-employment through household industries (e.g., making papad, pickle, incense sticks) and escape poverty.
*   **11. Dattopant Thegdi Vyaj Sahay Yojna:** This scheme provides low-interest loans to handicraft and handloom cottage industry artisans to purchase raw materials.
*   **12. Jyoti Gramodyog Vikas Yojna:** This plan aims to provide self-employment opportunities to unemployed rural individuals, increase income, and establish industries by offering economic help and subsidies for industrial plant, equipment, electricity, and land. Inspired by "*Start up India*", the government provides training, free electricity, land, and economic help to unemployed youth interested in starting industries.
*   **13. Bajpai Bankable Yojna:** Under this program, urban and rural unemployed individuals aged 18-65 with at least a 4th-standard education receive training to set up industries or continue traditional crafts. They also receive a fixed amount of finance to generate self-employment.
*   **14. Agro-Business Policy-2016:** The state government implemented this policy to create employment for 10 lakh people by establishing agro-food processing units and supporting processed food exports, thereby reducing poverty.
