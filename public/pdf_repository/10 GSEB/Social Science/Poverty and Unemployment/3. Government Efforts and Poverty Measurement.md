This section describes initial government efforts to improve the living standards of the poor, explains the concept of the poverty line, and details the methods used for measuring poverty.

# Government Efforts and Poverty Measurement

Efforts made by the government to improve the living standard of people 'Living Below Poverty Line':

Income of people living below the poverty line is very low in both cities and villages. Such families are often called '*Antyoday family*' or '*families living below poverty line (BPL)*'.

*   After identifying such families, the government initiated a *public distribution system* based on ration cards. These outlets, known as *Fair Price Shops*, provide essential items like rice, wheat, sugar, oil, salt, and kerosene to these families monthly.
*   These efforts aim to raise their living standard.

The concept of poverty was first introduced by Byod Orr, Director of WHO. For calculating the poverty line, certain factors are considered, including expenditure on food, clothing, accommodation, education, health, clean drinking water, electricity, sanitation facilities, transportation, income, and calorie intake. Based on these factors, a specific standard of living is fixed, known as the '*poverty line*'. The concept of the poverty line is dynamic, changing with time, location, and circumstances.

Measurement of Poverty: There are two primary methods for determining the number of people living below the poverty line:
1.  Based on a family's expenditure on various commodities and services.
2.  Based on the total income of the family (a family is often considered to have a minimum of 5 members for calculation purposes).
