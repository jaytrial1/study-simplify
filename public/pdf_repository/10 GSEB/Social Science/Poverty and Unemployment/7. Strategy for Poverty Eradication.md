This section discusses the overarching strategies adopted to combat poverty, emphasizing the importance of rural development, balanced economic growth, and equitable distribution of income.

# Strategy for Poverty Eradication

The cycle of poverty continues to tighten its grip. Understanding the reasons makes it easier to determine the appropriate strategy for poverty removal.

Up to the 11th Five-Year Plan, the neglect of rural areas in the strategy for Indian economic development and work plans was considered a primary cause of poverty. While the peak of the Indian economy might be in urban areas, its foundation lies in rural areas. Villages are the heart of the Indian economy, requiring a significant portion of the budget for their vitality and prosperity. True economic, social, and cultural development of India is possible only through village development. Therefore, the program '*Gramoday se Bharat Uday*' (Village Upliftment to India's Rise) implemented by the present government embodies the idea that the country's progress depends on the upliftment of villages. Central and state governments have focused on implementing new plans and programs for poverty eradication, stressing the improvement of basic needs, development of agriculture, and cottage/small-scale industries. Creating employment opportunities increases income flow, thereby reducing poverty.

Early strategies after Independence included promoting large-scale, heavy, and key industries with the slogan "*Gareebi Hatao*" (Remove Poverty). This encouraged urban development. Concurrently, initiatives like the Green Revolution and land reform acts were implemented to develop the agriculture sector, aiming to increase production, create employment, and reduce poverty. It was anticipated that benefits from industrial development would trickle down from owners and rich farmers to the poor, decreasing poverty. However, this "trickle-down" strategy proved overly optimistic. The pace of economic development was slow, and income distribution was unequal, concentrating wealth in the hands of a few and not significantly improving the condition of the poor.

Recognizing the need to address income disparity, the government formulated taxation policies aimed at increasing the production of essential commodities for the poor and making them accessible. Heavy taxes were levied on luxury goods and services used by the rich. Essential commodities were provided to the poor through Fair Price Shops (FPS) under the Public Distribution System (PDS) to improve their living standards. This approach aimed to shift resources from luxury goods production to necessities, increasing employment among the poor, enhancing their work capacity, production, and ultimately income and lifestyle.

To improve the agriculture sector, the government implemented programs like the Land Ceiling Act, Regulation of Tenancy Act, and Security of Cultivating Rights. These aimed to reduce the income of rich farmers and landlords and increase the income of farm laborers and tenants, improving the condition of the poor.

Policies were announced to encourage agriculture and agro-based industries (cattle rearing, dairy, pisciculture, afforestation), small irrigation schemes, household industries, and cottage/small-scale industries. Stress was laid on labor-intensive industries to increase employment and make people self-reliant. Traditional industries, handlooms, and cottage industries also received support. Arrangements were made for household industries to increase rural employment and provide alternative employment opportunities for rural youth through objective-based education, training, and skill development programs. Self-employment programs were implemented as part of the strategy to improve their condition, opening new fields of employment to increase income.

Improvements were made in the infrastructure for education, health, housing, employment, family planning, and communication. Irrigation facilities, roads, crop protection, agricultural skills/training, access to seeds/fertilizers/tractors through bank loans were improved for rural upliftment. Employment opportunities were created within villages to reduce migration to cities. Schools, secondary and higher secondary schools, and colleges offering technical and vocational courses and training centers were established nearby. Economic help (scholarships, fee reduction, residential schools) was provided to encourage higher studies, especially for girls. Women empowerment programs were started to make women economically self-reliant, and solid steps were taken to create self-employment. Thus, the government is strategically combating poverty. India aims to take firm steps towards eradicating deep-rooted poverty and contribute to the World Bank's mission of global poverty eradication by 2030.
