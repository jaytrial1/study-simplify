This section differentiates between two main types of poverty: absolute poverty, which relates to the inability to meet basic needs, and relative poverty, which is based on income inequality within a society.

# Types of Poverty

## Absolute Poverty

Individuals in society who cannot afford to purchase basic necessities like food grains, pulses, milk, and vegetables even at the lowest market rates are considered to be *absolutely poor*.

## Relative Poverty

Within a society comprising different income groups, some groups have lower incomes compared to others. Such a group is deemed *relatively poor*. This concept is more prevalent in developed countries.

For example, if the income of three persons is A: ₹10,000, B: ₹20,000, and C: ₹30,000. Person "A" is considered poor relative to "B" because their income is less. Similarly, both "A" and "B" are considered poor relative to "C" as their incomes are lower.
