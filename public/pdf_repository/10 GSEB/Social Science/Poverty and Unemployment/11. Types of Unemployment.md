This section details the different categories or types of unemployment observed in the Indian economy, explaining the specific conditions that lead to each type.

# Types of Unemployment

The following are the types of unemployment found in the Indian economy:

*   **(1) Seasonal Unemployment:** Due to insufficient irrigation facilities, irregular rainfall, and lack of alternative employment, farmers in India often remain unemployed for 3 to 5 months of the year. This is known as seasonal unemployment.
*   **(2) Frictional Unemployment:** When new technology replaces old technology, some laborers are temporarily unemployed during the transition period. This is called frictional unemployment.
*   **(3) Structural Unemployment:** This type of unemployment is attributed to the backward and orthodox nature of the Indian economy, social backwardness, traditional practices, customs, illiteracy, and a lack of structural facilities.
*   **(4) Disguised Unemployment:** This occurs when more workers than necessary are engaged in an occupation. Removing some of these extra laborers does not decrease the total production. These surplus laborers are considered unemployed in a hidden or disguised manner.
*   **(5) Industrial Unemployment:** This refers to individuals in the industrial sector who face unemployment, either short-term or long-term, due to changes within the industry.
*   **(6) Educated Unemployment:** A person who has completed at least secondary education or holds a degree but is unable to find employment is classified as educated unemployed.
