# Extent and Causes of Unemployment in India

Proportion of unemployment in India: The unemployment situation varies across states in India. Obtaining exact figures is challenging due to factors like the indifference of employment exchanges in registering job seekers. However, data from the Labour and Employment Ministry of India and the National Sample Survey (NSS) provide insights into the scale of unemployment.

According to the Census 2011 count, 116 million people were actively seeking employment. This included 32 million uneducated unemployed and 84 million educated unemployed. Approximately 4.70 crore people in the 15-24 age group were unemployed.

Based on the Labour Bureau survey of 2013-14, the unemployment rate in India was observed to be 5.4%. In Gujarat, the rate was 12 persons per 1000 (1.2%). In 2009-10, 34 persons per 1000 (3.4%) were unemployed in urban India, while 16 people per 1000 (1.6%) were unemployed in rural areas. The ratio of educated unemployed was higher in urban areas. The unemployment rate among women was 7.7% in 2013.

States like Sikkim, Kerala, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Jammu-Kashmir, and Tripura have witnessed higher unemployment rates. In contrast, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Karnataka, Chandigarh, and Gujarat have maintained lower unemployment rates. Gujarat's employment situation is considered good and noteworthy.

An estimation suggests that 15% of highly educated people are youth. 66% of the world's youth (under 35 years of age) live in India. For India to become a superpower based on its youth demographic, it must address the negative impact of unemployment.

Main reasons responsible for increasing unemployment in India include:

*   Increase in population.
*   Over-reliance on theoretical knowledge with insufficient practical knowledge.
*   Lack of technical knowledge or skill.
*   Failure to provide complete employment opportunities.
*   Irregular rainfall and high risk in agriculture, leading to loss of interest in the sector.
*   Improper irrigation facilities.
*   Lack of alternative employment during non-agricultural seasons.
*   Weak condition of cottage, household, and small-scale industries.
*   Influence of the caste system and joint family system, forcing people into traditional or family businesses.
*   Lack of courage to start new businesses.
*   Deficiencies in knowledge, skill, training, and experience.
*   Immobility of labor (reluctance to move for jobs).
*   Flaws in labor planning.
*   Low growth rate of industrial development.
*   Low saving tendency, resulting in a decreased rate of capital creation, which hinders the establishment of new businesses and industries due to lack of investment.
