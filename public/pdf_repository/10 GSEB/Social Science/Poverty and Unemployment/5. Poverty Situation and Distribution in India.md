This section provides statistical data on poverty levels in India based on different reports and criteria, discusses the state-wise distribution of poverty, and reflects on the paradox of poverty coexisting with abundant natural resources.

# Poverty Situation and Distribution in India

According to the poverty line defined for 2011-12 by the Planning Commission of India, the per capita expenditure for rural areas was set at ₹816 (meaning ₹4080 per family) and for urban areas at ₹1000 per person (meaning at least ₹5000 per family). This indicates the minimum required expenditure to reach the poverty line. Based on this new criterion, the number of poor people reduced to 27 crore in 2011-12, and the poverty rate decreased to 21.9 percent of the total population. In 2009-10, the poverty rate was 29.8 percent of India's population, affecting approximately 35.47 crore people.

For international comparison, the World Bank set the poverty line at 1.90 US\$ per capita income in 2012 (based on 2008 rates). According to a World Bank report, in 2010, India's total population was about 121 crore, with 32.7% living below the poverty line, totaling approximately 45.6 crore people.

As per the UNDP report in 2015, India's poverty rate in 2011-12 was 21.92%. This means out of a total of 26.93 crore poor people, 21.65 crore were in rural areas (25.7%) and only 5.28 crore were in urban areas. Chhattisgarh (36.93%) is noted as the poorest state in India, while Goa (5.09%) has the lowest poverty rate. In Gujarat, the poverty proportion is 16.63%. States like Chhattisgarh, Assam, Uttar Pradesh, Manipur, Bihar, Arunachal Pradesh, Jharkhand, and Orissa have a poverty proportion exceeding 30%.

India is rich in natural resources and raw materials. However, due to factors like the inability to utilize them profitably, lack of education, training, and skill, and defective planning over the years, these resources have not been fully exploited for public welfare. Consequently, the proportion of poverty has not decreased significantly. This leads to the saying, "*The poor live in wealthy India*".

Types of Poor:
*   **(A) Rural poor:** Generally include landless laborers, farm laborers, artisans from cottage or small-scale industries, marginal farmers, beggars, forced laborers, inhabitants of forests or mountainous regions, tribals, and temporary craftsmen.
*   **(B) Urban poor:** Typically consist of temporary laborers, daily wage earners, domestic workers, rickshaw pullers, workers in tea stalls, hotels, or dhabas, garage workers, and beggars – essentially those unable to meet their minimum and basic necessities and living in poverty.
