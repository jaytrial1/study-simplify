This section outlines the special provisions and facilities available for differently abled students, particularly in examinations and higher education, as well as broader schemes for their inclusion and support.

# Specific Educational Facilities for the Differently Abled

The Gujarat Board of Secondary and Higher Secondary Education provides the following facilities to differently abled students in public examinations:

*   Exemption from examination fee and relaxation in subject choice.
*   Exemption from figured questions for blind students.
*   Relaxation in linguistic spellings.
*   Providing 30 minutes extra time for a 3-hour question paper.
*   Facility of a writer for attempting the answer paper.
*   A minimum of 20 marks are required for passing an examination of 100 marks (Passing marks 20%).
*   Sitting arrangement on the ground floor in the examination center.
*   Differently abled examinees are allowed to use appropriate furniture and specific equipment. Question papers are provided in Braille for blind students.

Students can obtain information about these facilities from the school principal or from www.disabilityaffairs.gov.in. In higher educational institutions, 5% of seats are reserved for the disabled, along with a five-year relaxation in the upper age limit for admission.

## Information about Schemes for Divyang (Differently abled)

Several facilities and schemes are available to help the differently abled experience ease and equal opportunities in society and public places:

*   Ramp facilities in Government/Public institutions, sign boards in Braille, escalator facilities, etc.
*   Emphasis on imparting technical education and skills training.
*   Providing insurance, health and resource facilities, pensions, and necessary assistive devices.
*   Providing education through task analysis, work analysis, and separate toilet/bathroom facilities.
*   Facilitating education through mobile applications and theme-based learning.
*   The law provides 4% seat reservation for the disabled in government and private employment.
*   Concessions are given in ST bus passes and railway passes, along with scholarships.
*   Supportive equipment like Abacus, Callipers, Crutches, Audio CDs, Braille textbooks, large printed materials, hearing aids, wheelchairs, sticks, prosthetic hands and feet are provided.
*   Ensuring participation in Khel-Mahakumbh (sports events).
*   Facilitating accessible libraries.
*   Celebrating **December 3rd as 'World Disabled Day'**.
*   Arranging transport to polling booths for casting votes during elections and providing identity cards and ballot papers in Braille for blind persons.

The development of the differently abled is a collective duty of society. Our behavior and attitude should be motivating for them. We should all encourage them so they can become an integral part of society, experiencing equal rights and opportunities.

A constructive change in our attitude towards children, elderly people, and the differently abled will inevitably lead to positive social change.
