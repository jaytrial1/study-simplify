This section details the various steps and legislative measures taken by the government in India to curb corruption.

# Combating Corruption: Measures and Laws

The following steps have been taken to curb corruption:

1.  **Central Anti-bribery Bureau:** Established in 1964, its main function is to investigate allegations against government officials involved in corruption. It catches corrupt people red-handed for punishment.
    *   In Gujarat, there is a separate department with its headquarter in Shahibaug, Ahmedabad. People with corruption-related problems can call the helpline Toll-free Number 180023344444.
2.  **Anti Corruption Act, 1988:** Formulated by the Indian Government to stop corruption, aiming for clean public life and preventing misuse of position or power.
    *   It is compulsory for public servants, high officials, and politicians to declare their property before assuming and after leaving a post. If a person's property increases significantly beyond their known income during their tenure, it is considered an offense, punishable by law, and the government may confiscate such property.
3.  **Right to Information - 2005 and Citizens Charter:** These tools require government employees to complete administrative work within stipulated time periods, eliminating delays and making administration transparent and simple.
4.  **Black Money Act - 2015:** Enacted by the central government, treating corruption as a crime. Changes have also been made in FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act), Money Laundering Act, and Section 132 of the Custom Act. Efforts are made to curb corruption and find black money by appointing *Lokpal* (at the centre) and *Lokayukt* (in each state).
5.  **Gujarat Vigilance Commission, Gandhinagar:** Undertakes departmental investigations for corruption and misuse of power by government officials.
