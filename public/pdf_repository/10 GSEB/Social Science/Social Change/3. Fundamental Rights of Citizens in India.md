# Fundamental Rights of Citizens in India

In present social conditions, it is difficult for the common person to progress optimally without certain protections. *Rights* are an indispensable feature of citizenship. The United Nations Charter of Rights has enshrined human rights for all beings without discrimination and encourages nations to ensure citizens easily receive these rights.

The Indian Constitution grants six *fundamental rights* to all citizens without discrimination. These rights, previously studied, are:

(1) Right to equality
(2) Right to freedom
(3) Right against exploitation
(4) Right to religious freedom
(5) Cultural and educational right
(6) Right to constitutional remedies

If any state or nation violates these rights, citizens have the right to seek justice from the High Court or Supreme Court to protect their constitutional rights. This right is granted under the *Right to Constitutional Remedies*. It is often called the "**Soul of the Constitution**". The judiciary is responsible for protecting the fundamental rights of citizens and should provide simple, affordable, speedy, and effective judgments.
