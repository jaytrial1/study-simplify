This section emphasizes the importance of child development for national progress and outlines the rights of children as proclaimed by the United Nations and included in the Indian Constitution.

# Rights and Welfare of Children

Children are considered among the least protected sections of society. The progress of any nation depends on the overall development of its children. Educated, protected, and virtuous children grow into good citizens who contribute to the development of family, society, and nation. Such citizens are a boon for the nation. Therefore, child development and child welfare are preconditions for social development.

Children are considered property of the nation, and their upbringing, maintenance, and development must be handled with care and responsibility. It is our primary duty to ensure children are physically healthy, mentally cheerful, morally developed, and become healthy, sensible, and responsible citizens by promoting positive values.

The United Nations proclaimed the rights of children regarding their development and welfare in its Charter of Rights in 1992. These *juvenile rights* are included in our Constitution and are as follows:

(1) Every child has the right to live without discrimination based on caste, color, language, or nationality.
(2) Children have the right to proper upbringing by their parents and cannot be separated from them without a solid reason.
(3) Children have a legal right to education to develop their personality.
(4) Every child has the right to a healthy life and the right to live life happily by participating in games and entertainment.
(5) Every child has the right to conserve their culture and live within their religious community.
(6) Every child has the right to protect themselves against physical or mental torture, use of narcotic drugs, inhuman punishment, or fines.
(7) Every child has the right to security and a proper standard of living for their physical, mental, moral, and social development.
