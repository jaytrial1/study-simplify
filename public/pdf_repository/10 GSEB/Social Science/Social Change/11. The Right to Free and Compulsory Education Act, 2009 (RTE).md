This section explains the RTE Act in India, its necessity, and the various provisions it mandates for ensuring quality primary education for children.

# The Right to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (RTE)

![img-6.jpeg](Social Change/11. The Right to Free and Compulsory Education Act, 2009 (RTE)/img_img-6.png)

The Central Government implemented the law regarding the right to free and compulsory education for children in 2009. Based on this, the Gujarat government proclaimed the "Law Regarding Right to Free and Compulsory Education" on February 18, 2012.

What is the need of this Law?

> **Answer:** The need for this law arises from the 86th amendment to the Indian Constitution, which made primary education free and compulsory for all children aged 6-14 years. This is a significant step towards developing children's human capabilities. The law aims for the physical, mental, and all-round development of children by creating necessary educational opportunities and ensuring quality primary education.

Under this law, specific norms are set for schooling and physical facilities, keeping in mind children's education and health. These norms cover classrooms, laboratories, pure drinking water, electricity, midday meal arrangements, teacher quality and qualifications, recruitment standards, and provisions for grants to schools. Key provisions include:

(1) Every child aged 6 to 14 should get admission to a school near their locality. Children without a birth certificate as age proof cannot be refused admission.
(2) Even if a child attains 14 years of age but has not completed primary education, they can still receive free education.
(3) For admission, the child's age should be 6 years. If a birth certificate is unavailable, hospital records or an age-related affidavit from parents can be accepted as proof.
(4) All students should be given admission without discrimination.
(5) No kind of fees, like capitation fees, admission fees, or other deposits, can be taken.
(6) Interviews of children or parents should not be conducted at the time of admission. Admission should not be based on parents' income, educational qualification, or capabilities. No admission test for parents should be taken.
(7) For the first time, a revolutionary step has been taken for the education of children aged 3 to 5 years by including nursery education under the law and making rules for special training, courses, curriculum, and evaluation of pre-school teachers.
(8) The Act provides that children from weaker sections and backward classes (SCs and STs) whose families are in the BPL category and are interested in studying should be given admission in government-recognized private primary schools. It is compulsory for 25% of students in standard 1st in these schools to be from these sections.
(9) School teachers are not allowed to give private tuitions.
(10) All under-qualified teachers in schools must obtain academic qualifications within 5 years.
(11) Any child who has not completed primary education cannot be terminated from school until transferred.
(12) In private primary schools, the fees for SC and ST children are paid by the government under fixed conditions.
(13) A scheduled management, tribunal, or state council is provisioned for compliance with the law. For breaches, the Director of the school may be fined, or the school's recognition can be terminated.
