This section focuses on the rights and educational support provided to individuals with disabilities, emphasizing their potential and the importance of equal opportunities.

# Rights and Support for the Differently Abled (Divyang)

We are often inspired by the achievements of persons with physical disabilities. With enough opportunities and training, disability does not have to be a hindrance. The abilities of *differently abled* people can be used constructively in society. These children are an integral part of our society. The Government of India has taken steps under the law to highlight their potential and ensure they are not subjected to prejudiced injustice due to their disability.

Children who are physically, mentally, emotionally, or socially different from others are called differently abled children. They require special social and educational services to develop their abilities.

For the upliftment of the differently abled, the Government published the **Right of Persons with Disabilities Act 2016**. This act classifies different types of disabilities in 21 ways. It promotes equal opportunity, inherent dignity, respect, and friendship for such persons without discrimination, acknowledging that they can participate in every sphere of society.

The Government directs recognized educational institutions to provide necessary facilities based on the needs of differently abled children. The Act gives instructions to create a conducive and healthy environment for academic and social cohesion, encouraging students and preventing inferiority complexes. These instructions include:

(1) Giving differently abled children equal opportunity in admissions, education, sports, and other activities. Special permission should allow them to sit with normal students, ensuring no discrimination based on speech or behavior.
(2) Educational institutions should provide physical facilities that are easy for differently abled children to use, such as ramps, removing obstacles for free movement, and accessible chairs, benches, toilets, and bathrooms.
(3) Providing an environment conducive to the continuous and comprehensive development of their educational career and social development.
(4) Providing proper education for visually and hearing-impaired children, potentially through sign language skills.
(5) Taking necessary curriculum-oriented steps to help students study easily by identifying specific limitations and addressing them.
(6) Monitoring the continuous and comprehensive progress of differently abled children.
(7) Facilitating transportation for the disabled child or their assistant according to their physical disability.
(8) Conducting a survey every 5 years to ensure differently abled children are not deprived of education.
(9) Creating a resource room to assist at all levels of learning activities.
(10) Providing scholarships based on merit for students with Benchmark Disability.
(11) Providing facilities for differently abled children to appear for examinations without hindrance.
(12) Ensuring no offensive language, violence, exploitation, or ragging activities against differently abled students. A special committee should address issues of the disabled.
(13) Carrying out rescue, help, protection, and rehabilitation activities for the differently abled. If students drop out, reasons should be found, and they should be helped in rehabilitation.
(14) Creating awareness among students, parents, the public, and society about differently abled children.
(15) Children with Benchmark Disability have the right to admission to a school of their choice or a special school near their residence. Every child with Benchmark Disability should benefit from free education and specific equipment in a suitable environment until they reach 18 years of age.
