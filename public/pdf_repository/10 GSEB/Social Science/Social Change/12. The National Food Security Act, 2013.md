This section defines food security and explains the objectives and provisions of the National Food Security Act, including subsidized grain distribution and support for vulnerable groups.

# The National Food Security Act, 2013

*Food security* means ensuring "every person gets nutritious food for remaining active and for healthy life and procurement of food".

This bill was implemented by the Central Government on July 5, 2013.

Objective of Food Security Bill:
(1) To satisfy the food demands of the country's increasing population, ensure quality and quantity of food, and provide it at a low rate.

(2) To curb malnutrition among people and children and encourage increased production of nutritious food.
(3) To strengthen, make transparent, and simplify the Public Distribution System (PDS).
(4) To provide food security to families at the top of the Antyodaya Yojana and BPL lists, stipulating nutritious food in proper quantity and at a concessional rate.
(5) To help pregnant women and feeding mothers get nutritious food.

## Some Legislative Provisions

*   According to this act and "**Maa Annapurna Yojna**", needy, middle-class poor families in urban and rural areas are provided grain at a subsidized rate. Antyodaya families receive 35 Kg of grains free of cost.
*   Beneficiaries can buy wheat at ₹2.00 per Kg, Rice ₹3.00 per Kg, and Coarse grain at ₹1.00 per kg in fixed quantities and times. Good quality grain is provided under PDS on fixed conditions.
*   The Central Government provides ₹6000 as maternity help to mothers.
*   The bill provides for a food security allowance to beneficiaries in place of grains if necessary.
*   Under this Act, the Gujarat government provides sugar, iodized salt, kerosene, and edible oil (twice a year) at subsidized rates to Antyodaya and BPL families through ration shops every month.
*   State Governments are to update and publicly display lists of beneficiary families (under the woman's name) in Gram Panchayat, Gram Sabha, Ward Sabha, E-gram, fair price shops, Executive Magistrate's office, or the supply department website.
*   Steps like biometric identity, EPIC card, bar code ration card, food coupons, and taking images with a webcam have been started to improve and strengthen the PDS and make the distribution system corruption-free.
*   The bill requires setting up an "Internal complaint preventive mechanism". A "Nodal Officer" is to be appointed for regulating and controlling grain distribution. A "Rajya Ann Ayog" (State Food Commission) is to be set up in the State, and a Food Commissioner appointed for complaints.
*   Under various provisions of the "National Food Safety Act" and "Maa Annapurna Yojna", approximately 3.82 crore needy citizens receive grain at a concessional rate. State Governments implement such welfare schemes.
