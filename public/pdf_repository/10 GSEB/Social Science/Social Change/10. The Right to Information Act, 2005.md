This section explains the Right to Information (RTI) Act, how citizens can use it to obtain information, and the provisions for appeal and penalties.

# The Right to Information Act, 2005

The Central Government enacted the **Right to Information Act** on June 15, 2005. It applies to all states of India except Jammu and Kashmir (at the time the text was written). The act does not apply to secret services, institutions dealing with security and safety, institutions promoting unity and integrity, or foreign ambassador's offices. The Gujarat government implemented rules regarding the Gujarat Right to Information Act on February 18, 2012.

The legislative basis of this act is to achieve transparent, clean, simple, and quick administrative operations with public cooperation. Any citizen can approach a higher official of a department and ask questions to get information about their pending work, scheme implementation, or the status of public-oriented work.

## How to obtain information

As per this right, an applicant must pay a fixed fee (presently Rs. 20) in cash, postal order, pay order, or non-judicial stamp along with the application format to get information. The application can be self-signed, typed, or sent via email to the concerned department. Persons from families included in the BPL list are exempt from paying fees for copies. There is no need to mention why the information is being requested.

The concerned Assistant Public Information Officer (APIO) provides a receipt with a serial number (ID number) for the received application. This ID number must be mentioned in any subsequent correspondence regarding the application. The APIO will provide directions within 30 days of accepting the application for acquiring information. If a copy is demanded, it can be obtained by paying a fixed fee decided according to the law.

An APIO may refuse to give information as per the law if the required information affects the sovereignty or national interest and security of the country, is related to secret issues, leads to contempt of court, is connected to scientific secrets, or promotes crime.

## Provisions for Appeal

If the concerned department does not provide information within 30 days or refuses to give it, the affected applicant can appeal to the Public Information Officer (PIO) within 30 days of the rejection. No fee is required for this appeal. If the decision of the first appeal is not received within the stipulated time, or the required information is still refused, the affected applicant can make a second appeal to the State's Chief Information Officer within 90 days.

## Provisions for Fine

If an information officer refuses to give information without reasonable grounds, hides information with ill intention, intentionally gives improper, incorrect, or misleading information, or destroys information, the concerned information officer is fined a fixed charge per day for the delay in providing information.

For queries or guidance related to the Right to Information Act, a helpline number 9924085000 has been started (the first in the country) available during working hours except public holidays. Additionally, a *civil right letter* has been declared, fixing deadlines for solutions in concerned offices, allowing citizens to inquire about application status. The Gujarat government has started a common service portal where citizens can apply online, verify documents, and make payments for about 28 services and inquire about application status 24x7. This Act is considered a revolutionary step to combat corruption.
