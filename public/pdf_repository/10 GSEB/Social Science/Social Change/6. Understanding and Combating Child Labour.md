This section discusses the issue of child labour, its prevalence, causes, reasons for demand, and the negative impacts on children.

# Understanding and Combating Child Labour

*Child labour* is a global problem that needs to be overcome quickly. All laborers below 14 years of age are called child laborers. According to a UNICEF report, India has the highest number of child laborers in both organized and unorganized sectors relative to its population.

Child labour is prevalent in various sectors of the Indian economy, including hotels, factories, construction, hazardous jobs (like cracker businesses or kilns), agriculture (field labor, animal husbandry, fishery), and the service sector (servants, tea stalls, hotels/dhabas, garages, pulling carts, distributing newspapers, picking plastic/debris, begging, cleaning roads).

## Reasons for Child Labour

The condition of child labor is often pathetic in these sectors and can contribute to child delinquency. Various factors are responsible for child labour:
*   Poverty
*   Illiteracy among parents
*   Large family size
*   Family requirements
*   Unemployment of adults in the family
*   Need to increase family income

Children forced into labor for livelihood include those who have escaped to cities, are searching for shelter, are orphans or destitute, or those who have taken shelter with relatives who force them to work in return for food or shelter.

## Reason for More Demand of Child Labour

Owners and businessmen often prefer child labor over adult labor for several reasons:
(1) Child labor is often the *cheapest factor of production*. More work can be extracted from children with less wages compared to adults.
(2) Child laborers are often *unorganized*. Due to lack of organization, they cannot easily raise their voice or protest and can be exploited in various ways without their knowledge.
(3) Work can be taken from them in hard and hazardous conditions with low wages. They are often forced to work longer hours than scheduled through threats or temptations.
(4) Child laborers are easily available, leading to a higher supply.
(5) Lack of educational facilities in rural areas leads parents to send children to work to meet family needs at schooling age. Parents may view them as additional earning hands.

Thus, children at a young age are deprived of games, entertainment, rest, childhood, parental love, warmth, care, and education. Some of these children may become involved in crime at a tender age and become criminals.

## Remedies for abolishing Child Labour

The government has made many constitutional provisions to abolish child labour, exploitation, and abuse:

1.  **Constitutional Provisions:**
    *   No child below 14 years will be employed in a factory, occupation, or job. Legal action can be taken against employers who violate this.
    *   Children cannot be exploited in childhood or teenage. They cannot be deprived of moral security and physical comforts.
    *   Within 10 years of the Constitution's implementation, the government was to arrange free education for children up to 14 years of age.
