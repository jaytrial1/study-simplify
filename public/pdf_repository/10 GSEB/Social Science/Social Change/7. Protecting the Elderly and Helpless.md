This section discusses the growing population of elderly people, the challenges they face in modern society, and the steps taken by the government and international bodies for their welfare and protection.

# Protecting the Elderly and Helpless

The problem of elderly and helpless people is universal. Old age is a natural part of life. It is the responsibility of society to consider the security and well-being of elderly people.

Currently, the average life expectancy has increased due to improvements in health services, modern medical facilities, and treatments. In India, the average life span increased from 63.5 years (2001-2005) to 67.5 years (2015). Between 2001 and 2011, the number of elderly people in India increased by 2.75 crore. According to one estimate, there were 5.28 crore elderly women and 5.11 crore elderly men in 2011. Kerala has the highest number of elderly people, while Arunachal Pradesh has the minimum. In Gujarat, the number is approximately 35 lakhs. The increasing elderly population and average life span bring up social and physical problems.

Influenced by western culture and the desire for nuclear families, children are sometimes neglecting their ethical responsibilities, values, and culture towards the elderly. Due to a lack of sensitization, elderly people are sometimes compelled to live in 'Old Age Homes'. To draw attention to the problems of the elderly, the UN declared 1999 as the "**International Elderly Year**". Additionally, every year, **October 1st** is celebrated as "**World Elderly People Day**" internationally.

The government has taken the following steps for the safety and security of elderly people:

*   In 1999, the government developed the "**National Policy for Senior Citizens**", providing pensions like the Indira Gandhi National Senior Citizen Pension Scheme and Atal Pension Scheme.
*   Under various schemes, elderly people receive higher interest on deposits in post offices or banks. Men and women are given 30 to 50 percent discounts on bus, railway, and air tickets.
*   State governments have opened well-facilitated "Old Age Homes" in every district. Gardens have been developed for them in cities. Efforts are made to bring peace to their lives through music, yoga, games, and mental activities.
*   The government implemented the **Parents and Senior Citizens Care and Welfare related law in 2007** to protect them from domestic violence, exploitation, and abuse. This law includes provisions to punish children who harass their parents. The responsibility of caring for the elderly lies with their family and relatives. They have the right to receive maintenance from their children.
*   The Central Government has implemented programs to honor senior citizens for their contributions.
