This section provides detailed information about the town planning and key features discovered at the archaeological site of Mohenjo-Daro.

# Key Indus Valley Site: Mohenjo-Daro

In 1922, archaeologists Rakhal Das Banerji and Dayaram Sahni discovered remains of a huge town planning system during excavation work carried out under the guidance of Sir John Marshal and Colonel Meke, in Larkhana district (now in Pakistan). **Mohenjo-Daro** means 'the heap of the dead'.

## Construction of Town (Municipal Composition)

From the point of view of town planning, Mohenjo-Daro is considered excellent.
*   Houses were built on a **high plinth** to protect them from floods and dampness.
*   Houses of rich people were **double-storeyed** with five to seven rooms.
*   Houses of poor people were **single-storeyed** with two to three rooms.
*   The town was **fortified**.
*   Main entrances of houses opened onto **side lanes**, not the main roads.
*   Remains of a **store house, kitchen, and bathroom** were found in every house.
*   There was proper arrangement of **doors and windows** for air and ventilation.

## Roads

Roads are a main feature of this town planning.
*   Roads were **9.75 metres wide**.
*   Small link roads crossed the major ones at **right angles**.
*   Roads were wide enough for a number of vehicles to pass at a time.
*   Pits on the roadside suggest there might have been **lamp posts**.
*   Roads were straight main roads **without any turns**.
*   There were two main roads: one from north to south and the other from east to west, crossing each other at right angles.

## Drainage System

The drainage system is a unique feature of this town planning, found nowhere else except the island of Crete in the Mediterranean Sea.
*   This system was built to **drain dirty water** out from the town.
*   Each house had a **cesspit**.
*   This planned, systematic, and efficient drainage system proves they were very careful about their **health and hygiene**.

## Public Baths

A huge bath was excavated at Mohenjo-Daro.
*   There was an arrangement to put **fresh water in and dirty water out**.
*   There might have been facilities for **hot water** and small rooms for **changing clothes**.
*   These public baths might have been used on **festivals and religious ceremonies**.

## Public Buildings

The ruins of two huge buildings have been found.
*   They might have been used as a **town hall, theatre, administrative office, or granary**.
*   A barrack of such buildings was also found, which must have been used to **accommodate soldiers**.
