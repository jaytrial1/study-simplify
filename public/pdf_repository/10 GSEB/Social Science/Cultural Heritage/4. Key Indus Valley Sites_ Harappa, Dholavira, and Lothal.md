This section describes other important archaeological sites of the Indus Valley Civilization, detailing their unique characteristics and contributions to ancient Indian architecture and planning.

# Key Indus Valley Sites

## Harappa

In 1921, under the leadership of Sir John Marshal and Colonel Meke, Dayaram Sahni discovered very ancient remains of Indian civilization from Montegomary in Punjab district near **Harappa**. The remains of Indus valley civilization were found from various places, including:
*   Rapar in the Himalayan region
*   Alamgirpur at Meerut in Uttar Pradesh
*   Kalibangan in Rajasthan
*   Lothal at Dholka in Gujarat
*   Deshalpur-Shikarpur in Kutch
*   Dholavira
*   Rangpur near Limbdi in Saurashtra
*   Shrinathgad (Rozadi) near Gondal
*   Kuntasi near Morbi
*   Somnath

The region of the Saptsindhu river is a region of Indian culture. The culture that developed here is known as **Indus valley civilization**. Since its remains were found first from Harappa, it is also known as **Harappan civilization**. Copper and stone weapons and other things were found here, leading it to also be known as the **copper stone age**. Town planning of Harappa civilization was very systematic. Its **granaries** and **forts** were remarkable. People of that age were fond of wearing ornaments, and remains of such have been found.

## Dholavira

A huge and systematic ancient town, contemporary to Harappan civilization, has been found 2 km away from Dholavira village at Khadirbet, in the vast desert of Bhachau taluka, Kutch. Archaeological department of Gujarat surveyed these ruins. Research work was undertaken by officers of the Archaeological Survey of India in 1990 A.D., with excavation carried out under the guidance of Ravindra Singh Bisht.

Remains at Dholavira show that palaces and main walls of the town were painted with **white colour**. Fortified walls encircling the town suggest strong security. This wall is made up of clay, stone, and bricks. **Potable water** was available here, as well as a proper system of **water purification**, which was advanced for its time.

## Lothal

Lothal is situated in Dholka taluka of Ahmedabad district, between the rivers Bhogavo and Sabarmati. It is 18 km from the Bay of Khambhat, where three layers of human habitat were found.

A huge **dockyard** was constructed at the lower eastern part of the town to facilitate ships during high tides. This is a unique feature of Lothal. Such a dockyard, storage shops, etc., prove the existence of **exports and imports**. This suggests that Lothal might have been a rich and prosperous port of India. This is a matter of great pride for Gujarat and Indian history.
