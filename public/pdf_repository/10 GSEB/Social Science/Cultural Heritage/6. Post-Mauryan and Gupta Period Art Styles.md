This section briefly touches upon the development of art styles after the Mauryan period, including the Dravidian style in South India and the flourishing arts during the Gupta period.

# Post-Mauryan and Gupta Period Art Styles

## Dravidian Style of Art in South India

Many Buddhist Stupas were built in the regions near the Krishna and Godavari rivers during the reign of Satavahana kings. They were semi-circle, oval, and bell-shaped. Stupas in Nagarjunakonda and Amravati are considered the best specimens of the **Dravidian style** of art. Chola kings adopted the Dravidian style of architecture to a great extent.

## Art of Gupta Period

During the Gupta period, architecture, sculpture, painting, dancing, and music flourished. This period is known as the **golden period of art**. Examples of Gupta art include:
*   Parvati temple at Jabalpur (Ninava)
*   Bhumara (Nagoda) Shiva temple
*   Eran's (Madhya Pradesh) Narsinh temple
*   Gopmandir at Jamnagar
*   Stupas, Chaityas, Maths, Viharas, Flags, and Pillars

Best specimens of sculpture from the Gupta period include:
*   Buddha statue at Sarnath
*   Statue of Lord Vishnu at Mathura
*   Statue of Mahavir Swami
*   Caves of Udayagiri
*   Statue of Vishnu in the form of Varaha
