This section addresses the issue of gender equality in India and the various forms of discrimination faced by women.

# Gender Equality

The Indian constitution guarantees equality and justice to all citizens. According to the 2011 census, females constitute 48.46% of the total population, and males 51.54%. From this perspective, females play a leading role as a human resource not only in India but also globally.

However, due to biological differences and differences in upbringing and expectations from social and cultural perspectives, the path of development for males and females is often different. Even today, in many families, women are primarily engaged in household chores, cooking, or bringing up children. They may not have any contribution to the economic earnings of the family or the country and often lack decision-making power within the family.

Women often suffer from inadequate healthcare and are deprived of economic and educational rights. They experience discrimination in various aspects of life, including clothes, games, educational opportunities, food, social outings, ethics, and behavior. Due to low literacy rates, they can become victims of child marriage, the *parda* system, the dowry system, and other social evils. They are sometimes victims of female infanticide, face low respect, and are affected by the desire for a son, social traditions, and sexual discrimination. Inequality is evident in economic, political, social, cultural, and religious fields.
