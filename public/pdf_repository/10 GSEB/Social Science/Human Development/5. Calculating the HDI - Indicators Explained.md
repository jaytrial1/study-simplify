This section explains the specific indicators and their measurements used for calculating the Human Development Index since 2010.

# Calculating the HDI - Indicators Explained

Since 2010, the HDI calculation uses the following indices derived from the three core dimensions:

1.  **Life Expectancy Index (LEI)** (Average Age):
    *   This is for determining health and longevity.
    *   Life expectancy at birth predicts how many years an infant is expected to survive.
    *   Highest value set: 83.6 years; Lowest value set: 20 years.
    *   As per the Human Development Report 2015, life expectancy in India was 68 years.

2.  **Education Index (EI)**:
    *   This has two sub-dimensions:
        *   **(i) Mean Years of Schooling (MYS):** The average number of years spent in school by an adult aged 25 or older.
            *   Highest value set: 13.3 Years; Lowest value set: zero years.
            *   According to the Human Development Report 2015, the average years of schooling in India is 5.4 years.
        *   **(ii) Expected Years of Schooling (EYS):** The approximate number of years a child of 5 years is expected to spend in school.
            *   Highest value set: 18 Years; Lowest value set: zero years.
            *   The average expected years of schooling for India in the 2015 report was 11.7 years.

3.  **Income Index (II)** (Life Style):
    *   For determining subsistence, Gross National Income (GNI) per capita is used, replacing Gross Domestic Product (GDP) per capita.
    *   According to the Human Development Report 2015, India's per capita Gross National Income (GNI) was \$5497, while per capita Gross Domestic Product (GDP) was \$5238.
    *   For calculating per capita income, a nation's income is converted based on the currency of the United States, known as **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)**.
