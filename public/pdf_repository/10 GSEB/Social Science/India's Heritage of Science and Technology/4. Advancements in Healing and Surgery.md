# Science of Medicine and Surgery

Ancient India achieved unprecedented advancements in medicine and surgery.

*   **Maharshi Charak**, **Maharshi Sushrut**, and **Vagbhatta** were pioneers who advanced Indian medical science and surgery through intense research.
*   Maharshi Charak wrote '**Charak Samhita**', mentioning over 2000 medicinal herbs.
*   Maharshi Shushrut described instruments used in surgery, noted for their sharpness.
*   Vagbhatta wrote '**Vagbhatta Samhita**', another very important work.
*   Studying Charak Samhita, Shushrut Samhita, and Vagbhatta Samhita is considered very useful for doctors.

Ancient Hindu herbal science was rich in minerals and medicines from plants and animals, detailing complicated processes for making medicines, their classification, and usage.

Ancient Indian physicians could:

*   Stop blood circulation with a bowl-shaped bandage.
*   Skillfully operate on the abdomen, kidney, cataract, hernia, stone, piles, and bladder.
*   Classify, join broken or displaced bones, and easily extract objects pierced into the body.
*   Recognize symptoms and diagnose diseases.
*   Give dietary directions for post-recovery.
*   Perform plastic surgery, including joining noses and ears.
*   Teach surgery methods using dead bodies or wax statues.
*   Conduct risky operations during delivery.
*   Were expert gynaecologists and paediatricians.

Veterinary science also developed, with books on diseases of horses (*Ashwa*) and elephants (*Hasti*), such as '**Hasti Ayurveda**', '**Shalihotra**', and '**Ashwashastra**'.

Vagbhatta's work '**Ashtang Hriday**' made valuable contributions to diagnosis.
