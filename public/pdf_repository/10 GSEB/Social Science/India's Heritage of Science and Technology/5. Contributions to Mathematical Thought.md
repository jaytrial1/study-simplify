This section discusses the pivotal discoveries and advancements made by ancient Indian mathematicians that influenced global mathematics.

# Mathematics

Many epoch-making mathematical discoveries originated in India and are used globally today. India's gifts to the world include the discovery of:

*   *Zero* (0) - Aryabhatta discovered zero. The process of writing zero after figures was discovered by sage Grutsamad.
*   *Decimal system* - Evidence found in measuring and weighing instruments from Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro.
*   *Algebra*
*   *Theorem of Boddhayan* (triangle policy)
*   *Geometry*
*   *Arithmetic*

Ancient Indian mathematicians determined names for numbers with up to 53 zeros after 1.

**Bhaskaracharya** wrote '**Lilawati Ganit**' and '**Bij Ganit**' in 1150 A.D. He discovered the signs for addition (+) and subtraction (-).

**Brahmgupta** introduced types of equations.

**Boddhayan** discovered a theorem (triangle policy).

**Aapstambha** determined measurements for sacrificial pits (*yagyavedis*) used in Vedic Yagyas in '**Shulva Sutras**' (1800 B.C.), which also included analysis of principles.

**Aryabhatta** mentioned the value of **$\pi$ (Pi)** as $\frac{22}{7}$ (approximately 3.14) in his book "**Aryabhattiyam**". He also proposed that $\pi$ is a constant ratio of a circle's circumference to its diameter.

Aryabhatta introduced the '**Ashtang**' method for operations like multiplication, addition, subtraction, square-root, and cube-root. For this, he is known as the '**father of Mathematics**'. He also wrote other books like '**Dash Gitika**' and '**Aryabhattiyam**'. He described main principles of Astronomy concisely in "**Aryasiddhanta**", finding solutions to fundamentals of Arithmetic and Geometry.

Other scholars like Boddhayan, Aapastambha, Katyayan, Bhaskaracharya, and Brahmagupta also discussed various aspects of Mathematics in their books.
