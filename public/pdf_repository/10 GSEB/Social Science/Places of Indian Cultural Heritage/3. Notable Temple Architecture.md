# Notable Temple Architecture

## Mahabalipuram

**Mahabalipuram** is located 60 kms away from Chennai in Tamil Nadu. This city is famous for its splendid temple architecture and seashore. **Narsinh Varman-I**, popularly known as Mahamalla, of the Pallava dynasty, built 7 Chariot Temples here. Currently, only five chariot temples exist, as two have submerged in the sea. Notable sculptures include an amazing figure of **Lord Vishnu** in a smiling pose and an idol of **Goddess Durga slaying Mahisasura**. Mahabalipuram, known for its unparalleled architectural rock sculpture, was also a famous harbour in ancient times.

## Pattadakal Memorial

**Pattadakal**, the capital of the Chalukya dynasty, is located 16 kms away from Badami Nagar. The temples here were constructed using the **Dravid style of architecture** between the seventh and eighth centuries. The **Virupaksha temple** is the biggest temple in Pattadakal.

## Temples at Khajuraho

These temples are located in the small village of **Khajuraho** in Chattarpur district of Madhya Pradesh. Khajuraho was the ancient capital city of the **Chandela Rajputs of Bundelkhand**, who built these temples during their reign (950 to 1050 AD). Presently, 25 temples are still in existence. Most are **Shaiva (Shiva) temples**, while some are Vaishnav and Jain temples. They share a similar architectural and sculptural style. The **64 Yoginis temple** is a significant one. The **toran (arch)** of the temple is an excellent example of ornamental architecture. All temples were constructed with **granite stone** in the **Naagar style**. The art of sculpture and architecture at Khajuraho temples is captivating.

## Sun Temple of Konark

It is situated near the Bay of Bengal in Puri district in Odisha. Constructed in the 13th century during the reign of **King Narsinh Varman-I** of the Gang Dynasty, this chariot temple is designed in the form of the chariot of the Sun God, drawn by seven horses. It features 12 massive wheels, which adorn the pedestal of the temple. These wheels symbolize the twelve months, and each wheel has eight spokes, representing the eight *prahars* (divisions) of the day. The details of the motifs and subject diversity in this temple are unparalleled. The temple was built using **black stones**, earning it the name **"black pagoda"**. Its remains reflect the sculpture and civilization of 13th-century Odisha.

## Bruhdeshwar Temple

This temple is located at **Tanjur** in Tamil Nadu. Constructed between 1003 AD and 1010 AD by **King Rajraja-I** of the Chola dynasty, it is a Shiva temple, also known as **Rajrajeshwar temple** or **Bruhadeshwar**. The temple complex covers a vast area surrounded by a wall. The main structure is about 500 feet long and 250 feet wide. Its summit, or pinnacle, rises 200 feet high, placing it among temples with high pinnacles. This temple is a marvelous heritage of Indian architectural art due to its gorgeous and huge pinnacle and artistic decoration. It is considered one of the best temples of South India and was constructed in the **Dravidian style** of art.
