# Ancient Rock-Cut Caves

## Ajanta Caves

The famous **caves of Ajanta** are located in Aurangabad district near Ajanta village. They are significant from the point of view of the art of Vastu (architecture). These caves are divided into two types:

*   Caves based on **wall paintings**
*   Caves based on **sculpture**

Caves number 1, 2, 10, 16, and 17 feature excellent wall paintings and are considered among the highest rank in this art form. The theme of these wall paintings is **Buddhist religion**. Ajanta caves are also categorized into **Chaityas** and **Viharas**. Cave numbers 9, 10, 19, 26, and 29 are Chaityas, while the remaining caves are Viharas.

Ajanta caves were forgotten over time but were rediscovered in 1819 AD by an English captain, **John Smith**. They stand as magnificent examples of initial Buddhist architecture, painting, and sculpture. Despite damage from human interference and the passage of time, Ajanta caves are renowned globally for their extraordinary art. The fine blend of painting, sculpture, and architecture found here brings pride to Indian art.

### Things to Know

*   **Chaityas:** Temple-halls for Buddhist monks, often containing stupas in the interior.
*   **Stupas:** Dome-shaped structures built within the interior of Chaityas.
*   **Viharas:** Buddhist monasteries where monks reside and study.

## Ellora Caves

**Ellora caves** are situated in Aurangabad district in Maharashtra. There are 34 caves in total, divided into three distinct groups based on their religious affiliation:

*   1-12: **Buddhist caves**
*   13-29: **Hindu caves**
*   30-34: **Jain caves**

The Hindu caves were built during the reign of the **Rashtrakuta dynasty**. The famous **Kailash temple** is located in cave number 16. It measures 50 m long, 33 m wide, and 30 m high. This remarkable temple is carved out of a single massive rock and is adorned with beautiful doors, balconies, and a series of pillars. Its beauty is said to be indescribable. The Ellora caves were constructed between 600 AD and 1000 AD, serving as a live display of ancient Indian civilization. Dedicated to Hindu, Jain, and Buddhist religions, the Ellora complex is not only an example of magnificent art creation but also a testament to excellent technology and the patience of Indians.

## Elephanta Caves

These caves are located in the Arabian Sea, 12 km away from Mumbai in Maharashtra. There are 7 caves in total. The Portuguese named the island "Elephanta" after seeing a huge stone monument of an elephant there. Many beautiful figures have been carved, with the magnificent **"Trimurti Temple"** (representing Brahma, Vishnu, and Mahesh) in cave number 1 being the best specimen of sculpture. In 1987 AD, **UNESCO** included "Elephanta Cave" in the list of world heritage sites. Local fishermen refer to it as **'Dhara puri'**.
