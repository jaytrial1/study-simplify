This section focuses on iconic historical structures located in India's capital city, Delhi.

# Delhi's Historical Monuments

## Qutub Minar

**Qutub Minar** is located in Delhi. Its construction began in the 12th century by **Qutubbudin Aibak**, the founder of the Gulam dynasty. After his death, the remaining work was completed by his son-in-law, **Iltutmish**. This skyscraper is 72.5 m tall and is made from round red stone and marble. Its circumference is 13.75 m at the base, tapering to 2.75 m at the top. Verses from the **Quran** are engraved on it. It is recognized as the tallest stone minaret in India.

## Humayun's Tomb

The **tomb of Humayun** at Delhi is considered the best specimen of **Mughal architecture**. It was built after Humayun's death by his wife, **Queen Hammeda Begum**, in the **Iranian style** of art. Red and white stones were skillfully used in its construction.

## Red Fort

The **Red Fort** located in Delhi was built by **Shahajahan** in 1638 AD. It is made of red stone, hence its name. Shahajahan also founded a city named **Shahajahanabad** within the fort, named after himself. The fort complex includes structures like **Diwan-e-Aam**, **Diwan-e-Khas**, and **Rang Mahal**. Diwan-e-Khas is particularly magnificent, decorated with an amalgamation of gold and silver, embedded with precious stones. Other attractions include the **Mirror Palace (Aaina Mahal) of Mumtaz**, **Lahori Gate**, **Meena Bazar**, and the **Mughal Garden**. The exquisite **Peacock Throne**, originally from this fort, was taken by Nadir Shah to Iran. The Red Fort is an outstanding example of Mughal architectural art. Every year, the Indian flag is hoisted on the Red Fort during national festivals.
