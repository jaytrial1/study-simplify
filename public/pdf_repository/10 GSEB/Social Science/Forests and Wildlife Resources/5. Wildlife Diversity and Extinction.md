# Wildlife Diversity and Extinction

India's diverse climate and physiography support a large variety of insects, birds, animals, and vegetation. Out of approximately 15 lakh animal-bird species globally, 81,251 are found in India, placing the country 12th in global biodiversity. India is home to animals from various regions, including African zarakh, European wolf, wild goats, Kashmiri deer, elephants, and gibbons from South-East Asia. Notable Indian species include the black bear, one-horned rhino, deer, various snakes, peacock, bustard, hawk, kingfisher, flamingo, snow leopard (Himalayas), and Red panda (same altitude). India is currently the only country where both tigers and lions exist in their natural habitats. Migratory birds visit areas like Keoladev National Park (Bharatpur, Rajasthan) and Nal Sarovar (Gujarat) during winter. Sea turtles nest along Odisha's coasts. The southern rain forests are home to the Indian python, various snakes, and the King cobra.

## Wild Life on Extinction

Many wild animals worldwide are currently facing extinction. Tigers, once widespread in India, are now extinct from Gujarat's forests. Cheetahs are also extinct from Indian forests, now found only in Africa or in captivity in India. Many bird species previously common are now rarely seen or on the verge of extinction, including vultures, pink-throated ducks, cranes, and owls. The Chilotro bird from North-East Arunachal is also rarely sighted. Freshwater alligators and the Ganga River Dolphin face survival threats. The number of sea turtles on the coasts of Odisha and Gujarat is decreasing. The Water-fowl, once frequent in Gujarat's rivers (Narmada, Tapi, Mahi, Sabarmati), is almost extinct. This situation demands serious attention.

### Reasons for the Destruction of Wildlife

Several factors contribute to the decline of wildlife:

*   **Habitat Destruction**: Human interference in grasslands and forest watershed areas endangers natural animal habitats.
*   **Natural Imbalance**: Destruction of forests disrupts the natural balance, reducing wild animal populations.
*   **Hunting**: Hunting animals for body parts like hair, skin, bones, horns, or nails is a major reason.
*   **Over-exploitation and Displacement**: Human greed leads to over-exploitation of forests for roads, multi-purpose projects, mining, and new settlements, displacing wild animals.
*   **Encroachment**: Forestlands are encroached upon for grass, fuel, or grazing.
*   **Forest Fires**: Forest fires kill many species and negatively impact animal numbers, especially during breeding and hatching seasons.
*   **Human-Wildlife Conflict**: Loss of habitat forces animals into conflict with humans, sometimes resulting in their death.
*   **Poaching for Products**: Hunting to obtain materials for bio-medicine or perfumes also pushes species towards extinction.
