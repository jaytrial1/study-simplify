# Wildlife Protection Schemes

India has implemented various schemes to protect wildlife, initiating projects for species that are endangered or likely to become extinct.

## 1. Tiger Project

Started in 1971, this project aims to save tigers, whose population was estimated at over 40,000 in the early 20th century but faced significant danger due to illegal hunting and deforestation. The project involves protecting their natural habitats and maintaining ecological balance. It is currently implemented in about 44 regions.

## 2. Elephant Project

Started in 1992, the main goal is to protect elephants in their natural habitats and conserve their habitats and migratory corridors. There are currently about 26 elephant protection zones in India. The project has contributed to an increase in elephant numbers and also works for the maintenance of domesticated elephants.

## 3. Rhino Project

This project focuses on protecting India's One-Horned Rhino, primarily found in Assam and in smaller numbers in the Sundarbans. The 'Rhino Vision 2020' strategy targeted increasing the rhino population to 3000.

## 4. Crocodile Project

Started by the Government of India in the 1970s, this project aims to protect freshwater alligator species (Gharials) that were on the verge of extinction.

## 5. Vulture Project

Started in 2004 due to a drastic decrease in their numbers, this project focuses on the vulture, which acts as nature's cleanliness worker by eating dead animals. India has about 9 sub-species of vultures.

## 6. Snow Leopard Project

Initiated in 2000, this project aims to protect the snow leopard, found at altitudes around 3000 meters in the Himalayas. The purpose is to increase local awareness about the snow leopard to encourage its protection.

Other ongoing projects include the Kashmiri Hangool Project, Red Panda Project, Manipur Tamil Project (for a specific deer species), and the Ganga-Dolphin project on the Ganga-Brahmaputra rivers.
