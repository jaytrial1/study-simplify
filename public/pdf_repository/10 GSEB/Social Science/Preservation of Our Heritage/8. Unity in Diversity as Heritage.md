# Unity in Diversity as Heritage

This section explores India's unique characteristic of unity amidst diversity as a significant part of its cultural heritage.

As one of the world's most ancient civilizations, India has a varied and rich cultural heritage. Its diversity is a unique identity. India is a blend of different castes, communities, religions, traditions, cultures, and languages, yet it remains united. India embodies the feeling of **"Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam"** (The whole world is a family), a concept present since Vedic times. The Rig Veda's message, "Let us get good thoughts and vibrations from all four directions," reflects the grandeur of Indian culture. India has promoted religious tolerance and secularism globally.

Swami Vivekananda, at the World Religious Conference in Chicago (USA), stated, "I am proud to say that, I represent the religion that has taught the lessons of tolerance, compassion and universal fraternity to the world."

India is a secular country with a multi-religious landscape influenced by Hinduism, Islam, Buddhism, Jainism, Christianity, and others.

The name "Bharat Varsha" emphasizes India's unity, reflecting a broad outlook. The names of seven sacred rivers are used in prayers. Great figures like sages, Sufi saints, Swami Vivekananda, Dayanand Saraswati, and Mahatma Gandhi consistently stressed peace, coordination, and universal brotherhood.

Unity in diversity is a unique feature of Indian culture. Just as rivers merge into the ocean, India brings together different religions, castes, languages, traditions, creeds, customs, and festivals. The people of India live with a feeling of co-existence and have successfully preserved and enhanced this quality.
