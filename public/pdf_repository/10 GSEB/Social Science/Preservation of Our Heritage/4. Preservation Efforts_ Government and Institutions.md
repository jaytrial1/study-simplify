# Preservation Efforts: Government and Institutions

This section outlines the measures and acts implemented by the Indian government and various organizations to conserve wildlife, natural environments, and archaeological sites.

Efforts for heritage preservation and conservation include the following:

*   The Indian government established the **Indian Board of Wildlife** in 1952 to protect wildlife. It provides resources for wildlife conservation, suggests the development of national parks, bird sanctuaries, and zoological gardens, and works to raise awareness for wildlife protection.
*   The **Wildlife Protection Act**, enacted in 1972, provided strong legal status to national parks, sanctuaries, and bio-reserves.
*   New national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, and bio-reserves have been established.
*   India's laws for heritage preservation have a broad scope.
*   Strict laws have been framed to prevent the destruction of endangered vegetation and wildlife.

Apart from the government, several societies and organizations actively work towards wildlife and environmental protection. The **Bombay Natural History Society**, established in 1883, is the oldest of its kind. Institutions like the Gir Foundation and Natural Club also contribute significantly to environmental preservation.

### Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act

*   As threats to heritage grew, stricter actions and efforts were made over time for preservation, leading to various protective measures.
*   The **Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Sites and Remains Act** was enacted in 1958. This act mandates the preservation of antique artistic works, religious places, historical memorials, and archaeological sites.
*   This act ensures that no person or agency can conduct archaeological excavation without prior government permission, preventing unauthorized digging and preserving many heritage sites.
*   The Government of India has declared some historical places as "National Monuments." The **Department of Archaeology** is responsible for their preservation.
*   However, preserving these sites requires not just laws but also the moral responsibility of every citizen.
*   The Department of Archaeology maintains heritage sites that are damaged or on the verge of destruction.
*   When maintaining monuments, care is taken to preserve them in their original shape, size, and condition.
*   The **Archaeological Survey of India (ASI)** protects over 5000 monuments and sites. For example, Sangameshwar and Papnasham temples in Andhra Pradesh, which were threatened by the Nagarjun Sagar Multipurpose Project, were successfully relocated to Alampur in Mehboobnagar district.
*   The Taj Mahal in Agra, a World Wonder, faced damage from air pollution from the Mathura oil refinery and nearby industries, causing its white marble to dull. The Department of Archaeology took steps to close polluting industries nearby and implemented regular cleaning, restoring the Taj Mahal's brightness.
