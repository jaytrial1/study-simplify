This section focuses on key conventional energy resources like coal, mineral oil, and natural gas, explaining their formation, uses, and distribution in India.

# Conventional Energy Resources

## Coal

Man has used **coal** as an energy resource since ancient times.

**Formation of Coal:**
In earlier times, dense vegetation covered the earth. Due to tectonic movements, this vegetation was buried deep in the interior. The trees containing carbon content and some animals underwent gradual combustion due to internal heat and pressure, transforming the vegetation into coal. The period before about 25 crore years, known as the **carboniferous period**, is significant for coal formation.

Usage of coal increased with the invention of the **steam engine**, facilitating transportation like railways and steamers. With the invention of **electricity**, coal became important for producing **thermal electricity**.

Some **by-products** available from coal include:
*   Coal-tar
*   Ammonia gas
*   Benzoyl
*   Crude oil

Coal is available from **stratified rocks**. Based on carbon content, coal is divided into four types:
1.  **Anthracite coal**
2.  **Bituminous coal**
3.  **Lignite coal**
4.  **Peat coal**

**Indian Coal Reserves:**
Major coal-producing states in India are **Jharkhand**, **Odisha**, **West Bengal**, **Chhattisgarh**, **Madhya Pradesh**, and **Jammu & Kashmir**. Coal is also produced in Rajasthan, Tamil Nadu, Assam, and Gujarat.

Coal fields in Gujarat are located in Kachchh, Bharuch, Mahesana, Bhavnagar, and Surat, where **Lignite coal** is available.

## Mineral Oil

**Mineral oil** is found in stratified rock strata containing limestone, shale, etc. In ancient times, buried animals and vegetation were converted into **hydrocarbons** in a liquid form. Due to tectonic movements, these layers were gradually elevated, some towards the sea floor, others uplifted.

The first oil well in India was dug in Assam in 1866. Mineral oil was obtained from **Makum (Assam)** in 1867. More oil fields were subsequently located across India.

Oil fields in India are divided into five zones:
1.  Oil fields of North-East
2.  Oil fields of Gujarat
3.  Oil fields of Bombay High
4.  Oil fields of Eastern Coast
5.  Oil fields of Rajasthan

**Oil fields of Gujarat:**
After Independence, oil was first struck at **Lunej** of Kheda (now in Anand district) in 1958. Thereafter, oil was found at Ankleshwar, Mahesana, Kalol, Navagam, Kosamba, Sanand, Ahmedabad, Gandhinagar, Vadodara, Bharuch, and Bhavnagar.

**Mineral oil refining:**
In India, refineries are located at Guwahati, Barauni, Koyali, Kochi, Chennai, Mathura, Kolkata, and Haldia. The largest oil refinery in the world is located at **Jamnagar in Gujarat**.

## Natural Gas

**Natural gas** is associated with mineral oil and is separated from it. It is cheaper and considered a **non-pollutant** source of energy. Reserves of natural gas in India are found in the **Khambhat basin**, **Kaveri basin**, and **Jaisalmer (Rajasthan)**. **Ankleshwar of Gujarat** is considered to have the largest natural gas reserve.
