This section explores renewable or inexhaustible energy sources, discussing their importance, development efforts, and specific types like solar, wind, bio-gas, geothermal, and tidal energy.

# Non-Conventional Energy Sources

Resources like coal or mineral oil are available in limited quantity, prompting attempts to conserve them. **Non-conventional energy sources** are considered alternate resources, including **Wind energy**, **Solar energy**, **Bio-gas**, **Tidal energy**, and **Geothermal energy**. These are all **renewable** and sometimes identified as **inexhaustible resources**.

Many countries, including the U.S., Russia, France, Canada, Australia, Netherlands, and Japan, are advancing in this field. In India, the **Commission for Additional Sources of Energy (CASE)** was established in 1981. The **Gujarat Energy Development Agency (GEDA)** is also working in this direction.

## Solar Energy

The **Sun** is the main source of energy on Earth, visible for most days of the year. The biosphere thrives on solar energy. India has progressed well in **solar energy technology**.
*   **Solar cooker** is used for cooking.
*   **Solar heater** is used for heating water.
*   **Solar panels** are used to generate electricity.

**Gujarat** state receives maximum solar energy in India. GEDA has established a solar cold storage near Chhani (Vadodara) with a 10-tonne capacity. Today, solar panels are used for street lights in villages without electricity, for farm irrigation, and for television. A solar energy plant has been installed at **Madhopur near Bhuj in Gujarat** for **desalination** of saline ocean water. The use of solar-powered equipment is increasing in the country.

## Wind Energy

The Sun provides thermal energy, which creates high and low-pressure pockets in the atmosphere, leading to **winds**. In India, **wind energy** is obtained through windmills at sea coasts and in open spaces. India is the **fifth country in the world** in producing energy through wind.

States producing wind energy include **Gujarat**, **Tamil Nadu**, **Maharashtra**, **Andhra Pradesh**, **Odisha**, **Karnataka**, **Madhya Pradesh**, and **Kerala**.

In Gujarat, wind farms operate at **Lambha village near Jamnagar** and on the sea coast near **Mandavi in Kachchh**. Electricity is generated at windmills in Devbhumi Dwarka, Jamnagar, Kachchh, Rajkot, and Porbandar districts.

## Bio-Gas

**Bio-gas** is produced using useless agriculture materials, molasses (sugarcane remains), other vegetation, dung, and human excreta. **Methane gas** is released when these materials decompose, which is an inflammable gas. A precious, virus-free fertilizer is also obtained after the process. Thus, both energy and fertilizer are produced. This is a non-conventional energy resource. Solar energy and bio-gas can change traditional village life in India, making villages cleaner and helping remove domestic energy scarcity.

**Uttar Pradesh** and **Gujarat** hold first and second places respectively in bio-gas production. The largest collective bio-gas plant is installed at **Maithan near Siddhpur in Gujarat**. Other operational plants are located at Rudratal of Daskroi taluka (Ahmedabad) and at Dantiwada (Banaskantha). Individual and collective plants are increasingly used and funded.

## Geothermal Energy

**Geothermal energy** is obtained from the thermal energy coming from the earth's interior to the surface in the form of geysers or hot springs. The heat transforms groundwater into vapour, which exerts upward pressure, releasing energy.

Hot water springs in Gujarat are located at **Lasundra**, **Unai**, **Tuva**, and **Tulsishyam**, indicating possibilities for geothermal energy.

## Tidal Energy

The process of tides and ebbs in oceans, caused by the gravitational force of the sun and moon, can be utilized to generate **tidal energy**. Tidal waters have significant power. A turbine connected to harness this power generates electricity. France started a tidal energy project in 1910. India, with its long coastline, has potential for this energy.

This project has been started in the **Gulf of Kachchh** and the **Gulf of Khambhat**.
