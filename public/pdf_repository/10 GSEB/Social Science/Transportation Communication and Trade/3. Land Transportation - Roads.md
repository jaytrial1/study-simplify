This section details the importance and classification of road networks in India, including different types of roads and expressways.

# Land Transportation - Roads

Roads have been crucial for transportation since ancient times, with established networks existing during the rules of Samrat Ashok and Chandragupta Maurya. Roads are complementary to railways, sea routes, and air routes. Key characteristics of roads include their widespread reach, safety of goods, time-saving, and affordability. They are the primary means to connect people, goods, and areas. India has the third-largest road system globally, after the USA and China.

**Things to know:**
Roadways account for about 83%, railways 9%, airways 6%, and waterways about 2% of the total national transportation system.

**Classification of Indian Roadways:**
1.  National Highway
2.  State Highway
3.  District Roads
4.  Village (Approach) Roads
5.  Border Roads

## National Highways
National Highways are vital for both economic development and safety. The Central Government is responsible for their construction and maintenance. They connect state capitals, major industrial and commercial cities, and important ports. They also link India with neighboring countries like Myanmar, Pakistan, Nepal, Bhutan, and China. National Highway No. 44, connecting Srinagar and Kanyakumari, is the longest in the country. The Golden Quadrilateral Highway System connects the four metro cities: Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Kolkata.

Some National Highways passing through Gujarat include NH 27, 41, 47, 141, 147, among others (numbers changed in 2011). States like Chandigarh, Puducherry, Delhi, and Goa have a higher density of National Highways relative to their population, followed by states like Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh, Meghalaya, and Manipur. States with larger populations but relatively less total length of National Highways include Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, and Gujarat.

## State Highways
State Highways are important for trade and industries. They connect all districts and link them to National Highways. State Governments are responsible for their construction and maintenance.

## District Roads
These roads connect villages and main cities with district headquarters, as well as taluka and district headquarters. While many were originally unmetalled, most are now metalled roads. District Panchayats maintain these roads.

## Village Roads
Gram Panchayats are responsible for the construction and maintenance of Village Roads. These roads, connecting villages to main roads, are often unmetalled and may not be usable during the rainy season. The Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana aims to improve rural transportation by converting these roads into metalled ones on a large scale.

## Border Roads
The Border Road Organization, established in 1960, constructs and maintains roads in border areas for national defense and protection. This includes building and maintaining roads in inaccessible and remote regions and clearing snow.

## Expressways
Expressways are also known as *Drut Gati Marg*. These are four or six-lane highways designed for obstruction-free vehicle movement. Overbridges are built at railway crossings and cross roads. The Ahmedabad-Vadodara Express Highway in Gujarat is an example. A toll tax is usually charged for using these highways. Roads connecting all major ports in the nation have also been constructed.
