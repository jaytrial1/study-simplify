# Trade - Overview and Types

India is a vast country with diverse geographical regions, climates, vegetation, mineral resources, and energy resources. This diversity leads to different crops and industrial outputs in various regions, resulting in two types of trade systems:
1.  Internal Trade
2.  International Trade

## Internal Trade
Internal Trade involves the transportation of surplus commodities from one state to another within the country and vice versa. For example, Punjab exports its surplus wheat to other states but imports salt from coastal states like Gujarat because it lacks a sea coast. This exchange of products between states has fostered internal trade development in India.

## International Trade
International Trade is the system where different countries export and import goods and services to meet their requirements. Maintaining a **trade balance** is crucial in international trade.

*   **Positive Trade Balance:** Occurs when a country exports more than it imports. This increases the country's foreign exchange reserves and strengthens its currency value in the international market.
*   **Negative Trade Balance:** Occurs when imports exceed exports. This can decrease the currency value in international markets.

Following the Liberalization policy of 1991, India's international trade has undergone many changes. In recent years, India's trade balance has mostly been negative. To improve this, the government launched the "Make in India" initiative, encouraging foreign companies to manufacture goods in India for export, aiming to shift towards a positive trade balance.
