# Water Transportation

Water transportation has been practiced in India since ancient times, serving as the primary mode before the development of roads and railways. It is generally cheaper than road and rail transport as there are no construction or maintenance costs for the routes themselves.

There are two types of waterways in India:
1.  Internal Waterways
2.  Oceanic Waterways

## Internal Water Transportation
Internal water transportation is more developed in North-East Indian states like Assam, West Bengal, and Bihar, and is also widely used in South India. Transportation via rivers and canals is important in states like West Bengal, Assam, Tamil Nadu, and Bihar, where permanent waterways are used by steamers and large boats.

The Government has recognized several waterways as National Waterways to support internal water transportation:
*   **National Waterway 1:** Ganga River, Haldia to Allahabad (1620 km), passing through Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand, and West Bengal.
*   **National Waterway 2:** Brahmaputra River, Dhubri to Sadiya (891 km), located in Assam, West Bengal, Arunachal Pradesh, and Meghalaya.
*   **National Waterway 3:** West Coast Canal, Kollam to Kottapuram (250 km), entirely within Kerala.
*   **National Waterway 4:** Godavari-Krishna Canal, Kakinada to Puducherry (1078 km), covering Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Puducherry.
*   **National Waterway 5:** Brahmani River, Goenkhali to Talcher (588 km), located in Odisha and West Bengal.

## Oceanic Waterways
India has a long coastline of about 7516 km, with 13 major and about 200 minor ports. The establishment of the Shipping Corporation of India has significantly developed national and international waterways.

Major ports on the western coast include Kandla, Mumbai, Navasheva, Margao, New Mangalore, and Kochi. Main ports on the eastern coast are Kolkata, Haldia, Paradwip, Vishakhapattanam, Chennai, and Tuticorin.

Gujarat has a coastline of about 1600 km. Kandla is the largest port in Gujarat. Bhavnagar is unique for having an automatic lock-gate system. Porbandar is a free port accessible throughout the year. Other important ports in Gujarat are Veraval, Sikka, Pipavav, Navlakhi, Mundra, Poshitra, Okha, and Hajira. There is a project underway to develop Poshitra port.
