# Land Transportation - Railways

Indian Railways is the country's largest national institution and a primary mode of transportation. It plays a major role in supporting economic sectors like agriculture, industries, trade, and services. It also contributes significantly to national security, peace, management, and establishing and maintaining cultural and geographical unity. India ranks first in Asia and second globally in railway network size.

## Progress of Railways
The first railway line in India was started in 1853, connecting Mumbai and Thane. Initially, there were three types of railway gauges: Broad Gauge, Metre Gauge, and Narrow Gauge. In recent times, there has been a significant effort to convert metre gauge and narrow gauge tracks into broad gauge, which is a major achievement. Having different gauges caused delays and wasted time and money in transferring passengers and goods.

The railway network is denser in states with plain terrain, high population density, industrial development, intensive agriculture, and rich mineral resources. Major cities like Kolkata, Delhi, and Jaipur have Metro Rail systems. A metro rail project is also underway between Ahmedabad and Gandhinagar. Mono rail has become important for connecting Mumbai with its suburbs.

Railways are useful for transporting passengers and goods, as well as for the speedy transfer of food grains and fodder during droughts. For defense purposes, they are vital for moving soldiers and weapons. The Konkan Railways is a notable example, having laid tracks through tunnels in challenging mountainous terrain. Indian Railways is being rapidly modernized, aiming for excellence in time, safety, and facilities. The route between Dibrugarh and Kanyakumari, known as Vivek Express, is the longest railway route in India. In Gujarat, Ahmedabad is the largest railway station, with other important junctions including Mahesana, Viramgam, Rajkot, Vadodara, Surat, and Anand.
