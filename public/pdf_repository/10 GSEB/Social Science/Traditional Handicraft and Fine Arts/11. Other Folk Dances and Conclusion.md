# Other Folk Dances and Conclusion

## Other Dances of Gujarat

*   **Gof Gunthan Dance:** Performed to drumbeats and the rhythm (*taal* and *laya*) of music. Dancers tie a rope to a *mandapa*, pillar, or tree, holding the other end. They dance in a group, holding the rope in one hand and a *dandiya* (stick) in the other. They move in and out of the circle, creating and opening a plait-like formation with the ropes.
*   **Dhamaal Dance of Siddis:** Performed by the Siddi community, originally from Africa, now residing in Jambur village in Gir. They dance rhythmically striking a *Mashira* (coconut shell with lamp inside, covered with cloth), wearing peacock feathers and holding small drums, forming a circle. They dance with high and low pitch sounds of 'Ho-Ho' that echo in the forest, imitating animal and bird voices.
*   **Merayo Dance, Padhar Dance, Koli, and Mer Dances:**
    *   *Merayo Dance* is seen near the stepwell of the Banaskantha region. A *Toran* is woven from tall grass (*reed* and *Jhunjhadi*). This dance is performed to drumbeats and involves sword tricks.
    *   In the Surendranagar region, people of the *Padhaar* community dance with the rhythm of *dandiya* and *manjira*. Their movements create scenes resembling sea waves or a ship moving on waves.
    *   *Kolis* of Saurashtra perform *Koli dance*, wearing a *madhrasio* (colourful cloth for the head), an embroidered and mirrored turban, and a green embroidered belt. They wear a '*Bhet*' on their waist.
    *   Similarly, *Mer dance* of Saurashtra and other dances of the *Bharwad* tribe are well-known.

As discussed throughout the chapter, Indian culture is universal and assimilative. The arrival of various tribes throughout history has led to significant growth and development in handicrafts, home artistry, painting, dancing, and other fine arts. This demonstrates the rich and varied heritage of India, leaving a permanent impression on the world.
