# Indian Fine Arts: Dance

The word '*Nritya*' comes from the Sanskrit word '*Nrit*'. Dance is a medium for expressing beauty and achieving aesthetic joy through rhythm (*Taal*) and harmony (*laya*). Shiva is considered the originator and lord of dance, known as *Nataraj*. He is believed to have brought dance from heaven to earth to teach it to people.

India has a wide variety of traditional classical dances:
*   Bharatnatyam
*   Kuchipudi
*   Kathak
*   Kathakali
*   Odissi
*   Manipuri

### Bharatnatyam

The origin of Bharatnatyam is believed to be the Tanjore district in Tamil Nadu. The primary sources for this dance form are Bharatmuni's '*Natyasashtra*' and Nandikeshwar's '*Abhinav Darpan*'. Prominent figures who have preserved this heritage include Gopi Krishna, Mrinalini Sarabhai, Vaijanti Mala, and Hema Malini.

### Kuchipudi

This dance style was created in the 15th century and is largely based on depicting women's beauty. It incorporates various poses and gestures performed by both male and female dancers. Kuchipudi is famous in Andhra Pradesh. Well-known dancers who have preserved this tradition include Guru Prahlad Sharma, Raja Reddy, and Shobha Naidu.

## Kathakali

This dance style is very famous in Kerala. The word *Kathakali* is derived from dramas based on ancient epics (Ramayana, Mahabharata) and mixed dramas in Sanskrit and Malayalam. The characters wear beautiful, elaborate costumes (*plaited attires*) and artistic crowns. Recognising the character relies on reading their facial expressions. Dancers use facial expressions and hand poses to introduce characters from the 'Trilok'. They appear in front of a curtain illuminated only by an earthen lamp. Notable figures who have gained fame nationally and internationally include Poet Vallathod (founder of Kalamandal Kathakali), Kalamandalam Krishnaprasad, and Shivaraman.

## Kathak Dance

The saying 'Kathan kare so Kathak kahave' relates to Kathak dance, meaning "one who tells a story is called a Kathak". This dance narrates the life events and incidences of Lord Krishna. It is thought to have developed with the '*Shrungar Bhakti*' (devotion expressing love) of the Vaishnav community. In this style, the dancer turns around on one leg and incorporates various references through movement. Women typically wear narrow trousers and a frilled dress. This dance style has been kept alive by artists like Pandit Birju Maharaj, Sitara Devi, and Kumudini Lakhia.

# Manipuri Dance

People of Manipur dance on every occasion and festival. This style is primarily based on the *Rasalilas* and *Bal Lila* (childhood stories) of Shri Krishna. Manipuri dance has two main types: *Lasya* (gentle, graceful) and *Tandav* (vigorous, energetic). Dancers wear a plaited green coloured skirt called '*Kumin*', along with a silk blouse and a belt around the waist. Artists like Guru Amobisingh, Atombosingh, Guru Bipin Sinha, Nayna Zaveri, and Nirmal Mehta have gained worldwide fame.
