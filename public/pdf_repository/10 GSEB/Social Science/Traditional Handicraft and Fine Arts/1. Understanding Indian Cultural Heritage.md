# Understanding Indian Cultural Heritage

India is a country with a rich cultural heritage. Heritage includes both materialistic and genetic aspects. While *genetic inheritance* refers to physical and mental characteristics passed from parents, *material heritage* constitutes properties like house, land, and estates inherited from ancestors.

*Cultural heritage*, however, is what a person obtains or creates using their intellect and skill. It also includes traditions, customs, rituals, and specific lifestyles adopted by ancestors. Furthermore, elements like education, farming, trade, daily life rules, festivals, entertainment, art, handicrafts, beliefs, and skills are part of cultural heritage.

India, as an ancient country, possesses rich traditions, social values, customs, and a strong family system. Preserving this heritage is our duty. Ancient literature mentions 64 arts, including handicrafts, workmanship, art, drawing, music, dramatic art, and dance. The prosperity of India, partly due to valuable ancient currencies, attracted visitors. Yoga education, now accepted globally, is also considered part of cultural heritage, leading to the celebration of June 21st as 'World Yoga Day'.
