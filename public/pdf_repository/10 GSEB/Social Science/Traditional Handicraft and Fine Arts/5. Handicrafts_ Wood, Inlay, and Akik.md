This section covers traditional Indian crafts using wood, the art of inlaying precious stones, and working with Akik stones.

# Handicrafts: Wood, Inlay, and Akik

## Wooden Art

Humans, trees, and forests are closely connected. Initially, wood was used as firewood. Later, it was used for making weapons, constructing buildings, and other construction work. Gradually, wood found different uses:
*   Making idols
*   Toys for children
*   Wooden pillars
*   Windows and doors
*   Small huts on terraces
*   Thrones
*   Chairs
*   Grills

This led to the development of *wood carving*. Wooden furniture like swings and toys from Idar and Sankheda in Gujarat are well-known.

## Inlay Work

*Inlay work* is considered a very ancient art of India. Indian kings, emperors, rulers, and royal people wore ornaments like necklaces, bracelets, crowns, rings, nose rings, and *Damni* (Tiko) studded with precious gems such as diamonds, pearls, and rubies. Artisans were skilled in inlay art. The Bikaner area of Rajasthan is especially popular for inlay ornaments.

## Akik Work

*Akik* is a type of precious stone, often found in river valleys. It is mainly chalcedony mixed with silica, appearing as blue or white stone. *Carnelian*, a semi-transparent stone with a beautiful red colour, is a main type. In Gujarat, various sizes of precious stones are available at Ranpur and Surat. These stones are sent to Khambhat to be shaped into beautiful ornaments and designs. Beads and garlands are prepared from these stones.
