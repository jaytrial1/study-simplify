# Folk Dances of Gujarat

Gujarat has varied dance forms belonging to different communities, performed according to their traditions and customs. These include tribal and folk dances, Garba, Rasa, and many others. Such dances are often seen during festivals, marriages, and fairs.

## Tribal Dances

Tribal dances in Gujarat are performed during festivals, marriages, fairs, and to please deities. Most dances are performed by forming a circle, accompanied by traditional musical instruments like drum, manjira, thali, and dholak, with songs in regional languages.

In the dance form called *Chado*, dancers imitate birds like peacocks, squirrels, and sparrows. Dances like 'Mali No Chaalo' and 'Thakryo Chaalo' are seen in the Dang region. The '*Tippani Dance*' is performed by laborers (*Shramhari*) from Bhil and Koli communities. Dancers perform in a group using thick sticks joined with small sticks struck to the ground.

## Garba

The word *Garba* is derived from 'Garbh deep' (a lamp in a pot). An earthen lamp is placed in a holed earthen pot, which dancers balance on their heads while moving in a circle. Garbas are performed during the Navratri festival (Hindu month of Aso Sud Ekam to Navmi, sometimes up to Dasam or Sharad Poonam) throughout Gujarat. Gujarati people perform and sing Garba to worship and pray to Adhya Shakti (Goddess of power), Mother Jagdamba. It is generally performed in an open ground, encircling a '*Mandvi*' (a small structure) kept in the center, accompanied by handclaps and drum beats.

Gujarati poetry known as *Garba* and *Garbi* are chiefly associated with Krishna Bhakti (devotion to Lord Krishna). Gujarati poet Dayaram wrote poems that inspired ladies to sing colourful and emotional *garbi* expressing love for Lord Krishna.

## Rasa

*Rasa* is a dance performed in a circle along with singing. It is believed that Lord Krishna showed 'Rasa Lila' to the devotee Narsinh Mehta. Rasa is mostly performed in Gujarat during Navratri and Janmashtami festivals. *Dandiya Rasa*, using sticks, is a common type. Some people perform Rasa by placing an earthen pot on their heads. Due to the influence of the Vaishnav sect, Rasa has become more popular. Women wear traditional embroidered chaniya-choli, and men wear embroidered kediya-dhoti while performing Rasa.
