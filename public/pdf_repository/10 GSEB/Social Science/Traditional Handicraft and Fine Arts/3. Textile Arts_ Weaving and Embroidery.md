# Textile Arts: Weaving and Embroidery

## Art of Weaving

The process of making long threads from small pieces of cotton and joining them is called *spinning*. Mahatma Gandhi emphasized spinning as a cottage industry, linking it to the Swadeshi movement for self-reliance during the freedom struggle, which made history.

### Hand Weaving

India has been known for its textile expertise since ancient times. A famous example is the fine muslin of Dhaka, where an entire piece of cloth of a specific size could pass through a ring or fit into a matchbox.

Hand-woven items like carpets (*Galicha*) from various regions, knitted work on clothing, and other artistry have held prominent places. *Patola* of Patan, Kanjivaram and Banarasi sarees, and *Bandhani* of Rajasthan are uniquely Indian identities.

Gujarat flourished in weaving during the Solanki era. Many artisans settled in Patan during the reign of Siddharaj Jaysingh, making Patolas world-famous due to their skill. This art is over 850 years old, and only a few artisans possess the necessary complex skills. Silk fabric (*Bevad Ikt*) produced in Patan is called Patola. *Ikt* refers to the weaving technique. Patola sarees are designed to be worn on either side, are durable, and their colours remain intact. A Gujarati saying reflects this: 'પડ પટોળે ભાત, ફાટે પણ ફીટે નહિ' - The design woven into a Patola will never fade, even if the cloth tears.

## Art of Embroidery

Evidence of embroidery has been found on clothing of idols and statues from the Harappa and Mohenjo-Daro excavations. The art of weaving designs onto clothing for men, women, and children has been famous since ancient times. During the Indus civilization, embroidery was seen on clothes in Sindh, Saurashtra, and Kutch regions. Kashmiri (*kasab*) work from Kashmir is also well-known.

Besides embroidery, *Bandhani* from Jamnagar, Jetpur, Bhuj, and Mandvi in Gujarat is very popular. Traditional Bandhani, with its luster and beautiful designs like elephants, puppets, dice, and pitchers, remains popular. Women in the Kutch and Saurashtra regions have significantly contributed to this cottage industry. Traditional embroidery art is seen on items like:
*   Sakh torans
*   Chakda
*   Pillow covers
*   Bed sheets
*   Todaliya
*   Clothing like kediya

Embroidery is also done on quilts and shawls. The embroidery work of the 'Jat' community in the Banni area of Kutch is particularly unique.
