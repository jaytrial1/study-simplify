This section introduces the context of India's economic development strategy after independence and the reasons for implementing the reforms of 1991.

# India's Economic Path and the 1991 Reforms

After gaining independence in 1947, the Indian government adopted a path of planned economic development, implementing successive five-year plans. Various policies, including monetary, fiscal, and industrial policies, were announced to achieve rapid economic growth. However, despite completing many plans, the desired level of economic development was not fully achieved. Investigating the causes of these failures, the government decided to introduce new forms of economic policies to correct past errors.

In this context, the industrial policy of 1991 introduced economic reforms aimed at strengthening development. These reforms are broadly known as:
1.  Liberalization
2.  Privatization
3.  Globalization
