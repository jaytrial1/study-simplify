This section explains the meaning of economic liberalization, details the reforms implemented, and discusses its advantages and disadvantages.

# Understanding Economic Liberalization

**Economic Liberalization** refers to the process where the government gradually reduces the restrictions and controls placed on the private sector to encourage economic development.

Key economic reforms related to liberalization implemented in the initial period included:
*   Eliminating the requirement for government permission for industries, except for 18 reserved sectors.
*   Opening areas previously reserved for the public sector, such as railways, atomic energy, and defence, to private participation.
*   Canceling the mandatory registration of industries.
*   Removing the requirement for government permission to set up industries that do not cause pollution and are not hazardous to the environment.

## Benefits of Liberalization

*   The private sector gained opportunities for unrestricted development, leading to increased production.
*   Adopting the liberalization policy boosted global trade, resulting in its expansion.
*   Increased foreign trade led to a rise in foreign exchange reserves.
*   Internal infrastructural facilities improved as a result of liberalization.

## Drawbacks of Liberalization

*   Despite reduced restrictions, the issue of monopoly power could not be effectively controlled.
*   Focus was primarily on the industrial sector, leaving the agricultural sector relatively backward in development.
*   Income disparity increased.
*   An increase in imports coupled with a decrease in exports led to a rise in foreign debt.
