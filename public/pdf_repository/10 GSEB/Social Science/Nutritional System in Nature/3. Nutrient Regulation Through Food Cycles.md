This section details how nature regulates nutrients through various cycles, ensuring elements return to their origin, focusing on the carbon, water, and energy cycles.

# Nutrient Regulation Through Food Cycles

A fundamental law of natural arrangement is that elements originating from a source must eventually return there. This natural law dictates that any element returns to its original state.

Trees absorb **carbon dioxide** from the air, which returns to the air through processes like **combustion** and **decomposition** of tree remains. Similarly, water evaporates from the ocean, condenses into clouds, and returns to the ocean as rain.

## Carbon Cycle

Green leaves of plants take **carbon** from the air via **photosynthesis**, storing it in the plant's food. When the plant's life cycle ends, **carbon dioxide** is released back into the air by bacterial decomposition. If the plant is burnt, carbon dioxide enters the air as smoke, maintaining the carbon balance. This natural system ensures matter obtained from nature returns to it.

## Water Cycle

Approximately 71% of the Earth is covered with water. Solar heat increases the ocean surface temperature, causing **evaporation** and cloud formation. These clouds bring rain to land. Most rainwater flows through rivers back to the ocean or is stored as **underground water**, which eventually merges with the sea through springs. This natural system makes water available for living organisms and ensures its return to the ocean.

## Energy Cycle

The **Sun** is the primary source of energy. Matter is continuously converted into energy by the sun, and some of this energy reaches the Earth as sunlight.

A **herbivore** obtains energy from food. Green leaves are responsible for storing this solar energy through photosynthesis. The energy cycle is completed through the decomposition or burning of plant remains after their life ends.
