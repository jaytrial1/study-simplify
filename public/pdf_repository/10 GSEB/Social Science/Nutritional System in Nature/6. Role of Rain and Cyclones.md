This section discusses the vital importance of rain for life, particularly in India's monsoon system, and the role of cyclones in rainfall, contrasting their effects on different farming methods.

# Role of Rain and Cyclones

Without rain, forests might not exist, and water for drinking and irrigation would be unavailable. Rain is crucial for the sustenance of all living beings, including humans.

Most rainfall in India occurs during the monsoon season. **Cyclones** are a key atmospheric phenomenon in monsoonal rainfall. After March 21, as temperatures rise in the Northern Hemisphere due to intense solar heat, evaporation from the Indian Ocean accelerates. The monsoon enters the Indian Ocean, and the air over the ocean heats intensely, creating a **low-pressure area**. Air circulates around this low pressure, forming a cyclone.

Cyclones significantly impact Indian agriculture. Crops in chemical farming are more damaged during cyclones, while crops in natural agriculture are less affected because organic farming strengthens plants.

## Tree Water Release

A 40-year-old tree releases about 200 liters of water vapor into the air daily. To increase moisture for cloud formation and subsequent rainfall, planting more trees is essential. This allows empty clouds to absorb moisture, leading to rain when saturated.
