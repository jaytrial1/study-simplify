This section details the characteristics and significant contributions of local earthworms to building healthy soil and supporting plant growth.

# Activity of Local Earthworms

## Characteristics of Local Earthworms

*   Local earthworms consume soil.
*   If food is scarce, they remain dormant in the soil rather than moving elsewhere.
*   They continue their underground activities even in extreme temperatures, like high heat in Rajasthan deserts or low temperatures on the Himalayas.

## Contribution of Local Earthworms in Building Healthy Soil

Local earthworms continuously create billions of holes in the soil throughout their 15-year lifespan. They burrow deep, dig holes, and emerge from others, leaving behind mineral-rich waste called **Hagaar** on the surface. This process makes the soil **porous**.

As they move, they secrete enzymes that line the holes, making them resistant to external pressure. This porosity allows rainwater to easily seep into the ground, raising the groundwater level and preventing **soil erosion**.

Earthworms consume soil, sand, soft rock material, lime, etc., destroying disease-causing pathogens in their gut and strengthening beneficial organisms before releasing them back into the soil through excreta.

Earthworm excreta contains significantly more nutrients than the surrounding soil, playing a vital role in vegetation growth. Additionally, the excreta contains antibodies that boost the immunity of plant roots and beneficial microorganisms nearby, creating strong, suitable soil. Plants and trees growing in such soil are healthy, leading to healthier animals, birds, and humans who consume them.
