# Major Food Grains of India

Grains are cultivated in about 75% of the total sown area and account for about 50% of the total production.

## Paddy (Rice)

**Paddy** is India's most important crop. A large majority of the world population and about half of India's population consume rice. India ranks second globally in paddy production after China. Paddy is sown over one-fourth of the total sown area. It is a crop of the **torrid zone**, requiring a hot and humid climate, a minimum temperature of 20°C, fertile alluvial soil, and more than 100 cm of rainfall. In regions with less rainfall like Punjab, Haryana, and Uttar Pradesh, it is grown with irrigation. Paddy cultivation requires more human labour.

Major paddy-producing states include West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Bihar, and Odisha. The crop is taken two or three times in West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Odisha, and Tamil Nadu. In Gujarat, paddy is grown in Surat, Tapi, Panchmahal, Ahmedabad, Kheda, Anand, and Valsad districts. While paddy requires more water, modern techniques like sprinklers are used to irrigate fields with less water instead of keeping them fully submerged.

## Wheat

**Wheat** is the second most important crop in India after paddy, grown over one-third of the arable land. It is the staple food of North-West India. Wheat is a **rabi crop** of the temperate zone. It requires black or fertile loamy soil and more than 75 cm of annual rainfall. Wheat can be grown in regions with less rainfall using irrigation but not in areas receiving more than 100 cm rainfall. Less labour is needed due to mechanization.

After the **Green Revolution**, wheat production almost doubled. Cultivation is concentrated in Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh, which contribute about two-thirds of the national production. These states have high per-hectare production due to irrigation facilities, especially canal water in Punjab, which is why Punjab is called the '**Wheat Bowl**' of India. Wheat is also produced in Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, West Bengal, etc. '**Bhaliya wheat**' is produced in the Bhal region of Gujarat. Other producing districts in Gujarat include Mahesana, Rajkot, Junagadh, and Kheda.

Wheat is considered the best among all food grains for daily meals, used to prepare various items like rotli, bhakhri, bread, etc. It is highly nutritious and is called the **king of grains**.

## Coarse Grains

**Jowar**, **bajra**, **maize**, and **barley** are major coarse grains grown in India.

### Jowar

Jowar is the third most produced grain after paddy and wheat, grown widely in the dry and less rainy regions of the southern peninsula. It is both a **kharif** and **rabi** crop. Favourable conditions include temperatures between 25°C and 30°C, 125 cm of rainfall, and black and loamy soil. It is produced in Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, and Gujarat. In Gujarat, maximum jowar farming is in Surat and Tapi districts.

### Millet (Bajra)

Millet is considered the grain of labourers, grown in regions with temperatures between 25°C and 30°C, rainfall between 40-50 cm, and light sandy soil. Major states are Rajasthan, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, and Maharashtra. Banaskantha district leads in millet production in Gujarat.

### Maize

This is a **kharif crop**, the third most grown crop globally after paddy and wheat. Maize is grown more in hilly areas and is a staple food there. It contains starch, oil, protein, and bio-fuel components, making it useful in industrial production. Sloppy, black, hard, rocky soil with gentle water flow is suitable. Favourable factors include 50-100 cm rainfall and 21°C to 27°C temperature. Its use as animal fodder, popcorn, and edible oil is increasing. Major states are Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madhya Pradesh, Punjab, Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh. In Gujarat, maize is grown more in Panchmahal, Dahod, Sabarkantha, and Aravalli districts.

## Pulses

**Pulses** are the main source of protein for vegetarians. **Tuver**, **mung**, **gram**, **peas**, **beans**, **math**, **udad**, etc., are pulses. Tuver, udad, mung, and math are **kharif crops**, while gram, peas, and lentil (masur) are **rabi crops**. Pulses are grown in all states except where rainfall is excessive. Major producing states include Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Odisha, Bihar, and Andhra Pradesh. In Gujarat, tuver is sown more in Vadodara district, mung and math in Kachchh district, and udad in Patan district. Pulses reinstate nitrogen in the soil, so they are sown as intercrops or after main food crops.

### Things to know: Nagli (Ragi)

**Nagli** (ragi) holds a unique place among grass food-crops in Gujarat, being a staple food for adivasi people in hilly regions. It has the maximum per hectare production among grass food-crops in Gujarat and India. Nagli is known as **Finger millet** or **African millet** in English and **Bavta** in Gujarati. It is extremely nutritious, rich in protein, minerals, and vitamins. Due to high fibre content, it benefits patients with diabetes and cardiac issues. It has more calcium and iron than other crops, used for combating malnutrition and in baby food. Adivasi farmers eat rotla made from nagli flour. Biscuits, chocolates, toasts, nankhatai, wafer, papdi, etc., are also made from its flour.
