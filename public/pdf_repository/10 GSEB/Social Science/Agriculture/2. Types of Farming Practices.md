This section classifies different types of farming methods practised in India based on factors like irrigation, economic return, and specific techniques.

# Types of Farming Practices

The socio-economic condition of the people of India, administrative policy, and the entire Indian economy are associated with agriculture. Farming types are classified according to irrigation methods, farm outputs, and economic return.

1.  **Subsistence Farming**: Even after Independence and the implementation of many agricultural development projects, the economic condition of the Indian farmer remains weak. Costly seeds, fertilizers, and insecticides are not affordable for smaller farm holdings. The farm production is just enough for the farmer's family and is consumed for family maintenance. This is called **subsistence farming**. Thus, Indian agriculture is often considered an activity for life maintenance.
2.  **Dry Farming**: Where rainfall is inadequate and irrigation facilities are limited, farming depends only on rain. Only one crop is taken using the humidity accumulated in the soil. This is known as **dry farming**. Crops like **jowar**, **millet**, and **pulses** which require less water are grown. In Gujarat, **wheat** and **gram** are grown in humid soils after the monsoon.
3.  **Wet Farming**: This is carried out in regions with heavy rain and adequate irrigation facilities. If there is no rainfall or if it is insufficient, more than one crop is taken with the help of irrigation. Crops like **paddy**, **sugarcane**, **cotton**, **wheat**, and **vegetables** are grown here.
4.  **Shifting (Jhoom) Agriculture**: Here, forests are burnt and cleared, and farming is carried out for two or three years. When soil fertility reduces, that area is abandoned, and the same method is applied elsewhere. This is called **Jhoom cultivation**. Cereals or vegetables are grown, but production is less in this type of agriculture.
5.  **Plantation Agriculture**: This is a special type of agriculture involving rearing crops like **rubber**, **tea**, **coffee**, **cocoa**, **coconut**, **apple**, **mango**, **oranges**, **grapes**, **amla** (myrobalan), **lemon**, **kharek** (dried date) with great care. It requires more capital investment, skill, technical knowledge, machines, fertilizers, protection, acquisition, and transportation facilities.
6.  **Intensive Farming**: This is a mechanized way of farming utilizing increased irrigation, chemical fertilizers, insecticides, and mechanical equipment. **Cash crops** are grown more in this type. Per hectare production is significantly increased, and the area sown under these crops has expanded. As economic returns are prioritized, it is also known as **Commercial Farming**.
