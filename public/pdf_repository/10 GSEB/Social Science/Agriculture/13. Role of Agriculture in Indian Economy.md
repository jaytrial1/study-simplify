# Role of Agriculture in Indian Economy

Agriculture is the main occupation in India. The pressure on agriculture has reduced somewhat since Independence, but it still has an important contribution to the Indian economy.

*   It provides employment to about half of the country's population.
*   Agriculture contributes about 17% to the total **Gross Domestic Product (GDP)**.
*   Major agricultural products like **paddy**, **wheat**, **oil seeds**, **cotton**, **jute**, **sugarcane**, **tobacco**, and **potatoes** are exported, earning significant foreign exchange.
*   India ranks second globally in agrarian production.
*   Industries like cotton textile, sugar, paper, and oil, as well as food processing industries, rely on agricultural products for raw material.
*   Agriculture provides food for India's population.

India's contribution to the world agricultural market is inconsistent due to irregular rain and inadequate irrigation.

Food security is vital for any country. If food grain demand increases and imports are needed, a country's political independence might be at risk. India achieved self-reliance in grain production due to the Green Revolution. Along with increased production, the country's population has also grown significantly, increasing the demand for food grains. In 1951, India's population was 36.1 crore, now over 125 crore. However, food grain production has increased over the last 5 years. From 51 crore tonnes in 1950-51, it reached a record 265.04 crore tonnes in 2013-14.

With current food grain stocks, the minimum requirement can be fulfilled. Maintaining and increasing buffer stock is necessary to prevent grain scarcity during droughts or inadequate production. Wastage can be prevented by preserving grains in warehouses. Distributing buffer stock free of charge could satisfy the hunger of thousands of families. Stopping food grain wastage is crucial. Strengthening food stocking and conservation arrangements is necessary. The government's provision to supply food grains to the poor through the **Food Security Act** is a good step.
