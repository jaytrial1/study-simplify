This section explains the concept and impact of the Green Revolution in India, highlighting its role in increasing food grain production and achieving self-sufficiency.

# The Green Revolution

The **Green Revolution** took place in India in the 1960s. Before this, there was an acute shortage of food grains, forcing India, an agrarian country, to import food grains. The country struggled against this dependency.

The extraordinary increase in agricultural production due to improved seeds, increased use of chemical fertilizers, intense efforts by farmers, widespread electricity distribution, and improved irrigation facilities is known as the **Green Revolution**.

*   The main objective was to increase agricultural production. Farmers were encouraged to use more chemical fertilizers and insecticides, which helped achieve this target.
*   There was record production of **wheat** and **paddy** due to the Green Revolution.
*   Where there was scarcity, there is now sufficient storage of food grains.
*   The adverse effects of drought, once a regular hazard, are less severe after the Green Revolution due to buffer stocks of food grains, allowing convenient handling of scarcity conditions.
*   Self-sufficiency in food grains is a landmark historic achievement of the Green Revolution.

Today, cash crop production has increased, while pulse and other cereal production has decreased, partly due to repeated cropping. To match the progress of other countries using better agricultural technology, India needs a **second Green Revolution**.
