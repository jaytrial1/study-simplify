This section examines how the policy of globalization has affected Indian agriculture, including market access, competition, and the use of new technologies like genetically modified seeds.

# Impact of Globalization on Indian Agriculture

The policy of **globalization** was implemented to allow Indian farmers to sell products in world markets for profit. Globalization brought many changes to agriculture. Exporting and importing crop productions became simpler. Indian products like cotton, chillies, and til are now in Chinese markets, and various global fruits are available in India. Costly "**genetically modified**" **BT seeds** from multinational companies are available, making farming more expensive. Production of cotton and maize has increased.

With smoother import, domestic products face tough competition. Some products have gained global markets, necessitating **Patent** registration for qualitative products in the world market.

To face global competition, India must adopt new technology and focus on quality. Planned steps are needed to increase economic prosperity and solidarity. With increasing population and future demand for agricultural products, a strategy for a **second green revolution** is necessary to maintain economic progress.
