This section introduces the historical and current importance of agriculture in the Indian economy, highlighting its contribution to employment, national product, and exports, while also discussing the challenges faced by Indian farmers.

# Introduction to Indian Agriculture

Since ancient times, agriculture has remained a prime economic activity for most people in India. Even today, the main support for the Indian economy is agriculture, making it an important resource. About 60% of the labour force is engaged in agriculture. Besides supplying food, agriculture provides raw material for many industries. Agriculture occupies about 22% of the national product and contributes about 18% to exports, earning significant foreign exchange. The Indian economy is mainly dependent on agriculture, thus India is an **agrarian country**.

In most areas of India, two or more crops can be grown annually due to factors like fertile plains, favourable climate for round-the-year cultivation, irrigation, and skilled farmers. However, progress in agriculture has not been sufficient. Indian farmers are generally poor and uneducated. Agricultural production is less compared to global standards due to reasons such as inadequate irrigation facilities, irregular rainfall, high population, larger families, smaller farms, reluctance towards experimentation, less usage of chemical fertilisers and modern machinery, lack of scientific approach, educated people moving away from agriculture, and the perceived lower status of agriculture in society.
