# Ancient Universities: Nalanda

This section describes the ancient Nalanda University, its location, significance, and features.

Ancient **Nalanda University** was located at Badgaon village in Patna district, Bihar. It gave importance to Buddhist and Jain traditions. It was a pilgrimage site for Jains as Mahavir Swami spent fourteen 'chaturmas' here. Kumar Gupta built a monastery here in the fifth century, increasing its fame. It housed thousands of priceless manuscripts and was a pious place of Indian culture. Students from around the world came to study here. The great traveler **Heun Tsang** visited in the 7th century.

A student graduating from Nalanda was considered an ideal student of India. From the fifth to eleventh century, education at Nalanda was considered the best in India, and it had some of the world's best libraries. Students from India and abroad came to study and research in its library, as well as in the library of Takshashila University. Heun Tsang took 657 books from here to China.

In the 7th century, the university had seven large halls and 300 rooms for lectures. Special monasteries were built for student dwelling. Many villages were donated for the university's sustenance, providing free food and clothing from the earnings. Its library area was known as "**Dharmgunj**". From the 5th to 11th century, Nalanda was a reputed and esteemed center of education.
