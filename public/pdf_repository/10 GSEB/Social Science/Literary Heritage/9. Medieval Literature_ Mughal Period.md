# Medieval Literature: Mughal Period

This section details the literary developments during the Mughal era, including royal autobiographies, historical works, translations, and poetry.

The Mughal period saw significant development in literature. **Babar**, the first Mughal ruler, was a pioneer of Turkish poetry and author of the valuable autobiography **'Tuzuk-e-Babar'** in Turkish, later translated into Persian as **'Babarnama'**. **Gulbadan Begum**, Humayun's sister, wrote **'Humayun-nama'**. **Jahangir** wrote his autobiography, the **'Tuzuk-e-Jahangiri'**. **Aurangzeb** was a prolific writer, and the last Mughal emperor, **Bahadur Shah Zafar**, was a notable Urdu poet.

**Hindi literature** made significant progress during Akbar's reign. **Tulsidas** and **Surdas** wrote during this time. **Keshavdas** wrote on love and separation. **Rahim's** 'dohas' remain popular.

Many noteworthy books were written. **Abdul Fazl** wrote the **'Ain-e-Akbari'** and the **'Akbarnama'**. The 'Ain-e-Akbari' is particularly detailed, covering Indian customs, manners, religion, philosophy, economic condition, and almost every aspect of life, considered unparalleled as a historical work. Abdul Fazl's brother **Faizi** was a great Persian poet who translated many Sanskrit works into Persian. Akbar established a department specifically for translating Sanskrit works like the *Mahabharata*, *Ramayana*, *Atharvaveda*, *Bhagwad Gita*, and *Panchtantra*.
