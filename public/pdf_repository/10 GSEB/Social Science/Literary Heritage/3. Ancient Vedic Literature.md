# Ancient Vedic Literature

This section details the Vedas and related ancient literature like Upanishads, Brahmanical texts, Aranyakas, and Vedangas.

The **Vedas** mean knowledge, and there are four of them:
1.  **Rigveda**
2.  **Samveda**
3.  **Yajurveda**
4.  **Atharvaveda**

## Rigveda

The Rigveda is the most ancient work of Indian literature, consisting of 1028 verses divided into 10 divisions. Most verses are prayers to God, used during yagnas. Verses worshipping Usha (the Goddess of dawn) are noted as fascinating. This work describes the political, social, and religious matters of the Aryans residing in the Saptsindhu region.

## Samveda

Composed after the Rigveda, the Samveda focuses on the process of reciting verses. It is known as the **Gangotri of music**.

## Yajurveda

Called the veda of Yagnas, it is composed in both prose and verse forms. It describes the hymns recited during Yagyas, religious practices, and rituals.

## Atharvaveda

The Atharvaveda describes various types of rituals and sanskaras.

## Upanishads

Upanishadic literature provides detailed descriptions and analyses of the beginning of the universe, mysteries of life and death, and the materialistic and spiritual world. 'Brihdaranya' and 'Chhandogya' are among the earliest Upanishads and are in dialogue form. There are 108 Upanishads in total, as mentioned in Muktiko Upanishad.

## Brahmanical Literature

Many Brahmanical epics were composed to help understand the meaning of Vedic literature. Critical appreciations based on Vedas, composed in poetic form, are included in this category. Vedic literature and directive principles regarding behavior are explained broadly.

## Aranyakas

Aryans spent the last phase of their lives in forests (aranyakas), establishing ashrams there. They composed philosophical literature based on their deep thinking, known as 'Aranyakas'.

## Vedangas

The Vedangas literature consists of six branches, including religious practices and rituals, grammar, astrology, and astronomy.
