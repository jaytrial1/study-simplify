# Language Development and Sanskrit

This section discusses the historical emergence of various languages in India and focuses on the significance of Sanskrit.

Historically, a variety of languages emerged over centuries, influencing one another and leading to the development of literature. This interaction resulted in the creation and flourishing of new languages and literature. **Sanskrit** is a prime example. While less used currently, it is still used in religious ceremonies and worshipping rituals.

Language generally provides opportunities for humans for better expression and understanding. India's most ancient script belongs to the Harappan civilization, but it has not been deciphered, limiting information about their languages.

Maharshi Panini was a great Sanskrit grammarian. His 'Ashtadhyayi', from the 4th century, is a noteworthy book on Sanskrit grammar. Sanskrit is considered the language of 'Aryans', sages, or scholars. It is internationally accepted as being suitable for computer use. Sanskrit was the chief language for knowledge, science, religion, and philosophy.
