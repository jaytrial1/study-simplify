# Medieval Literature: Delhi Sultanate and Regional Growth

This section examines the literature developed under the Delhi Sultanate, focusing on Persian influence, the rise of new languages, and the role of the Bhakti movement.

The development of Indian languages and composition was promoted during the Delhi Sultanate. Two forms of Delhi language, **Khadi Boli** and **Brij Bhasha**, began to be used for writing literature, especially devotional songs. Many heroic poems and stories were composed in **Rajasthani** language, which is similar to Hindi and Gujarati. Popular heroic works included 'Alha', 'Udal', and 'Visaldev Raso'. **'Chandrayan'** by Mulla Daood is noted as the oldest book in **Awadhi** language. However, critical appreciation of ancient books was still often written in Sanskrit.

**Persian** was the court language of the Delhi Sultanate, leading to the inclusion of many Persian words in Indian languages. Several historians emerged during this period, providing historical descriptions through Turkish writers in Persian. **Ziauddin Barani** wrote the *Tarikh-e-Firoz Shahi*, detailing the reigns of the Khilji and Tughluq kings, and *Fatwa-e-Jahangiri*, a book on political theory.

The most outstanding literary figure was **Amir Khusro**, a poet, historian, mystic saint, and music composer, and a disciple of Nizamuddin Auliya. His works include:
*   *Ashiqua*
*   *Nah Sipiher*
*   *Kiratul sadayan*
*   *Khazain-ul-Futuh*
*   Several other poetry works

Amir Khusro took pride in being Indian, praising India as the 'Earthly Paradise'. He believed aspects of Hinduism resembled Islam. He considered **'Hindawli'** (the Hindi spoken around Delhi) his mother tongue and composed many verses in it, as well as bilingual quatrains and verses in Hindi and Persian. This tradition continued for centuries.

Regional kings greatly encouraged regional languages and literature. **Bhakti saints** preached in the languages of the people, many being great poets like **Kabir**. **Bhojpuri** and **Awadhi** were major dialects. Kabir's works, chiefly composed in 'Sadhukhadi', include Dohas that became folklore. **Malik Muhammad Jayasi** wrote **'Padmawat'** in Awadhi. **Tulsidas's** well-known **'Ramcharit Manas'** was also written in Awadhi.

Literature in other languages also developed. In **Bengali**, the *Ramayana* by **Kritta Vasa** and hundreds of lyrics by **Chandidas** were written under royal patronage. With saint Chaitanya, the tradition of writing devotional songs began. **Narsinh Mehta** wrote devotional songs in Gujarati, and **Namdev** and **Saint Eknath** in Marathi. In Kashmir, under Zainulabidin, Sanskrit works like the *Mahabharata* and *Rajtarangini* were translated into Persian.

In the Vijaynagar reign, Sanskrit literature continued to develop, but this was an important period for **Telugu** literature. **Raja Krishnadevarai**, the Vijaynagar king, was a Telugu and Sanskrit writer, composing the **'Amukta Malayd'**.
