# Medieval Literature: Transition and Early Developments

This section covers the beginning of the Medieval age in North India, the continued use of Sanskrit, and the emergence of early forms of modern Indian languages.

At the beginning of the Medieval age in North India, Sanskrit remained the language of literature, and significant works were composed in it. Two major works from Kashmir during this period are:
*   **Somdeva's 'Kathasaritsagar'**
*   **Kalhana's 'Rajtarangini'** (considered the first historical book of India)

Another famous Sanskrit work from this time is **Jaydeva's 'Geetgovind'**, one of the finest poems in Sanskrit literature.

This was also the period when **Apabhramsa** languages began developing into modern Indian languages. One of the earliest works in an early form of Hindi was **'Prithviraj Raso'** by Chandbardai, which marks the beginning of Hindi literature and deals with the heroic deeds of Prithviraj Chauhan.

Sanskrit literature also made great strides in South India during this time. For a period, Jainism influenced Kannada literature. Notable poets known as the **trio of early Kannada literature** include:
*   **Pampa**: composed *Adipuran* and *Vikramarjun vijayant*
*   **Ponna**: wrote *Shantipuran* (describing the life of the 16th Jain tirthankar)
*   **Ranna**: composed *Ajitnathpuran* and *Gada Yudh*

Poet **Kamban** composed the **'Ramayana'** in Tamil language, alongside other famous Tamil literary works.
