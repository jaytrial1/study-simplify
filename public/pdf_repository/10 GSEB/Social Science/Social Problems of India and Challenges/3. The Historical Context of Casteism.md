This section delves into the historical roots of casteism in India, explaining its original basis in occupation and how it evolved to impact social and economic status, including the isolation of certain communities.

# The Historical Context of Casteism

Castes have been integral to India's social system since its inception, forming the basis of its social structure. The early concept of caste differed from today's. Initially, task-based communities formed the basis of work division to meet societal needs. The early hypothesis of the **varna system** (Brahman, Kshatriya, Vaysya, Shudra) was based on four occupations. Dwelling arrangements and occupations were linked to caste. Since occupation was the source of income and some castes earned less, they remained economically poor.

Before the British rule, some communities lived in isolation in inaccessible forest and mountainous regions. Their social and cultural life was distinct, with their own culture and language. Due to this generational separation, these communities remained underdeveloped, resulting in backward economic and social conditions.
