# Internal Security Challenges in India

India faces several internal security challenges rooted in diverse factors.

## Naxalist Movement

Inspired by the Chinese Revolution, the Naxalist insurgency began in India in 1967 in West Bengal, specifically in the village of Naxalbari, giving the ideology its name. Starting in West Bengal, it has spread to states like Jharkhand, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, Chhattisgarh, Kerala, Odisha, Tripura, and mountainous/forest regions of Madhya Pradesh. Two major organizations are the **People's War Group** and the **Maoist Communist Centre**.

## Insurgence in North-east

Insurgency in North-eastern India has been a persistent problem since independence. Contributing factors include:
*   Many tribal communities.
*   Hilly and forested landscape.
*   Mutual cooperation among militant groups.
*   International borders in some areas.
*   Involvement of foreign agencies.

## Terrorism in Kashmir

Jammu and Kashmir is an integral part of India. After independence in 1947, Pakistan captured some parts of Kashmir and continues attempts to capture it, including fighting wars, in which India has been victorious. Terrorism in Kashmir increased after 1988. Terrorists use tactics like kidnapping, murders, and bomb explosions to achieve their goals, leading to the migration of many Kashmiri Pandit families who now live as refugees outside Kashmir. Terrorism in Kashmir is supported from across the border.

The aim of terrorists is to create fear and instability in India. India is resolved to face them firmly and opposes terrorism globally in all forms and at all times. Indian soldiers have sacrificed their lives to combat terrorism.
