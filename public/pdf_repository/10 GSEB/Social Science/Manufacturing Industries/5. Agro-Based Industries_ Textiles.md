# Agro-based Industries

Industries that use raw materials obtained from agriculture-based activities are called agro-based industries. Examples include cotton textile, jute, silk, woollen textile, sugar, and paper industries.

## Cotton Textile Industry

The garment industry holds a significant position in India's industrial economy, providing employment to about 3.5 crore people, making it the largest employer in the country's industrial sector. India ranks second globally, after China, in the export of cotton textiles. It is a primary industry in terms of both production and employment.

The first cotton mill in India was established in Mumbai. Subsequently, Shahpur Mills and Calico Mills were founded in Ahmedabad, Gujarat. Initially, cotton textile mills were concentrated in Mumbai and Ahmedabad due to factors like:
*   Availability of cheap cotton
*   Abundant labour supply
*   Good transport facilities
*   Access to ports for export
*   Favourable markets

Today, cotton textile mills are spread across approximately 100 cities in India. Major traditional and important centres include Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Bhiwandi, Solapur, Kolhapur, Nagpur, Indore, and Ujjain.

*   **Maharashtra:** Mumbai has the highest concentration of mills, earning it the nickname "Cottonopolis of India." Other centres include Pune, Kolhapur, Aurangabad, and Jalgaon.
*   **Gujarat:** Ahmedabad is known as the "Manchester of the East" or "Denim city of India." Other cities include Vadodara, Kalol, Bharuch, Surat, Porbandar, Bhavnagar, and Rajkot.
*   **Tamil Nadu:** Coimbatore is the main centre, along with Chennai and Madurai.
*   **Uttar Pradesh:** Main centres are Kanpur, Itawah, Agra, and Lucknow.
*   **Madhya Pradesh:** Indore, Gwalior, Ujjain, and Dewas are major centres.
*   **West Bengal:** Kolkata, Howrah, and Murshidabad are major centres.
*   Cotton textile industry is also established in Rajasthan, Punjab, and Haryana.

The decentralization of this industry has been significantly aided by extensive market networks, transportation infrastructure, banking facilities, and reliable electricity supply.

Currently, the cotton textile industry faces challenges such as:
*   Inadequate supply of high-quality cotton varieties
*   Use of outdated machinery
*   Irregular electricity supply
*   Competition from synthetic fibre cloth in the market
*   Intense competition in global markets

India exports cotton textile cloth to countries including Russia, United Kingdom, United States of America, Sudan, Nepal, Australia, France, and South African countries.

## Jute Textile Industry

Jute is the second-largest major industry in India. India leads the world in the production of raw jute and jute goods and ranks second after Bangladesh in jute exports. Approximately 80% of India's total jute production comes from West Bengal, about 10% from Andhra Pradesh, and the remaining from Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, Assam, and Tripura.

A large amount of water is required to process jute, which is why most jute mills are located along the banks of the Hooghly river. The concentration of this industry in West Bengal is also due to factors like:
*   Availability of cheap labour
*   Banking and insurance coverage
*   Port facilities for export

The demand for jute has decreased recently due to the availability of alternative packing materials. The jute industry is struggling with problems like high production costs and declining international demand.

## Silk Textile Industry

Silk production in India has a long history. India is the second-largest producer of silk globally, after China. Four main types of silk are produced in India:
*   Mulberry
*   Eri
*   Tasar
*   Muga

There are currently about 300 silk weaving mills in India. The major silk-producing states are Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, West Bengal, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, and Jammu-Kashmir. Silken clothes are exported to Europe, Africa, and Gulf countries, as well as specifically to Germany, Singapore, U.S.A., Kuwait, Malaysia, and Russia. Indian silk faces strong competition from China in the international market.

## Woollen Textiles Industry

The woollen textile industry has a long history as a cottage industry. Punjab has the maximum number of woollen textile mills, followed by Maharashtra and Uttar Pradesh. Ahmedabad and Jamnagar in Gujarat are important centres for woollen clothes. Other major centres include Bikaner and Jaipur in Rajasthan, Srinagar in Jammu-Kashmir, and Bengaluru in Karnataka. India is also a producer of woollen carpets. Woollen cloth is exported to countries like America, Canada, Germany, France, and Russia.

## Synthetic Textiles Industry

The synthetic textile industry has grown significantly because cloth made from man-made synthetic fibres is strong, durable, and wrinkle-resistant. Synthetic fibres are often mixed with cotton fibres to produce blended fabrics. Major producing states include Kerala, Gujarat, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, and Tamil Nadu. Other important centres are Surat, Kanpur, Mumbai, Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Chennai, and Modinagar.
