This section introduces the concept of industry and provides a historical overview of manufacturing traditions in India, from ancient times to the pre-industrial revolution era.

# Introduction to Manufacturing Industries

Industry is the process by which natural resources are transformed into usable conditions according to human intellectual, cultural, and economic capacity. India has industrial traditions dating back to the Indus Valley civilization, where items like cotton textiles, clay utensils, bronze goods, and beads were produced. Until the eighteenth century, India was prominent in shipbuilding and was a leader in handicraft and cottage industries. Indian cotton cloth, muslin, metal utensils, and ornaments were in high demand globally.

With the onset of the Industrial Revolution in England, the British began exporting raw materials, particularly raw cotton, from India. They implemented a policy of selling goods manufactured in England at high prices in India, aiming to cripple the Indian market. This severely damaged India's textile industry and the livelihood of Indian craftsmen.
