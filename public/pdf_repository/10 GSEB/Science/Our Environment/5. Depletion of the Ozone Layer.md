## Ozone Layer and How it is Getting Depleted

**Ozone** ($\mathrm{O}_{3}$) is a molecule formed by three atoms of oxygen. While $\mathrm{O}_{2}$ (molecular oxygen) is essential for aerobic life, ozone is a deadly poison at ground level. However, at higher levels of the atmosphere, ozone performs an essential function: it shields the surface of the Earth from harmful **ultraviolet (UV) radiation** from the Sun. This radiation is highly damaging to organisms; for example, it is known to cause skin cancer in human beings, damage eyes, and harm plant life.

### Formation of Ozone in the Atmosphere

Ozone at the higher levels of the atmosphere is a product of UV radiation acting on oxygen ($\mathrm{O}_{2}$) molecules. The higher energy UV radiations split apart some molecular oxygen ($\mathrm{O}_{2}$) into free oxygen (O) atoms. These highly reactive free oxygen atoms then combine with molecular oxygen ($\mathrm{O}_{2}$) to form ozone ($\mathrm{O}_{3}$):

$$
\mathrm{O}_{2} \xrightarrow{\mathrm{UV}} \mathrm{O}+\mathrm{O}
$$

$$
\mathrm{O}+\mathrm{O}_{2} \rightarrow \mathrm{O}_{3} \text{ (Ozone)}
$$

### Ozone Depletion

The amount of ozone in the atmosphere began to drop sharply in the 1980s. This decrease has been linked to synthetic chemicals, primarily **chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)**. CFCs were widely used as refrigerants (in refrigerators and air conditioners) and in fire extinguishers, aerosol propellants, and foam plastics. Once released into the atmosphere, CFCs rise to the stratosphere where they are broken down by UV radiation, releasing chlorine atoms that catalytically destroy thousands of ozone molecules.

In 1987, the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) played a crucial role in forging an international agreement known as the **Montreal Protocol**. This agreement aimed to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels and eventually phase out their production. As a result, it is now mandatory for manufacturing companies worldwide to produce CFC-free refrigerators and other products.

## Activity 13.4: Researching Ozone Depletion

**Prompt:** Find out from the library, internet or newspaper reports, which chemicals are responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer.
> **Answer:** The primary chemicals responsible for the depletion of the ozone layer are:
> *   **Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs):** Widely used in refrigerants, aerosols, and foam blowing agents.
> *   **Halons:** Used in fire extinguishers.
> *   **Carbon Tetrachloride (CCl4):** Used as a solvent and in fire extinguishers.
> *   **Methyl Chloroform (CH3CCl3):** Used as a solvent.
> *   **Hydrochlorofluorocarbons (HCFCs):** Introduced as transitional substitutes for CFCs, they also deplete ozone, though to a lesser extent.

**Prompt:** Find out if the regulations put in place to control the emission of these chemicals have succeeded in reducing the damage to the ozone layer. Has the size of the hole in the ozone layer changed in recent years?
> **Answer:** Yes, the regulations put in place (primarily through the Montreal Protocol) have largely succeeded in reducing the damage to the ozone layer. The production and consumption of most ozone-depleting substances have been phased out globally. As a result:
> *   The ozone layer is showing signs of recovery. Scientists have observed a gradual thickening of the ozone layer, particularly over the Antarctic.
> *   The size of the ozone hole (a seasonal thinning over Antarctica) has generally been shrinking in recent years, though there can be year-to-year variations due to meteorological conditions. Projections suggest that the ozone layer could return to 1980 levels by mid-century for most regions and by 2066 over the Antarctic. This success is often cited as a prime example of successful international environmental cooperation.
