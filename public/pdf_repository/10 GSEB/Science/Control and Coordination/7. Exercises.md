4.  What is the function of receptors in our body? Think of situations where receptors do not work properly. What problems are likely to arise?
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Function of Receptors:** Receptors are specialized sensory structures (often tips of nerve cells) that detect specific stimuli (changes) from the environment or inside the body. They convert these stimuli into electrical impulses that can be transmitted to the nervous system for processing. Examples include photoreceptors for light, gustatory receptors for taste, olfactory receptors for smell, thermoreceptors for temperature, and pain receptors.
    > *   **Problems if Receptors Don't Work Properly:**
    >     *   **Loss of Sensation:** If touch receptors don't work, one might not feel pain from an injury, leading to severe damage.
    >     *   **Impaired Vision/Hearing:** Faulty photoreceptors or auditory receptors could lead to blindness or deafness, significantly impacting interaction with the world.
    >     *   **Safety Risks:** Not detecting heat or cold could lead to burns or frostbite. Not detecting a foul smell (e.g., gas leak) could be dangerous.
    >     *   **Nutritional Issues:** Impaired taste or smell receptors could reduce appetite and lead to poor nutrition.
    >     *   **Coordination Problems:** If proprioceptors (receptors detecting body position) malfunction, it can lead to poor balance and coordination.

5.  Draw the structure of a neuron and explain its function.
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Structure of a Neuron:** A neuron typically consists of three main parts:
    >     *   **Dendrites:** Branch-like projections that receive incoming electrical signals (impulses) from other neurons.
    >     *   **Cell Body (Soma):** Contains the nucleus and other organelles. It integrates the incoming signals from dendrites.
    >     *   **Axon:** A long, slender projection that carries electrical impulses away from the cell body towards other neurons, muscles, or glands. It often ends in axon terminals (nerve endings). Many axons are covered by a myelin sheath, which insulates them and speeds up impulse transmission.
    > *   **Function of a Neuron:** Neurons are the fundamental units of the nervous system, specialized for transmitting information. Their primary function is to receive, process, and transmit electrical and chemical signals throughout the body. They enable communication between different parts of the body, allowing for sensation, thought, movement, and all bodily functions. Information flows from dendrites to the cell body, then along the axon, and is transmitted across a synapse to the next cell.

6.  How does phototropism occur in plants?
    > **Answer:** Phototropism, the directional growth of plants in response to light, primarily occurs due to the unequal distribution of a plant hormone called **auxin**. When light falls on a plant from one side, auxin, which is synthesized at the shoot tip, migrates and accumulates on the shaded side of the shoot. A higher concentration of auxin on the shaded side stimulates cells on that side to elongate more rapidly than cells on the illuminated side. This differential growth causes the shoot to bend towards the light source, thus exhibiting positive phototropism.

7.  Which signals will get disrupted in case of a spinal cord injury?
    > **Answer:** A spinal cord injury can disrupt virtually all signals traveling between the brain and the rest of the body, both sensory (afferent) and motor (efferent).
    > *   **Sensory Signals:** Information from receptors (touch, pain, temperature, pressure, proprioception) from below the injury site will not reach the brain, leading to loss of sensation.
    > *   **Motor Signals:** Commands from the brain to muscles below the injury site will not reach them, resulting in paralysis or weakness.
    > *   **Autonomic Signals:** Signals controlling involuntary functions (like breathing, heart rate, blood pressure, digestion, bladder, and bowel control) can also be disrupted, leading to significant dysfunction of internal organs.
    > *   **Reflex Arcs:** Depending on the severity and location, local reflex arcs involving the spinal cord might also be affected.

8.  How does chemical coordination occur in plants?
    > **Answer:** Chemical coordination in plants occurs through **plant hormones (phytohormones)**. These hormones are synthesized in specific parts of the plant and then transported (often by diffusion or through vascular tissues) to other parts where they exert their effects. They regulate various physiological processes such as:
    > *   **Growth and Development:** Auxins, gibberellins, and cytokinins promote cell elongation, division, and overall growth.
    > *   **Ripening and Senescence:** Ethylene promotes fruit ripening and leaf aging.
    > *   **Stress Responses:** Abscisic acid helps plants respond to stress (e.g., wilting in drought).
    > *   **Dormancy:** Abscisic acid induces dormancy in seeds and buds.
    > *   **Tropic Movements:** Hormones like auxin are crucial for directional growth responses to light (phototropism), gravity (geotropism), and touch (thigmotropism).

9.  What is the need for a system of control and coordination in an organism?
    > **Answer:** A system of control and coordination is essential for an organism's survival and efficient functioning because:
    > *   **Survival:** It allows the organism to detect and respond appropriately to changes in its internal and external environment (stimuli), such as seeking food, escaping predators, or maintaining stable internal conditions (homeostasis).
    > *   **Integrated Functioning:** It ensures that all the different organs, tissues, and cells of a multicellular organism work together in a synchronized and integrated manner. Without coordination, various bodily processes would occur haphazardly, leading to chaos and dysfunction.
    > *   **Adaptation:** It enables the organism to adapt to changing conditions, optimize its growth, development, and reproductive processes.
    > *   **Complex Actions:** It facilitates the execution of complex voluntary actions (e.g., walking, thinking) and vital involuntary actions (e.g., breathing, digestion).

10. How are involuntary actions and reflex actions different from each other?
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Involuntary Actions:** These are actions that occur automatically and unconsciously, without conscious thought or control from the brain. They are often regulated by the mid-brain and hind-brain and typically involve the functioning of internal organs. Examples include heartbeat, breathing, digestion, blood pressure regulation, and salivation. They are generally slower and continuous processes.
    > *   **Reflex Actions:** These are a specific type of involuntary action characterized by being rapid, sudden, and automatic responses to a specific stimulus. They typically involve a reflex arc, with processing often occurring in the spinal cord, bypassing the brain for immediate reaction. Examples include withdrawing a hand from a hot object, blinking, or knee-jerk. They are usually protective and immediate responses.

11. Compare and contrast nervous and hormonal mechanisms for control and coordination in animals.
    > **Answer:**
    > **Comparison (Similarities):**
    > *   Both systems are involved in **control and coordination** of bodily functions.
    > *   Both use **chemical signals** for communication (neurotransmitters in nervous system, hormones in endocrine system).
    > *   Both are crucial for maintaining **homeostasis** and adapting to environmental changes.
    >
    > **Contrast (Differences):**
    > | Feature           | Nervous System                                  | Hormonal System (Endocrine System)                                 |
    > | :---------------- | :---------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- |
    > | **Nature of Signal** | Electrical impulses (nerve impulses) and chemical (neurotransmitters) | Chemical (hormones)                                                |
    > | **Medium of Transmission** | Neurons (nerve fibers)                          | Bloodstream                                                        |
    > | **Speed of Transmission** | Very fast (milliseconds)                        | Slower (seconds to minutes or hours)                               |
    > | **Target Cells/Specificity** | Specific cells/muscles/glands connected by nerves | Widespread, affects all cells with specific receptors for the hormone |
    > | **Duration of Effect** | Short-lived, localized, precise                   | Long-lasting, widespread, often generalized                        |
    > | **Control**       | Precise, voluntary and involuntary actions      | Broad, typically involuntary actions (growth, metabolism, reproduction) |
    > | **Example**        | Reflexes, muscle contraction, thinking          | Growth, puberty, blood sugar regulation, stress response           |

12. What is the difference between the manner in which movement takes place in a sensitive plant and the movement in our legs?
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Movement in a Sensitive Plant (e.g., *Mimosa pudica*):**
    >     *   **Mechanism:** It is a **growth-independent** movement caused by rapid changes in **turgor pressure** within specialized cells (pulvini) at the base of the leaves. When touched, water quickly moves out of these cells, causing them to lose turgor and the leaves to fold or droop.
    >     *   **Speed:** Very fast.
    >     *   **Control:** No nervous or muscular tissue is involved; it's an electrical-chemical signaling process leading to turgor changes.
    >     *   **Nature:** Non-directional (nastic movement).
    > *   **Movement in Our Legs:**
    >     *   **Mechanism:** It is a **voluntary** movement (e.g., walking, running) initiated by conscious thought in the **brain**. Electrical impulses travel from the brain, down the spinal cord, and along **motor neurons** to the **skeletal muscles** in the legs. These impulses cause specialized proteins within muscle cells to slide past each other, shortening the muscle fibers and leading to muscle contraction.
    >     *   **Speed:** Can be fast or slow, depending on the desired action.
    >     *   **Control:** Highly coordinated by the nervous system (brain and spinal cord) and executed by muscular tissue.
    >     *   **Nature:** Directional and precise.
