This section introduces the concepts of oxidation and reduction, defining them in terms of the gain or loss of oxygen and hydrogen, and explains how these processes often occur simultaneously in what are known as redox reactions.

### Oxidation and Reduction

## Activity 1.11: Heating Copper Powder

*   Heat a china dish containing about 1 g copper powder (Fig. 1.10).
*   What do you observe?

> **Answer:** You will observe that the surface of the reddish-brown copper powder becomes coated with a black substance.

The surface of copper powder becomes coated with black copper(II) oxide. Why has this black substance formed?
This is because oxygen is added to copper and copper oxide is formed.

$2\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} 2\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s})$

If hydrogen gas is passed over this heated material ($\mathrm{CuO}$), the black coating on the surface turns brown as the reverse reaction takes place and copper is obtained.

$\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} \mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})$

If a substance **gains oxygen** during a reaction, it is said to be **oxidised**. If a substance **loses oxygen** during a reaction, it is said to be **reduced**.

During the reaction $\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} \mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})$, the copper(II) oxide is losing oxygen and is being reduced. The hydrogen is gaining oxygen and is being oxidised. In other words, one reactant gets oxidised while the other gets reduced during a reaction. Such reactions are called **oxidation-reduction reactions** or **redox reactions**.

Some other examples of redox reactions are:

$\mathrm{ZnO}+\mathrm{C} \rightarrow \mathrm{Zn}+\mathrm{CO}$
In this reaction, carbon (C) is oxidised to CO (gains oxygen) and ZnO is reduced to Zn (loses oxygen).

$\mathrm{MnO}_{2}+4\mathrm{HCl} \rightarrow \mathrm{MnCl}_{2}+2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}+\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$
In this reaction, HCl is oxidised to $\mathrm{Cl}_{2}$ (loses hydrogen) whereas $\mathrm{MnO}_{2}$ is reduced to $\mathrm{MnCl}_{2}$ (loses oxygen).

From the above examples we can say that if a substance **gains oxygen** or **loses hydrogen** during a reaction, it is **oxidised**. If a substance **loses oxygen** or **gains hydrogen** during a reaction, it is **reduced**.

Recall Activity 1.1, where a magnesium ribbon burns with a dazzling flame in air (oxygen) and changes into a white substance, magnesium oxide. Is magnesium being oxidised or reduced in this reaction?

> **Answer:** In Activity 1.1, magnesium (Mg) reacts with oxygen ($\mathrm{O}_{2}$) to form magnesium oxide (MgO). Since magnesium is gaining oxygen, it is being **oxidised** in this reaction.
