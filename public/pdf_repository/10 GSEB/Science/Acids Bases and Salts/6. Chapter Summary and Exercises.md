This section provides a concise summary of the key concepts learned in the chapter, followed by a set of exercises and activities to reinforce understanding and application of the knowledge of acids, bases, and salts.

### What you have learnt

*   Acid-base indicators are dyes or mixtures of dyes which are used to indicate the presence of acids and bases.
*   Acidic nature of a substance is due to the formation of $\mathrm{H}^{+}(\mathrm{aq})$ ions in solution. Formation of $\mathrm{OH}^{-}(\mathrm{aq})$ ions in solution is responsible for the basic nature of a substance.
*   When an acid reacts with a metal, hydrogen gas is evolved and a corresponding salt is formed.
*   When a base reacts with a metal, along with the evolution of hydrogen gas a salt is formed which has a negative ion composed of the metal and oxygen.
*   When an acid reacts with a metal carbonate or metal hydrogencarbonate, it gives the corresponding salt, carbon dioxide gas and water.
*   Acidic and basic solutions in water conduct electricity because they produce hydrogen and hydroxide ions respectively.
*   The strength of an acid or an alkali can be tested by using a scale called the pH scale (0-14) which gives the measure of hydrogen ion concentration in a solution.
*   A neutral solution has a pH of exactly 7, while an acidic solution has a pH less than 7 and a basic solution a pH more than 7.
*   Living beings carry out their metabolic activities within an optimal pH range.
*   Mixing concentrated acids or bases with water is a highly exothermic process.
*   Acids and bases neutralise each other to form corresponding salts and water.
*   Water of crystallisation is the fixed number of water molecules present in one formula unit of a salt.
*   Salts have various uses in everyday life and in industries.

### Exercises

1. A solution turns red litmus blue, its pH is likely to be
    (a) 1
    (b) 4
    (c) 5
    (d) 10
> **Answer:** (d) 10
> **Explanation:** A solution that turns red litmus blue is basic. Basic solutions have a pH greater than 7. Among the given options, only 10 is greater than 7.

2. A solution reacts with crushed egg-shells to give a gas that turns lime-water milky. The solution contains
    (a) NaCl
    (b) HCl
    (c) LiCl
    (d) KCl
> **Answer:** (b) HCl
> **Explanation:** Crushed egg-shells are primarily calcium carbonate ($\text{CaCO}_3$). When a substance reacts with a carbonate to produce a gas that turns lime-water milky, the gas is carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$), and the reacting substance must be an acid. Among the given options, HCl (hydrochloric acid) is an acid.

3. 10 mL of a solution of NaOH is found to be completely neutralised by 8 mL of a given solution of HCl. If we take 20 mL of the same solution of NaOH, the amount HCl solution (the same solution as before) required to neutralise it will be
    (a) 4 mL
    (b) 8 mL
    (c) 12 mL
    (d) 16 mL
> **Answer:** (d) 16 mL
> **Explanation:** The amount of acid required is directly proportional to the amount of base to be neutralized. If 10 mL of NaOH requires 8 mL of HCl, then 20 mL of NaOH (twice the amount) will require twice the amount of HCl, which is $2 \times 8 \text{ mL} = 16 \text{ mL}$.

4. Which one of the following types of medicines is used for treating indigestion?
    (a) Antibiotic
    (b) Analgesic
    (c) Antacid
    (d) Antiseptic
> **Answer:** (c) Antacid
> **Explanation:** Indigestion is caused by excess acid production in the stomach. Antacids are mild bases that neutralize this excess acid, providing relief.

5. Write word equations and then balanced equations for the reaction taking place when -
    (a) dilute sulphuric acid reacts with zinc granules.
    (b) dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with magnesium ribbon.
    (c) dilute sulphuric acid reacts with aluminium powder.
    (d) dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with iron filings.
> **Answer:**
> (a) **Dilute sulphuric acid reacts with zinc granules.**
>     Word equation: Zinc + Sulphuric acid $\rightarrow$ Zinc sulphate + Hydrogen gas
>     Balanced equation: $\text{Zn(s) + H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{ZnSO}_4\text{(aq) + H}_2\text{(g)}$
>
> (b) **Dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with magnesium ribbon.**
>     Word equation: Magnesium + Hydrochloric acid $\rightarrow$ Magnesium chloride + Hydrogen gas
>     Balanced equation: $\text{Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{MgCl}_2\text{(aq) + H}_2\text{(g)}$
>
> (c) **Dilute sulphuric acid reacts with aluminium powder.**
>     Word equation: Aluminium + Sulphuric acid $\rightarrow$ Aluminium sulphate + Hydrogen gas
>     Balanced equation: $\text{2Al(s) + 3H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq)} \rightarrow \text{Al}_2\text{(SO}_4\text{)}_3\text{(aq) + 3H}_2\text{(g)}$
>
> (d) **Dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with iron filings.**
>     Word equation: Iron + Hydrochloric acid $\rightarrow$ Iron(II) chloride + Hydrogen gas
>     Balanced equation: $\text{Fe(s) + 2HCl(aq)} \rightarrow \text{FeCl}_2\text{(aq) + H}_2\text{(g)}$

6. Compounds such as alcohols and glucose also contain hydrogen but are not categorised as acids. Describe an Activity to prove it.
> **Answer:** Refer to Activity 2.8 in the "Understanding Acid and Base Behavior in Water" section.
> **Activity Description:**
> 1.  Set up an electrical circuit consisting of a 6-volt battery, a bulb, a switch, and two nails fixed on a cork (acting as electrodes).
> 2.  Place the cork with nails in a 100 mL beaker.
> 3.  Pour dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) into the beaker and switch on the current. Observe if the bulb glows. (It will glow, indicating conductivity).
> 4.  Repeat the experiment separately with solutions of glucose and alcohol.
> **Observation:** The bulb will glow in the case of dilute HCl solution, but it will **not glow** when glucose or alcohol solutions are used.
> **Proof:** The glowing of the bulb indicates the flow of electric current due to the presence of free ions. Acids like HCl produce $\text{H}^+$ ions in water, which conduct electricity. However, glucose and alcohol, despite containing hydrogen, do not dissociate into $\text{H}^+$ ions when dissolved in water. Since no ions are produced, they do not conduct electricity, proving that they are not acidic.

7. Why does distilled water not conduct electricity, whereas rain water does?
> **Answer:** Distilled water is a pure form of water that contains virtually no dissolved ionic impurities (like salts, acids, or bases). Since electrical conductivity in water requires the presence of free-moving ions, distilled water is a very poor conductor of electricity.
> Rainwater, on the other hand, is not pure. As rain falls through the atmosphere, it dissolves gases like carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide, and nitrogen oxides, forming weak acids (e.g., carbonic acid, sulfuric acid, nitric acid). It also picks up dust and other impurities, which may contain ionic compounds. These dissolved acids and ionic impurities dissociate into ions in rainwater, making it a conductor of electricity.

8. Why do acids not show acidic behaviour in the absence of water?
> **Answer:** Acids show acidic behavior (such as turning blue litmus red, reacting with metals, etc.) due to the production of hydrogen ions ($\text{H}^+$) or hydronium ions ($\text{H}_3\text{O}^+$). The dissociation of an acid into $\text{H}^+$ ions only occurs in the presence of water. In the absence of water, acids exist as molecular compounds and do not ionize. Therefore, no $\text{H}^+$ ions are produced, and they cannot exhibit their characteristic acidic properties. For example, dry $\text{HCl}$ gas does not change the colour of dry litmus paper.

9. Five solutions A, B, C, D and E when tested with universal indicator showed pH as 4, 1, 11, 7 and 9, respectively. Which solution is
    (a) neutral?
    (b) strongly alkaline?
    (c) strongly acidic?
    (d) weakly acidic?
    (e) weakly alkaline?
    Arrange the pH in increasing order of hydrogen-ion concentration.
> **Answer:**
> (a) **Neutral:** Solution D (pH 7)
> (b) **Strongly alkaline:** Solution C (pH 11)
> (c) **Strongly acidic:** Solution B (pH 1)
> (d) **Weakly acidic:** Solution A (pH 4)
> (e) **Weakly alkaline:** Solution E (pH 9)
>
> **Arrangement in increasing order of hydrogen-ion concentration (which means decreasing order of pH):**
> Solution C (pH 11) < Solution E (pH 9) < Solution D (pH 7) < Solution A (pH 4) < Solution B (pH 1)

10. Equal lengths of magnesium ribbons are taken in test tubes A and B. Hydrochloric acid $(\mathrm{HCl})$ is added to test tube A, while acetic acid $\left(\mathrm{CH}_{3} \mathrm{COOH}\right)$ is added to test tube B. Amount and concentration taken for both the acids are same. In which test tube will the fizzing occur more vigorously and why?
> **Answer:** Fizzing will occur more vigorously in **test tube A** (with dilute hydrochloric acid, HCl).
> **Explanation:** Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is a **strong acid**, meaning it completely dissociates in water to produce a high concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions. Acetic acid ($\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$) is a **weak acid**, meaning it only partially dissociates in water, producing a lower concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions. The rate of reaction between a metal (magnesium) and an acid depends on the concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions. Since HCl produces a higher concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions than $\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}$ (for the same molar concentration), the reaction with magnesium will be faster and more vigorous, leading to more rapid fizzing (evolution of hydrogen gas).

11. Fresh milk has a pH of 6. How do you think the pH will change as it turns into curd? Explain your answer.
> **Answer:** When fresh milk (pH 6) turns into curd, its pH will **decrease** (become more acidic).
> **Explanation:** Curd formation is a process driven by lactic acid bacteria present in the milk. These bacteria convert lactose (milk sugar) into lactic acid. The production of lactic acid increases the concentration of $\text{H}^+$ ions in the milk, thereby lowering its pH and making it more acidic. This increased acidity causes the milk proteins to coagulate, forming curd.

12. A milkman adds a very small amount of baking soda to fresh milk.
    (a) Why does he shift the pH of the fresh milk from 6 to slightly alkaline?
    (b) Why does this milk take a long time to set as curd?
> **Answer:**
> (a) The milkman shifts the pH of fresh milk from 6 (slightly acidic) to slightly alkaline by adding baking soda (sodium hydrogencarbonate) primarily to **slow down the curdling process**. Baking soda is a mild base, and increasing the pH makes the milk less acidic, thus inhibiting the rapid growth of lactic acid bacteria which are responsible for converting lactose into lactic acid and causing curdling. This helps the milk stay fresh for a longer period.
> (b) This milk takes a longer time to set as curd because the **alkaline nature of the milk needs to be neutralized first**. The lactic acid produced by bacteria will initially react with the baking soda present in the milk. Only after all the added baking soda has been consumed (neutralized) will the pH of the milk start to drop significantly, allowing it to become acidic enough for the milk proteins to coagulate and form curd. This extra neutralization step prolongs the curdling time.

13. Plaster of Paris should be stored in a moisture-proof container. Explain why?
> **Answer:** Plaster of Paris ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}\text{H}_2\text{O}$) should be stored in a moisture-proof container because it is a white powder that has a strong affinity for water. When it comes into contact with moisture (even atmospheric humidity), it readily absorbs water and undergoes a chemical reaction to form gypsum ($\text{CaSO}_4 \cdot 2\text{H}_2\text{O}$), which is a hard, solid mass. This setting process is irreversible. If Plaster of Paris is not stored in a moisture-proof container, it will absorb moisture from the air, lose its powdery form, and become a hard, unusable lump, thus losing its primary property of setting into a desired shape.

14. What is a neutralisation reaction? Give two examples.
> **Answer:** A **neutralisation reaction** is a chemical reaction in which an acid and a base react quantitatively with each other to form a salt and water. In this reaction, the characteristic acidic properties (due to $\text{H}^+$ ions) and basic properties (due to $\text{OH}^-$ ions) are mutually nullified. The net ionic reaction for neutralization is the formation of water from hydrogen and hydroxide ions: $\text{H}^+(\text{aq}) + \text{OH}^-(\text{aq}) \rightarrow \text{H}_2\text{O(l)}$.
> **Examples:**
> 1.  Reaction of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide:
>     $\text{HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) \rightarrow NaCl(aq) + H}_2\text{O(l)}$
> 2.  Reaction of sulphuric acid with potassium hydroxide:
>     $\text{H}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq) + 2KOH(aq) \rightarrow K}_2\text{SO}_4\text{(aq) + 2H}_2\text{O(l)}$

15. Give two important uses of washing soda and baking soda.
> **Answer:**
> **Uses of Washing Soda ($\text{Na}_2\text{CO}_3 \cdot 10\text{H}_2\text{O}$):**
> 1.  It is used in various industries such as glass, soap, and paper manufacturing.
> 2.  It serves as a cleaning agent for domestic purposes and is also used for removing the permanent hardness of water.
>
> **Uses of Baking Soda ($\text{NaHCO}_3$):**
> 1.  It is a key ingredient in baking powder (a mixture of baking soda and a mild edible acid like tartaric acid), used in baking to make bread and cakes soft and spongy due to the release of carbon dioxide gas.
> 2.  It is used as an antacid to neutralize excess acid in the stomach, providing relief from indigestion, as it is a mild alkaline salt.

#### (II) Preparing a soda-acid fire extinguisher

The reaction of acids with metal hydrogencarbonates is used in the fire extinguishers which produce carbon dioxide.

- Take 20 mL of sodium hydrogencarbonate $\left(\mathrm{NaHCO}_{3}\right)$ solution in a wash-bottle.
- Suspend an ignition tube containing dilute sulphuric acid in the wash-bottle (Fig. 2.10).
- Close the mouth of the wash-bottle.
- Tilt the wash-bottle so that the acid from the ignition tube mixes with the sodium hydrogencarbonate solution below.
- You will notice discharge coming out of the nozzle.
- Direct this discharge on a burning candle. What happens?
