This section explains why the clear sky appears blue and why the Sun appears red at sunrise and sunset, linking these phenomena to the principles of light scattering.

# Color of the Sky and Sun at Sunrise/Sunset

### Why is the Color of the Clear Sky Blue?

The molecules of air and other fine particles in the atmosphere are smaller than the wavelength of visible light. These tiny particles are more effective at scattering light of shorter wavelengths (like blue light, which is at the blue end of the spectrum) than light of longer wavelengths (like red light, which is at the red end). Red light has a wavelength approximately 1.8 times greater than blue light.

Therefore, when sunlight passes through the atmosphere, the fine particles in the air scatter the blue color (shorter wavelengths) more strongly than red. This scattered blue light enters our eyes, making the sky appear blue.

If the Earth had no atmosphere, there would be no scattering of sunlight. In such a scenario, the sky would look dark. This is why the sky appears dark to astronauts flying at very high altitudes, as scattering is not prominent at such heights due to the much lower density of air particles.

### Reddening of the Sun at Sunrise and Sunset

You might have observed that 'danger' signal lights are red in color. This is because red light is least scattered by fog or smoke compared to other colors. Therefore, it can be seen in its original color from a distance, making it effective as a warning signal.

Similarly, at sunrise and sunset, the Sun appears reddish. This happens because, at these times, the sunlight has to travel a longer distance through the Earth's atmosphere to reach our eyes. During this longer path, most of the shorter wavelength blue light and other colors are scattered away by the fine particles in the atmosphere. The light that reaches our eyes is predominantly the longer wavelength red and orange light, which are scattered the least. Consequently, the Sun appears red or orange. During midday, when the Sun is overhead, the light travels a relatively shorter distance through the atmosphere, so less scattering of blue light occurs, and the Sun appears white.
