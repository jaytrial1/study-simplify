This section details the different structural forms of carbon, known as allotropes, highlighting how their distinct atomic arrangements lead to widely varied physical properties despite having the same chemical composition.

# Allotropes of Carbon

The element carbon occurs in different forms in nature with widely varying physical properties. Both diamond and graphite are formed by carbon atoms; the difference lies in the manner in which the carbon atoms are bonded to one another.

*   In **diamond**, each carbon atom is bonded to four other carbon atoms forming a rigid three-dimensional structure.
*   In **graphite**, each carbon atom is bonded to three other carbon atoms in the same plane giving a hexagonal array. One of these bonds is a double-bond, and thus the valency of carbon is satisfied. Graphite structure is formed by the hexagonal arrays being placed in layers one above the other.

These two different structures result in diamond and graphite having very different physical properties even though their chemical properties are the same. Diamond is the hardest substance known while graphite is smooth and slippery. Graphite is also a very good conductor of electricity unlike other non-metals that you studied in the previous Chapter.

Diamonds can be synthesised by subjecting pure carbon to very high pressure and temperature. These synthetic diamonds are small but are otherwise indistinguishable from natural diamonds.

**Fullerenes** form another class of carbon allotropes. The first one to be identified was $\text{C}_{60}$ which has carbon atoms arranged in the shape of a football. Since this looked like the geodesic dome designed by the US architect Buckminster Fuller, the molecule was named fullerene.

## Questions

1.  What would be the electron dot structure of carbon dioxide which has the formula $\text{CO}_2$?
2.  What would be the electron dot structure of a molecule of sulphur which is made up of eight atoms of sulphur? (Hint - The eight atoms of sulphur are joined together in the form of a ring.)

> **Answer:**
> 1.  **Electron dot structure of carbon dioxide ($\text{CO}_2$):**
>     Carbon has 4 valence electrons, and oxygen has 6. Carbon forms two double bonds with two oxygen atoms to achieve its octet. Each oxygen also achieves its octet.
>     ```
>     ..      ..
>     :O :: C :: O:
>     ..      ..
>     ```
>     This can also be written as $\text{O=C=O}$ with two lone pairs on each oxygen atom.
>
> 2.  **Electron dot structure of a molecule of sulphur ($\text{S}_8$):**
>     Sulphur has 6 valence electrons and forms two single bonds to achieve a stable octet. In $\text{S}_8$, the eight sulphur atoms are joined in a puckered ring (crown shape). Each sulphur atom forms single bonds with two adjacent sulphur atoms and has two lone pairs.
>     ```
>        S—S
>       /   \
>     S       S
>     |       |
>     S       S
>       \   /
>        S—S
>     ```
>     Each S atom in the ring will have two lone pairs of electrons, for example:
>     ```
>     ..  ..
>     :S—S:
>     ..  ..
>     ```
>     (This is a simplified representation; the actual ring is 3D).
