To understand what causes electric charges to flow, we can draw an analogy with water flow. Water in a perfectly horizontal tube does not flow on its own. However, if one end of the tube is connected to a tank of water kept at a higher level, a pressure difference is created, causing water to flow out of the other end. Similarly, for charges to flow in a conducting metallic wire, there must be a difference in "electric pressure," which is called **potential difference**, along the conductor.

This potential difference can be generated by a battery, which consists of one or more electric cells. The chemical reactions within a cell produce this potential difference across its terminals, even when no current is being drawn. When the cell is connected to a conducting circuit element, this potential difference causes the charges to move in the conductor, creating an electric current. To maintain this current, the cell continuously expends its stored chemical energy.

We define the **electric potential difference (V)** between two points in an electric circuit carrying some current as the *work done to move a unit charge from one point to the other*.

Potential difference $(V)$ between two points = Work done $(W)$ / Charge $(Q)$

$$
V = W / Q
$$

The SI unit of electric potential difference is **volt (V)**, named after Alessandro Volta, an Italian physicist. One volt is defined as the potential difference between two points in a current-carrying conductor when 1 joule of work is done to move a charge of 1 coulomb from one point to the other.

Therefore, $1 \text{ volt} = \frac{1 \text{ joule}}{1 \text{ coulomb}}$
$$
1 \mathrm{~V}=1 \mathrm{~J} \mathrm{C}^{-1}
$$

The potential difference is measured by an instrument called a **voltmeter**. A voltmeter is *always connected in parallel* across the points between which the potential difference is to be measured.

## Example 11.2

How much work is done in moving a charge of 2 C across two points having a potential difference of 12 V?

### Solution

Given:
Charge $Q = 2 \mathrm{~C}$
Potential difference $V = 12 \mathrm{~V}$

From the formula $V = W/Q$, we can find the work done $W$:
$W = V \times Q$
$W = 12 \mathrm{~V} \times 2 \mathrm{~C}$
$W = 24 \mathrm{~J}$

The amount of work done is 24 J.

## Questions

1.  Name a device that helps to maintain a potential difference across a conductor.
2.  What is meant by saying that the potential difference between two points is 1 V?
3.  How much energy is given to each coulomb of charge passing through a 6 V battery?

> **Answer:**
> 1.  A **battery** or an **electric cell** helps to maintain a potential difference across a conductor.
> 2.  Saying that the potential difference between two points is 1 V means that 1 joule (J) of work is done to move 1 coulomb (C) of electric charge from one point to the other.
> 3.  The potential difference ($V$) is defined as the work done ($W$) per unit charge ($Q$), i.e., $V = W/Q$.
>     Given $V = 6 \mathrm{~V}$ and $Q = 1 \mathrm{~C}$.
>     The energy (work done) given to each coulomb of charge is:
>     $W = V \times Q$
>     $W = 6 \mathrm{~V} \times 1 \mathrm{~C}$
>     $W = 6 \mathrm{~J}$
>     So, 6 joules of energy is given to each coulomb of charge passing through a 6 V battery.
