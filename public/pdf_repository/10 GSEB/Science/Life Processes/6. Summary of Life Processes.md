This section provides a concise recap of the key concepts and processes covered in the chapter, reinforcing the understanding of how organisms maintain life.

# What you have learnt

*   **Movement**, even at molecular scales, is a fundamental indication of life.
*   **Life processes** such as nutrition, respiration, transport of materials, and excretion of waste products are essential for the maintenance of life.
*   **Autotrophic nutrition** involves synthesizing complex, high-energy organic material from simple inorganic materials using an external energy source (like sunlight).
*   **Heterotrophic nutrition** involves obtaining complex organic material prepared by other organisms.
*   In human beings, food is broken down in various steps along the **alimentary canal**, and the digested food is absorbed in the **small intestine** and transported to all body cells.
*   During **respiration**, organic compounds (e.g., glucose) are broken down to provide energy in the form of **ATP**, which fuels cellular activities.
*   Respiration can be **aerobic** (with oxygen, yielding more energy) or **anaerobic** (without oxygen).
*   In human beings, the **circulatory system** (heart, blood, blood vessels) is responsible for transporting materials like oxygen, carbon dioxide, food, and excretory products.
*   In highly differentiated plants, the **vascular tissue** (xylem and phloem) transports water, minerals, food, and other materials.
*   In human beings, soluble nitrogenous excretory products are removed by **nephrons** in the kidneys.
*   Plants use various techniques to dispose of waste material, including storage in cell vacuoles, removal in falling leaves, storage as gum and resin, or excretion into the surrounding soil.
