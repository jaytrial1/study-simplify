This section highlights the positive characteristics and progress of the Indian economy, demonstrating its transition from a less developed to a developing nation.

# Characteristics of India as a Developing Economy

Despite some indicators of lower development, India cannot be classified solely as a less developed nation. The Indian economy exhibits characteristics of a progressing economy, including:

## Growth Rate

The economy's growth rate, measured by the annual increase in national output, has risen, particularly after the economic reforms (LPG - Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization).

*   Average annual growth rate (1950-51 to 1990-91): Around 3.5%.
*   Average annual growth rate (after 1991 reforms): Above 6.8% (a significant achievement).
*   Growth rate slowed after 2012-13, less than 5% in 2014-15.

The Net National Income at factor cost (NNPFC) at constant prices increased significantly:

*   1950-51: ₹ 2,69,724 crores
*   2013-14: ₹ 87,51,834 crores

NNP grew by 18 times over 63 years.

## Changing Share of Various Sectors in National Income and Employment

As an economy develops, the contribution of industrial and service sectors to national income and employment increases, while dependence on agriculture decreases. This indicates a shift of labor to more productive sectors due to technological improvements and capital formation. India's service sector has grown significantly since the 1991 reforms.

### Share of Various Sectors in NI (%)

| Sector                            | 1950-51 | 1990-91 | 2000-01 | 2014-15 (P) |
| :-------------------------------- | :------: | :------: | :------: | :---------: |
| Agriculture and allied activities | 53.1     | 29.6     | 22.3     | 17.6        |
| Industries                        | 16.6     | 27.7     | 27.3     | 29.7        |
| Services (excluding construction) | 30.3     | 42.7     | 50.4     | 52.7        |

*Source: Economic Survey (2014-15)*

The share of agriculture in national income has declined, while that of industrial and service sectors has increased.

## Per Capita Income

India's per capita income at constant prices has increased over time.

*   1950-51: ₹ 7,114
*   2013-14: ₹ 39,904

PCI increased by approximately 5.6 times over 63 years. The growth rate was about 1.6% annually between 1950-51 and 1990-91, increasing to 5.5% annually after 1991.

The share of agriculture in employment has declined, with corresponding increases in the industrial and service sectors.

## Level of Employment

Employment in India is categorized into three sectors:

### Primary Sector

Includes agriculture and allied activities like dairy farming and animal husbandry.

### Industrial Sector

Includes manufacturing, construction, mining, and quarrying – essentially all production activities.

### Service Sector

Includes activities like trade, banking, transport, information and broadcasting, health, and education.

### Share of Various Sectors in Employment (%)

| Sector                     | 1951 | 1991 | 2001 | 2011-12 |
| :------------------------- | :--: | :--: | :--: | :-----: |
| Primary/Agricultural Sector | 72.1 | 66.9 | 56.7 | 48.9    |
| Secondary/Industrial Sector | 10.6 | 12.7 | 18.1 | 24.2    |
| Tertiary/Service Sector    | 17.3 | 20.4 | 25.2 | 26.9    |

*Source: Economic Survey (2014-15) Volume-II*

From 1951 to 2011-12, employment has shifted from the primary sector to the industrial and service sectors.

## Improvement in Basic Utilities

India has significantly improved the provision of various utilities in both urban and rural areas, such as electricity, water supply, roads, irrigation, transport, communication, health, and education.

### Irrigation

Irrigated land increased from 22.6 million hectares in 1950-51 to 63 million hectares in 2012-13. Approximately 45% of agricultural land has irrigation facilities today.

### Literacy

The literacy rate increased from 18.33% in 1951 to 73% in 2011 (Source: Economic Survey, 2013-14). The number of universities and colleges also grew significantly, indicating a rise in higher education.

### Electricity

Electricity generation increased from around 2300 MW in 1950-51 to 2,43,000 MW in 2011-12.

### Road Network

India now has one of the longest road networks globally, with approximately 48.6 lakh km of paved roads.

### Railway

The Indian railway network is the fourth largest worldwide, with a length of 65,000 km.

Overall, India is making rapid progress and is recognized as a prestigious emerging economy. There have been increases in per capita consumption, advancements in science, research, and technology, and an increase in average life expectancy.

In the International Comparison Programme (ICP) by the World Bank, India was ranked the third largest economy after USA and China in 2011, a significant rise from its 10th position in 2005.
