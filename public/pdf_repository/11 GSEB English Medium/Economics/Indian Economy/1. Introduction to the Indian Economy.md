# Introduction to the Indian Economy

India is a country known for its rich cultural diversity, being the largest democracy globally and demonstrating unity despite variations in languages, castes, religions, cultures, and traditions. This gives India a unique identity.

Historically, India was named 'Bharat' after the son of King Dushyant and Queen Shakuntala. The country was highly prosperous, recognized for its agriculture, traditional crafts, and trade, which attracted foreigners.

However, foreigners who initially came for trade began ruling India to exploit its rich resources, damaging its ancient economic and cultural heritage. The Mughals ruled parts or the whole nation between 1526 and 1858. The French, Portuguese, and Dutch also held sway over some areas temporarily.

The British established their rule from approximately 1757 to 1947. The period from 1757 to 1858 is known as the rule of the East India Company, which came for trade but established political control. From 1858 until August 14, 1947, the British government directly ruled India.
