# Indian Economy after Independence

Immediately after independence, India faced significant challenges for development. Today, India exhibits characteristics of a **developing country**, showing both areas of lower development and significant improvement.

## Per Capita Income

According to the Human Development Report 2014, India's per capita income (PCI) in 2013 at purchasing power parity was $5,150. This was lower than developed countries and also many developing countries like China ($11,477) and Sri Lanka ($9,250).

## Agriculture

India remains an **agrarian nation**. While the share of the agricultural sector in employment has decreased, it is still relatively high compared to developed nations, indicating lower economic development. Developed countries typically have a smaller percentage of the workforce in agriculture and higher percentages in industrial and service sectors.

*   At independence: 72% dependent on agriculture.
*   2001-02: 58% dependent on agriculture.
*   2013-14: 49% dependent on agriculture (Human Development Report).

## Industry

The industrial sector's contribution to employment and national income has grown, but India is not yet considered a fully industrialized nation by development criteria.

*   Share in employment: 10.6% (1950-51) -> 18.2% (2001) -> 24.3% (2011-12).
*   Share in national income: 16.6% (1950-51) -> 26% (2013-14) (Source: CSO).

The industrial sector also contributes significantly to export earnings (2/3rd in present times). Despite progress, India is not fully industrialized.

## Service Sector

The service sector's share in employment and national income has increased significantly, indicating progress towards development.

*   Share in employment: 17.3% (1951) -> 25.2% (2001) -> 27% (2011-12).
*   Share in national income (GDP): 30.3% (1951) -> 38% (1980-81) -> 50.4% (2000-01) -> 52.7% (2014-15).

The growing share of the service sector shows India moving quickly on the path of growth and development.

## Population Growth

A high population growth rate has been a characteristic of India's lower development. The average annual increase after independence has been around 1.5%, signifying **population explosion**.

*   1901: 23.84 crores
*   1951: 36.1 crores
*   2001: 102.7 crores
*   2011: 121.02 crores

This high growth rate remains a concern.

## Poverty

Indian economy is characterized by high levels of **absolute poverty**. While the percentage of the population below the poverty line has decreased, it is still considered high.

*   1973-74: 54.9% below poverty line.
*   1993-94: 45.3% below poverty line.
*   2004-05: 37.2% below poverty line.
*   2011-12: 21.9% below poverty line.

## Unemployment

**Structural unemployment** constitutes a significant portion of unemployment in India.

*   1951: 33 lakh unemployed.
*   NSSO data: 7.31% (1999-2000) -> 8.2% (2004-05) -> 6.6% (2009-10) -> 5.6% (2011-12) of total population unemployed.

In rural areas, **disguised unemployment** is higher, which is difficult to measure precisely.

## Human Development

India ranks lower on various human development indices, measured by indicators like average life expectancy, literacy, gender ratios, and infant mortality rates. The **Human Development Index (HDI)** shows India's low ranking.

*   HDI in 2000: 0.463
*   HDI in 2010: 0.547
*   HDI in 2012: 0.554
*   HDI in 2013: 0.586

These HDI values are considered low. In 2013, India ranked 136th out of 187 countries, indicating a very low position.
