This section describes the economic characteristics of India during the ancient period, focusing on its occupational structure and overall prosperity before the advent of foreign rule.

# Ancient Indian Economy

The ancient Indian civilization, influenced by the Aryan tribe and the Indus Valley (Harappan) civilization, shows evidence of organization, prosperity, urban planning, and development. Important sites include Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, and Dholavira.

From a manufacturing perspective, India was known for products like cloth, muslin, jute, and indigo even before British times. The British introduced tea plantations, and India remains a significant tea exporter. Prior to the British period, India was generally considered rich in agriculture, industry, and civilization.

Let's examine some key economic aspects of ancient and pre-British India:

## Occupational Structure

Ancient India was primarily an **agricultural nation** with a predominantly rural economy. However, basic industries also existed, and both the agricultural and industrial sectors were prosperous.

### Agriculture

The agricultural sector was prosperous, growing a variety of crops. Villages were **self-reliant**, producing basic necessities like grains, vegetables, fruits, clothes, and shoes. Cattle rearing and dairy farming were also common. This contributed to a happy village life.

### Industries

According to historian Rai Chaudhary, before the 19th century, India was a major manufacturing center for items like:

*   Cotton
*   Jute
*   Muslin
*   Wool
*   Idols
*   Indigo
*   Terracotta
*   Earthenware

Some of these goods were also exported.

### Services

Even before 600 B.C., India had systems for:

*   Minting metal coins
*   Advanced town planning and sewage systems
*   A stable political system
*   Unity among people

These factors facilitated prosperous trade in agricultural and manufactured goods, with evidence found at sites like Lothal.

## National Income/Economic Prosperity

Historian Angus Maddison suggests that India was extremely prosperous during the Maurya dynasty, holding about **25% of the world's wealth**. Most of present-day India's geographical area was under one rule then.

During the Mughal rule, India commanded almost 90% of the wealth in the South Asian region, generating high taxes and excise duties. Around 1700 A.D. during Aurangzeb's rule, India's national income was estimated at 10 crore Pounds. Foreigners were primarily attracted to India by its riches.

The ancient age is often considered a **golden age** for India's economy.
