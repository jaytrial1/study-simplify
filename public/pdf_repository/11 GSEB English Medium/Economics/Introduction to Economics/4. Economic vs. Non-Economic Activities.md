This section differentiates between activities that fall under the purview of economics (economic activities) and those that do not (non-economic activities), explaining the key characteristics that distinguish them, primarily the purpose of obtaining economic benefits and involvement of exchange and cost.

# Economic Activity and Non-Economic Activity

Human beings perform many activities daily, but not all are the subject matter of economics. Economics studies only **economic activities**.

**Meaning of Economic Activity:**
An activity performed to get **economic benefits** (incomes), involving **exchange** of goods, services, and/or factors, and involving **costs**. Key aspects are the purpose of making economic gains, satisfying a want, and involving exchange and cost.

*   **Examples of economic activities:**
    *   Eating in a restaurant (satisfy need by incurring expense).
    *   Watching a movie in a theatre (incur expense for entertainment).
    *   Working on one's own farm (exert labour and get output).
    *   Being employed to teach (provide service and get salary).

An activity fulfilling a requirement through a transaction with costs and benefits on both sides is an economic activity. Farmers, lawyers, teachers, actors, and governments all perform economic activities.

**Meaning of Non-Economic Activity:**
An activity performed **without the specific purpose of obtaining economic gains**. It does not involve exchange of benefits on both sides. Examples include charitable activity or activities done out of love, affection, or compassion. These involve costs but not for monetary/economic benefits in exchange.
