This section highlights the growing relevance and importance of economics in the modern world. It explains how economics helps in understanding everyday behaviour, interpreting international and historical events, making personal and professional decisions, and comprehending government policies.

# Importance of Economics

In the modern world, most human needs have become **economic goods** due to increasing use and scarcity, making human activities increasingly **economic activities**. Hence, the relevance of economics as a philosophy and science is growing. Economics is important for understanding human, societal, and state behaviour in managing daily activities, and it provides scientific theories and principles for professional decision making.

# Understanding Everyday Behaviour

Economics helps in understanding everyday behaviour of humans, society, and state while managing routine tasks.

*   **Understanding International Events:** Economics helps understand the impact of international events (like crude oil price changes) on daily life and why some countries are more developed than others. With increasing information availability, the scope of economics in everyday life expands.
*   **Understanding Historical Events:** Knowledge of economics helps understand historical events better, such as reasons for wars, colonial trade motives (like East India Company in India), resistance movements (like revolt against British goods), and labour movements.

# Economic Importance

*   **Decision Making by Individuals:** Individuals (households, lawyers, actors, etc.) try to maximize gains from their efforts and manage resources optimally. They engage in economic decision making, consciously or unconsciously.
*   **Understanding Government Policies:** Knowledge of economics is useful for understanding state policies like tax policy, wage policy, etc. People make decisions based on central bank rates (like REPO and Reverse REPO rates) which guide saving and investment decisions.

# Professional Decision Making

Economics provides scientific principles and theories guiding professional decisions. Concepts like revenue, cost, demand, and supply are used in all economic activities. Economic theories help businesses with daily decisions on pricing, wage determination, employing factors of production, etc. Economics offers insights into the impact of decisions, e.g., what happens if price is reduced in a highly competitive market.

Economics is a science providing insights for day-to-day decision making. Humans act as consumers, producers, or labour. Economics helps them make rational decisions to maximize gains by minimizing costs.

5. Answer the following questions in detail :
(1) Give an idea of the development of economics as a science in the west.
> **Answer:** The development of economics as a science in the West evolved from philosophical views, such as those by Aristotle, to a distinct field. A major turning point was the Industrial Revolution, which introduced specialization not only in industry but also in knowledge. Social sciences, including economics, began to develop as specialized branches using scientific tools and methods, moving beyond purely descriptive philosophy. Adam Smith is considered a key figure in this development with his 1776 book "Wealth of Nations," which focused on the nature and causes of wealth. Smith's work established economics as an independent social science, studying human efforts scientifically. Subsequent economists like Alfred Marshall broadened the scope to include human welfare, while Lionel Robbins emphasized the problem of scarcity and choice. Paul Samuelson further refined the definition to include the dynamic process of allocating scarce resources over time and analyzing costs and benefits. This progression shows economics evolving from a study of wealth to a comprehensive science of human behavior in the face of scarcity and choice.
(2) 'Graphs/diagrams are a better method of precisely presenting economic information.' Explain.
> **Answer:** Graphs and diagrams are often a better method for presenting economic information precisely because they offer a visual representation of data and relationships that can be quickly and easily understood. Unlike dense descriptive text or large data tables, diagrams like bar charts, grouped bar charts, and pie charts can immediately convey trends, comparisons, and proportions. For example, a bar graph showing production over years makes the trend visually apparent, while a pie chart clearly shows the relative share of different sectors in national income. Graphs can effectively illustrate cause-effect relationships, such as the inverse relationship between price and demand shown by a demand curve. While descriptive text explains concepts, and tables provide exact numbers, diagrams translate complex numerical data into easily digestible visual patterns, making economic information more accessible and impactful for both economists and the general public, thus offering a precise and clear picture of economic facts and trends.
