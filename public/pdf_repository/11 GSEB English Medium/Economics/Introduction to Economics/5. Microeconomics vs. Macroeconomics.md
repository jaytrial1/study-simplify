This section explains the fundamental division of economic analysis into two main branches: Microeconomics and Macroeconomics. It describes the scope and focus of each branch, highlighting that microeconomics studies individual economic units while macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole.

# Microeconomics and Macroeconomics

Economic analysis is classified into two branches:
1.  **Microeconomics**
2.  **Macroeconomics**

**Microeconomics** is the study/analysis of **individual units** of the economy. It studies the rational behaviour of individual units. Microeconomic analysis uses the principle of **'marginalism'** to analyze individual decision-making.

*   **Examples of microeconomic studies:**
    *   Behaviour of a consumer
    *   Behaviour of a firm
    *   Labour market analysis
    *   Price determination of a product in a market
    *   Equilibrium output level for a firm
    *   Marginal productivity and wage determination of labour

Microeconomics has scientific principles showing the behaviour of individual units.

**Macroeconomics** studies issues that emerge from and impact the **entire economic setup**.

*   **Examples of macroeconomic studies:**
    *   Determination of national income
    *   Unemployment
    *   Poverty
    *   Growth rate
    *   Demographic profile of population

Macroeconomic analysis helps provide principles for resource management to increase national income, reduce unemployment, poverty, inflation, etc.

The behaviour of individual units impacts macro parameters, and macro parameters impact individual unit decisions.
