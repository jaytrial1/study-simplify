This section presents key definitions of economics provided by prominent economists like Adam Smith, Alfred Marshall, Lionel Robbins, and Paul Samuelson, highlighting the evolution of the definition from wealth-centric to focusing on human welfare, scarcity, choice, and resource allocation.

# Definitions of Economics

**Definition of Economics by Adam Smith:**
"Economics is the study of the nature and causes of wealth of nations". This definition focuses on the exchange of physical wealth produced by labour.

**Definition of Economics by Alfred Marshall:**
In **"Principles of Economics"** (1890), Marshall defines, "Economics is the study of mankind in the ordinary business of life". This implies studying everyday activities where people seek material wellbeing. It's narrow as it focuses only on material consumption but important for centering human wellbeing.

**Definition of Economics by Lionel Robbins:**
Economics addresses the problem: "how to allocate limited resources which have alternative uses in the satisfaction of recurring and unlimited human wants in order to increase the wellbeing in a society"?
In **"Nature and Significance of Economics"** (1931), Robbins defined, "Economics is the science which studies human behaviour as a relationship between ends and scarce means which have alternative uses".
Human wants are unlimited and recurring, while resources are limited and have alternative uses. Economics deals with allocating scarce resources to satisfy wants maximally. Robbins viewed economics as a **positive science**, studying 'how human beings behave', not 'how they should ideally behave' (not a normative science).

**Definition of Economics by Paul Samuelson:**
In **'Foundations of Economic Analysis'** (1947), Samuelson defines, "Economics is the study of how people and society end up choosing, with or without the use of money, to employ scarce productive resources that could have alternative uses to produce various commodities over time and distributing them for consumption, now or in the future, among various persons in the society. It analyses costs and benefits of improving patterns of resource allocation". Samuelson emphasizes **choice, allocation of scarce resources, and evaluating costs and benefits**.

Commonly, these definitions suggest economics studies human behaviour, is positive, uses scientific methods, and is a social science.
