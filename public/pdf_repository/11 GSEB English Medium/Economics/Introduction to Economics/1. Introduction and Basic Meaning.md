This section introduces economics as a field of study that helps understand human behavior in managing resources and resolving complexities in social, political, and economic systems. It traces the origins of the word 'economics' from both Indian ('arthshashtra') and Greek ('Oikonomos') perspectives, highlighting its core meaning related to purpose, benefit, wealth, and household management.

# Introduction

Human beings, being intellectual creatures, analyze their surroundings to ensure happy living. They have created various institutions like social, political, and economic systems. To understand, sustain, and resolve complexities within these systems, principles based on philosophies and sciences were formulated.

In earlier times, differences were resolved through conflict. However, with civilization's progress, logical resolution through philosophical principles became possible. Economics developed as a philosophy and science of rational economic behaviour.

# Meaning of Economics

In Indian thought, the word for economics is **'arthshashtra'**, derived from the Sanskrit word **'arth'** meaning 'purpose' or 'goal'. Thus, 'arthshashtra' means "the science of benefits or purpose in practical life," describing the purpose of human behaviour as seeking benefits.

The English word **economics** comes from the Greek word **'Oikonomos'**, where **'Oikos'** means households and **'nomos'** means management. Hence, economics means **'management of households'**.
