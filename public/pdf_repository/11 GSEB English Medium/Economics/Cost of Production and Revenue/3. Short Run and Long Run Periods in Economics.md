This section explains the distinction between short-run and long-run periods in economics based on the flexibility of changing factors of production and their associated costs.

# Short Term and Long Term

In economics, time periods are classified based on the ability of a firm to change its factors of production.

## Short Term

A *short term* is a period in which a producer cannot change the *size of the firm* (like the factory building or heavy machinery) but can increase production by changing the quantity of *variable factors*. In the short term, some factors of production are *fixed* (e.g., plant, heavy machinery, factory building), while others are *variable* (e.g., raw material, labor, electricity). Production can be increased or decreased by adjusting only the variable factors. Expenditure on fixed factors is *fixed cost*, and expenditure on variable factors is *variable cost*.

## Long Term

A *long term* is a period in which a producer can change *all* factors of production. In this period, all factors are considered *variable*. The producer can change the size of the firm, install new machinery, build new factories, or adjust any other factor to change total production significantly. Firms can also adopt new and modern technology in the long run.
