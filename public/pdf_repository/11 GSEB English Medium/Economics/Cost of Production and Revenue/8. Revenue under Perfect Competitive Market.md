# Total Revenue, Average Revenue and Marginal Revenue under Perfect Competitive Market

A *perfectly competitive market* is one where a firm is a *price taker* and sells its commodity at the market-determined price. In this market structure, commodities are homogeneous, there are many buyers and sellers, and both have complete market knowledge. Price is determined by market demand and supply, and individual firms cannot influence this price. Therefore, the price is fixed and constant for an individual firm.

In a perfectly competitive market, *Price = Average Revenue = Marginal Revenue (P = AR = MR)*. If the market price is ₹50, the firm's Average Revenue and Marginal Revenue are also ₹50, regardless of the quantity sold by that firm. Therefore, the firm's Average Revenue and Marginal Revenue curves are the same and are a horizontal line parallel to the X-axis, often represented by the demand curve (DD) for the individual firm.

In the diagram, the revenue curves of a firm in perfect competition are shown.
1.  In a perfectly competitive market, a firm's Marginal Revenue and Average Revenue are constant and equal, so they are represented by a single horizontal line (DD). All points on this line show AR = MR = P.
2.  The Average Revenue and Marginal Revenue curve (DD) is parallel to the X-axis, indicating that the price is constant regardless of the quantity sold by the firm. The slope of this curve is zero.
3.  The Total Revenue curve is an upward-sloping straight line from the origin (often shown at a 45° angle if scales are equal). This indicates that total revenue increases at a constant rate as the sale of goods increases, because the price per unit is constant. The slope of the total revenue curve is positive and constant.
