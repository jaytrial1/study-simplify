# Inter-relationship between Average Cost and Marginal Cost

The relationship between average cost (AC) and marginal cost (MC) is important in the study of production cost. AC is the cost per unit of output, while MC is the cost added by producing one extra unit. Producers use these concepts for decision-making: in the long run, a firm might continue production if price is above AC; in the short run, if price is above AVC (though the text mentions MC, AC is typically compared to price for shutdown decisions). AC and MC play important roles in production decisions.

Let's explain the relationship with the help of a table and diagram.

| Output (Units) | Total Cost (₹) (TC) | Average Cost (₹) (AC) | Marginal Cost (₹) (MC) |
| :------------: | :-----------------: | :-------------------: | :--------------------: |
| 1              | 20                  | 20                    | -                      |
| 2              | 35                  | 17.5                  | 15                     |
| 3              | 45                  | 15                    | 10                     |
| 4              | 60                  | 15                    | 15                     |
| 5              | 85                  | 17                    | 25                     |
| 6              | 115                 | 19.2                  | 30                     |
| 7              | 150                 | 21.5                  | 35                     |

As shown in the schedule, as output increases, initially both Average Cost and Marginal Cost decrease, often because of increasing returns. At the 4th unit of output, Average Cost and Marginal Cost are equal (₹15), and AC is at its minimum (or begins to rise after this point). After this point, due to decreasing returns, both AC and MC increase.

## Relation:

### Marginal Cost < Average Cost (MC < AC)

Initially, both average cost and marginal cost decrease. However, marginal cost decreases more rapidly than average cost. Therefore, when the marginal cost curve is decreasing, it remains below the average cost curve. When MC < AC, producing an additional unit reduces the average cost.

### Marginal Cost = Average Cost (MC = AC)

When Average Cost is at its minimum point, the Marginal Cost curve intersects the Average Cost curve from below. At this point, Marginal Cost and Average Cost are equal (AC = MC). This point represents the efficient scale of production where average cost is minimized.

### Marginal Cost > Average Cost (MC > AC)

After the point where the Marginal Cost curve intersects the Average Cost curve, both costs start to increase. However, the increase in MC is more rapid than the increase in Average Cost. Therefore, the Marginal Cost curve lies above the Average Cost curve. When MC > AC, producing an additional unit increases the average cost.
