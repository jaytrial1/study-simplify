This section details the different categories of costs a firm faces in the short run, including fixed costs, variable costs, total costs, and their average counterparts.

## Fixed Cost

In the short period, *fixed cost* is the cost that does *not change* regardless of whether production increases, decreases, or remains zero. Fixed cost is also known as *overhead cost*. In the short period, there is no relation between fixed cost and the quantity of production. Examples include permanent staff's salary, rent of factory building, property tax, license fees, interest on capital, and insurance premiums.

## Variable Cost

*Variable cost* is the cost incurred on variable factors of production. In the short term, this cost *changes* with the change in the quantity of production. As production increases, variable cost increases; as production decreases, variable cost decreases. If production is zero, variable cost is also zero. Variable cost is also known as unstable, direct, or main cost. It has a direct (positive) relation with the quantity of production. Examples include the price of raw material, energy consumption, transportation expenditure, labor wages, and taxes on product and sale.

As production increases, this cost increases, hence it is called variable cost. The distinction between fixed cost and variable cost is possible only in the short period. In the long run, all costs are variable.

When production is zero, variable cost is zero, but as production increases, variable cost also increases. From the table below, we can see that up to 30 units, variable cost increases at a diminishing rate because increasing returns are applicable. After 30 units, decreasing returns are applicable, causing variable cost to increase at an increasing rate.

## Total Cost

There is a cause-and-effect relationship between total production and total cost. If total production is more, total cost is also more. The sum of total fixed cost and total variable cost is equal to *total cost*.

$$
\mathrm{TC}=\mathrm{TFC}+\mathrm{TVC}
$$

With the increase in production, fixed cost remains fixed, but variable cost increases. Total cost increases as the amount of variable cost increases. Therefore, the TC curve is above the TVC curve. The difference between TC and TVC is constant (equal to TFC), which is why the TC and TVC curves are parallel to each other.

## Average Fixed Cost

*Average Fixed Cost (AFC)* is the fixed cost per unit of output. It is calculated by dividing the total fixed cost by the total production units.

$$
\text { Average Fixed Cost } = \frac{\text { Total Fixed Cost }}{\text { Total Production Unit }}
$$

$$
\mathrm{AFC}=\frac{\mathrm{TFC}}{\mathrm{TP}}
$$

Where:
AFC = Average Fixed Cost
TFC = Total Fixed Cost
TP = Total Production

Example: If a company's total fixed cost is ₹50,000 and it produces 1000 units, the average fixed cost is:
$$
\mathrm{AFC}=\frac{50000}{1000}=₹ 50
$$

When production increases, the total fixed cost is spread over more units, so the average fixed cost decreases. Therefore, the average fixed cost curve is a *downward sloping curve*.

## Average Variable Cost

*Average Variable Cost (AVC)* is the variable cost per unit of output. It is calculated by dividing the total variable cost by the total units produced. This concept is useful for decisions regarding continuing production, increasing production, or shutting down.

$$
\text { Average Variable Cost } = \frac{\text { Total Variable Cost }}{\text { Total Production (Units of Production) }}
$$

$$
\mathrm{AVC}=\frac{\mathrm{TVC}}{\mathrm{TP}}
$$

Where:
AVC = Average Variable Cost
TVC = Total Variable Cost
TP = Total Production

Example: Suppose a firm's total variable cost is ₹150 and it produces 20 units of output, then:
$$
\mathrm{AVC}=\frac{150}{20}=₹ 7.5
$$

As output increases, average variable cost initially decreases, then reaches a minimum, and after that, it increases with further increases in production. This relationship is due to the law of variable proportions (or returns to scale in some contexts).

## Average Cost

*Average Cost (AC)*, also known as *Average Total Cost (ATC)*, is the total cost per unit of production. It is found by dividing the total cost by the units of production. Since total cost is the sum of total fixed cost and total variable cost, average cost can also be calculated by dividing the sum of fixed cost and variable cost by total production units.

$$
\text { Average Cost } = \frac{\text { Total Cost }}{\text { Total Production (Units of Production) }}
$$

OR

$$
\text { Average Cost } = \frac{\text { Total Fixed Cost } + \text { Total Variable Cost }}{\text { Total Production (Units of Production) }}
$$

$$
\mathrm{AC}=\frac{\mathrm{TC}}{\mathrm{TP}}
$$

Where:
AC = Average Cost
TC = Total Cost
TP = Total Production

## Marginal Cost

### Meaning

*Marginal cost (MC)* is the change in total cost that results from producing one additional unit of output. When production increases or decreases by one unit, the change in total cost is the marginal cost.

### Formula and Example

Marginal cost (MC) of the *n*th unit is the difference between the total cost of *n* units and the total cost of *(n-1)* units.

$$
\mathrm{MC}_{n} = \mathrm{TC}_{n} - \mathrm{TC}_{(n-1)}
$$

Where:
$n$ = Number of units
$\mathrm{MC}_{n}$ = Marginal cost of $n$ units of output
$\mathrm{TC}_{n}$ = Total cost of $n$ units of output
$\mathrm{TC}_{(n-1)}$ = Total cost of $(n-1)$ units of output

Using the example table for Average Cost:
For $n=3$:
$$
\mathrm{MC}_{3} = \mathrm{TC}_{3} - \mathrm{TC}_{(3-1)} = \mathrm{TC}_{3} - \mathrm{TC}_{2}
$$
Given $\mathrm{TC}_{3} = 21$ and $\mathrm{TC}_{2} = 16$:
$$
\mathrm{MC}_{3} = 21 - 16 = ₹5
$$
The marginal cost of the 3rd unit is ₹5.

It is important to note that marginal cost is independent of fixed cost. Changes in total cost due to a change in output are solely due to changes in variable cost. Therefore, marginal cost is also the change in total variable cost when one more unit is produced.

| Output (Units) | Total Cost (TC) (in ₹) | Marginal Cost (MC) (in ₹) |
| :------------: | :--------------------: | :-----------------------: |
| 1              | 09                     | 09                        |
| 2              | 16                     | 07                        |
| 3              | 21                     | 05                        |
| 4              | 28                     | 07                        |
| 5              | 40                     | 12                        |
| 6              | 54                     | 14                        |
| 7              | 70                     | 16                        |

From the table, marginal cost decreases up to the 3rd unit. At the 3rd unit, marginal cost is minimum (₹5). After that, marginal cost continuously increases. This behavior can be shown in a diagram.
