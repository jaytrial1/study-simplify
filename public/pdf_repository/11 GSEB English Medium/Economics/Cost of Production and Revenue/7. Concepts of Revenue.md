This section introduces the concepts of revenue for a firm, explaining total revenue, average revenue, and marginal revenue.

# Concepts of Revenue

In a capitalist market structure, the main objective of production is to maximize profit. Therefore, the concept of *revenue* is significant. Profits occur when a firm's total revenue is higher than its total cost. Losses occur if total revenue is lower than total cost. While total revenue and total cost determine overall profit, the concepts of average revenue and marginal revenue are often used in short-run analysis.

## Total Revenue

*Total Revenue (TR)* is the total income received by a firm from the sale of its produced units. This income is also known as sale revenue. A firm's total revenue depends on two things: (1) *Price per unit* and (2) *Total sale quantity*. Changes in either or both factors change the firm's revenue.

Total Revenue = Units Sold × Price of Commodity

$$
\mathrm{TR} = \mathrm{Q} \times \mathrm{P}
$$

Example: If a firm produces pens, the market price per unit is ₹50, and the total sale is 100 units, the total revenue is:
$$
\mathrm{TR} = 100 \times 50 = ₹5000
$$
If the sale unit of the commodity increases or decreases, or the price increases or decreases, or both change, the firm's total revenue changes.

## Average Revenue

*Average Revenue (AR)* is the revenue per unit of output sold. It is found by dividing the total revenue by the total units sold.

$$
\text { Average Revenue } = \frac{\text { Total Revenue }}{\text { Total Sale }}
$$

$$
\mathrm{AR}=\frac{\mathrm{TR}}{\mathrm{Q}}
$$

Where:
AR = Average Revenue
TR = Total Revenue
Q = Quantity Sold

Example: If a firm sells 1000 units of pens and the total revenue is ₹50,000, the average revenue is:
$$
\mathrm{AR}=\frac{50000}{1000}=₹ 50
$$
This is the revenue per unit of pen. Normally, firms sell all units at the same price, so Average Revenue is equal to the price (AR = P). When price and Average Revenue are equal, the firm's Average Revenue curve is also its *demand curve* from the perspective of the consumer. The demand curve shows how many commodities consumers are willing to purchase at different prices. The Average Revenue curve shows the average revenue a producer receives from the sale of a commodity. In short, Average Revenue from the producer's perspective is the price from the consumer's perspective.

## Marginal Revenue

*Marginal Revenue (MR)* is the additional revenue generated from the sale of one additional unit of a commodity. It is the change in total revenue resulting from selling one more unit.

Example: A firm receives ₹50,000 by selling 1000 units of pens. If the firm sells 1001 units, and the total revenue increases from ₹50,000 to ₹50,045, the increase of ₹45 is the Marginal Revenue.

Marginal Revenue is the change in total revenue on account of the sale of an additional unit of output.

$$
\mathrm{MR}_{n} = \mathrm{R}_{n} - \mathrm{R}_{(n-1)}
$$

Where:
MR = Marginal Revenue
$n$ = Number of sold units
$\mathrm{R}_{n}$ = Revenue from the sale of $n$ units of commodity
$\mathrm{R}_{(n-1)}$ = Revenue from the sale of $(n-1)$ units

Using the example:
$n = 1001$, $(n-1) = 1000$
$\mathrm{R}_{1001} = ₹50,045$, $\mathrm{R}_{1000} = ₹50,000$
$$
\mathrm{MR}_{1001} = \mathrm{R}_{1001} - \mathrm{R}_{1000} = ₹50,045 - ₹50,000 = ₹45
$$
The Marginal Revenue from the 1001st unit is ₹45.
