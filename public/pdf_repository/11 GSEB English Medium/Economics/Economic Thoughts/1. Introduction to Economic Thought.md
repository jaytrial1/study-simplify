# Introduction to Economic Thought

**Economic thought** refers to the ideas related to human life and economic activities such as the management of economy, production, revenue, distribution, employment, money, exchange, economic development, banking, and solutions to financial and environmental problems.

Throughout history, great thinkers have influenced state decisions regarding resource management, production, cost, and distribution.
*   **Adam Smith's** ideas focus on the creation, distribution, and exchange of wealth.
*   **Prof. Alfred Marshall** emphasized keeping human welfare central to decision-making.
*   **Prof. Robbins** explored concepts of scarcity, the problem of choice, and the optimum utilization of resources.
*   **Prof. Keynes** provided new directions for macroeconomics with principles on consumption, investment, employment, and government intervention, particularly after the Great Depression.

These individuals contributed significantly to modern Western economic thought.

Economic ideology is also reflected in Indian scriptures and ancient writings, such as:
*   'Shantiparva' in 'Mahabharat'
*   'Manusmruti'
*   'Shukra-niti'
*   'Kamandikiya'

Among Indian writings, **'Kautilya's Arthshashtra'** is considered foremost. Indian economic thought has been influenced by thinkers from ancient times to the present day. This chapter explores the economic ideas of Kautilya, Gandhiji, and Pandit Deendayal Upadhyaya.
