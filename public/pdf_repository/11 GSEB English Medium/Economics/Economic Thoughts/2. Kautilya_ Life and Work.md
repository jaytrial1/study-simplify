This section introduces Kautilya (also known as Chanakya or Vishnu Gupta), the author of 'Arthshashtra', detailing his background and his role in establishing a prosperous state.

# Kautilya: Life and Work

Kautilya, the author of **'Arthshashtra'**, was a strategist and scholar who envisioned a perfect state. His real name was Vishnu Gupta, and he was born the son of a Brahmin named Chanak. During the third century B.C., Kautilya, through his moral values and strategy, aided Chandra Gupta in ending the rule of Dhanananda, the last king of the Nanda dynasty, to create a prosperous state.

Kautilya minutely studied subjects including Politics, law, economics, management, taxation, social order, trade, agriculture, and industry. He compiled his extensive knowledge into a unique text known as **'Kautilya's Arthshashtra'**.

The idea of state management is deeply rooted in Indian culture, with ancient literature providing insights into politics, diplomacy, policy-making, governance rules, and 'dandniti' (policy of state punishment). Kautilya's 'Arthshashtra' is one such significant work. Discovered by Pandit Shyam Shashtri, this volume was written on 'Bhojapatra' in 'Granthlipi'. Pandit Shyam Shashtri compiled and published it in 1909, dating its creation to around 321-300 B.C.

Kautilya's thoughts are centered on the concept of **'arth'**. He believed that 'arth' (resources, wealth, economic activity) is the key to governance and authority. According to him, even a person without resources can obtain 'arth' through **labour**. He considered manual labour as the true 'arth', explaining:
*   "The intention of a human being is 'arth'."
*   "The piece of land with human settlement is 'arth'."
*   "The science that gives solution pertaining to maintenance of land and attaining benefits from land is 'arthshashtra'."

Thus, Kautilya considered land and human settlement as assets for livelihood and deemed it important to describe the science related to their maintenance and benefits. His 'Arthshashtra' is applicable to the state's role across various periods and is essentially a **science of policy making**. His 15-volume work covers strategies for domestic administration, relations with neighboring states, knowledge about medicinal plants, and political diplomacy.
