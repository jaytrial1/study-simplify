This section introduces Mahatma Gandhi, highlighting his role as a reformer, his core principles of truth and non-violence, and the significant influences on his economic, social, and political ideology.

# Gandhiji: Background and Influences

Societies undergo continuous change for development, often guided by reformers. **Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi**, known as Gandhiji, was such a leader. Born in Porbandar, Gujarat, he possessed strong will power and idealism but advocated for a realistic and practical approach. His ideology was fundamentally based on **Truth and Non-violence**. He strived to align his thoughts and actions and emphasized the importance of religion and ethics in individual and social life.

Gandhiji worked tirelessly to eradicate untouchability, discourage alcohol consumption, achieve communal harmony, and empower women. His ethical way of living formed the basis for his views on politics and economics.

His simple life and ideas are often referred to as 'Gandhism'. However, Gandhiji did not present a specific, classified economic ideology. He did not wish to create 'Gandhism' or have followers. He saw himself as applying eternal truths to solve everyday problems rather than discovering new theories.

## The Influence of Books and Thinkers on Gandhiji

Gandhiji's economic, social, and political ideology was shaped by religious books, philosophers, and events:
*   **Henry David Thoreau (American thinker):** Influenced Gandhiji's idea of **'Simple living and high thinking'**.
*   **John Ruskin (British writer):** His book **'Unto This Last'** inspired Gandhiji's concept of **'Sarvodaya'** (upliftment of all). From this book, he also derived the importance of labour and ideas for eradicating poverty in India.
*   **Leo Tolstoy:** His works **'What Shall We Do Then?'** and **'The Kingdom of God is Within You'** influenced Gandhiji.
*   **Ruskin's theory of 'bread labour':** Influenced Gandhiji's motto that those who do not work have no right to eat.
*   **Play 'Satyawadi Raja Harishchandra':** As a child, watching this play left a lasting impression, teaching him the importance of truth and shaping him into a 'Satyagrahi' (one who practices truth force).
*   **Bhagwad Gita (Hindu Scripture):** A shloka in the third chapter reinforced his belief in 'labouring for one's own bread', stating that one who enjoys fruits without effort and sharing is sinful.
*   **Other influences:** Scriptures like the 'Upnishads', 'Ramayana', 'Mahabharata', and saint poets like Kabir, Gurunanak, Narsimha Mehta, and spiritual leader Shrimad Rajchandra also impacted his ideas.
