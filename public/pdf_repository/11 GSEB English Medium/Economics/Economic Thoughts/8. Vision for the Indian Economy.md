This section summarizes Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay's proposed objectives for the Indian economy, offering an alternative path focused on human progress and happiness, distinct from purely capitalist or socialist models.

# Vision for the Indian Economy

Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay believed that capitalism and socialism failed to fully understand human beings and their problems. Therefore, he argued that India needs neither of these systems but rather **'progress and happiness of her people'**. He outlined the following objectives for the Indian economy:

*   Ensure a **minimum standard of living** for all.
*   Make **national security** an important goal.
*   Achieve gradual progress, devising methods that allow India to contribute to world development in its own unique way.
*   Provide **work opportunities** for all young and able people to attain the goal of development.
*   Use **natural resources prudently**.
*   Adopt and devise production processes considering the availability of factors of production.
*   Ensure that human beings are **not neglected** in economic planning; planning must consider the social, cultural, and other values of human life.
*   Make practical decisions regarding ownership in various sectors (state, private, or other organizations).

Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay also contributed ideas for the development of agriculture, industry, marketing, rural economics, and the concept of 'Swadeshi'. Schemes based on his ideology have been implemented in various states. The 'Shramave Jayate' plan (October 16, 2014), based on his theory of labour importance and employment for all, was introduced in India. The 'Gram Jyoti Yojana' was implemented for agro and rural industry development.

His economic thoughts continue to offer potential solutions to contemporary economic problems.
