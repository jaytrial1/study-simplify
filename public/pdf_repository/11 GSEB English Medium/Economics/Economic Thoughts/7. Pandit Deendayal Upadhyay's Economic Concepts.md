This section explores the main economic thoughts of Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay, including his search for a 'Third Option' beyond capitalism and socialism, his philosophy of Integral Humanism, views on resource use, ownership of assets, decentralized economy, limiting consumption, and promoting labour-intensive production methods.

# Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay's Economic Concepts

Pandit Deendayal Upadhyay offered a unique perspective on economic challenges, particularly for India.

## The Search for the Third Option

Observing the world's prevalent economic systems, capitalism and socialism, Pandit Deendayal recognized their advancements in production and technology but also their inherent problems like exploitation, inequality, instability, class struggle, and pollution. He noted that India, by adopting Western systems, faced similar issues despite economic planning, such as low productivity, insufficient rural infrastructure, poverty, unemployment, inflation, excessive urbanization, pollution, and currency devaluation.

To overcome these, he proposed a **'third option'**: an **'integral economic policy'** based on **'integral humanism'**, as an alternative to both capitalism and socialism.

## Integral Humanism

Pandit Deendayal is a key proponent of **Integral Humanism**, which he advocated as the basis for economic policies. He saw Integral Humanism as representing Indian culture. Through this idea, he presented thoughts on 'Integral Economics'.

Integral Humanism views a human being not merely as an 'economic human being' but as a **composite of all human characteristics**. It is an ideology that considers the mutual relationship between humans and with the world as crucial for a happy and prosperous life.

Achieving Integral Humanism involves:
*   Serving the uninformed, ignorant, and deprived members of society.
*   Making them self-sufficient through technical education and skill development.
*   Enabling them to increase their income and providing better housing.

He aimed to select successful policies from the West that suited the nature of the Indian economy and could contribute to the country's overall development.

## An Insight into what is Attainable and the use of Resources

Pandit Deendayal felt that post-independence India adopted a purely economic perspective, losing the nationalist perspective that previously guided solutions to public life problems. He attributed this to a loss of insight into the difference between what is attainable and the factors needed to attain it.

He argued that without defining the goals of human life and understanding the place of wealth within it, society cannot choose the right factors for achieving those goals. Therefore, **economic development must be seen as only a part of the overall development of human life**. The ultimate goal is happiness, for which wealth is sought, but wealth requires human effort (labour). A society cannot develop if labour is unemployed. Production processes and economic policies (monetary, etc.) should prioritize the overall development of human beings and ensure work for all.

## Ownership of Assets

Regarding wealth ownership, Pandit Deendayal examined capitalism (private ownership without state control) and socialism (abolition of private property as the source of problems). He argued against the complete abolition of private ownership, stating it would lead to:
*   Loss of incentive and willingness to work.
*   Loss of dignity for enterprising individuals.
*   Reduced safety and satisfaction.

While allowing private wealth ownership, he stressed the need for **limits** based on human values. These limits should prevent the possession or deprivation of private property from causing devastation for individuals. He believed uncontrolled possession and self-willed use of property damage the ethical and intellectual character of society. Therefore, he suggested law, social governance, and absolute decentralization of power in setting these limits. He emphasized that assets and wealth should be used for human development.

## Decentralized Economy

Centralization of authority and assets, present in both capitalism and socialism, hinders overall human development. Pandit Deendayal advocated for a **decentralized economic setup** to promote overall development and increased interaction among people. Decentralization can be achieved by entrusting production, distribution, and consumption decisions to the people. Producers should monitor distribution to prevent wasteful consumption, saving resources for investment and production. He believed decentralization could solve major Indian economic problems and emphasized the development of **cottage and small industries**.

## Restricting Consumption

Pandit Deendayal stated that the pursuit of unlimited wants leads to new methods and problems, risking the destruction of human values. Thus, an economic goal should be to **limit consumption**. Consumption and production limits should be decided and maintained for national development. He proposed **limiting consumption** to achieve self-reliance.

Even developed capitalist nations face issues (raw material scarcity, rising prices, inflation, arms race, quality issues, stress, environmental problems) and are influenced by the idea of limited consumption as a solution.

While acknowledging the need to raise the standard of living for many in India, Pandit Deendayal believed this requires increased production and appropriate distribution, made possible only by limiting consumption. He stated that requirements should stay within one's income limit.

## Labor Intensive Methods of Production

Considering India's abundance of labour and scarcity of capital, Pandit Deendayal proposed **labour-intensive methods of production**. Adopting capital-intensive methods would mean spending scarce capital on foreign technology and would employ less labour, increasing unemployment. He suggested reducing the burden on land by establishing small industries using simple machines. His planning motto was **'work for all'**, promoting labour-intensive methods to raise employment.
