This section provides the background for India's economic reforms initiated in 1991, explaining the reasons for the shift from a state-regulated economy and outlining the key goals of these policy changes.

# Introduction
The planners of independent India faced challenges in social and economic development. To address these, they adopted a mixed economic system with a strong emphasis on socialist planning.

However, by the 1980s, many experts felt that the planning strategies from 1947 to 1990 had not achieved the desired economic growth and development goals due to extensive state regulations on economic activities. Furthermore, in the early 1990s, international monetary organizations, influenced by developed nations, conditioned further monetary assistance to India on reforming its economic policies by reducing excessive controls. India also faced a severe balance of trade deficit, requiring borrowing from international institutions. **Balance of trade** is an accounting statement showing a country's expenditure on imports and income from exports. Consequently, since 1991, India reformed its economic policies, introducing necessary institutional and regulatory changes.

# Objectives of Economic Policy Reforms Since 1991
The changes in economic policies since 1991 aimed to transform India's economic system from one heavily regulated by the state to one more market-oriented, while also reducing the extent of the public sector in the mixed economy. These changes are known as **economic reforms**. The objectives of these reforms were:

-   To increasingly encourage private and foreign investments to utilize India's abundant natural and human resources more productively in economic development.
-   To achieve optimum and efficient allocation of the nation's resources.
-   To restrict state expenditures and channel resources recovered from disinvestment in public enterprises towards increasing public welfare utilities.
-   To increase domestic income, employment, and export income for the country.
-   To increase the competitiveness of the Indian economy.
-   To ensure steady economic growth and development of the Indian economy in the long run.

To fulfill these objectives, systematic economic policy reforms were initiated in 1991, consisting of three main components:
1.  Liberalization
2.  Privatization
3.  Globalization
