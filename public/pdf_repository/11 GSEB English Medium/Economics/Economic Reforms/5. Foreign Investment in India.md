This section discusses the role of foreign investment in India's globalization process, explaining its importance and detailing the two main types of foreign capital inflow: Foreign Institutional Investment (FII) and Foreign Direct Investment (FDI).

# Foreign Investment in India
Foreign investment became an important component of the globalization process. In a relatively closed and controlled economy, India's excessive foreign exchange needs for investment, essential imports (goods, services, technology), etc., were met by borrowing from international organizations and foreign governments. Due to controls on foreign investments, India had to buy more goods and technology from abroad, requiring foreign exchange. After 1991, India allowed direct investment in certain areas in increased proportion. Such investment was expected to decrease dependence on foreign borrowings, as investment by efficient foreign companies would create employment, produce a variety of goods, raise tax incomes, and bring technology.

# Types of Foreign Investment
Foreign capital comes in two main ways:

1.  Foreign Institutional Investment (FII)
2.  Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)

## Foreign Institutional Investment (FII)
Some foreign companies do not invest in physical assets like plant and machinery in another country (e.g., India). Instead, they invest funds in financial institutions and bond/stock/share markets in that country by buying stocks from the market. Such investment is called **Foreign Institutional Investment (FII)** or **Portfolio Investment**. These companies must register in India as Foreign Institutional Investors. In India, they can invest only up to a limit specified by rules. They have no direct stake in the management of the local company. Hence, financial capital comes to the local country through the stock market.

This type of investment can flow into the country very quickly when foreign companies buy shares in large amounts and can leave just as quickly when they sell shares in bulk. Funds can easily move in and out of the country, making this a risky and unstable kind of foreign capital.

## Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)
When a country invites capital by allowing foreign investors or companies to produce and sell directly within its borders, such investment is called **Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)**.

In FDI, foreign companies directly establish their business in India by constructing plants, bringing in technology and production, or by collaborating with Indian companies. These companies either manage the entire business or have a say in management if they are collaborating partners.

## Nature of Foreign Direct Investment
The nature of FDI can be described as follows:

1.  It involves a physical establishment in the form of direct investment and is therefore a **stable** form of investment.
2.  It brings **machines, materials, and wealth** to the host country.
3.  It brings **new technology** to the country.
4.  It introduces a **different work culture**.

India has systematically allowed increased FDI in various sectors, leading to an increase in India's foreign exchange earnings.
