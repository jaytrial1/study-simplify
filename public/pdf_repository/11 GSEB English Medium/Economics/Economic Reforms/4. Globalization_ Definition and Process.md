This section defines globalization in the economic context and describes the specific steps and policy changes undertaken by India after 1991 to integrate its economy more closely with the rest of the world.

# Globalization
**Globalization** is the process of increasing a country's economic integration with the rest of the world. This is achieved by increasing:

-   Trade in goods and services.
-   Movement of physical and financial capital.
-   Exchange of technology.
-   Investments across borders.

It can be undertaken by gradually decreasing policy controls that served as impediments to foreign trade.

## Process of Globalization in India
In 1991, the International Monetary Fund (IMF) declared several nations as highly indebted and imposed upon them the condition to globalize and upgrade their technologies and growth in return for further loans. India was one of these nations and, accordingly, had to relax its policies protecting domestic industries from foreign competition. Thus, India began globalizing by allowing more trade with other nations. The following marked a systematic globalization process:

-   The import-export licensing policy was simplified and made easier.
-   India became a member of the **World Trade Organization (WTO)** in 1995, agreeing to abide by its rules for freer world trade.
-   Convertibility of the Indian rupee into other currencies was introduced at market rates, gradually reducing conversion at the official rate. This meant the value of the currency was determined by trade.
-   Foreign direct investment (FDI) was systematically increased in various sectors.
-   Investors and producers in India were allowed to increase financial collaborations with their foreign counterparts.
-   The state became more neutral in policy matters between domestic and foreign investors and producers, lifting undue protection for Indian investors against foreign competition.
-   Social and cultural ties with other nations were also encouraged, including relaxations by many nations in granting visas.
