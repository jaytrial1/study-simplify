This section defines privatization as a component of India's economic reforms and explains the various methods through which the process was implemented, including disinvestment.

# Privatization
**Privatization** means the process of introducing private ownership in publicly owned enterprises and increasing the size of the private sector.

In India, public sector enterprises are owned and managed by the state (Government). Privatization means the process of transferring ownership of economic enterprises from the public sector to the private sector, either partially or fully.

## Process of Privatization
Privatization can take place in the following ways:

1.  Through **disinvestment**.
2.  By reducing the number of areas reserved exclusively for public sector investment and opening them for private sector investment.
3.  By establishing **public-private partnership** businesses.

### Meaning and Process of Disinvestment in India
**Meaning**: **Disinvestment** means a process where the state reduces its share of investment in a public enterprise or withdraws its investment completely by selling its shares to the private sector. Thus, the process by which the state 'disinvests' from public enterprises is called disinvestment.

**Process**: The following two aspects are important to understand the process of disinvestment:

-   Selling all the shares of the state in a public enterprise to the private sector, which is called **complete disinvestment**.
-   Selling some shares of the state in public enterprises to the private sector (e.g., 29% or 49%), which is called **partial disinvestment**. If less than 51% shares are transferred to the private sector, it is called **minor disinvestment**. If more than 51% shares are transferred to the private sector, it is called **major disinvestment**.

Besides owning public enterprises, the state also controlled certain areas of investment. Areas of strategic importance and public utility services were not open for investment by the private sector. However, after privatization in 1991, most of these areas were declared open for private sector investment.
Examples include Banking, Education, Communication, and Transportation, which are now open for private and foreign investment. Currently, only atomic energy, certain minerals related to atomic energy, and railways are reserved for investment by the public sector.

After independence, there was a significant rise in the number of public sector enterprises under the central government. After 1991, their number did not rise significantly. While the process of disinvestment from old enterprises continues, the state may still set up new enterprises.
