This section defines goods and services and categorizes various types of goods based on their characteristics, use, and ownership.

# Goods and Services

**Goods**: Commodities which have physical existence are called goods. In economics, they are meant to satisfy consumption needs.

**Services**: Commodities which are intangible or do not have a physical existence are called services. In economics, they are meant to satisfy human needs.

Economics studies activities pertaining to production, consumption, and distribution of goods or services. The nature of goods and services is different, though both are important in economics.

Food is a good (commodity), but cooking is a service. Units of goods or stock of goods can be measured cardinally (quantitatively). Services generally cannot be measured in cardinal terms. However, the time spent providing a service can be measured. The impact of a service in satisfying human wants is also abstract. For example, the need for knowledge and curiosity is satisfied by the service of education. However, the exact contribution of the service of education to knowledge creation is abstract. A book, however, is a good, and it may be possible to quantify the amount of information obtained from the book (e.g., number of pages read).

## Types of Goods

Various goods and services in economics can be classified as:

### Physical (Tangible) and Intangible Goods

*   **Physical (Tangible) Goods**: Some goods have physical existence, like a ball, bat, television, refrigerator, stove, mobile phone, and so on. These can be measured.
*   **Intangible Goods (Services)**: Some goods do not have physical existence, cannot be measured cardinally, and are called services. For example, education, medical consultancy, rendition of music, cooking, and so on.

### Non-economic Goods and Economic Goods

*   **Non-economic Goods**: Goods which do not command exchange value are called non-economic goods. They are abundant in supply and do not go through the economic process of production and distribution, though they are consumed by people. For example, sunlight, air, and so on.
*   **Economic Goods**: Goods which command an exchange value in the market are called economic goods. They command an exchange value as they are scarce in supply in relation to their demand, and their supply can be held under control.

### Durable Goods and Perishable Goods

*   **Durable Goods**: Goods which can be used for a long period of time and repeatedly. For example, shoes, clothes, television, and refrigerator.
*   **Perishable Goods**: Goods which cannot last for a long time and are consumable only once or for a very short period. For example, milk, fruits.

### Private Goods and Public Goods

Goods are classified as private goods and public goods based on their ownership and nature of use.

*   **Private Goods**: Goods which can be possessed and owned by a private individual; they make all decisions regarding these goods. These goods possess the characteristics of *excludability* and *competitiveness*.
    *   **Excludability**: Once a good is owned and being used by one individual, another individual cannot possess and use it at the same time. The individual who pays a price gets the good, and others cannot have the same unit. For example, one who does not pay the price for a mobile phone does not get it.
    *   **Competitiveness**: Individuals have to compete for the ownership of these goods. For example, a packet of biscuits purchased by one child in a market which has competition among buyers comes under the ownership of this child, and another child is excluded from having the same packet of biscuits.
*   **Public Goods**: Goods which can be used by many individuals at the same time. They possess the characteristics of *joint demand* and *collective consumption*.
    *   **Joint Demand**: Such goods are jointly demanded by many people.
    *   **Collective Consumption**: If one individual uses it, others cannot be excluded from its use, and in this way, they are collectively consumed. Hence these are called public goods. They are either provided by the government for all or some people pay for it together. For example, a public garden or the garden of a housing society.

In short, private goods are owned and used by private individuals, and public goods are owned by the government or a group of people and are used collectively.

### Consumers' Goods and Producers' Goods

Goods can satisfy human wants directly or indirectly.

*   **Consumers' Goods**: When a consumer consumes a good, and this good is capable of satisfying a particular want directly, it is called a consumer good. These goods have passed the final stage of production. For example, cooked food. They satisfy human wants directly.
*   **Producers' Goods**: When a good is used at the intermediate stage of production of a final good, when it is purchased by a producer to produce a final good, or when it is not directly consumed by consumers but helps in the production of a final good, then such a good is called a producer's good. For example, cotton used to make cloth, machines used in factories to produce garments, a tractor which facilitates producing food grains, a utensil used to cook food, etc. They satisfy human wants through the final good and not directly.
