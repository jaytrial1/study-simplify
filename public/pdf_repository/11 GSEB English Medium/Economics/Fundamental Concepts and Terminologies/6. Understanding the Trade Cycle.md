This section describes the dynamic fluctuations in economic activity over time, defining the trade cycle and its characteristic phases.

# Trade Cycle

Economic activities go through phases of dynamic changes, like a human life goes through changes. Such changes are basically of four types:
1.  Irregular changes
2.  Seasonal changes
3.  Long run regular trend
4.  Long run dynamic changes which are cyclical in nature

### Irregular Changes

These are accidental changes which occur in an economic activity. Examples include floods, famines, storms, fire, and so on. Such accidents impact an economic activity in the short run and sometimes in the long run. These changes may generally occur and/or have their impact in some sectors or some regions of an economy only. For example, only in the agricultural sector or only in the industrial sector.

### Seasonal Changes

These changes regularly occur in economic activities with changing seasons. For instance, demand for certain goods changes by seasons, and accordingly, production and employment may change.

### Long Run Regular Changes

Some changes occur in a given direction in the form of a trend. These are directional changes which occur in an activity. For example, some regular sales trends.

### Cyclical Changes

Dynamic fluctuations arising in an economy over its long run growth, leading to rise (upward swing) in growth of economic activities and fall (downward swing), are called **trade cycles**. The rise and fall in economic activity is cyclical in nature.

Such changes occur in economic activities, and the growth process of economies also goes through these changes. The study of these dynamic changes called 'trade cycles' is very important in economics.

## Definitions of a Trade Cycle

According to Heberler, "Trade Cycle is an interval that embraces alternating periods of prosperity (good time) and depression (bad time)."

According to Hawtrey, "Trade cycles are continuous phases of good and bad changes occurring in the economy."

## Characteristics of a Trade Cycle:

*   They depict dynamic changes in the economy.
*   They depict the positive and negative changes in the economy.
*   These arise because of several factors.
*   Trade cycles have various phases, and each phase does not last for a uniform period.

## Phases of a Trade Cycle

A trade cycle has the following four phases:
1.  Boom/Inflation
2.  Recession
3.  Depression
4.  Recovery

### Boom

This is a period when economic activity reaches the maximum growth level in a given time period. Demands have peaked, and so have incomes and profits. Thus, this period is also called the '**peak**'.

### Recession

This period follows the period of boom. When economic activity peaks in a given period and investments and employment have reached the highest possible levels, a slow down occurs. Demand slows down, and investment and employment follow.

### Depression

The slowdown in the recession phase continues to cause a **depression** when all activities reach a minimum level under those circumstances. The confidence of buyers, producers, and investors in economic activity is at its lowest. In the phase of recession, consumers start expecting a further fall in prices and reduce demand in expectation of further price fall. With the fall in demand, production falls, and employment falls, causing further reduction in demand. Thus, a depression is caused.

### Recovery

When depression lasts for some time, the suppressed demand starts emerging. Governments may try to boost investments and employment, and sometimes technological changes happen in the long run. This leads to a **recovery** of demand, employment, and investment.

These types of cycles occur in all economies and all economic activities in the long run. However, in market-oriented economies, these occur more openly, while they may be suppressed by the state in state-controlled economies.
