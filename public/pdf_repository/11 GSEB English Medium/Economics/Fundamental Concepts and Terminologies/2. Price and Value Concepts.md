This section explains the economic distinction between price and value, exploring the concepts of use-value and exchange-value.

# Price and Value

In everyday language, the words 'price' and 'value' are often used interchangeably. For instance, we might say, "this good is expensive," "this good is valuable," or "this good has a high price," without differentiating between price and value.

In economics, the concepts of price and value are distinct. It is necessary to first understand the concept of *value*. Value can be understood from two viewpoints:
1.  From the viewpoint of *use*, called **use-value**.
2.  From the viewpoint of *exchange*, called **exchange-value**.

## Use-Value

**Use-value** is determined by the usefulness of a commodity to a consumer for their various needs. For example, food, water, and sunlight have a high use-value for all people in general.

## Exchange-Value

When a commodity or service is exchanged for another commodity or service, the worth of one unit of this commodity is measured in terms of the amount of the other commodity for which it can be exchanged. Such worth is called **exchange-value**. For example, if 1 kg of wheat exchanges for 1 kg of rice, then the exchange value of one kg of wheat is 1 kg of rice.

## Relationship between Use-Value and Exchange-Value

If a commodity has high use-value, it is not necessarily said to have a high exchange-value. However, if a commodity has high exchange-value, it surely has some use value. Exchange value is determined by the scarcity of a resource in relation to its need/use (scarcity of supply in relation to its demand). Goods are demanded if they have some use value. And, if they are scarce in supply in relation to their demand or their supply (flow in the market) is controlled by a few people, then they have a higher exchange value. For example, precious metals, diamonds, etc. On the other hand, if goods are highly useful but have unlimited supply, they do not have exchange value or may have very low exchange value. For example, though air and sunlight have very high usefulness for life, they have no exchange-value as they are abundant in supply. In earlier times, even water had high use-value but no exchange value. However, in present times, the availability of water is reducing in relation to its need and use, and so it has started commanding an exchange-value.

In economics, the word *value* is usually used in the context of the exchange-value of a commodity with another commodity.

Thus, **value** means the worth of one unit of a commodity in terms of units of another commodity in exchange.

## Price

If a commodity is exchanged for *money*, then such monetary exchange value is termed as '**price**'.

**Price** means the exchange value (worth) of a commodity measured in terms of units of currency.
