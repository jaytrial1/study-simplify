# Wealth and Welfare

The concepts of wealth and welfare frequently appear in economic discussions and are very important in economics; hence, they must be clearly understood at this stage.

## Meaning and Characteristics of Wealth

Since the time when Adam Smith defined economics in the context of wealth, the subject of wealth became extremely important.

**Meaning**: According to Alfred Marshall's idea, wealth is something which is useful, scarce, capable of getting exchanged, and can be owned by somebody.

**Characteristics**: The following characteristics can be derived about wealth:

1.  **Usefulness**: It must be useful in satisfying human wants or needs. Therefore, goods which are useful in satisfying needs of human beings are wealth. For example, a house, vehicle, precious jewellery, etc.
2.  **Scarcity**: Those commodities which are scarce in supply in relation to their demand and possess exchange-value besides being useful are called wealth. Goods which are useful but not scarce and do not have exchange-value like sunlight and air are not called wealth in economics.
3.  **Physical or Intellectual Existence**: Wealth should have an existence which can be exchanged. The innate abilities and assets of human beings like the ability to understand, aptitudes, physical abilities, etc., are usually not exchanged. However, in today's world, the intellectual abilities of human beings, like the ability to create new ideas, are exchanged for money in the market, and hence the intellectual ability to create novel ideas and goods is called wealth. Yet, the ability of a scientist to think is her quality and not wealth, but the novel idea created by the scientist is wealth.
4.  **Capability of Exchange**: Wealth is an economic concept and therefore must be capable of getting exchanged. Wealth is meant to satisfy present as well as future consumption and other needs and therefore must be capable of exchange. A house purchased today satisfies the present need of housing and can be sold in the future to buy other goods. Precious metals, land, etc., also possess this characteristic.
5.  **Durability**: Wealth must possess durability. Durable goods can be used for future exchange and for satisfying future needs and therefore become wealth. Goods which perish after single use cannot be used for future transactions and are therefore not wealth. For example, land, house, precious jewellery are durable goods and hence can be considered wealth along with their other characteristics. Shares are also wealth. Food grains produced by a farmer are not wealth if they perish in a very short time. Likewise, the labour of a labourer is also not called wealth. However, if food grains can be stored in cold storage for a long time and their market value increases in the future, then they become wealth. Hence, new technology can redefine wealth.

## Types of Wealth

Wealth can be classified as:
1.  Individual Wealth and Social Wealth
2.  National Wealth and International Wealth

### Individual Wealth and Social Wealth

*   **Individual Wealth**: Wealth under the ownership of an individual and meant for private consumption. For example, a house.
*   **Social Wealth**: Wealth under the ownership of society and meant for collective consumption. For example, a dam.

### National Wealth and International Wealth

*   **National Wealth**: Wealth belonging to a nation and possessed and treasured by a nation. It helps to generate exchange value directly or indirectly for a nation. For example, rivers, mountains, literature, and scriptures.
*   **International Wealth**: Wealth which is not owned by one nation but belongs to the whole world and can be shared by several nations.

## Welfare

**Welfare** means enhancement of life situations, standard of living; an overall state of improvement. The concept of welfare is used by all philosophies, from spiritual sciences to economics.

All human activities, economic and non-economic, are ultimately done with the purpose of improvement of life. Welfare can be measured quantitatively in the context of growth and qualitatively in the context of development.

In economics, the subject of welfare can be studied as:
1.  Individual welfare
2.  Collective welfare

When an individual seeks to make efforts to improve their standard of living and well-being, it becomes a subject of **individual welfare**.

When nations and governments seek to improve growth, development, etc., it becomes a subject matter of **collective welfare**.

### Relationship between Wealth and Welfare

Generally, when wealth increases, welfare increases, as more wealth means a higher capacity to generate monetary value, greater power to exchange goods or purchasing power for the owner, and hence greater well-being. This is largely true in the case of individuals.

But when the wealth of a nation increases in the form of national income, the welfare of people will increase only if this national income is equitably distributed among all sections of society and among all sectors. Besides, a nation's welfare also depends upon social and cultural formation, and hence there is not always a direct relation between wealth and welfare in a society.
