This section categorizes and explains the various factors that influence the supply of a commodity in the market.

# Determinants of Supply

Factors affecting supply are grouped under two categories: (1) Price of a good and (2) Factors other than price. Supply of a commodity is affected by the following factors:

## Price of the Product
Price is an important determinant of supply. A producer sells for profit and thus, she/he supplies more when the price of a product rises and supplies less when the price of a product falls. Thus, there is a *positive relationship* between price of a good and its supply.

## Factors other than Price

### Price of Factors of Production / Cost of Production
Supply is affected by changes in production cost. For example, when rent paid to the owner of land, wages paid to the labourer, decrease, the cost of production decreases. When cost of production decreases, profits increase if price remains unchanged, and hence the seller is willing to sell greater amounts. Hence, supply expands when prices of factors of production fall. While if cost of production rises, vice-versa happens, that is, supply contracts.

### Level of Technology
When the state of technology advances, time and efforts are saved. Hence, greater amounts and better quality of goods are produced with the same or lower costs. If the market price does not fall, then profits increase, and sellers are willing to sell more; supply expands. Countries that use more advanced technologies are able to produce more at lower costs.

### Future Expectations Regarding Price
Future expectations about price affect the current supply of goods. If sellers speculate the price of a product to rise in the future, then they will contract the supply in the present time period to build up stock for the future and vice-versa.

### Other Factors
Supply increases when there is an increase in the number of firms producing the product, when political stability exists, natural conditions remain conducive, when efficient law and order mechanisms and legal systems exist, when industrial relations are maintained well between owners and workers of production and marketing activities etc. And, supply decreases when the above factors act against supply.
