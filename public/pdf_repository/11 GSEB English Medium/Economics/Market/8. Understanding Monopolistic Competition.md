# Monopolistic Competition

Real-world markets are rarely purely perfectly competitive or pure monopolies. Many markets exhibit a mix of partial monopoly and partial perfect competition, known as Monopolistic Competition. This is one of the most commonly observed market structures.

## Monopolistic Competition: Definition

*   According to Prof. Chamberlin: A market where perfect competition and perfect monopoly coexist is known as Monopolistic Market.
*   According to Smt. Robinson: If each firm establishes a degree of monopoly while also competing with others, the market is called 'Imperfect Competition' (another term for monopolistic competition).

## Characteristics of Monopolistic Competition

*   **Large Number of Sellers and Numerous Buyers:** There are many sellers, more than in oligopoly but fewer than in perfect competition. This number is sufficient for competition to be significant. Buyers are numerous and cannot individually influence the market price.
*   **Product Differentiation:** This is a key characteristic. Products are differentiated based on form, quality, nature, or minor differences in fragrance, taste, shape, weight, etc. These differences, however small, make products distinct from competitors and allow producers some degree of control over their own product's price.
*   **Free Entry and Exit of Firms:** Firms can freely enter the market when attracted by super normal profits and exit when suffering losses. This free movement is primarily a short-term phenomenon. In the long run, with normal profits, the movement of firms decreases as there is neither the attraction of high profits nor the pressure of losses.
*   **Selling Cost:** Expenses incurred specifically to promote and sell the product are called selling costs. This includes packaging, making the product attractive, sales taxes, transportation (sometimes included), expenses for intermediaries, showroom costs, promotional expenses, prizes, gifts, and importantly, advertisement cost. Selling costs are distinct from production expenses. Selling cost is typical of monopolistic competition, used by sellers to attract consumers and highlight product differentiation.
*   **Competition Other than Price:** Besides price competition, firms also compete on non-price factors. Sellers keep prices relatively fixed but try to attract consumers by advertising, improving quality, or offering better services. They compete by differentiating their product and influencing consumer preferences.
*   **Imperfect Knowledge Regarding the Market:** Buyers and sellers may not have complete information about all available identical or substitute products and their prices. This lack of perfect knowledge allows for price differences for similar products and contributes to the monopolistic element where firms create a distinct identity for their product.
