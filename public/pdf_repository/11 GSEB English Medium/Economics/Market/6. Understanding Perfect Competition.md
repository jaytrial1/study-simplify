# Perfect Competition

Perfect competition is considered an ideal market structure, primarily theoretical and rarely observed in practice, except perhaps in certain agricultural sectors. Despite its theoretical nature, studying perfect competition is important for understanding other market structures like monopoly, monopolistic competition, and oligopoly.

## Perfect Competition: Definition

*   According to Mrs. Robinson: Perfect competition exists where the demand for a producer's product depends entirely on its price.
*   According to Prof. Leftwich: Perfect competition is a market system where there are many firms selling identical products, and no single firm is large enough to influence the market price.

## Characteristics of Perfect Competition

*   **Numerous Buyers and Sellers:** This market has a very large number of buyers and sellers. Each individual seller is a tiny part of the total market and cannot control or influence the overall market price. Similarly, the numerous buyers cannot individually influence the market price. The market price is determined solely by overall market demand and supply.
*   **Identical Products:** Products in this market are homogeneous, meaning they have similar features, form, shape, color, taste, weight, and quality. They are considered perfect substitutes for each other. Because products are identical, sellers cannot charge different prices; buyers are unwilling to pay more for a product that is the same as others.
*   **Free Entry and Exit of Firms:** There are no barriers to firms entering or leaving the market. New firms can freely enter when existing firms are making abnormal profits, and firms suffering losses can freely exit. This free movement occurs temporarily. In the long run, firms tend to earn only normal profits, which reduces the incentive for entry or exit.
*   **Perfect Knowledge of the Market:** Producers, buyers, and sellers have complete information about product availability, prices, and quality. Sellers know the prices and quality offered by competitors, preventing them from charging different prices for identical products. Buyers know the prices and quality, ensuring sellers cannot overcharge. This perfect knowledge leads to a perfectly elastic demand curve for individual firms.
*   **Mobility of Factors of Production:** Factors of production (land, capital, labor, entrepreneurship) are assumed to be perfectly mobile physically, professionally, and in terms of usage. Since the price (compensation) for factors is the same across the market, there is no incentive for factors to move between firms seeking higher returns.
*   **No Transportation Expenses:** Transportation costs are considered negligible compared to the total expense or assumed to be zero. This is treated as an important characteristic for the theoretical model of perfect competition.
