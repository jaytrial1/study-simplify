# Oligopoly

The term 'Oligopoly' originates from the Greek words 'Oligos' (meaning 'Few') and 'Pollein' (meaning 'Sellers'), referring to a market with a small number of producers.

Oligopoly markets consist of a few dominant producers selling either identical or substitute products. This structure often involves intense competition among the few firms.

## Oligopoly: Definition

*   According to Prof. Stigler: Oligopoly is a market in which a firm decides its policy based on the behavior of its competitors.
*   According to Prof. Baumol: Oligopoly is a market in which even a small number of sellers, relative to the total, are significant enough to affect the product's price.

## Characteristics of Oligopoly

*   **Few Sellers and Numerous Buyers:** The market is dominated by a small number of firms, typically ranging from more than two to less than ten or twenty. These few sellers have significant control over the market. The number of buyers is large, making the influence of any single buyer negligible.
*   **Similar or Substitutable Products:** Firms in an oligopoly sell either identical (homogeneous) or differentiated (substitutable) products. Examples of identical products include salt, crude oil, and tea. Markets with differentiated products like cold drinks or motorcycles represent imperfect oligopoly.
*   **Entry or Exit of Firms:** Entry and exit conditions vary depending on the type of oligopoly. In a free oligopoly, entry and exit are unrestricted. In a restricted oligopoly, entry and exit are regulated, often by significant barriers like high capital requirements, patents, or control over raw materials.
*   **Selling Cost:** Selling costs, including advertising, promotions, and distribution expenses, are very important in oligopoly due to intense competition among the few firms. Firms use selling costs to attract consumers and differentiate their products, even if the products are similar. Examples include advertisements for televisions or cars.
*   **Inter-Dependence:** Because there are only a few sellers, each firm's decisions regarding price, output, quality, or marketing significantly affect the other firms. Firms are highly aware of and dependent on the actions and reactions of their competitors. They must consider how rivals will respond before making strategic decisions.
*   **Price Stickiness (Kinked Demand Curve):** The interdependence among firms can lead to price rigidity. If one firm lowers its price, competitors are likely to follow to avoid losing market share, leading to a price war that benefits no one. If one firm raises its price, competitors are unlikely to follow, causing the firm raising its price to lose significant market share. This asymmetrical response to price changes creates a "kink" in the demand curve faced by an individual firm, making prices resistant to change.
