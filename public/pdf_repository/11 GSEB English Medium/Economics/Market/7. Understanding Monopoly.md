# Monopoly

Economists view Perfect Competition and Monopoly as opposite market structures. Pure monopoly is often an imaginary concept; what is observed in reality is typically imperfect monopoly.

The term 'Monopoly' comes from the Greek words 'Monos' (meaning 'Single') and 'Polein' (meaning 'Seller'), indicating a market with only one seller.

## Monopoly: Definition

*   According to Prof. Chamberlin: Monopoly exists when the supply of a product is controlled by a single enterprise.
*   According to Prof. Stigler: Only one enterprise is the seller of the goods or products.

## Characteristics of Monopoly

*   **Only one Producer or Seller and Numerous Buyers:** There is a single seller or producer who controls the entire supply of the product. With no competition, the seller can control the product's price and is known as the 'Price Maker'. Buyers are numerous, making the importance of a single buyer negligible, and they cannot affect the product price.
*   **Absence of Substitute Goods:** Ideally, there are no substitute goods in a pure monopoly. In reality (imperfect monopoly), there is an absence or ignorance of close substitutes. While a close substitute might be rare, a different product or service might fulfill a similar need (e.g., airplane travel as a substitute for a specific train ticket).
*   **Restriction Over the Entry of New Firms:** Monopoly means only one seller exists. While ending a monopoly is possible, it's difficult, allowing the seller to maintain control for a long time. Despite the potential for super normal profits, new firms cannot easily enter due to natural, legal, skill-based, or experience-based barriers.
*   **Control Over the Price or Sales:** To maximize profits, the seller controls the supply. However, a monopolist cannot control both price and the quantity sold simultaneously. To sell more units, the price must typically be lowered, while a higher price means fewer units sold. Selling a large quantity at a high price is generally not possible.
*   **Super Normal Profit:** In a monopoly, the producer and seller are the same. The seller can earn super normal profits in both the short and long run due to the absence of competition. They can set prices higher than their total expenses.
*   **Price-Discrimination:** Without competition, a seller can charge different prices for the same product based on its use or the buyer. This allows the seller to gain higher profits through price discrimination (e.g., different fees charged by a doctor based on patient income).
*   **Firm is Industry:** In economics, a firm is a production unit, and an industry is a collection of firms producing similar products. In a monopoly, since there is only one producer/seller, the single firm constitutes the entire industry.
