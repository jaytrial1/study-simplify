# Classification of Markets

Markets can be classified in many ways, including by location, time, product form, quantity, control, and competition. Here, we focus on classification based on location, quantity, and competition.

## Markets Based on Location

Markets are classified based on their geographical area:

*   **Local Market:** Products and services are produced and sold within the same limited area, such as a city or village. For example, a market for clay utensils.
*   **Regional Market:** Selling is limited to a specific region or state. Diverse markets within a state fall under this category. They are spread across various regions of a state. For example, markets in Ahmedabad and Vadodara in Gujarat, or regional specialties like 'Ghari' of Surat, regional films, and regional books.
*   **National Market:** Products and services are bought and sold throughout the entire country. This market spans various states of a nation. Examples include dairy products, the sari market, and Hindi novels.
*   **International Market:** Also known as the Global Market, this market extends across various countries. Sales and purchases are generally referred to as 'Import-export'. Examples include mobile phones and English novels.

Advances in transportation, communication, and cold-storage facilities have blurred the lines in location-based classification. The same items can sometimes be considered part of both regional and national markets simultaneously.
