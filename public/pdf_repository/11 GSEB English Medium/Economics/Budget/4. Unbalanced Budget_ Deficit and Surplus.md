This section explains the concept of an unbalanced budget, detailing deficit and surplus budgets, and discussing their respective merits and demerits.

## Unbalanced Budget

The total expenditure and total income in such a budget are not equal. Accordingly, this budget can be:
1.  A **deficit budget**
2.  A **surplus budget**

### Deficit Budget
In this type of budget, the government's anticipated total expenditure is more than the anticipated total income.
*Deficit budget = anticipated total expenditure > anticipated total income*

In present times, government budgets are mostly deficit budgets. Any economy which is developing usually spends more than the incomes as expenditures on development activities like education, social welfare, creation of public utilities etc. are higher; While owing to lower economic development, income from taxes and other sources are lower.

### Surplus Budget
A surplus budget is one in which the government spends lesser than its total anticipated income.

This means the government is collecting more revenues from citizens by way of taxes than what it is spending for the citizens. The overall development and welfare activities will be lower in the economy. In reality, most governments do not do this. This type of budget is usually seen in developed countries.

# Merits of a Deficit Budget

*   This budget is known to promote **development and welfare activities**.
*   In times of low economic activity, the government spends more in the economy for investment and creating employment. Thus, the economy gets a **boost during depression** and economic growth can pick up.
*   The **tax burden upon people is lower** in proportion of economic activity as expenditures are high and incomes are lower and taxes are a source of income for the government.

## Demerits of a Deficit Budget

*   In order to meet the deficit, governments **borrow**, thus increasing **debt burdens** in the economy.
*   Deficit in the budget also shows that governments do not have **control on expenditures**.
*   In case of high deficits, it can also be concluded that the tax revenues collected from public is going in **wasteful expenditures**.

### Merits of a Surplus Budget

*   This budget is useful in times of severe **inflation**. When the government spends lesser; employment, income and demand reduce and inflation can be restricted.
*   There is **no burden of borrowing** in this type of a budget.
*   **Savings of the government increase** which can be used for development in future periods.

### Demerits of a Surplus Budget

*   People are made to pay more taxes to enhance government incomes but the **welfare they receive from government spending reduces**.
*   If there is no inflation (or there is deflation) then lower spending will result in lower investment, employment, income and production which may lead to **depression** in the economy.
*   If the surplus in the budget persistently rises for several years then there may be problems arising out of **excess savings**.
