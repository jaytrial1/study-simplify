This section provides an overview of the budget structure and components for the three tiers of government in India: the Central Government, State Governments, and Local Governments (Municipal Corporations).

## Idea of Budgets of Different Tiers of Governments in India

Let us now get an idea of the budget of the three tiers of government in the Indian federal system viz., Central government, State governments and local governments (Municipal corporations, Municipalities and Panchayats).

### Concept of Union Budget (Central Government's Budget)
In India, the Central Government's budget is presented by the Finance Minister in the Loksabha usually in the last week of February every year and the Loksabha approves the budget after discussions and if necessary, amendments are made later. The budget is effective from April 1 to March 31 of the next calendar year.

#### An Idea of the Budget of Central Government in India:

| Current (Revenue) Account of Central Government's Budget |   |
| --- | --- |
| **Income (Credit)** | **Expenditure (Debit)**  |
| (1) Tax revenue: <br> (A) Revenues from direct taxes <br> (B) Revenues from indirect taxes <br> (C) Indirect taxes from goods and services which are not covered under GST <br> (D) Tax revenues from union territories | (1) Non-plan expenditures (are incurred on programmes for various sectors which are not detailed in the plans) such as: <br> (A) Interest payments (on loans borrowed in earlier periods) <br> (B) Social services like education, health, public utilities and administration and general services <br> (C) Economic services like agricultural services, industries, electricity, transport, technology etc. <br> (D) Non-plan Grants and assistance given by centre to states and union territories <br> (E) Current expenses on defence <br> (F) Subsidies <br> (G) Salaries, Pensions etc.  |
| (2) Incomes other than tax incomes: <br> (A) Interest incomes earned from loans given by the centre in earlier periods <br> (B) Profits and dividends from public sector enterprises <br> (C) Fees and fines from public utilities <br> (D) Assistance received from abroad | (2) Planned expenditures (are incurred on programmes for various sectors which are detailed in the plans) such as: <br> (A) Agriculture, industries, irrigation, information and communication, energy, minerals, transport and such sectors <br> (B) Planned grants and assistance given to states and union territories  |

| Capital Account of Central Government's Budget |   |
| --- | --- |
| **Income (Credit)** | **Expenditure (Debit)**  |
| (1) Recovery of Loans <br> (2) Borrowings <br> (3) Other capital incomes like those from disinvestment, small savings schemes etc. | (1) Repayment of loans borrowed earlier <br> (2) Loans given to other governments <br> (3) Capital expenditure on social and economic services <br> (4) Capital expenditure in defence  |

*Note*: The expenditure of central government is classified into plan and non-plan expenditures (changed in budget of 2016). While the expenditure of state governments is classified into developmental and non-developmental expenditures.

**Developmental expenditure** means expenditure which provides a direct boost to economic development. For example, expenditure on irrigation. **Non-developmental expenditure** means the expenditure which does not have a direct impact on development. For example, expenditure on pensions.

### Budget of Local Governments
In India, local governments are called Municipal corporations in big cities, Municipalities in smaller cities, District (Jilla) Panchayats in districts, Taluka panchayats in talukas and Gram panchayat in villages. Constitution has suggested the role of these governments and these governments can also collect some income of their own.

The main functions of local governments pertain to **water supply, water pumps, sewage, roads, cleanliness, public health, electrification** of their concerned region and so on.
