# Introduction to National Income

National Income is a basic concept of macroeconomics used to measure the economic activity of an entire economy. It serves as a balance sheet reflecting the performance and economic health of a country. An increase in national income generally signifies well-being, while low or slow-growing national income suggests an unhealthy economy. It is widely used as a criterion for a country's economic prosperity.

Generally, national income is defined as the market value of final products and services produced using national wealth (land, capital, labor) within a country during a year. Key concepts associated with national income include **GNP (Gross National Product)**, **NNP (Net National Product)**, **GDP (Gross Domestic Product)**, and **NDP (Net Domestic Product)**, which are also used for international economic comparisons.

This chapter explores what national income is, how it is created and measured, the various methods of measurement, problems encountered during measurement, and the distinction between monetary and real income.
