This section differentiates between monetary national income (calculated at current prices) and real national income (calculated at base-year prices), explaining why real income is a better indicator of true economic growth and standard of living.

# Monetary Income and Real Income

National income is typically measured in monetary terms. However, if the value of money is unstable due to price fluctuations (inflation or deflation), changes in monetary national income may not accurately reflect changes in actual production or economic well-being. To address this, we distinguish between monetary and real national income.

## Monetary National Income

**Monetary National Income**, also known as nominal national income, is the national income measured at *current market prices*. It is calculated by multiplying the production of all goods and services in a year by their respective market prices in that same year.

While easy to calculate, monetary national income can be misleading. If production remains the same as the previous year but prices double, the monetary national income will also double (Production × Price), even though the actual quantity of goods and services produced has not increased. This does not reflect a true improvement in the standard of living.

## Real National Income

**Real National Income** is the national income measured at *base-year prices* (also called constant prices or fixed prices). A specific year is chosen as the base year, and the production of subsequent years is valued using the prices from this base year.

Real national income provides a truer picture of a country's economic situation. It isolates the change in national income that is due to changes in the *volume of production*, removing the effect of price changes. If real national income increases, it means the actual quantity of goods and services produced has increased, which is generally associated with a higher potential for consumption and an improved standard of living. Economists primarily use real national income to track economic growth over time and compare living standards.
