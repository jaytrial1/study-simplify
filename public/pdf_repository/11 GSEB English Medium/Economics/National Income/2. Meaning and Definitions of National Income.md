This section presents various definitions of national income as proposed by different economists, highlighting their focus areas such as production, consumption, and monetary value.

# Meaning and Definitions of National Income

## General Meaning

The **national income** of a country is the monetary value of the total production across agriculture, industries, and the service sector within that country during a year. For example, stating that India's national income is ₹ 128 lakh crores implies that goods and services worth this amount were produced in these sectors during the year.

## Alfred Marshall (Production Based Definition)

Prof. Alfred Marshall defined national income as the **net production of physical (tangible) and non-physical (service) things** produced using natural wealth (land) along with capital and labor in a country during a year. This definition emphasizes the *net output* of goods and services, making it production-oriented.

## Irving Fisher (Consumption Based Definition)

According to Irving Fisher, national income is the **proportion of direct consumption of goods and services by the people of a country during a year**. Fisher's definition focuses on the *consumption* of physical and non-physical goods and services, hence it is consumption-oriented.

## A.C. Pigou (Money Based Definition)

A.C. Pigou defined national income as a **flow of those things (goods) and services whose payments have been made through money or which can be easily represented by money**. It includes the total income of society plus foreign income, which can be easily measured using money. This definition is money-oriented, stressing the role of money in measuring national income.

## Inferences from Definitions

Based on these various definitions, several key inferences about national income can be drawn:
*   It is a measurement of income obtained over a definite period, typically one year.
*   Only final products and services are considered.
*   It represents the monetary value of these final products and services.
*   It is calculated after deducting depreciation.
*   The goods and services must be produced or consumed.
