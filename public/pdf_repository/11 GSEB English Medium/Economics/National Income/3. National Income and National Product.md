This section clarifies the relationship and distinction between national income and national product, explaining how they are related but not always identical, especially in the presence of government intervention like taxes and subsidies.

# National Income and National Product

While often considered similar, national income and national product are distinct concepts.

## National Income

**National Income** is defined as the sum of total income obtained from the contribution of the four factors of production (land, labor, capital, entrepreneurship) in national production.
National Income = Rent + Wages + Interest + Profit

## National Product

**National Product** is the sum of the total production value of final products and services generated by various factors of production across different sectors (agriculture, industries, services) in a year. It represents the goods and services produced by firms within a country during a year.
National Product = Total Production of Agriculture + Industries + Services sector.

In a simplified economy without government intervention (taxes, subsidies) and foreign trade, national income and national product are equal. However, with taxes, national product tends to be higher than national income because taxes increase the price of goods (market price is higher than production cost). Conversely, with government subsidies, national product tends to be lower than national income because subsidies reduce the market price below the production cost.
