This section defines and explains key concepts used in national income accounting, including Gross Domestic Product (GDP), Net Domestic Product (NDP), Gross National Product (GNP), Net National Product (NNP), and Per Capita Income.

# Important Concepts of National Income

Several important concepts are used in national income accounting to measure and analyze economic activity.

## Gross Domestic Product (GDP)

**Gross Domestic Product (GDP)** is the market value of all final goods and services produced *within the geographical boundaries* of a country during a year, regardless of the nationality of the producers (citizens or foreigners).

**Important Facts about GDP:**
*   It includes the value of final products produced within the country's borders by both residents and non-residents, or derived from domestic natural resources (like crude oil).
*   The concept is strictly related to the country's geographical boundary. Income earned by a country's citizens in foreign countries or income generated from foreign countries is *not* included.
*   GDP is widely used in practice for comparing the economic size and progress of countries.

## Net Domestic Product (NDP)

During production, capital goods like machines, buildings, and equipment undergo wear and tear or become obsolete (depreciation). **Net Domestic Product (NDP)** is calculated by deducting this depreciation from the Gross Domestic Product. It represents the net value of production available after accounting for the cost of maintaining capital stock.

NDP = GDP - Depreciation

## Gross National Product (GNP)

While GDP focuses on production within a country's borders, **Gross National Product (GNP)** focuses on the production by the *citizens* of a country, regardless of where they are located (within the country or abroad). GNP is the monetary value of final goods and services produced by the citizens of a country in a year.

**Important Facts about GNP:**
*   Only the value of production from the current year is considered.
*   GNP is calculated by adding the net income earned by the country's citizens from abroad to the GDP. Net income from abroad is the difference between the total income earned by citizens from foreign countries and the total income paid to foreign citizens within the country.
    GNP = GDP + Net income obtained from foreign countries (Income from abroad by citizens - Income paid to foreigners in the country).
*   GNP is also commonly used for economic analysis and comparison.

## Net National Product (NNP)

Similar to how NDP is derived from GDP, **Net National Product (NNP)** is calculated by deducting depreciation from the Gross National Product. It represents the net value of goods and services produced by the country's citizens after accounting for capital depreciation.

NNP = GNP - Depreciation

NNP at factor cost is often considered the national income.

## Per Capita Income

While national income indicates the overall economic growth, **Per Capita Income (PCI)** is considered a better criterion for national development as it reflects the average income available per person. It is calculated by dividing the total national income of a country by its total population in a given year.

Per capita Income = (Gross National Income) / (Total Population)

**Example Calculation:**
If the national income of a country is ₹ 60,000 crores in a year and the population is 2 crores, the per capita income is:
Per capita Income = ₹ (60,000 crores) / (2 crores) = ₹ 30,000

This means the average income per citizen is ₹ 30,000 per year.

*   If calculated using current year prices, it's called current price per capita income.
*   If calculated using base year (fixed) prices, it's called fixed price per capita income.

A higher per capita income generally implies a higher proportion of goods and services available to citizens, suggesting a higher standard of living.

**Important Facts about Per Capita Income:**
*   Per capita income decreases if population growth rate exceeds the national income growth rate.
*   It is an *average* measurement and does not reflect income distribution inequality.
*   It may not accurately reflect the standard of living if income inequality is high.
*   Many consider per capita income, rather than just national income, as a truer criterion for a country's progress.
*   The UNO (United Nations Organization) uses per capita income data alongside national income data for comparing countries' progress.
*   It helps in making assumptions about the standard of living of a country's citizens.
