# Circular Flow of National Income

The circular flow of national income illustrates the interconnectedness of economic activities. In theoretical terms, economies can be classified as **closed economies** (no foreign trade) or **open economies** (involving foreign trade, government, savings, investment). Here, we will focus on the circular flow in a closed economy for simplicity.

To understand this flow, the economy is typically divided into two major sectors: business firms and households (owners of factors of production).

Business firms purchase necessary factors of production (land, capital, labor, entrepreneurship) from households to use in the production process. Thus, factors of production flow from households to firms. In return for using these factors, firms pay rent, interest, wages, and profit (factor income) to the households. This creates a flow of money from firms to households.

# Circular Flow of National Income in a Closed Economy

Firms produce goods and services using the factors purchased from households and offer them for sale in the market. Households then purchase these goods and services from the market. This creates a flow of goods and services from firms to households. In exchange, households pay money to firms for these purchases (expenditure), causing a flow of money back to firms from households. This money allows firms to purchase factors of production again, restart production, pay households, and the cycle continues. This continuous flow of production, income, and expenditure is called the **circular flow of national income**. It keeps the economy constantly vibrant.

Based on this circular flow, national income can be measured in three ways:
1.  Production (value of goods/services produced)
2.  Income (sum of factor incomes)
3.  Expenditure (total spending on final goods/services)

In this closed economy model, goods flow from firms to families, and payment flows from families to firms for those goods. Simultaneously, factors of production flow from families to firms, and factor income flows from firms to families.

In reality, economies are open, involving government, savings, investment, and international trade (import/export). The circular flow becomes more complex in such economies, involving additional leakages (savings, taxes, imports) and injections (investment, government spending, exports).
