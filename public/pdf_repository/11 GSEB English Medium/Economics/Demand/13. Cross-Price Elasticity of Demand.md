# Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand

This section introduces cross-price elasticity, which measures how the demand for one good changes in response to a change in the price of a related good (substitute or complementary).

Goods can be related as substitutes or complements.

*   **Substitute Goods:** Goods that can be used interchangeably to satisfy a want (e.g., tea and coffee).
*   **Complementary Goods:** Goods that are consumed together to satisfy a want (e.g., car and petrol).

Cross-price elasticity of demand measures the percentage change in the demand for one good (Good X) when the price of a *related* good (Good Y) changes.

$$
\text{Cross elasticity of demand} = \frac{\text{Percentage change in demand for good X}}{\text{Percentage change in price for good Y}}
$$

The sign of cross-price elasticity indicates the relationship between the goods:
*   Positive for substitute goods (Price of Y rises, demand for X rises).
*   Negative for complementary goods (Price of Y rises, demand for X falls).
*   Zero or close to zero for unrelated goods.
