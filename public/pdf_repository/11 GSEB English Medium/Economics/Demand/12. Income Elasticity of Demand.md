# Income Elasticity of Demand

This section defines and explains income elasticity, which measures how demand changes in response to consumer income changes.

Just as price elasticity measures the effect of price changes on demand, income elasticity measures the effect of changes in consumer *income* on demand, holding other factors constant.

## Meaning of Income Elasticity of Demand

Income elasticity of demand measures the extent to which the demand for a good changes because of a change in the consumer's income. The formula is:

$$
\text{Income elasticity } (\varepsilon_{y}) = \frac{\text{Proportionate change of demand}}{\text{Proportionate change of income}}
$$

## Types/Degrees of Income Elasticity of Demand

There are three main types of income elasticity:

1.  **Positive Income Elastic Demand:** Demand increases when income rises, and decreases when income falls. This is typical for normal goods. There are three degrees within positive income elasticity:
    *   **(A) Unit Income Elastic Demand ($\varepsilon_{y}=1$):** Percentage change in demand equals percentage change in income.
    *   **(B) Elasticity of Demand Greater than Unity ($\varepsilon_{y}>1$):** Percentage change in demand is greater than percentage change in income (typical for luxury goods).
    *   **(C) Elasticity of Demand Less than Unity ($\varepsilon_{y}<1$):** Percentage change in demand is less than percentage change in income (typical for necessary normal goods).

2.  **Negative Income Elastic Demand:** Demand decreases when income rises, and increases when income falls. This is characteristic of inferior goods, including Giffen goods.

3.  **Zero Income Elastic Demand:** Demand remains unchanged despite changes in consumer income. This is usually the case for very low-priced essential goods consumed in fixed quantities, like salt or matchsticks.
