# Demand Function

This section explains how the relationship between demand and its determinants can be expressed mathematically using a demand function.

A demand function shows the relationship between the demand for a good and the factors that determine it. It indicates that the demand for a good depends on various factors like its price, the prices of related goods, consumer income, tastes, preferences, and other influences. For market demand, population size is also a factor.

The demand function can be represented as:

$$
\mathrm{D}_{x} = f(\mathrm{P}_{x}, \mathrm{P}_{y}, \mathrm{P}_{e}, \mathrm{~T}, \mathrm{Y}, \mathrm{U})
$$

Where:
*   $\mathrm{D}_{x}$ = Demand for commodity X
*   $f$ = Functional notation (means "depends on")
*   $\mathrm{P}_{x}$ = Price of commodity X
*   $\mathrm{P}_{y}$ = Price of related commodity Y
*   $\mathrm{P}_{e}$ = Expectations regarding future prices
*   $\mathrm{T}$ = Tastes and preferences of the consumer
*   $\mathrm{Y}$ = Consumer's income
*   $\mathrm{U}$ = Other factors

Economists often focus on the relationship between price and demand, holding other determinants constant, especially when explaining the Law of Demand.
