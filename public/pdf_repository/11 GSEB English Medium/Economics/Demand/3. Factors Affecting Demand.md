# Factors Affecting Demand

This section explores the various influences on the demand for a commodity or service, classifying them into two main categories: the price of the good itself and other non-price determinants.

The determinants of demand for a commodity or service can be broadly classified into:
1.  **Price of the commodity/service:** This is the most significant factor. Generally, when the price of a good falls, consumers tend to buy more (demand expands), and when the price rises, they tend to buy less (demand contracts).
2.  **Factors other than price (Other Determinants):** These include various influences apart from the good's own price.

## Other Determinants

*   **Tastes and Preferences of a Consumer:** Demand is significantly influenced by what a consumer likes or dislikes. These preferences can change over time, affecting demand for different goods at various stages of life.
*   **Income of a Consumer:** For most goods, demand increases as consumer income rises and decreases as income falls. This shows a direct relationship. However, for *inferior goods*, demand decreases as income increases, making them an exception.
*   **Prices of Related Goods:** The demand for a good is also affected by the prices and availability of related goods, which are typically classified as substitute goods or complementary goods.
    *   **(I) Price of Substitute Goods:** These are goods that can be used in place of one another to satisfy a similar want (e.g., different brands of electronics). If the price of a substitute good falls, consumers may switch to the cheaper substitute, causing the demand for the original good to fall.
    *   **(II) Price of Complementary Goods:** These are goods that are consumed together (e.g., mobile phone and SIM card). If the price of a complementary good rises, the combined cost of consumption increases, leading consumers to demand less of both goods.
*   **Expectations about Future Prices:** If consumers expect the price of a good to rise in the future, they may increase their current demand for it, and vice versa.
*   **Size and Demographic Profile of Population:** The total market demand for a good is influenced by the total number of consumers and the characteristics of the population (like age distribution). A larger population generally means higher total demand.
