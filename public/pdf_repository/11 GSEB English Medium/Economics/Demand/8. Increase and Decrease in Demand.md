# Increase and Decrease in Demand

This section explains how changes in factors *other than* the price of a good cause the entire demand curve to shift.

Increase and decrease in demand refer to changes in the quantity demanded of a good caused by changes in *non-price determinants* of demand, while the price of the good itself remains constant. This results in a *shift* of the entire demand curve.

*   **Increase in Demand:** Occurs when, at the same price, consumers are willing and able to buy more of a good. This is caused by favorable changes in other factors (like higher income, change in tastes towards the good, etc.). It is represented by a rightward shift of the entire demand curve.
*   **Decrease in Demand:** Occurs when, at the same price, consumers are willing and able to buy less of a good. This is caused by unfavorable changes in other factors (like lower income, change in tastes away from the good, etc.). It is represented by a leftward shift of the entire demand curve.
