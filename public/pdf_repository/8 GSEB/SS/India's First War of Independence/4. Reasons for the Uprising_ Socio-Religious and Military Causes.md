This section discusses the social, religious, and military factors that contributed to the widespread discontent leading to the 1857 uprising.

## Socio-Religious Reasons

The British also interfered in the social and religious matters of the Indians.

*   Christian pastors made efforts to convert Hindus and Muslims, often with government protection.
*   A law enacted in 1850 AD allowed Hindus or Muslims who became Christians to inherit ancestral property, strengthening suspicions among Hindus.
*   The British often treated Indians with contempt, believing them to be inferior.
*   They avoided social dealings with Indians, and their homes were often separate.
*   Taxes were levied on the property of temples and mosques.

Due to such religious and social discrimination, Indians felt resentment and hatred towards the British.

## Military Reason

The War of Independence was first fought by Indian soldiers due to the exploitative British policy towards them.

*   Despite rigorous service, salaries and facilities for Indian soldiers were meagre.
*   No Indian soldier could attain a rank higher than a subedar.
*   The British avoided social dealings with Indian soldiers.
*   Indian soldiers were banned from wearing turbans, tilak, and beards.
*   They were required to serve overseas, which went against the religious sentiments of some.

All these reasons incited the Indian troops against the British.
