This section defines the sex ratio, provides its formula, and describes the trends and variations in sex ratio in India.

# Sex Ratio

The **sex ratio** is defined as the number of females per 1000 males. It is calculated using the following formula:

$$
\text{Sex ratio} = \frac{\text{Population of Female}}{\text{Population of Male}} \times 1000
$$

Historically, the proportion of males has been higher than females in India. At the beginning of the twentieth century (1901 AD), the sex ratio was 972 females per 1000 males. This rate generally declined in the following decades but showed some improvement in the decade leading up to 2011 AD, increasing to 943.

Some states have a sex ratio favorable to women, meaning they have more females than males per 1000 males. Kerala has a sex ratio of 1084, and Puducherry has 1037 (as per 2011 data).

(Source: Ministry of Census of India Year: 2011)
