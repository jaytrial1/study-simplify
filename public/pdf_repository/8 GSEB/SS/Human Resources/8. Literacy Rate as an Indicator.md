This section defines literacy, provides the formula for the literacy rate, and discusses literacy trends and variations across states in India, including its correlation with population growth.

# Literacy Rate

A person aged 7 years or above who can read and write in at least one language is considered **literate**.

The **literacy rate** is calculated using the formula:

$$
\text{Literacy rate} = \frac{\text{Population of literate person aged seven years and above}}{\text{Population of 7 years age or more}} \times 100
$$

Literacy is generally linked to attending school, whether through formal or informal education. India's literacy rate has significantly increased from 5 percent in 1901 to 74.04 percent in 2011. In 2011, approximately 82.1 percent of men and 65.4 percent of women in India were literate.

Kerala has the highest literacy rate among Indian states at 93.91 percent, followed by Lakshadweep (92.28 percent) and Mizoram (91.58 percent). Bihar has the lowest literacy rate at 63.82 percent (2011 data).

According to the 2011 census, there is a positive correlation between population growth and illiteracy. States with very low literacy rates, such as Arunachal Pradesh, Rajasthan, Jharkhand, and Andhra Pradesh, also tend to have high rates of population growth. Women's literacy is considered particularly important in this context.
