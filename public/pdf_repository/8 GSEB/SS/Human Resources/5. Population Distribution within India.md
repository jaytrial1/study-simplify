This section focuses on the uneven distribution of population specifically within India, highlighting differences between regions and states based on geography and resources.

# Distribution of Population in India

Population distribution within India is highly uneven. Plains are significantly more populated than mountains, deserts, and forests. While larger states might seem more populated, the presence of plain land is a key factor. Consequently, states, districts, and urban areas along rivers tend to have higher populations.

There is considerable disparity in state-wise population distribution due to differences in area and resource availability. For example, the small Himalayan state of Sikkim has a population of only 6.10 lakh, while Uttar Pradesh has a population of 19.98 crore. Ten states in India have a population exceeding 50 million. More than half of the country's population resides in just five states: Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Bihar, West Bengal, and Andhra Pradesh.

However, a large area does not always mean a huge population. Rajasthan, the largest state by area, holds only about 5.66 percent of India's population. Madhya Pradesh, the second largest by area, has about 6 percent of the population. In contrast, Uttar Pradesh, with 7.26 percent of the area, has about 16.51 percent of the population, and Bihar, with only 2.6 percent of the area, holds about 8.60 percent of the population.
