This section discusses how the population is engaged in different economic sectors (agriculture, industry, services) in India compared to developed countries and the implications for economic growth and employment.

# Workforce Distribution and Economic Structure

India's economic structure shows a significant imbalance in workforce distribution. About two-thirds (approximately 66 percent) of the population remains dependent on agriculture, including related activities like fisheries, forestry, and animal husbandry. This contrasts sharply with countries like the USA, where only 5 percent of the population is involved in such primary activities, and Japan, with slightly less than 10 percent.

In developed countries, roughly one-fourth of the workforce is engaged in the **industrial sector** (secondary activities), which is crucial for growth value, increasing national wealth and income. In India, only about 10 percent of the population is engaged in this sector, hampered by lack of capital and modern technology.

About one-fourth of India's total labor force is associated with the **tertiary sector**, which includes social facilities and services like health, education, transport, communication, banking, insurance, entertainment, and arts.

The large proportion of the population dependent on agriculture suggests significant **underemployment** in this sector. Shifting this workforce to other sectors could increase the gross national product and national income, potentially improving living standards.

The proportion of people in secondary and tertiary occupations in India is about 13 percent and 20 percent, respectively. India has a relatively young population compared to other countries, expected to grow by 32 percent in 20 years. Investing in quality health, education, and training skills can harness this **demographic benefit**, leading to increased growth potential and providing employment opportunities for the youth, transforming the young population into a valuable human resource. Approximately 19.4 percent of India's population is young, with Gujarat having a similar figure of 19.45 percent.
