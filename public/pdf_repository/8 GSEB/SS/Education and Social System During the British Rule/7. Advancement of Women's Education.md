# Women's Education

Women's education started in Sirampur. From 1813 AD to 1851 AD, Christian priests and wealthy families established institutions for educating girls in Bengal, Mumbai, and Madras (Chennai). Figures like **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** and **Dayanand Saraswati** advocated for women's education in the 19th century. However, the government largely neglected women's education during this period.

In 1849-50 AD, **Bethune**, an English educator in Bengal, and the ardent Brahmo Samaji **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** established the **Hindu Balika Government School** in 1849 AD, strengthening the foundation for women's education in India. By 1873 AD, there were 1640 girls' schools in British India. Despite this, only 4.89 percent of girls attended school, indicating that the state of female education remained poor throughout the 19th century.
