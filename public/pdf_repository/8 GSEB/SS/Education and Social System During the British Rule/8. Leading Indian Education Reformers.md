This section introduces the key Indian figures who championed educational reform during the British era, emphasizing their motivations and contributions.

# Role of Indian Reformers in Education

The 19th century saw the rise of a modern educated middle class in India, some of whom became prominent education reformers. They recognized that the lack of education was a primary cause of India's subjugation. They understood that national development required an educated populace. Consequently, these reformers undertook numerous educational activities individually, through institutions, or in collaboration with the British.

**Raja Ram Mohan Roy** was a pioneer among them. He founded **Brahmo Samaj** in 1828 AD and supported William Bentick's efforts to introduce English education, marking its beginning in India. **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**, another leading Brahmo Samaji, worked to establish schools in Bengal around 1849-1850 AD, setting up schools for both boys and girls.

In Maharashtra, **Mahadev Govind Ranade** and his wife **Ramabai Ranade** founded schools for girls and widows. **Jyotiba Phule** and his wife **Savitribai Phule** also established schools for girls and widows. **Maharshi Karve** founded a separate university for women in 1916 AD, now known as **SNDT University**. In Ahmedabad, **Harkunwar Sethani** established a girls' school called **'Chhodiyoni Nishal'** in 1850 AD.

Disciples of **Dayanand Saraswati**, a significant social reformer, founded the **Dayanand Anglo Vedic College** in Lahore. His disciple **Swami Shraddhananda** started **Kangadi Gurukul** in 1902 AD. To promote education among Muslims, **Sir Syed Ahmed Khan** established an English school in Ghazipur in 1864 AD and the **Anglo-Mohammedan Oriental College** at Aligarh in 1875 AD.

Some native kingdoms also excelled in education. **Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad of Vadodara** introduced free, compulsory, and universal primary education in his state in 1901 AD. He also provided scholarships for students to study abroad. His wife, **Chimnabai Gaekwad**, enacted a law granting scholarships to Dalit students for foreign studies. Similarly, **Maharaja Bhagwat Singhji of Gondal** established girls' schools in the late 19th century and provided free, compulsory, and universal education for girls.
