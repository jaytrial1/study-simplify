This section details the initial efforts by foreigners, particularly Christian missionaries and early British initiatives, to introduce a Western-style education system in India.

# Early Western Education Initiatives

Western-style education in India was initially introduced by foreigners. The first such institution was established by William Carey in Sirampur, near Kolkata, in 1789 AD. This institution taught subjects like Sanskrit, Bengali, Marathi, Hindi, Ramayana, and Grammar. In Sirampur, his colleagues Marshman and his wife founded a girls' school. Thus, British individuals involved in missionary activities were among the first to establish this new education system in Bengal.
