This section specifically addresses the issue of child marriage and the legislative and social efforts made to combat it.

# Combating Child Marriage

Upon the British arrival, the harmful practice of child marriage was widespread. Child marriages often resulted in the exploitation of women in mismatched unions and hindered the development of both partners, limiting academic progress and overall growth.

Another Brahmo Samaji leader, **Keshavchandra Sen**, extensively campaigned against child marriage in 1870 AD, considering it a significant societal evil that impeded physical, mental, and academic development. His efforts led to the passage of the **Child Marriage Restraint Act in 1872 AD**, which made marriage illegal for boys and girls under the age of 12.

### Know This (Laws Against Child Marriage)

Child marriage was a detrimental practice for women. With Keshavchandra Sen's efforts, the 'Indian Reform Association' was founded in 1870 AD. In 1872 AD, Brahm marriage rules were formed. The minimum marriage age was fixed at 10 years in 1846 AD. The 'Age of Consent Act' (March 19, 1891) increased the girl's marriage age from 10 to 12 years. The "Sharda Act" in 1930 AD raised the girl's marriage age to 14 years. After independence, this age was further raised to 15 years in 1949 AD and 18 years in 1978 AD. These laws were gradually adopted. The influence of Western culture, the decline of the joint family system, and the spread of education have legally helped stop the practice of child marriage.
