# Reform Organizations and Social Reform

Institutions founded in the 19th century were crucial in bringing about social reforms. The **Brahmo Samaj**, established by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** in 1828 AD, was a foremost organization. Roy was a pioneer of social reforms. He founded the **'Atmiya Sabha'** in 1815 AD. He launched a major campaign against the Sati system in Bengal, publishing a magazine called **'Samvad Kaumudi'** in 1821 AD. Due to the efforts of Brahmo Samaj, **William Bentick** enacted a law prohibiting the Sati system in 1829 AD, eradicating this centuries-old evil.

Similarly, Brahmo Samaj's efforts led to laws against **'human sacrifice' (Narbali)** and **'female infanticide' (Dudhpiti)**. After Raja Ram Mohan Roy, the noted Brahmo Samaji **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** advocated for widow remarriage through his magazine **'Somprakash'**. He highlighted the difficult lives of young widows in society. **Dalhousie** passed the **Widow Remarriage Act** in 1856 AD, legalizing it and removing a major social evil.

**Mahatma Gandhi** held strong views against untouchability. He prioritized the emancipation of Scheduled Castes in his constructive work. Gandhi housed a Scheduled Caste family, Dudabhai and his family, in his ashram. **Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad** also worked for the welfare of Scheduled Castes. **Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar** dedicated his life to fighting for the social, educational, and political rights of Scheduled Castes, organizing Satyagrahas for temple entry and access to public places. **Mamasaheb Phadke** established a school for Scheduled Caste children in Godhra. **Parikshit Lal Majumdar** founded schools and hostels for Scheduled Castes, promoting their educational development. **Thakkar Bapa** worked in tribal areas, establishing ashrams and arranging education to bring them into the mainstream. The reform process was gradual but progressed positively, leading to improvements seen today.
