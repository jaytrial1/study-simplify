This section provides a brief overview of the state of education in India before and during the early phase of British rule, highlighting traditional systems and initial limitations.

# Education in India Before British Rule

Ancient India boasted renowned educational institutions like Takshshila, Nalanda, Vallabhi, and Vikramshila, though many eventually declined. During the Mughal period, particularly under Akbar, education in Persian, Urdu, and local languages was widespread. Following the decline of the Mughal Empire, teaching continued in schools associated with Hindu monasteries and madarsas linked to mosques. However, these systems were often limited and primarily offered primary-level education, restricting the broader scope of learning. English rule began in India in 1757 AD, and their administrative power (Diwani) was established in Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha after 1765 AD. The British policy during this period hindered the proper expansion of education across India.
