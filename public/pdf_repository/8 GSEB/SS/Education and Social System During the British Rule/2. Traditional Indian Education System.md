# Traditional Education Before British Influence

Before the arrival of the British, a prominent method of education existed in India. Educational institutions were commonly referred to as village schools, Pandya's schools, or traditional schools (Dhulia Nishalo). This system lacked formal structure; schools were often set up in public village spaces, like under a banyan tree. There was no standardized curriculum, textbooks, or formally trained teachers.

The teacher tailored education to the child's family background. Learning typically began orally with numbers and tables, followed by basics and the alphabet. Written education commenced later. The teacher determined the course content. Teachers did not receive a fixed salary; parents paid based on their financial capacity. Oral education was emphasized. There was no system for grouping students by standard, and the teacher had discretion over what to teach.
