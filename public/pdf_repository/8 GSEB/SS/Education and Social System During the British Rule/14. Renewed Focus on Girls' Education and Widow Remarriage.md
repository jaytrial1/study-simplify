# Promoting Girls' Education and Widow Remarriage

The negative consequences of denying education to women were evident. Depriving nearly half of society of education was harmful, especially considering women's crucial role in raising children; lack of education for women could cripple future society. Thus, girls' education was essential.

**Wood's dispatch** advised the Governor General to take steps to promote women's education among Indians. The proposed grant-in-aid system included establishing educational institutions for girls.

**Governor General Dalhousie** had a positive view on women's education. Guiding the Council for Education, he stated that the government must accept responsibility for education as outlined in the 1854 dispatch, leading to the opening of many new girls' schools.

### Widow Remarriage

In medieval India, widows were generally not allowed to remarry. As men were often the primary earners, their death made life difficult for widows. Educating women, supporting them financially, and allowing remarriage were seen as necessary to improve their condition.

Widow remarriage was practiced in ancient India but later ceased. Social reformers made significant efforts to revive it. **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** and **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** were prominent figures in this movement, publishing books and pamphlets to raise awareness. In Maharashtra, **Mahadev Govind Ranade, Ramabai Ranade, Jyotirao Phule, Savitribai Phule, Maharshi Karve, and Gopalkrishna Gokhale** also campaigned extensively for widow remarriage and against child marriage. They challenged these evils and established widow ashrams and institutions for women's education.

In Gujarat, **Durgaram Mehtaji** founded the **Manav Dharmasabha** in Surat in 1844 AD, working against superstition and blind faith. Great reformers like **Narmad, Karsandas Mulji, Mahipatram Rooparam, and Dalpatram** also agitated against child marriage and the prohibition of widow remarriage. **Narmad** set an example by marrying a widow.

A bill known as the **Widow Remarriage Act** was introduced by J.B. Grant, a member of the Governor General's Council, and passed in 1856 AD. Under this law, Srichandra Vidyaratna (a Sanskrit College professor) and Kalimati Devi were married in Kolkata. **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar** was highly influential in the field of widow remarriage. **Kandukuri Veerasalingam** in Andhra and **Mahadev Govind Ranade, D. K. Karve, R. G. Bhandarkar, and B. M. Malabari** in Western India also did commendable work.
