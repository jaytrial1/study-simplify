# Ramakrishna Mission and Swami Vivekananda

**Ramakrishna Paramahansa** was a revered saint near Kolkata. His personality attracted **Narendranath Dutt**, a young graduate from the University of Kolkata, who became his disciple and later known as **Swami Vivekananda**. (Born January 12, 1863, son of Vishwanath Dutt and Bhuvaneshwari Devi). Swami Vivekananda founded the **Ramakrishna Mission in 1897 AD** to propagate Ramakrishna Paramahansa's liberal religious ideas and dedication to humanitarian service.

Swami Vivekananda attended the **World Parliament of Religions in Chicago, USA (1893 AD)**, captivating the audience by addressing them as 'Brothers and Sisters'. He subsequently traveled globally, spreading Indian culture.

### Teachings and Influence

He strongly opposed prevalent social evils and religious practices, preaching social service and reform. He famously stated he didn't believe in a religion or God unable to alleviate suffering. He emphasized, "First food, then religion," seeing God in every human being and believing "Service to man is service to God." He inspired the youth with the call, 'Arise, Awake and stop not until the goal is reached.' His speeches reflected profound wisdom, experience, and powerful language, making him a symbol of new thought and a source of strength for the future.

The British structured the education system primarily to recruit clerks for smooth government operation, which limited its broader momentum in India. To address deeply entrenched social vices, reformers established organizations and used pamphlets, books, and meetings to raise awareness. The British government also enacted laws to eradicate these vices. Over time, society gradually accepted these changes, progressing towards improvement.
