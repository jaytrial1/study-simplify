This section examines the establishment and growth of higher and technical education institutions under British rule, including key colleges and universities.

# Higher and Technical Education Development

Following the establishment of British rule, Western-style higher education was introduced. Initially, **Wellesley** founded **Fort William College** in Calcutta in 1801 AD. In 1817 AD, through the efforts of **David Hare** and **Vaidyanath Mukherjee**, **Hindu College** was established in Calcutta, teaching Indian languages, English, Mathematics, History, Geography, and Astronomy. It became known as **Presidency College** in 1855 AD. Earlier, to train staff for government offices, **Warren Hastings** established the **Calcutta Madrasa** (Muslim College) in 1780 AD, and **Jonathan Duncan** founded the **Banaras Sanskrit College** in 1791 AD. **Amherst** established a Sanskrit College in Calcutta in 1823 AD.

A significant reform for higher education, often called the 'Magna Carta' of Indian education, came through **Wood's dispatch in 1854 AD**. This dispatch specifically recommended establishing a university in each province, creating a separate education department, maintaining government colleges and schools, providing government grants to private schools, starting teacher training institutes, expanding vocational education, establishing primary and secondary schools in every taluka and district, and promoting women's education. It also provided scholarships.

The Wood's dispatch led to the appointment of a director of education in each province and the creation of a separate education department. Universities modeled after the University of London were established in **Kolkata, Mumbai, and Madras (Chennai)**. Universities were also founded in **Punjab and Allahabad** in 1882 AD. **Elphinstone College** was established in Mumbai in 1844 AD.

These institutions across India provided higher education. In 1904 AD, **Curzon** passed a university law that reduced elected members and gave places to nominated members in the Senate. In the 20th century, important higher learning institutions like **Banaras Hindu University (1916 AD)**, **Jamia Millia University (1920 AD)**, and **Shantiniketan Visva-Bharati University (1922 AD)** were established. By 1946 AD, India had 16 universities, which were considered inadequate for a nation with a large population and geographical area to promote higher education widely.
