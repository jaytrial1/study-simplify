This section explains how the states of India were restructured after independence, moving from initial categories to states formed primarily based on language.

# Formation and Reorganization of States

After the integration of native states, the question arose about forming states within the Indian Union. Initially, the units formed by combining former British Provinces and native states were divided into four categories: A, B, C, and D, with unequal status. This was a temporary arrangement.

*   **Demand for Linguistic States:** After India became a republic, there was a strong demand for forming states based on language. Fierce agitations began in some areas. Telugu-speaking people in Madras state demanded the formation of Andhra Pradesh. The central government accepted this demand, and a separate state of **Andhra Pradesh** was formed in 1953 AD.
*   **State Reorganization Commission:** Following the formation of Andhra Pradesh, other language groups also demanded separate states based on language. To address this, a **State Reorganization Commission** was formed, chaired by former Supreme Court Justice Dr. Faizal Ali.
*   **The 1956 Reorganization:** The commission submitted its report, which came into effect on November 1, 1956. The previous four-category system was abolished. India was reorganized into **14 States and 6 Union Territories**.
*   **Exceptions and Further Reorganization:** Most of these states were formed based on language, but Punjab and Mumbai were exceptions. Mumbai was bilingual, with Marathi and Gujarati speakers. There was a strong demand for separate states for these language groups, leading to the **Mahagujarat movement**. Leaders like Indulal Yagnik, Bhailalbhai, and Brahmakumar Bhatt were prominent in this movement. As a result, separate states for Marathi and Gujarati speakers were formed. The new state of **Gujarat** was inaugurated by Ravishankar Maharaj on May 1, 1960, at Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad. Mehndi Nawaz Jung became the first Governor, and Dr. Jivraj Mehta became the first Chief Minister.
*   **Subsequent State Formations:** Punjab was divided into Punjab and Haryana in 1966 AD. Over time, seven states were formed in Northeast India: Assam, Nagaland, Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh, and Mizoram, known as the **'Seven Sister States'**. Demands for smaller states continued. In 2000 AD, Jharkhand was formed from Bihar, Chhattisgarh from Madhya Pradesh, and Uttarakhand from Uttar Pradesh. In 2014, Telangana was created from Andhra Pradesh.
*   **Current Structure:** As of October 2019, Jammu and Kashmir and Ladakh were made Union Territories. India currently consists of **28 States, 8 Union Territories, and the National Capital Territory of Delhi**.
