This section covers the immediate aftermath of India's independence, including the challenges posed by partition, the influx of refugees, and the need to integrate princely states and foster national unity.

# India Becomes Independent: Initial Challenges

Our country became independent on August 15, 1947. This was a moment of tremendous joy, but it also brought significant challenges. With the partition of India, two nations, India and Pakistan, were created. This resulted in a large movement of people, with about 8 million refugees coming to India from Pakistan.

*   **Challenge 1: Refugee Resettlement:** Accommodating these refugees and providing them with employment was a huge problem. The Government of India handled this tactfully, with support from the people. Large refugee camps were opened, and after resettlement, the refugees integrated and began new lives.
*   **Challenge 2: Integration of Princely States:** At the time of independence, there were 562 small and large independent states within India. Convincing these states to join the Union of India was a daunting task.
*   **Challenge 3: Building a Nation:** It was also challenging to create a constitution and a political system that would meet the expectations of the people.
*   **Challenge 4: National Integration:** India had a population of around 35 crores in 1947, with diverse languages, dress, regions, and cultures. Developing national integration despite these differences was essential.
*   **Challenge 5: Socio-Economic Issues:** A large part of the population lived in villages and depended on farming. Cities faced issues like dirt, overcrowding, poverty, and lack of education and health facilities. Rural areas struggled with agriculture and employment. Development with national unity was the goal.
