This section describes how the territories previously controlled by France and Portugal in India were successfully merged into the Indian Union after independence.

# Merger of French and Portuguese Institutions into the Union of India

While the Indian states had joined the Union by January 26, 1950, territories controlled by France and Portugal remained separate.

*   **French Territories:** Puducherry, Mahe, Yanam, Karaikal, and Chandranagar were under French control. Local residents started a **'Hind Chhodo' (Quit India) campaign** against the French government. The Government of India negotiated peacefully with France. Recognizing the strong desire of the people to join India, France handed over its colonies. On October 13, 1954, France ceded its territories to India.
*   **Portuguese Territories:** Diu, Daman, Goa, Dadra, and Nagar Haveli were occupied by the Portuguese. The Portuguese were more resistant than the French. Despite India's attempts at persuasion and negotiation, the Portuguese government refused to hand over the territories. Locals started the **'Goa Liberation Movement'** to join India. Many people participated in satyagrahas (non-violent protests), and some became martyrs.
*   **Operation Vijay:** Since peaceful methods did not work, the Indian government launched **'Operation Vijay'** under the leadership of General Chaudhary to liberate Goa. On December 18, 1961, the Portuguese rulers surrendered, and Goa, Diu, and Daman were merged with India.
