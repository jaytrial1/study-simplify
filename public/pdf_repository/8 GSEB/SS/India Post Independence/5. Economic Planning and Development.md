This section focuses on India's approach to economic development after independence, including the establishment of planning bodies and the impact of Five Year Plans and key agricultural revolutions.

# Five Year Plans and Economic Development of India

To achieve the goals of the Constitution, the Government of India established the **Planning Commission** in 1950 AD. This body is now known as the **'NITI Aayog'**. It consists of experts in economics, administration, and various fields, chaired by the Prime Minister.

*   **Objectives of Planning:** India's economic planning has both long-term and short-term goals, aiming for:
    *   Rapid economic growth and development
    *   Reduced economic inequality
    *   Full employment
    *   Self-reliance
    *   Price stability
    *   Educational development
    *   And more.
*   **First Five Year Plan (1951-56):** The first plan focused on planned economic development. Heavy industries were established for industrial growth, making India almost self-sufficient in industrial production.
*   **Agricultural Revolutions:**
    *   **Green Revolution:** Increased grain production significantly, turning India from a grain importer into an exporter.
    *   **White Revolution:** Increased milk production.
    *   **Yellow Revolution:** Increased oilseed production.
*   **Addressing Poverty:** Poverty alleviation programs were implemented to reduce poverty and economic inequality.
*   **Impact of Planning:** As a result of the Five Year Plans, national income and per capita income increased. However, poverty numbers did not reduce as much as hoped, partly due to population growth.
*   **Continuous Efforts:** The government has continuously worked to reduce poverty and inequality, increase employment, promote education, and ensure essential goods are available to the poor.
*   **Health and Education:** Public health has improved. Smallpox was eradicated. Average life expectancy increased, and child mortality decreased. Literacy rate (education) also increased.
