This section details the process by which the numerous independent princely states within India were persuaded and integrated into the newly formed Indian Union.

# Unification and Merger of Native States

The most immediate problem after independence was the amalgamation of 562 native states into the Indian Union. These states varied greatly in size, from large ones like Kashmir, Hyderabad, and Mysore to very small ones. Persuading all of them to join India was a mammoth task.

*   **Early Steps:** Maharaja Krishnakumar Singh of Bhavnagar was the first king to start a 'responsible government' in his state. Through the efforts of Sardar Patel, the United State of Saurashtra was formed, and Bhavnagar was merged into it. Jawaharlal Nehru described this event as "the most significant unification in contemporary history."
*   **Sardar Patel's Role:** After independence, Deputy Prime Minister and Home Minister Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel appealed to the native kings to voluntarily merge their states with India, considering the interests of their people and the Indian Union. He used practical intelligence and awakened patriotism in the kings.
*   **The Integration Process:** With the efforts of Sardar Patel and his secretary V. P. Menon, almost all states except Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir were merged into the Union of India.
    *   **Hyderabad:** Hyderabad was merged with the Indian union through **police action**. Assurance was given to protect the Nizam's interests. Kanhaiyalal Munshi played an important role in this process.
    *   **Junagadh:** The Nawab of Junagadh initially signed a treaty to join Pakistan. However, citizens of Junagadh in Mumbai formed the 'Aarzi Hukumat' (temporary government) to push for joining India. Most states and people of Saurashtra opposed the Nawab's decision. The Nawab migrated to Pakistan, and India took control of Junagadh. A **referendum** was held, and the people overwhelmingly supported joining India. Thus, Junagadh was merged with the Union of India.
    *   **Kashmir:** Maharaja Harisinh Dogra of Kashmir did not initially sign a treaty with India. Meanwhile, Pakistan invaded Kashmir to annex it. Harisinh sought India's military help and signed a pact to merge with India. The Indian army defended Kashmir, but Pakistan illegally occupied one-third of the territory, now known as P.O.K. (Pakistan Occupied Kashmir). India complained to the UN Security Council, which called for a ceasefire. Although one-third remains occupied by Pakistan, Kashmir is legally considered a part of India.
