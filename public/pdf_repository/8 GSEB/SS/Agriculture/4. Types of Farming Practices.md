# Types of Farming Practices

Farming methods vary widely across the world, influenced by geography, market demand, irrigation, technology, labor, and economic considerations.

## Subsistence Farming
*   Characteristics: Practiced by farmers with limited economic means, using small farms. Cannot afford expensive inputs like improved seeds, fertilizers, or pesticides.
*   Goal: Produce just enough grain to feed the farmer's own family.
*   Crops: Mainly cereal crops.
*   Trend: Area under this type is gradually decreasing.

## Shifting (Jhoom) Farming
*   Method: Practiced by tribes in forests by cutting and burning trees, clearing land, and mixing ash into the soil for fertility.
*   Duration: Cultivation for two-three years until soil fertility declines.
*   Process: Farmers then abandon the site and move to a new location to repeat the process.
*   Crops: Cereal crops and vegetables.
*   Production: Very low.
*   Distribution: Currently found in deep forests of the Amazon Basin, tropical Africa, Southeast Asia, and small amounts in Northeastern India.

## Intensive Farming
*   Method: Modern farming using extensive irrigation, improved seeds, new technology, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and machinery.
*   Goal: High production per hectare, focused on economic returns.
*   Also known as: 'Commercial farming'.
*   Crops: Mainly *cash crops*.
*   Trend: Area under this cultivation is constantly increasing.
*   Distribution in Gujarat: Surat, Valsad, Anand districts.

## Dry Farming
*   Conditions: Practiced in areas with low rainfall, inadequate irrigation, or low-lying areas where water collects during monsoon.
*   Method: Cultivation is done after the stored water dries up.
*   Crops: Those requiring less water, like jowar, millet, and pulses. Wheat and chickpea are grown this way in moist soil after monsoon in Bhal Pradesh of Gujarat.

## Wet Farming
*   Conditions: Practiced in areas with high rainfall and good irrigation facilities.
*   Method: Irrigation allows growing more than one crop per year, even with less rainfall.
*   Crops: Paddy, sugarcane, cotton, wheat, and vegetables.

## Horticulture (Plantation Farming)
*   Method: Cultivation focused on caring for orchards and perennial crops.
*   Characteristics: Once planted, crops yield produce seasonally or year-round for many years.
*   Crops: Rubber, tea, coffee, cocoa, coconut, apple, mango, orange, grape, gooseberry, lemon, guava, berries, date, etc., cultivated with care.
