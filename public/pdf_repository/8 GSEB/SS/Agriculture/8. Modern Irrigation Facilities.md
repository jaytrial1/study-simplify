# Modern Irrigation Facilities

Adequate water is essential for farming. Due to uncertain rainfall in India, sufficient water is not always available naturally, necessitating artificial irrigation systems. Irrigation involves artificially watering plants using wells, borewells, and checkdams.

## Canal Irrigation
*   Method: Water stored in dams (like Sardar Sarovar, Dharoi in Gujarat) is transported to farms via canals.
*   Caution: Continuous overuse of canal water can reduce soil fertility.

## Farm-pond
*   Method: A pond built on the sloping land ridges within a farm to collect and store rainwater.
*   Use: Provides water for irrigation after the monsoon ends when crops still need water.

## Checkdam
*   Method: A strong barrier built across a river, spring, or stream to hold back water flow.
*   Benefit: Helps raise the water level in nearby wells and borewells.

*   Method: A type of *micro-irrigation* where water and nutrients are delivered slowly and directly to the plant roots through a network of valves, pipes ('dripperlines'), and small emitters ('drippers'). Also called *trickle irrigation*.
*   Goal: Reduce evaporation and deliver water precisely to the root zone.
*   Benefits: Saves water (40-60%), saves fertilizer (25-30%), saves labor (no need for check basins, furrows, etc.), effective on undulating land, reduces weed growth and associated costs, reduces pest spread by avoiding waterlogging, saves electricity (30-35%).
*   Suitability: More useful for crops planted with space between rows. Suitable crops include cotton, castor, sugarcane, tomato, eggplant, cabbage, cauliflower, potato, lady finger, chilli, mango, chiku, lemon, coconut, pomegranate, papaya, guava, berry, tobacco, teak.
*   Effectiveness: Depends on proper design, maintenance, and implementation. More effective than other methods when done correctly.

## Sprinkler Irrigation System
*   Method: Water is supplied using a pump, valve, pipe, and a rotating nozzle that sprays water over the ground like raindrops.
*   Uses: Useful in agricultural fields, parks, lawns, landscapes, golf courses, and for cooling or settling dust.
*   Suitability: Useful for uneven land, sandy soil, and short-term crops.
*   Suitable Crops: Wheat, millet, jowar, maize, groundnut, gram, onion, ryegrass, fodder crops, fenugreek, cottonseed, cauliflower, cabbage, lady finger, potatoes, sugarcane.

Using modern irrigation systems helps conserve water and maintain soil quality, leading to increased agricultural production.
