This section emphasizes the critical need to conserve resources for present and future generations and outlines practical measures for conservation.

# Conservation of Resources

Resources are indispensable for human life; our existence is unimaginable without them. With population growth and technological advancements, resource use is increasing rapidly. This overuse is depleting some resources, pushing them towards exhaustion. Continued human progress and modern lifestyles depend on the availability of resources. Considering future needs, resources must be used in a planned and wise manner. This is known as **resource conservation**.

## Measures of Resource Conservation

Various measures can be taken to conserve resources:

-   **Soil Conservation:** Prevent soil erosion, as it reduces fertility. Use chemical fertilizers judiciously and favor natural manure to maintain soil quality long-term. Avoid excessive irrigation, which lowers productivity; increase the use of drip irrigation instead. Encourage bio-pesticides as an alternative to chemical pesticides, which pollute soil and water.
-   **Wildlife Conservation:** Enact stringent laws against hunting to protect wildlife, which is crucial for ecological balance.
-   **Forest and Pasture Conservation:** Take special measures to prevent deforestation and protect pasture lands.
-   **Water Conservation:** Prevent water scarcity through rainwater harvesting. Increase water storage capacity in ponds and lakes by removing silt and deepening them. Construct check dams to stop rainwater flow and solve agricultural water problems. Use recycled water. Reuse household water (e.g., from washing vegetables) for kitchen gardens.
-   **Energy Conservation:** As traditional energy resources become scarce, explore and utilize alternatives like solar energy, wind energy, and tidal energy.
-   **General Resource Use:** **Reuse** resources that can be used again. For non-renewable resources, find and use **alternatives**.

It is the collective responsibility of everyone to conserve resources to meet the needs of future generations.

## Think and Say

-   What will happen if resources become scarce?
-   A pond near a village or town used to get filled with rainwater. After a few years, it was completely filled with dumping of village waste. So, now what must be the situation there every monsoon?

> **Answer (Resource Scarcity):** If resources become scarce, it will lead to increased costs, reduced availability of essential goods and services, potential conflicts over remaining resources, slower economic development, and a decline in the quality of life for humans.
> **Answer (Pond Filled with Waste):** If the pond is filled with waste, it will no longer be able to store rainwater. During the monsoon, the water will likely overflow, potentially flooding surrounding areas, or it will simply run off, not recharging groundwater. The waste will also pollute the area and any water that collects.
