This section explains how resources are categorized based on their origin and availability.

# Classification of Resources

Resources are primarily classified into two main types based on their formation:
1.  **Natural Resources**
2.  **Human-made Resources**

## Natural Resources

A **natural resource** is something obtained directly from nature that can be used with minimal processing. Examples include air, water, land, minerals, and energy resources. These are essentially gifts from nature that are suitable for human use.

Natural resources can be further classified based on their **renewability** and **distribution**.

### Key Concepts & Examples

-   **Crude oil** is a *non-renewable* resource, meaning it will eventually run out. Its increasing price affects the cost of many goods.
-   Burning large amounts of coal and petroleum products contributes to rising Earth temperatures.
-   Vehicles using petrol and diesel release pollutants like carbon monoxide and nitrous oxide.
-   Using Compressed Natural Gas (**CNG**) can help reduce pollution.
-   Future transportation may increasingly rely on battery power or solar energy.

The availability and variety of resources differ across the Earth. Based on their **distribution** or source, resources are classified as follows:

1.  **Ubiquitous Resources:** Available everywhere. Examples: Oxygen and Nitrogen in the atmosphere.
2.  **Common Resources:** Generally readily available. Examples: Water, pasture land.
3.  **Rare Resources:** Available only from limited places. Examples: Coal, various metals, mineral oil, natural gas, uranium.
4.  **Uniquities Resources:** Minerals found in only one or two places globally. Example: Cryolite from Greenland.

Resources are also categorized as **renewable** and **non-renewable**.

| Renewable Resource                                                               | Non-renewable resource                                                            |
| :------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning: Automatically replenish their quantity in a specific time period or are inexhaustible. | Meaning: Once used, it is impossible to renew them in the near future.            |
| Examples: Forests, animals and birds, sunlight, wind                             | Examples: Coal, Crude oil, natural gas, nuclear minerals                          |

## Human-made Resources

A **human-made resource** is a natural element that has been processed and modified by human effort to make it usable.

For instance, cement is a human-made resource created by processing natural materials like lime and gypsum. Other examples include electricity, buildings, roads, bridges, tunnels, and technology. Humans also alter the land itself, like creating terrace farms in mountains or multi-purpose river valley projects, which are forms of human-made resources or modifications. The list of human-made resources is extensive.

## Human Resources

Humans themselves are a powerful resource. Through their knowledge and skills, they utilize natural elements as resources. Humans are unique because they are both the **creator** and the **user** of resources. Using their intellect, they select and make the best use of what nature offers. Effective use of natural resources depends on human skills, abilities, knowledge, and technology. Education and health services are crucial in making humans a special resource. The process of developing human capabilities to become a better resource is called **human resource development**.
