# Specific Natural Resources - Land and Water

## Land

**Land** is one of the most fundamental resources due to its wide range of uses. It occupies about 29% of the Earth's surface. While this proportion seems small, not all of it is suitable for human habitation. Population density is heavily influenced by the varied characteristics of landforms and climate. Areas suitable for living tend to be densely populated, while deserts, dense forests, and mountainous regions are sparsely populated or uninhabited.

# Think and Say

-   The Gangetic plains are densely populated, whereas population density is low in Sahara desert. Why?

> **Answer:** The Gangetic plains have fertile soil, ample water from rivers, and a suitable climate for agriculture, supporting a large population. The Sahara desert, conversely, has extreme temperatures, scarce water, and barren land, making it very difficult to sustain human life in large numbers.

Land is essential not just for agriculture, minerals, and forests, but also for building roads and establishing industries. With the continuous growth in population, human needs are increasing, while the amount of land remains fixed.

**Land scarcity** has become a significant problem. The pressure to build more houses in cities and expand agricultural land in villages has led to environmental issues, including deforestation as land is cleared to meet human needs.

## Think and Say

-   Discuss the effects of deforestation in classroom.
-   Why is the number of high-rise buildings increasing in the metrocities of the world.

> **Answer (Deforestation Effects):** Effects include soil erosion, loss of habitat for wildlife, climate change (reduced carbon dioxide absorption, altered rainfall patterns), decreased groundwater levels, and loss of valuable forest products.
> **Answer (High-Rise Buildings):** High-rise buildings are increasing in metrocities because land is scarce and expensive. Building upwards allows more people and businesses to occupy a smaller land area, accommodating the growing urban population and economic activity.

## Water

The saying '**Water is life**' highlights its fundamental importance for all living things. Water is a vital **renewable natural resource**. About three-fourths of the Earth's surface is covered by water, with land covering only one-fourth. Although the total amount of water is vast, most of it (in seas and oceans) is saline and not directly usable. Only about 2.7% is freshwater. A large portion of this freshwater is locked up in ice sheets and glaciers in polar regions and mountain peaks. Consequently, only about 1% of the total water is available as groundwater, rivers, lakes, or springs, which is the amount used for agriculture, drinking, household needs, and industries. Water from rivers can also be used to generate hydroelectricity by building dams.

Today, many parts of the world face scarcity of drinking water, including regions in South America, Africa, the Middle East, and Australia. In India, areas like Saurashtra, Kutch, and North Gujarat in Gujarat, and parts of Rajasthan experience drinking water shortages, especially in summer, sometimes leading to droughts. Factors contributing to the water crisis include population growth, increased cultivation of water-intensive cash crops, modern lifestyles, urbanization, industrial use, and deforestation.

Make a list of the effects of drought.

> **Answer (Effects of Drought):** Effects include crop failure and reduced agricultural production, shortage of drinking water for humans and animals, drying up of rivers, lakes, and wells, increased risk of wildfires, dust storms, economic losses for farmers, and potential migration of people and animals seeking water.
