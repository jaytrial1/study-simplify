This section covers the significance of natural vegetation and wildlife, their interconnectedness, and the threats they face.

# Specific Natural Resources - Vegetation and Wildlife

**Natural vegetation** refers to plants that grow on their own, including creepers, shrubs, and trees. They adapt to different geographical conditions, altitudes, shapes, and forms. A large area covered by trees and shrubs is commonly called a forest. The presence and type of natural vegetation are distinctive features of different regions. Variations in altitude and climate lead to diversity in the structure and form of vegetation, which experts use for classification.

In an **ecosystem**, organisms survive by interacting with each other. Vegetation fulfills many human needs and provides habitat and food for animals (**fauna**). Forests provide numerous benefits: they help maintain the balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, provide timber and fuel wood, prevent soil erosion, help conserve groundwater, and offer various fruits, herbs, edible gum, and raw materials for industries.

**Wildlife** includes various animals, birds, and insects. They provide products like meat, leather, fur, and wool. Insects like bees are essential for pollination. Birds help control insect populations. Every organism, regardless of size, plays a unique role in the ecosystem's balance.

# Think and Say

-   If there are no pests, agricultural production will decrease. (This is a statement often used to emphasize the role of beneficial insects/organisms or the balance needed, not a question requiring an answer here).
-   Have you seen a field being ploughed? Why do the birds gather behind the plough or tractor?
-   If the number of birds decrease, what will be the effects on cultivation?

> **Answer (Birds behind Plough):** Birds gather behind the plough or tractor because the ploughing action exposes insects, worms, and larvae from the soil, which the birds then eat as food.
> **Answer (Effects of Bird Decrease):** If the number of birds decreases, there would be fewer natural predators for insects and pests that harm crops. This could lead to an increase in pest populations, potentially reducing agricultural yields and requiring more use of pesticides.

Over the last two centuries, humans have extensively cut down forests to acquire land, driven by increasing needs. This continuous decline in forest cover poses a serious threat to valuable forest resources and wildlife. Deforestation contributes to climate change and endangers the existence of many species. Human greed for animal products like horns, teeth, skin, fur, and bones, along with reckless hunting, has drastically reduced wildlife numbers.
