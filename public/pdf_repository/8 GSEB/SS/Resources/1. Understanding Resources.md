This section introduces the concept of resources, their definition, and how they become useful to humans.

# Understanding Resources

Resources form the basis for human societal development and economic growth. They are elements that satisfy our needs. While many things exist in nature, they are only considered resources when human skills make them useful for satisfying needs or desires. This usefulness is called **utility**.

For example, iron, other metals, and crude oil were not resources when early humans lacked the ability to mine or refine them. They became resources only after humans developed the technology to extract and use them. Similarly, wind is a significant resource today because of advancements in windmill technology for power generation. As science and technology progress, more natural elements are likely to become resources in the future.

> **Answer (Classroom):** Resources used in a classroom include desks and chairs (made from wood or metal), books and paper (made from trees), pens and pencils (made from various materials), electricity for lights and fans, and potentially water for drinking fountains.
