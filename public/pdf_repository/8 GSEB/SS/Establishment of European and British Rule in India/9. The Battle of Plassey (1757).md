This section describes the events leading up to and the outcome of the pivotal Battle of Plassey.

# The Battle of Plassey (1757 AD)

*   News of the British defeat at Calcutta reached Madras (Chennai).
*   The British quickly dispatched an army to Calcutta under the leadership of **Robert Clive**.
*   Clive used **diplomacy and bribery** to undermine the Nawab.
*   **Manekchand**, a trusted official of the Nawab, was bribed and handed Calcutta back to the British.
*   The British secured the support of the Nawab's commander-in-chief, **Mir Jafar**, by promising to make him the next Nawab.
*   They also gained the support of influential figures in Bengal, including **Jagat Seth, Rai Durlabh, and Amichand**.
*   In **March 1757 AD**, the British attacked the French colony, further challenging the Nawab's sovereignty.
*   On **June 23, 1757**, the battle took place near Murshidabad at a place called **Plassey**.
*   Despite the bravery of some of the Nawab's generals, the Nawab's army was defeated largely due to **Mir Jafar's betrayal**, as his large contingent did not fight.
*   Siraj-ud-Daula was captured and killed.
*   **Mir Jafar** was installed as the new Nawab.
*   The new Nawab granted the British the **'jagirdari'** (right to collect revenue) of **24 parganas** and allowed them to trade without paying excise duty.
*   The Battle of Plassey brought Bengal effectively under British control, marking a shift from being mere merchants to becoming rulers.
*   This victory is considered the beginning of the British conquest of India, which was largely complete by **1818 AD**.

## Know This

The original name of Plassey was **Palash**. The British pronounced it as Plassey. The area had many **Palash** trees (also known as Khakhro or Kesudo). A color used in the festival of Holi was traditionally prepared from the petals of these flowers.
