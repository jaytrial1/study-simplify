# Natural Disasters: Pandemic

An *epidemic* is a condition in which a disease affects many people over a wide area. When caused by a virus, the number of infected people increases much faster than with common diseases, and hundreds of people can lose their lives. Diseases like Ebola, Swine Flu, Dengue, and Corona have caused millions of deaths worldwide. In ancient times, without modern treatments, advanced medicines, and vaccines, people died from deadly diseases like plague and smallpox. However, today, many lives can be saved with proper treatment.

When existing medicines cannot treat diseases caused by new viruses, they can quickly spread globally, infecting many people. The death toll can be very high until a vaccine is developed to prevent the disease. Such diseases spread uncontrollably and affect large populations.

Unknown viral diseases continue to pose a threat to mankind. This reality must be accepted. Measures such as *lockdown* and *quarantine* of infected people must be strictly enforced to prevent the spread of such diseases when an outbreak occurs and no cure is available. This has a negative impact on a country's economic progress and educational performance. It can take years to recover from these effects, as experienced worldwide during the Corona outbreak in 2019-2020.

Until a vaccine is discovered, strict measures like lockdowns, travel bans, and quarantine of outsiders are necessary to prevent the spread of an epidemic. People's daily lives are disrupted when education, trade, and production stop. This creates great chaos. The hardships faced by millions who earn their living daily are immense. Despite official orders, people may still fall victim due to lack of awareness. Hospitals become overloaded, and even wealthy nations can feel helpless. Millions get infected, and thousands die. Centuries ago, millions worldwide died from cholera, plague, and malaria before vaccines were discovered. At one point, such epidemics led to a decline in the world's population.

During an epidemic, people should be made aware of necessary measures and precautions through various communication channels. Arranging separate wards or hospitals for infected people helps stop the spread. The World Health Organization (WHO) provides special guidance in such circumstances, which must be followed. People from outside should only be allowed to enter after adequate medical examination or quarantine, if necessary.

As human society progresses, new viral diseases continue to emerge, and until a vaccine is found, the world can be vulnerable to their impact.

### Know This

*   How was Covid-19 named?

> **Answer:** Covid-19 is the name given by the World Health Organization (WHO) on February 11, 2020, for the disease caused by the novel Corona virus SARS-Cov-2. It started in Wuhan, China, in late 2019 AD and has since spread worldwide. COVID-19 is an acronym for "corona virus disease of 2019".
