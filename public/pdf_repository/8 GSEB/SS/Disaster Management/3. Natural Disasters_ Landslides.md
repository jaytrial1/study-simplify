This section explains what landslides are, their causes (natural and human-related), and the significant damage they cause to infrastructure, settlements, and the environment.

# Natural Disasters: Landslide

A *landslide* is the sudden fall of a large mass of earth, rock, or debris down a mountain or slope. This phenomenon occurs most often in mountainous areas. Many people can die instantly by getting buried under the fallen material, leading to massive destruction of life and property.

Landslides are mainly caused by natural disasters such as:
*   Earthquakes
*   Cloudbursts
*   Floods

Other factors contributing to landslides include:
*   Mining activity using explosions in mountainous areas.
*   Deforestation (cutting down trees).
*   Cutting into mountains to build roads.
*   Obstruction of the natural flow of rainwater.
*   Construction activities on slopes.

Landslides cause severe damage to transportation roads. Human settlements located at the foot of hills or on slopes become severely damaged or destroyed. Many people and domesticated animals lose their lives. Rehabilitating injured people or those with permanent disabilities is a challenging task. Arable land becomes useless. Forests on mountain slopes are destroyed, changing the landscape of the area. Rebuilding roads and bridges is very expensive, resulting in massive economic losses.

Areas frequently affected by landslides in India include Kumaon and Garhwal in Uttarakhand and Darjeeling in West Bengal. In Gujarat, such incidents are rare in places like Saputara (Dang), Idar (Sabarkantha), and Ambaji-Danta (Banaskantha), and their occurrence is very limited compared to the Himalayan regions.

### Know This

*   You might have seen such a sign board while travelling in mountainous regions. What does it indicate?

> **Answer:** This sign is displayed to warn people in areas where a landslide is likely to occur.
