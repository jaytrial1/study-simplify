# Natural Disasters: Forest Fire

Disasters caused by natural forces or circumstances are called *natural disasters*. These inflict heavy loss of life and property over wide areas. Some natural disasters, like earthquakes, volcanic eruptions, forest fires, and landslides, are not easily predictable and cause great damage. However, precautionary measures can help prevent loss of life from predictable disasters such as floods, hurricanes, tsunamis, locust plagues, and epidemics.

## Forest Fire

Fires that spread through forests or grasslands are known as *forest fires*. Once started, the fire moves in the direction of the wind. Dry grass and leaves further help it spread quickly over a large area.

Human activity is often responsible for starting forest fires. Examples include:
*   Carelessly throwing away burning cigarettes or bidis.
*   Leaving temporary stoves used by herdsmen without properly extinguishing them.
*   Accidents involving electricity or gas lines passing through forests.
*   Negligence by tourists in forest areas.

Natural lightning can also cause forest fires.

Forest fires severely damage the *flora* (plants) and *fauna* (animals) in the affected area. It can take decades for a destroyed forest to recover naturally. The *ecological balance* of the area is disturbed, and nearby villages are also put at risk. To prevent forest fires, awareness should be spread among people living near forests, and the work of the forest department should be made more extensive and focused.

In India, fires have been reported in forests on the slopes of the Himalayas and in South India. Gujarat has also experienced forest fire incidents in the past.
