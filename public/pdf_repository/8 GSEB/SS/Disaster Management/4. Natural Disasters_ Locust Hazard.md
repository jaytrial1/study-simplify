This section describes locusts, their characteristics, behavior, and the severe damage they cause to crops and vegetation, leading to significant economic losses for farmers.

# Natural Disasters: Locust Hazard

A *locust* is a type of insect. It is estimated that there are approximately 11,000 species worldwide. They prefer to stay in groups, sometimes numbering in the millions. They are usually found in desert areas. Locusts are typically one and a half to two inches long and have strong wings for flying. Their front two legs are longer than their hind legs, and they have two antennae-like hairs on their heads. They can jump up to two and a half feet and tend to move in the direction of the wind.

The locusts that attack Gujarat are *desert locusts*. This species is found in India, Pakistan, and some arid countries in Africa. It is also known as a '*Voracious locust*' because it eats an amount equal to its own weight. As they mature, they lay their eggs in the sand. They like to fly during the day and rest at night.

The locusts originate from the southwestern tip of the African continent and enter Rajasthan and Gujarat in India through countries in the Middle East and Pakistan. A swarm can travel up to 200 km per day, depending on wind speed. They eat almost all crops and other vegetation except neem. Therefore, in areas where locusts rest overnight, they destroy crops and other vegetation. If locusts strike horticultural areas, orchards like mango, lemon, dry dates, pomegranate, and gooseberry, which have taken years to cultivate, are severely damaged. Farmers suffer huge economic losses. Many years ago, when locusts attacked, crops were completely destroyed, leading to drought conditions. However, today, famine can be prevented by transporting grain from other regions.

To repel locusts, people sometimes beat drums or make loud noises to stop them from entering fields. However, such efforts are often not enough. Currently, special measures are taken by the government to warn people about the direction of locust travel and the areas likely to be affected. In areas where a locust attack is possible, damage can be reduced by spraying locust-control pesticides as instructed by the Department of Agriculture.

Gujarat and Rajasthan are the major locust-affected states in India. Incidents of locust attacks have been reported in Kutch, Patan, Banaskantha, Surendranagar, and Mehsana districts of Gujarat in recent years.
