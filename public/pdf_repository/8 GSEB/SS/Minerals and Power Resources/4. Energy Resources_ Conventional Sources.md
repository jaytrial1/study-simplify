# Energy Resources
This section introduces the concept of energy resources and discusses conventional sources like fossil fuels.

Energy plays a very important role in our lives. We need energy for industry, agriculture, transportation, communication and defence. The resources that give us energy to operate machines and produce goods are called **energy resources**. Energy resources can broadly be classified into conventional and non-conventional resources.

## Conventional Sources
Conventional sources are those which have been in common use for a long time. Firewood and fossil fuels are the two main sources of conventional energy.

### Fossil Fuels
Coal is formed after a long process of decaying of vegetation for lakhs of years while crude oil is formed due to decaying of animals in the earth. These power sources are known as **fossil fuels**.

### Coal
This fossil fuel is found in abundance. It is used in domestic fuels, industries such as iron and steel, steam engines, for generating electricity etc. The electricity obtained from coal is called **thermal power**. Industrial development has become possible because of coal.

China, USA, Germany, Russia, South Africa and France are the world's leading coal-producing countries. Raniganj (West Bengal) and Jharia, Dhanbad and Bokaro (Jharkhand) are coal-producing areas of India. Coal fields in Gujarat are in Kutch, Bharuch, Mehsana, Bhavnagar and Surat. **Lignite** coal is obtained from here. It is estimated that Pandhro in Kutch, Tadkeshwar in Surat, Rajpardi in Bharuch, Thordi in Bhavnagar, Tagadi and Samatpar have reserves of lignite coal.

### Crude Oil
Crude oil is found from the earth's crust. It is obtained between the layers of rocks. It is obtained in semi liquid form and contains many substances. After extracting, it is sent for refining where various products like diesel, petrol, kerosene, wax, plastics and lubricants are processed from crude oil. Crude oil is very valuable. Useful substances are obtained from it. None of its part is useless. It is called **'black gold'** due to its wide commercial importance.

Iran, Iraq, Saudi Arabia and Qatar are the major producers of petroleum, followed by the USA, Russia, Venezuela and Algeria. The major producing areas in India are Digboi in Assam, Bombay High in Mumbai and the delta of the Krishna and Godavari rivers. In the beginning crude oil was obtained from Lunej of Anand district in Gujarat. Ankleshwar in Bharuch district is considered to be the largest oil field in Gujarat. Crude oil is found in Ankleshwar, Mehsana, Kalol, Kadi, Navagam, Kosamba, Sanand, Ahmedabad, Gandhinagar, Vadodara, Bharuch, Anand, Kheda and Bhavnagar areas.

### Natural Gas
Natural gas is found with petroleum deposits and is released when crude oil is extracted. It is used as a fuel for domestic and industrial purpose. Russia, Norway, UK and The Netherlands are major producers of natural gas.

Natural gas producing areas of India are Jaisalmer, Khambhat Basin, Krishna-Godavari Delta, Tripura and Bombay High. Ankleshwar and Gandhar of Gujarat are considered to be rich in crude oil and natural gas. Very few countries in the world have sufficient natural gas reserves of their own.

The sharp increase in use of fossil fuel consumption is leading to alarming depletion of it. The toxic pollutants released by burning these fuels is also a matter of concern. In this situation the load on conventional sources can be reduced by making extensive use of non-conventional energy sources.
