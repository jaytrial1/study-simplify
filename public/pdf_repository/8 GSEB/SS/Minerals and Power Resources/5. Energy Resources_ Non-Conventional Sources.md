# Non-conventional Sources of Energy
This section discusses alternative energy sources that are renewable and less polluting, unlike conventional fossil fuels.

Energy resources such as coal or crude oil are available in limited quantities. It is estimated that if present rate of consumption continues, reserves of these fuel will get exhausted. Moreover, their use also causes environmental pollution, so it is necessary to use non-conventional sources such as solar energy, wind energy, tidal energy which are renewable. Many countries of the world have taken steps in this direction. A Commission for Additional Sources of Energy (CASE) has been formed in India. Gujarat Energy Development Agency (GEDA) is working in this direction in Gujarat.

## Solar Energy
Sun is the main source of energy. We can feel the heat and light energy of the sun everyday. Solar energy trapped from the sun can be used in solar cells to produce electricity. The technology of using solar energy is very beneficial for tropical countries with abundant sunshine. Solar energy is also used in solar water-heaters, solar-cookers, solar dryers besides being used for community lighting and traffic signals. Solar energy is inexhaustible and pollution free.
'Solar Energy Project', Rewa in Madhya Pradesh is the largest solar energy project in Asia.

Gujarat is a leading state in the country in terms of solar energy. Attempts have been made to obtain solar energy through solar panels, on roof tops canal based solar projects. A 590 MW Solar Park is set up on unused land at Charanka village in Patan district in Gujarat. Gujarat Energy Development Agency (GEDA) has installed solar cold storage with capacity of 10 ton near Chhani (Vadodara). Gujarat State Government provides financial assistance for adoption of Solar Roof Top System. At present, streetlights are installed in the villages, solar panels are installed in the fields for irrigation. A solar power plant has been set up in Kutch district of Gujarat in Modhwa village near Mandvi to desalinate seawater (to make potable water). The prevalence of solar powered devices has increased in the country.

## Wind Energy
Wind energy is also an inexhaustible and pollution free source of energy. Power-generation costs less once it is installed. Windmills have long been used in flour mills and to extract water. In modern times it is paired with a generator to generate electricity. A set of windmills is called a **wind farm**. It is set up in the coastal and mountainous region where strong and steady wind blow constantly. Germany, USA, Denmark, Spain, India are leading producers of wind energy.

Gujarat has wind farms in Lamba village in Devbhoomi Dwarka and on the beach of Mandvi in Kutch. Electricity is generated by installing windmills in Devbhoomi Dwarka, Kutch, Rajkot, Porbandar, Surendranagar etc. districts.

## Geothermal Energy
The energy that is obtained by controlling the steam generated from the ground due to the geothermal process is called **geothermal energy**. The temperature in the interior of the earth increases, as we go deeper. Occasionally this heat energy may appear in the form of hot springs on the ground surface. This heat energy can be used to generate power. Geothermal energy in the form of hot water spring has been used for cooking, heating and bathing for several years. Geothermal energy is pollution free, eco-friendly and always available.

In India, geothermal power plants are located at Manikaran in Himachal Pradesh and Puga ghati in Ladakh. In Gujarat, there are hot springs at Lasundra, Unai, Tuva and Tulsishyam where geothermal energy is likely to be found.

## Tidal Energy
The energy generated from tides is called **tidal energy**. This energy is obtained by constructing dams near narrow opening of the sea. During high tide the tidal energy is used to turn the turbine installed in the dam to produce electricity. Tidal energy is inexhaustible and pollution free.

## Biogas
Organic material such as dead plants and animals residues, animal dung, kitchen waste can be converted into a gaseous fuel called **biogas**. The decomposition of these substances release methane and carbon dioxide gases. Methane gas is combustible. Biogas is an excellent fuel for cooking and generating electricity and produces a large amount of organic manure. Thus, both energy and fertilizer can be obtained from it. Biogas is cheap and easy to use. Uttar Pradesh and Gujarat rank first and second in Biogas production respectively. There are large biogas plants at Rudatal in Daskroi taluka of Ahmedabad and at Dantiwada of Banaskantha.
