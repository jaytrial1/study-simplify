This section describes the early steps taken by the British East India Company to gain political control and revenue collection rights in Bengal after key battles.

# Establishment of British Political Power

After the battle of Plassey, the British initially replaced Mir Jafar with Mir Kasim as the Nawab. However, Mir Kasim's attempts to regulate tax-free private trade led to conflict. The British defeated Mir Kasim in the **Battle of Buxar**.

Following the Battle of Buxar, Clive negotiated a treaty with the Nawab of Awadh and the Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II. According to this treaty:
*   The Nawab of Awadh agreed to pay fifty lakh rupees to the Company.
*   The Company agreed to pay an annual amount of 26 lakhs to the Mughal Emperor.
*   In return, the **Diwani power** (Civil power) of Bengal, Bihar, and Odisha was transferred to the East India Company.

On August 12, 1765, the East India Company officially received the Civil power of Bengal from the Mughal Emperor. This marked a significant step for the British in gaining political power in India.
