# Tribal Revolts and Resistance

The exploitation faced by tribal communities under British rule led to numerous revolts in different regions.

## Notable Adivasi Revolts

*   **1831-32 AD:** The Kol Adivasi revolt.
*   **1855 AD:** The Santhal Revolt.
*   **1910 AD:** A revolt broke out in Basir, Chhattisgarh.
*   **1940 AD:** Warli revolt in Maharashtra.

### Koya Movement
During 1879-1880 AD, the Koya uprising occurred in the East Godavari region of present-day Andhra Pradesh, also affecting parts of Odisha. The leader was Konda Dora. This movement aimed to reclaim the traditional rights of the Adivasis over the forest. Konda Dora was hailed as the 'King of Malkangiri'. The movement was suppressed after Konda Dora's death in a clash with police.

## Birsa Munda and the Ulgulan Movement

Birsa Munda was a prominent tribal leader born on November 15, 1875. He grew up in poverty, migrating with his family, but received some education. He was aware of the struggles between the Munda people and outsiders ('Dikus') and was influenced by Vaishnav preachers. From a young age, he dedicated himself to the upliftment of tribal society.

The severe exploitation of tribals by pro-British moneylenders, jagirdars, and landlords deeply affected Birsa Munda. He led the mass movement known as **'Ulgulan'** (meaning 'Great Tumult' or 'Revolt') starting in 1895 AD. This movement impacted a large area in the Chhota Nagpur region. The Mundas asserted their claim that Chhota Nagpur rightfully belonged to them and protested against the Company taking away their traditional rights and forcing them into labor.

Birsa Munda urged the tribes to:
*   Stop drinking alcohol.
*   Keep their houses and villages clean.
*   Not believe in witchcraft.
*   Live according to their glorious past.
*   Settle in one place and practice cultivation.

His words had a strong impact on the Munda community. The British feared the Birsa movement, as the tribes aimed to establish 'Mundaraj' (Munda rule) under his leadership. When the agitation intensified, the British arrested Birsa Munda in 1895 AD on false charges of obstructing administration.

### Ideals of the Birsa Munda Movement
The movement sought to liberate the Munda community from exploitation by both internal (moneylenders, landlords) and external (British) forces. Birsa aimed to make the Mundas resilient against tyranny. Women actively participated in the movement. While there were instances of violence, the movement was characterized by a focus on development and had a relatively low level of hatred compared to its developmental goals.

Birsa Munda was released from prison in 1897 AD and resumed his public awareness efforts. He strengthened the movement against the 'Dikus' and Europeans, advocating for 'Birsaraj' (Birsa's rule), symbolized by a white flag. Birsa Munda died in 1900 AD. Following his death, the movement slowed down, which allowed the British to continue expanding their empire.
