This section introduces the concept of Fundamental Rights guaranteed to citizens and details each of the six key rights, explaining their importance.

# Fundamental Rights and Duties

The Constitution of democratic India outlines the fundamental rights and duties of its citizens. As citizens, we enjoy certain rights. However, to ensure that our rights do not harm others and to encourage participation in national development, some fundamental duties are also listed. Rights and duties are seen as two sides of the same coin.

## Fundamental Rights

Fundamental rights are crucial for the nation's stability and for protecting individual freedom.

1.  **Right to Equality:** All citizens are equal before the law. There should be no discrimination based on religion, gender, caste, or color. All citizens should receive equal opportunity based on their merit or competence.
2.  **Right to Freedom:** Every citizen is free to express their ideas and is free to travel, reside, and pursue a livelihood in any part of the country.
3.  **Right to Protest against Exploitation:** A main goal of the Constitution is to create a society without exploitation. Pushing children under 14 into employment is a form of exploitation. This right allows anyone to stop or protest against exploitation.
4.  **Right to Freedom of Religion:** Each citizen is free to follow and spread any religion, provided it is not against morality and health.
5.  **Cultural and Educational Rights:** Each citizen is free to preserve, maintain, and develop their language, dialect, traditions, values, etc.
6.  **Right to Constitutional Remedies:** Laws and provisions for rights are meaningless if not implemented. Therefore, provisions exist for their implementation. Any citizen feeling deprived of fundamental rights can approach the court. Dr. Baba Saheb Ambedkar called this right the very **'Soul of the Constitution'**.

However, under specific circumstances, these fundamental rights can be temporarily suspended. No one can exercise their fundamental rights in a way that harms the interest of another person, community, or the nation.

## Know This

Human rights were declared by the United Nations General Assembly on December 10, 1948. Therefore, **December 10 is celebrated as 'Human Rights Day'**.
