# Methods and Styles of Painting
This section describes various methods and styles used in Indian painting, from ancient cave art to modern techniques and specific regional styles.

The **Indian style of painting** primarily includes:
*   **Cave painting**: Encompasses paintings by primitive people and those found in caves like Ajanta-Ellora until the 9th century.
*   **Rock painting**: The tradition of painting on rocks.
*   **Ancient religious styles**: Including Hindu, Buddhist, and Jain paintings, often found on monastery walls and centered around religious scriptures.

Other important painting forms include **murals**, and **rangoli** or decorative art made on courtyards or floors during festivals. A **miniature style** of painting developed to complement manuscripts of Hinduism, Buddhism, and Jainism, found within the texts themselves. **Textile painting** involves making impressions of pictures on fabrics.

In modern times, techniques like **oil painting**, **spray painting**, and **canvas painting** with multicolor dimensions have also developed.

## Styles of Painting
Painting styles in India evolved over time, often associated with different dynasties and regions. Indian painting can be understood based on its time period and location. Here are some important styles:

1.  **Pal style**: Flourished under the patronage of Pal kings in Bengal, Bihar, Nepal, and Tibet. Its main subject was the **Mahayana Buddhist sect**, depicting Jataka stories and Bodhisattvas. Some Hindu manuscripts also show this miniature style.

2.  **Jain style**: Developed in Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Malwa regions from the 12th century. These are **miniature paintings** on palm leaves and manuscripts, centered on Jainism. Examples are found in texts like *Kalpasutra* and *Kalkacharya Katha*. As this style was prominent in Gujarat, it is also known as **'Gujarat style'**.

3.  **Rajput style**: Famous in Rajasthan and Madhya Pradesh from the 10th to 16th century under Rajput kings. It includes miniatures and murals. Subjects often depicted the life of Rajput kings, their customs, attire, and festivals, as well as **Radhakrishna**, Krishnabhakti, Raslila, and folk life. It is also known as **Rajasthan style** due to its development in areas like Bundi, Kishangarh, Bikaner, and Jodhpur.

4.  **Mughal style**: Mughal emperors were great patrons of painting. From Babur to Shah Jahan, they significantly promoted this art. The Mughal style is a blend of **Indian and Iranian styles**. Biographies and portraits became common during Akbar's reign, including paintings from *Baburnama*. The style reached its peak under Jahangir, whose court included famous painters like **Mansoor** and **Bishandas**. Paintings depicted animals, birds, natural scenes, court life, wars, and hunts. Mughal painting was primarily a court art, reflecting royal elegance.

5.  **Kangra style**: A prominent style developed by Rajasthan and Mughal painters in the Himalayan region, with centers in Kangra, Kullu, Garhwal, Chamba, and Mandi. **Molaram** was a famous painter of this style. The main themes are **Lord Krishna** and devotion, along with the beauty of the Himalayas.

6.  **Other painting styles**: In addition to the above, other notable Indian painting styles include:
    *   Mithila painting (Madhubani) of Bihar
    *   Kalamkari painting of Andhra Pradesh
    *   Patt painting of Orissa
    *   Phad painting of Rajasthan
    *   Gond painting of the Santhal tribe
    *   Warli painting of Maharashtra tribes
    *   Pithora painting of Gujarat
    *   Kalighat painting of Bengal
