# History of Indian Painting
This section traces the long history of painting in India, from ancient cave paintings to developments across various historical periods and regions.

The history of painting in India is as old as the **Stone Age**. Paintings dating back to 7000 BC have been discovered in the caves of **Bhimbetka** in Madhya Pradesh, considered the earliest evidence of Indian painting. These were painted on cave walls using natural colours. Paintings from around 2000 BC have also been found in caves at Narasinhagarh in Maharashtra.

Most ancient paintings are found in caves, on birch leaves, rocks, and the walls of temples and monasteries. Such pictures are also mentioned in Buddhist texts.

The **Gupta period** is regarded as a golden age for Indian painting. The world-famous paintings in the caves of **Ajanta** and **Ellora** were created during this era, depicting **Jataka stories**. Among these, the paintings in Ajanta caves no. 1, 9, and 10 are particularly famous, including the painting of **Padmapani Buddha**.

Indian painting traditions extend from North to South India. Excellent paintings have been found in the **Badami caves** in Karnataka, and **Kanchipuram** and **Sittanavasal caves** in Tamil Nadu. The walls of the **Brihadeshwara temple** are adorned with depictions from Indian epics.

Painting further developed during the pre-medieval **Rajput period** and the **Mughal period**. Mughal emperors like **Babur**, **Humayun**, **Akbar**, and **Jahangir** significantly promoted painting. Jahangir even established a painting gallery. The **Bhakti movement** also contributed to the development of painting.

Post-1750 AD, a **semi-western style** of painting emerged, developed by British and Indian artists. This style evolved from works like Sheikh Ziauddin's bird studies for Lady Impey and Ghulam Ali Khan's portraits for William Fraser and Colonel Skinner. **Raja Ravivarma** of Travancore is considered a leading figure of this period. He created excellent oil paintings based on Indian mythology and social issues. His painting of **Goddess Saraswati** is particularly renowned.

The 19th century saw significant artistic development during the Indian renaissance. The **'Sir J. J. School of Art'** was established in Mumbai in 1858 AD, and **'Kala Bhavan'** (now Faculty of Fine Arts) was founded in Vadodara in 1890 AD. Masterpieces were created at **'Shantiniketan'**, established in 1901 AD.

In 1948 AD, the **Progressive Artists Group** was founded under the leadership of **Francis Newton Souza**, **S. H. Raza**, and **S. K. Bakre**, presenting a strong modern painting movement in India. Photo galleries were established in Kolkata and Mumbai in 1950 AD. In Chennai, **K.C. S. Paniker** and **Devi Prasad Roy Chowdhury** established the **Madras School of Art**. The **'National Gallery of Modern Art'** in independent India holds an important place for its large collection of modern Indian paintings.
