# Introduction to Indian Art and Painting
This section introduces the concept of art in India, its significance, and distinguishes between visual and performing arts, focusing on painting in modern times.

Since ancient times, **Indian art and culture** have held a prominent position globally. Art serves as a medium for human expression, reflecting the human mind and society. Artists typically categorize art into two main parts:
1.  **Visual art**: Includes painting, sculpture, and handicrafts.
2.  **Performing art**: Includes music, dance, musical instruments, and drama.

Art is a medium that evokes culture. Indian art embodies elements of **globalism**, **unity in diversity**, and **secularism**. This chapter will discuss painting, one of the forms of visual arts in modern times.

## Painting
Historically, **nature** and **religion** have been central themes in painting. Initially, subjects included episodes from scriptures, Gods and Goddesses, and animals and birds. Over time, paintings also began depicting individuals and social customs. Art education was traditionally provided in universities like Takshashila and Nalanda.

During the **British rule**, Indian art and culture were largely neglected. There was a lack of dedicated institutions for art education, particularly in Gujarat. Maharaja Pragmalji of Kutch established an art school in Bhuj in 1877-78 AD. The **'Kala Bhavan'** founded by Maharaja Sayajirao in Vadodara also imparted art education, although its focus was more on promoting art industry.

In 1951 AD, a **Kalashala** was established in Ahmedabad at Seth Chimanlal Nagindas Vidyalaya (Seth C. N. Vidyalaya) to offer a teachers' training course. **Rasiklal Parikh** was the first principal. The school started a **DTC (Drawing Teacher Certificate)** course and was later transformed into a **Kala Mahavidayalaya** in 1960 AD.
