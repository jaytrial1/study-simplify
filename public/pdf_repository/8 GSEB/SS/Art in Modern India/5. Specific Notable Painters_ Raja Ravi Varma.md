# Specific Notable Painters: Raja Ravi Varma
This section provides a biography of Raja Ravi Varma, highlighting his life, style, and contributions to Indian painting.

**Raja Ravivarma** (1848-1906 AD) was born in the village of Kilimannur in Kerala and was part of the royal family. During his time, art was heavily influenced by western styles. Ravivarma developed his unique style after receiving training from Shri Ramaswami Naidu, Theodore Jensen, and European guest painters.

His paintings are known for their **realistic style**. He was exceptional at creating portraits. Technique was often emphasized more than emotion in his work. He gained significant praise for his **oil paintings** depicting scenes and characters from mythological scriptures and Sanskrit literature. Famous works include *Virat Darbar*, *Ganga-Avataran*, *Urvashi*, *Shakuntala*, and *Portrait of Lady*.

*Raja Ravivarma.*

In 1894 AD, Ravivarma started a **lithography printing press** in Ghatkopar, Mumbai. This press made pictures of Hindu Gods and Goddesses available to the common people at affordable prices. Maharaja Sayajirao Gaekwad of Vadodara and the royalty of Bhavnagar commissioned Raja Ravivarma for mythological and royal family paintings. His works are preserved in the Trivendrum Museum, the Fatehsinharao Art Gallery in Vadodara, and the Darbar in Bhavnagar. The British government honored him with the title of **'Kaiser-e-Hind'**. He is considered a master of art and an invaluable heritage of India.
