# Cotton Textile Industry

Weaving cloth from yarn is an ancient craft using fibers like cotton, wool, silk, jute, and flax. Fibers, the raw material for textiles, can be **natural** (from wool, silk, cotton, linen, jute) or **man-made** (like nylon, polyester, acrylic, rayon).

The **cotton textile industry** is one of the world's oldest. Before the 18th-century Industrial Revolution, cotton cloth was made manually with handlooms. The introduction of **powerlooms** in the 18th century spurred the industry's development, starting in Britain and spreading globally. Today, India, China, Japan, and the USA are major producers.

India has a history of producing high-quality cotton fabrics. Indian hand-woven cloth, like Dhaka's muslin, Machilipatnam's chintz, and textiles from Calicut, Burhanpur, Surat, and Vadodara, were world-famous. However, hand-woven production was slow and costly, making it difficult to compete with mechanized textiles from the West.

The first successful mechanized textile mill in India was established in **Mumbai in 1854 AD**. The region's hot, humid climate, port facilities for import, raw material availability, and skilled labour supported rapid expansion. Initially concentrated in Maharashtra and Gujarat, the industry later spread across India to centres like Coimbatore, Kanpur, Chennai, Ahmedabad, Kolkata, Ludhiana, Puducherry, and Panipat.

## Ahmedabad (India)

Located on the Sabarmati river in Gujarat, Ahmedabad's first cotton mill opened in 1861 AD. It became India's second-largest cotton textile city after Mumbai and was known as the **'Manchester of India'**. Its favourable location aided its development:
*   Close to the cotton-producing region, ensuring raw material availability.
*   Flat land suitable for factory construction.
*   Densely populated Gujarat and Maharashtra providing skilled and semi-skilled labour.
*   Well-developed transport networks (roads and railways) for distributing cloth.
*   Mumbai Port for importing machinery and exporting textiles.

However, some textile mills in Ahmedabad have faced problems and closed down in recent years.

## Osaka (Japan)

Osaka is a significant textile manufacturing hub in Japan, also called the **'Manchester of Japan'**. Its development is linked to geographical factors:
*   Extensive plains around Osaka offer land for mills.
*   Humid climate is suitable for weaving and spinning.
*   The Yodo River provides ample water.
*   Labour is readily available.
*   Its port location facilitates importing raw cotton (from Egypt, India, China, USA) and exporting finished textiles.

Osaka's textile industry relies entirely on imported raw materials. Its cloth is mainly exported and has gained a good market due to high quality and competitive prices. While historically important for textiles, Osaka's industrial focus has recently shifted towards other industries like iron-steel, machinery, shipbuilding, automobiles, electrical appliances, and cement.
