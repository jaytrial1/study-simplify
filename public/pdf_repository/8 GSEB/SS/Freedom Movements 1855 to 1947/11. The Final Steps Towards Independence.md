This section describes the events leading to India's independence, including the naval revolt, the Cabinet Mission, the Muslim League's actions, the Mountbatten Plan, and the final act of partition and gaining independence.

# Moving Towards Independence

In 1946 AD, Indian Navy soldiers revolted in Mumbai, indicating to the government that the army's loyalty was shifting. As a result, the British Prime Minister announced that three cabinet-level ministers would be sent to India to negotiate complete independence.

The **Cabinet Mission** members met with leaders of political parties in India. The Cabinet Mission Plan had two parts: a long-term plan and an interim plan.
*   The long-term plan proposed dividing India into three groups: Hindu-majority areas, Muslim-majority areas, and mixed populations.
*   The interim plan proposed forming an interim government.

The Muslim League insisted that Muslim seats in the interim government be filled only by the Muslim League, but the British government refused. Consequently, the Muslim League rejected the plan and called for **direct action** to demand Pakistan, leading to terrible communal riots in Kolkata and other parts of the country.

Elections for the Constituent Assembly were held in July 1946. The Muslim League took a rebellious stance in the new interim government, endangering communal harmony.

Amidst this crisis, in February 1947, British Prime Minister **Attlee** announced that the British government would leave India by June 1948. **Mountbatten** replaced Wavell as the Viceroy of India. Violent riots continued when Mountbatten took office. The interim government could not function due to the League's non-cooperative attitude.

Since a compromise between Congress and the League could not be reached, Mountbatten decided to divide India. Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Patel, and other Congress leaders found Mountbatten's argument convincing: that a strong central government in a divided India would bring more peace than a weak central government with many autonomous and opposing units in a united India.

According to the **Mountbatten Plan**, the **Indian Independence Act** was passed in July 1947. This act divided India into two parts: **India** and **Pakistan**. After midnight on August 14, 1947 (transitioning to August 15), the Union Jack was lowered from the Red Fort in Delhi and replaced by the tricolor flag, granting India the status of an independent nation.
