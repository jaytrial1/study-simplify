This section focuses on the life and activities of Subhash Chandra Bose, his formation and leadership of the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj), and its role in the freedom struggle.

# Subhash Chandra Bose and Indian National Army

**Subhash Chandra Bose's** revolutionary activities and efforts for armed revolution continued during World War II. He was born on January 23, 1897, in Cuttack, Odisha. Despite passing the difficult **ICS** examination, he refused British service and joined the independence movement.

He became an active member of the Rashtriya Mahasabha (Congress) and was elected President twice, in Haripura (1938 AD) and Tripura (1939 AD). Due to ideological differences with Congress leaders, he resigned and formed a new political party called '**Forward Bloc**'.

He was arrested during World War II for protesting British policies and started a fast in prison for political prisoners' treatment. His health deteriorated, leading to house arrest. He escaped disguised as a Pathan and reached Japan via Afghanistan and Berlin.

With the help of **Rasbihari Bose**, the '**Azad Hind Fauj**' (Indian National Army) was established with **Captain Mohan Singh** as its commander. Subhash Chandra Bose came from Japan and was made the chief of the Indian National Army. He modernized the army and instilled new vigor.

He gave powerful slogans like "**Chalo Delhi**" (March to Delhi) and "**Tum Mujhe Khoon Do Mein Tumhe Azadi Doonga**" (Give me blood and I will give you freedom). He formed military brigades named after leaders like Mahatma Gandhi, Pandit Nehru, and Maulana Azad, and a women's brigade named '**Lakshmibai Brigade**'.

The Indian National Army achieved some victories in Arakan and Imphal divisions on the eastern border but retreated due to changing conditions in Japan during World War II, shortage of supplies, heavy rains, starvation, and natural calamities. Subhash Chandra Bose is believed to have died in a plane crash on August 18, 1945, while returning to Japan, although his death remains a mystery.
