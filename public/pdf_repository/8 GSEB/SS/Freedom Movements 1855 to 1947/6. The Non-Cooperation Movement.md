This section details the Non-Cooperation Movement launched by Gandhiji in 1920, outlining its different aspects (constructive and non-constructive) and the reasons for its suspension, including the Chauri-Chaura incident.

## Non-cooperation Movement

In 1920 AD, Gandhiji started the **Non-cooperation Movement**. This movement had two main aspects:
1.  The **non-constructive aspect** (boycott)
2.  The **constructive aspect** (building alternative institutions and promoting self-reliance)

Non-constructive aspects included:
*   Boycott of schools, colleges, legislatures, courts, and institutions of local self-government.
*   Boycott of foreign goods.

Thousands of students left government schools and colleges. Well-known lawyers like **Motilal Nehru, Chittaranjandas, Vitthalbhai Patel, Vallabhbhai Patel, Rajendra Prasad** gave up their practices to serve the nation. Titles given by the British were returned. Foreign clothes were burnt in public. The arrival of the Prince of Wales in India during this movement was met with protests and strikes.

Constructive aspects included:
*   Use of the spinning wheel in every house.
*   **Khadi** production and propagation of **Swadeshi** (indigenous) goods.
*   Prohibition of alcohol.
*   Untouchability prevention.
*   Hindu-Muslim unity.

National education institutions were started, including **Gujarat Vidyapeeth, Bihar Vidyapeeth, Banaras Vidyapeeth, Jamia Millia Vidyapeeth**, etc. The movement also incorporated local issues, such as the 'Van Satyagraha' in Andhra Pradesh and the tea garden labour movement in Assam.

Mahatma Gandhi believed in non-violent satyagraha. However, in 1922 AD, violence occurred in **Chauri-Chaura** village near Gorakhpur, Uttar Pradesh. When police fired on a peaceful procession of farmers, an angry mob attacked the police station and set it on fire, resulting in the deaths of 22 policemen. Upon hearing this news, Gandhiji announced the **postponement of the Non-cooperation Movement**.

The government arrested Gandhiji. Gandhiji urged people to continue with constructive work. At this time, **Motilal Nehru** and **Chittaranjandas** in the Congress believed the party should participate in Assembly elections to offer an alternative program, leading them to form the **Swaraj Party**.
