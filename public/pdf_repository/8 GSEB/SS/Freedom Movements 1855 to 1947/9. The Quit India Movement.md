This section describes the circumstances leading to the Quit India Movement, its launch, the arrest of leaders, the widespread nature of the protests, and the British response.

# 'Quit India' Movement

During the Second World War, it was decided to start **individual satyagraha** to avoid adding difficulties for the government. **Vinoba Bhave** was selected as the first individual satyagrahi.

In 1942 AD, the British government sent the **Cripps Mission** to persuade Indians, but it failed as it could not satisfy the demand for independence. Dissatisfaction against the British government grew among the people.

In a meeting of the All-India Congress Committee held in Mumbai on August 8, 1942, the historic "**Quit India**" resolution was passed, demanding the British leave India. On August 9th, early morning, Gandhiji, Vallabhbhai Patel, Jawaharlal Nehru, Maulana Azad, and other leading Congress leaders were arrested.

The movement became fierce and widespread. A large number of farmers and students joined. Government buildings, railways, and telephone lines were severely damaged across the country. Strikes occurred in many parts. People in some areas formed their own national governments. The British used oppressive measures to suppress the movement. By the end of 1943 AD, many freedom fighters were arrested, and many people lost their lives. This struggle convinced the British government that India could no longer be subjugated.
