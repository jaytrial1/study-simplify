This section focuses on the launch of the Civil Disobedience Movement through the iconic Dandi March, describing the march, the breaking of the salt law, and the spread of the movement.

## Dandi March

In 1930 AD, Gandhiji announced his plan to march to break the unjust **salt law**. The British government had a monopoly on salt production and sale, and levying a tax on salt, a basic necessity, was considered sinful by Mahatma Gandhi and national leaders.

On March 12, 1930, he started the **Dandi March** with his companions from Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad. He covered a distance of 370 km, holding meetings in villages and towns along the way like Aslali, Bareja, Nadiad, Anand, Raas, Jambusar, Surat, Navsari. He reached Dandi village on April 5th.

On April 6th, Gandhiji violated the salt law by taking salt in his hands on the shores of Dandi, marking the beginning of the **Civil Disobedience Movement**. The movement of breaking the salt law spread to several parts of the country.

When Gandhiji announced the **Dharsana Satyagraha** in Gujarat, he was arrested on May 5, 1930, and sent to Yerawada jail. After his arrest, **Abbas Tayyabji** led the Satyagraha, and upon his arrest, **Sarojini Naidu** took the lead. Salt satyagraha also took place in Viramgam, Dholera, Surajkaradi, and Wadala.

In the Civil Disobedience Movement, programs included:
*   Boycott of foreign clothes
*   Non-payment of taxes, including revenue
*   Ban on alcohol and picketing liquor bars
*   Prevention of untouchability

A large number of farmers, tribals, and women participated. In the border areas, a non-violent 'no tax' fight was led by '**Frontier Gandhi**', **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan**. The British government attempted to suppress the movement by arresting thousands of satyagrahis.

A **Round Table Conference** was held to discuss India's constitution. The first conference failed due to Congress's absence. As civil disobedience intensified, the government sought an agreement, leading to the **Gandhi Irwin Pact**, which included freedom to make salt, peaceful picketing, and release of satyagrahis. Gandhiji attended the second Round Table Conference in 1931 but was disappointed by the issue of communal constituencies, and the conference failed.
