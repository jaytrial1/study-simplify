This section covers the controversial Rowlatt Act and the tragic Jallianwala Bagh Massacre, highlighting the British government's harsh response to dissent and the resulting public outrage.

## Rowlatt Act (1919)

After the First World War, the government passed the **Rowlatt Act** in 1919 AD. This act allowed the arrest of any person without giving a reason and punishment through trial in a special court. The law was widely opposed as it restricted people's freedom. Gandhiji called this a '**black act**' and started a Satyagraha against it.

## Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

The government took drastic measures to suppress Gandhiji's satyagraha against the Rowlatt Act. The situation was particularly explosive in Punjab.

In Amritsar, Punjab, on April 13, 1919, a large crowd gathered to protest the arrest of leaders Dr. Saifuddin Kichlu and Dr. Satyapal. Many people also came to Jallianwala Bagh for the Vaishakhi festival. **General Dyer**, the military commander of Amritsar, ordered his troops to fire on the unarmed crowd. The firing continued until the ammunition ran out.

About a thousand people died, and many more were injured. The government imposed martial law and tortured people in Punjab, creating an atmosphere of fear across the country. This incident led to fierce opposition to the British regime. Disturbed by the massacre, Gandhiji renounced the **Kaiser-e-Hind** title given by the government, and **Rabindranath Tagore** returned his knighthood.
