# Freedom Movements (1885 AD to 1947 AD)

We studied about the establishment of British rule in India, the first war of independence and the social, economic, political and educational conditions of the 19th century India. Here we will study the origin and development of nationalism in India and the national movements of the freedom struggle.

## Emergence and Development of Nationalism

**Nationalism** is the spirit of sacrificing body, mind and wealth for the nation. Nationalism means a sense of unity and pride towards one's nation. Thus, nationalism is the expression of the people living in a certain part of the world who consider themselves united with their heritage and culture and express it emotionally. There is a sense of geographical, cultural similarity in a sublime spirit. Let us study which factors or conditions were responsible for the formation of nationalist ideology.

Factors responsible for the rise of nationalism:
*   The British unintentionally fostered a sense of unity by introducing **uniform law and administration** across the country.
*   The **economic exploitation** by the British government ruined peasants and artisans, creating widespread dissatisfaction and a sense of injustice that united people against the government.
*   **English education** created a class of intellectuals who were exposed to Western ideas of self-governance and independence.
*   Literature in various Indian languages (Hindi, Gujarati, Marathi, Punjabi, Bengali, Urdu etc.) promoted nationalism, mythological pride, and ideological awakening.
*   Vigilant **newspapers** played a significant role in developing nationalism.
*   **Archaeological research** during British rule revealed the glory of India's past, making people question their enslaved status.
*   Improved **communication** through telegraph, postal service, and railway facilitated contact and the spread of national unity among people from different regions.
*   Unjust British policies and events like the injustice in chartered jobs, Lytton's **Vernacular Press Act** (curtailing press freedom), the **Indian Arms Act**, and the withdrawal of the **Ilbert Bill** (which would have allowed Indian judges to hear cases of Europeans) further prepared Indians for national unity by highlighting discrimination.
