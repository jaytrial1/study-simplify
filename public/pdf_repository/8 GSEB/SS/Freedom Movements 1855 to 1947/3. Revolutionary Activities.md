This section details the rise of revolutionary movements in India and abroad, highlighting key figures and events associated with the armed struggle for independence, including the Mangarh Massacre.

# Revolutionary activities in India

A class of young people in India desired **Swaraj** (self-rule) at any cost. They were willing to sacrifice everything and were ready to give their lives for the motherland, and also had the courage to take the lives of others.

Revolutionary activity in India was started by **Vasudev Balwant Phadke**. He organized backward castes and gave them combat training.

The **Chafekar brothers (Damodar and Balkrishna Chafekar)** killed Rend, the Chief Plague Commissioner, because people were harassed by him and his assistants during the plague in Maharashtra and Mumbai.

**Vinayak Savarkar** founded a revolutionary organization called '**Mitramela**' in 1900 AD, which was later known as '**Abhinav Bharat**'. His book '**Indian War of Independence 1857**' was reportedly the first book in the world to be banned before publication. He also carried out revolutionary activities abroad, was sentenced to life imprisonment, and kept under house arrest in India after his health deteriorated in Andaman Jail.

The **Anushilan Samiti** was established in Kolkata, with **Barindra Ghosh** later becoming its main leader. This organization spread revolutionary activities through literature and training.

**Khudiram Bose** and **Praful Chandra Chaki** were active in Bengal. They planned to kill magistrate Kingsford but mistakenly threw a bomb on a wagon carrying advocate Kennedy's wife and daughter, who died. Khudiram Bose was sentenced to death, while Praful Chaki shot himself.

**Ramprasad Bismil** and **Ashfaq Ullah Khan** demonstrated Hindu-Muslim unity. They participated in the **Kakori train robbery** plan to obtain money for revolutionary activities and weapons. Ashfaq, Ramprasad Bismil, Roshan Singh, and Rajendranath were arrested and sentenced to death for this action.

**Durgabhabhi** served as a communication link among revolutionaries and actively involved women in revolutionary activities such as sticking posters, distributing leaflets, raising funds for court cases, and using guns.

**Chandrashekhar Azad** was active in revolutionary activities from childhood and participated in the Kakori robbery. He vowed never to be captured alive by the British government. He became a martyr in a clash with the British at Alfred Bagh in Allahabad in 1931 AD, using his own pistol.

# Revolutionary activities abroad

Many Indians sacrificed their lives for freedom, including those who carried out revolutionary activities in foreign countries before World War I, such as England, America, and France.

**Shyamji Krishna Verma**, a native of Kutch, founded the '**Indian Home Rule Society**' in England. He launched a magazine called '**Indian Sociologist**' and named 'India House' as the organization's office.

**Madanlal Dhingra, Vinayak Savarkar**, and **Madam Bhikhaiji Kama** joined revolutionary activities with Shyamji Krishna Varma in London. Madanlal Dhingra shot and killed William Wylie in 1909 AD and was hanged. After Shyamji Krishna Varma moved to Paris, Savarkar took over operations in London.

**Madam Bhikhaiji Rustam Kama** started a newspaper '**Vandemataram**' in Europe in 1902 AD. She hoisted India's first tricolor flag at the Second International Socialist Conference in Stuttgart, Germany, in 1907 AD.

**Sardar Singh Rana**, an ally of Shyamji Krishna Varma, held rallies in Paris against British oppression but was deported by the French government.

Other revolutionaries who made efforts abroad include **Taraknath Das, Lala Hardayal, Raja Mahendra Pratap, Barkatullah, Rasbihari Ghosh**, and **Champak Raman Pillai**.

# Mangarh Massacre

The **Mangarh Massacre** took place on November 17, 1913, on the Mangarh hills, on the Gujarat-Rajasthan border. It is considered a glorious event of tribal sacrifice.

**Govind Guru's Bhagat movement** revived the Bhils, fostering self-respect and making efforts to remove blind faith, superstitions, malpractices, and evil customs from their lives. Thousands of Bhils gathered at Mangarh hills on November 17, 1913, dreaming of freedom from native kingdoms and the British.

On that day, the British army and allied princely state armies attacked Mangarh hill. According to scholars, over 1200 tribals were martyred. The British brutally suppressed the movement and arrested Govind Guru. His samadhi is presently at Kamboi (Ta. Limdi, Dist. Dahod).

Other suppressed tribal movements included the **Vyara tribal movement** (1914 AD) in Tapi district and the **Dadhavav tribal movement** (1922 AD) in Vijayanagar taluka (Sabarkantha district).
