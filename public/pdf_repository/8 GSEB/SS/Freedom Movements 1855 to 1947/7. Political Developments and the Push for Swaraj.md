This section covers key political events like the boycott of the Simon Commission, the Nehru Report, the Bardoli Satyagraha, and the Congress resolution for complete independence, highlighting the growing demand for self-rule.

## Boycott of the Simon Commission and the Nehru Report

The **Simon Commission** came to India. It faced a boycott because it did not include a single Indian member. Protests were held with the slogan '**Simon go back**'.

**Lala Lajpat Rai** died after being injured during a peaceful protest in Lahore against the commission. His death provoked revolutionaries like **Bhagat Singh, Sukhdev, and Rajguru**, who killed the British police officer **Saunders** who ordered the lathi charge.

When the Simon Commission failed, the Indian Wazir **Burkenhead** challenged all parties to draft an acceptable constitution. The Rashtriya Mahasabha formed a '**Nehru Committee**' under the chairmanship of **Motilal Nehru**. Its report, known as the '**Nehru Report**', covered issues like dominion status, federalism, fundamental rights, and the Supreme Court. However, it was rejected by the British government.

In 1928, a '**no tax**' movement, the **Bardoli Satyagraha**, started in Bardoli taluka against the government's increase in land revenue. This movement was notable for the government's repressive policy, the people's determination, and the leadership of **Vallabhbhai Patel**. His successful management of this satyagraha earned him the title '**Sardar**'.

# Resolution of Complete Independence (Purna Swaraj)

Younger leaders in the Congress, such as **Jawaharlal Nehru** and **Subhash Chandra Bose**, were insistent on complete independence. A resolution for **complete independence (Purna Swaraj)** was passed in the Congress session at Lahore in 1929 AD, chaired by Jawaharlal Nehru. It was resolved to celebrate January 26th as 'Purna Swaraj Diwas'. In remembrance of this day, the Indian constitution was implemented on January 26, 1950, which is celebrated as **'Republic Day'**.

In revolutionary activities around this time, **Bhagat Singh** and **Batukeshwar Dutt** threw a bomb in the Central Legislative Assembly, intending not to kill but to make the British government listen. They were arrested and prosecuted. Bhagat Singh and his associates were sentenced to death. **Jatindas**, a revolutionary from Bengal, died on the 64th day of a lifelong fast in jail protesting bad food and unacceptable treatment.
