This section discusses the formation of the Indian National Congress, its initial phase, the differences between moderate and radical leaders, and the impact of the Partition of Bengal.

# The Establishment of the Hindi Rashtriya Mahasabha (Congress)

There was a need for a national institution to give a definite direction to the ideas of nationalism which represented the interests of Indians against the British. Some institutions existed before the establishment of the Hindi Rashtriya Mahasabha, which helped create an environment for its formation.

A retired English officer, **A. O. Hume**, played a significant role in the establishment of the Hindi Rashtriya Mahasabha. He informed the British government about the growing indignation and discontent among the Indian people, believing that if it wasn't addressed, it could lead to another revolt like the one in 1857.

The first session of the Congress was held on December 28, 1885, at the Gokuldas Tejpal Sanskrit School in Mumbai. About 72 delegates attended, and **Vyomeshchandra Banerjee** presided over the session. Prominent leaders present included **Dadabhai Naoroji, Feroz Shah Mehta, Badruddin Tayyabji, K. T. Telanga, Gopalkrishna Gokhale, Dinsha Vachchha**, etc.

During its first phase (1885-1905 AD), the Rashtriya Mahasabha demanded political rights, economic and social development, and educational progress from the government. Although these demands were often ignored, they laid a strong foundation for future struggles.

Demands made by the Mahasabha in its first phase included:
*   Expansion of legislature
*   Reduction in military expenditure
*   Reduction in taxes
*   Relief to farmers in debt
*   Civil Service Examination to be held simultaneously in India and London
*   Freedom of Expression

Looking at the objectives and functions of Mahasabha in the first phase, it used constitutional and democratic methods to oppose unfair British policies, sending demands through petitions to the Viceroy. Due to this approach, leaders of Mahasabha were known as '**Moderates**'. They organized educated middle-class people to raise national awareness.

During this period, leaders like **Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai**, and **Bipinchandra Pal** demanded radical change in the mild policies of Congress. They criticized the policy of requesting or praying, stating, "We must believe in our power, not in the intentions of the government." They were known as '**Radicals**'. Tilak Maharaj famously said, "**Swaraj is my birthright, I shall have it**." The actions of Lal-Bal-Pal (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipinchandra Pal) awakened Indian youth and made them enthusiastic, confident, and aggressive.

### Know This

To spread their ideas, the radical leaders used newspapers:
*   **Lokmanya Tilak** started 'The Maratha' (in English) and 'Kesari' (in Marathi).
*   **Lala Lajpat Rai** started 'The Punjabi' and 'The People'.
*   **Bipin Chandra Pal** started 'New India' weekly and 'Vande Mataram' newspapers.

In 1905, Viceroy Curzon partitioned Bengal under the pretext of administrative ease, as part of his '**divide and rule**' policy. The **Bang Bhang movement** (Anti-Partition Movement) was launched through boycott, national education, and indigenous propaganda. Due to the movement's impact, the British government nullified the partition of Bengal in 1911 AD. This was a significant victory for the Indians.
