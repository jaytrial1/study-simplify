This section covers Mahatma Gandhi's return to India, his initial activities, and his successful early satyagrahas like Champaran and Kheda, which helped him gain important allies.

# Gandhiji's arrival in India

Gandhiji successfully fought a **satyagraha** (truth force) against apartheid in South Africa. He returned to India in 1915 AD. Inspired by **Gopalakrishna Gokhale**, he travelled to many parts of India and realised that British exploitation was the root cause of India's poverty.

To accelerate his activities, he established **Kocharab Ashram** in 1915 AD and **Sabarmati Ashram** in 1917 AD.

Gandhiji's early satyagrahas included:
*   The **Champaran Satyagraha**
*   The **Kheda Satyagraha**
*   The struggle over the question of the **labours of Ahmedabad** (Ahmedabad Mill Workers' Strike)

As a result of these satyagrahas, Gandhiji gained allies like **Vallabhbhai Patel** and **Rajendra Prasad**.

## Champaran Satyagraha

Champaran is located in the foothills of the Himalayas in Bihar. Europeans had established indigo plantations and factories there since the early 19th century. Farmers were forced to cultivate indigo on 3/20th part of their land, a method known as '**Teen Kathia**'. The imposition of more revenue and taxes increased the farmers' hardships.

Gandhiji came to Motihari at the insistence of **Rajkumar Shukla**, a farmer from Champaran. Considering Gandhiji's presence a threat, the magistrate ordered him to leave immediately, but he disobeyed. Eventually, Gandhiji was allowed to investigate the farmers' problems, and his satyagraha was successful in addressing their grievances.

# Kheda Satyagraha

In 1917 AD, despite crop failure due to heavy rains, the British government decided to collect revenue instead of waiving it. People started a satyagraha under the leadership of **Gandhiji** and **Vallabhbhai Patel**. Vallabhbhai Patel emerged as a key leader in this struggle, giving up his legal practice for social service.

Gandhiji advised the farmers not to pay the revenue if their demand wasn't met. The government began confiscating land. When the onion crop was ready on a confiscated farm, **Mohanlal Pandya**, on Gandhiji's advice, harvested it with colleagues. The government arrested Mohanlal and sentenced him to 15 days imprisonment, earning him the title of '**Dungli Chor**' (Onion Thief) from Gandhiji upon his release.

Ultimately, a compromise was reached: well-to-do families would pay the revenue, while the poor would not. This struggle raised awareness among farmers and exemplified the people's power of renunciation.
