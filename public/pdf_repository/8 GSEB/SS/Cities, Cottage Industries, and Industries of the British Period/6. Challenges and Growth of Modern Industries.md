This section discusses how traditional Indian industries declined under British rule and how, despite hindrances, some modern industries began to emerge towards the end of the 19th century.

# Challenges and Growth of Modern Industries

With the beginning of British rule, India's traditional industries, such as **cotton textiles**, **sculptures**, **metallurgy**, and **spices**, started to decline. The development of modern factories in England was a major factor in the decline of Indian cottage industries. India's cotton and silk textile industries struggled to survive under the Company regime. The British aimed to expand their control over more Indian territories to support the growth of English industries, viewing India primarily as a **market**. The Company made large profits by obtaining cheap raw materials from India and selling finished goods at higher prices in Europe. Thus, England's industries developed at the expense of India's economy.

However, towards the end of the 19th century, influenced by scientific research, some modern industries like **linen, cotton textile, iron and steel, paper, chemicals, leather making, and shipbuilding** began to grow slowly. The British government often hindered the development of these Indian industries, which slowed their progress. Nevertheless, the textile and iron and steel industries managed to flourish to some extent.
