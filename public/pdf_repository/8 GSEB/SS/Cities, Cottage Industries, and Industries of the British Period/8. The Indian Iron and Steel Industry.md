This section details the development of the modern iron and steel industry in India, including the pioneering efforts of Jamshedji Tata, the decline of traditional methods, and the establishment of factories in various locations.

# The Indian Iron and Steel Industry

Industrial development in India accelerated partly due to the disturbances in Europe during the First World War. **Jamshedji Tata** established the **first iron factory in Sakchi** (now Jamshedpur). The establishment of this factory marked the beginning of basic industries like **iron and steel** in India. The traditional era of iron smelting, which required large amounts of coal, declined with the advent of innovative production methods. Under new British laws, obtaining coal from forests also became difficult. With the start of iron and steel production, other related industries also began to develop. The establishment of the **'Indian Institute of Science' in Bengaluru** provided new direction for the iron and steel industry. Iron and steel factories were also started at Kulti, Burhanpur, and Bhadravati. Thus, metallurgy gradually developed in India.

## Know This

### Ranchhodlal's Perseverance

Ranchhodlal had previously closed a factory in Bharuch and decided to start a textile mill in Ahmedabad. He ordered looms from England with the help of Dadabhai Navroji. The looms were sent by steamer to Mumbai. Since there was no railway to Ahmedabad at the time, they were transported by sea, but they sank. Adding to the difficulties, the English engineer hired to set up the looms died of cholera. Despite these challenges, Ranchhodlal ordered looms again from England. The 'Ahmedabad Cotton Spinning and Weaving Company' was established in a desolate area of Shahpur in 1859 AD with a capital of one lakh rupees, initially under a thatched roof. The first whistle blew in this mill on May 30, 1861, at quarter to five in the afternoon.

## Know This

### Tipu Sultan's Sword

The sword of **Tipu Sultan** is considered a masterpiece of Indian metallurgy. It was exceptionally sharp, capable of easily cutting through an enemy's armor. This was because it was made using **Woods steel**, a type of steel with high carbon content.

## Know This

### About the Establishment of Jamshedpur

In April 1904, Dorabji, the eldest son of Jamshedji Tata, and American geologist Charles Weld were searching for iron ore deposits in what is now Jharkhand. After a long search, they met people of the **Agaria community** who were carrying baskets of iron ore. The Agaria people directed them to a mountainous area. Dorabji and Weld found the **Razara Hills**, which contained some of the world's finest iron ore. However, this area was difficult to access. Later, an industrial city, known today as **Jamshedpur**, was established by clearing forests on the banks of the **Suvarnarekha river**.
