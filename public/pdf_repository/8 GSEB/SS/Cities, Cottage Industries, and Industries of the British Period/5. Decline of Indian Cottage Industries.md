This section explains the state of India's traditional cottage industries and how they suffered and declined due to competition from British manufactured goods and the impact of the Industrial Revolution.

# Decline of Indian Cottage Industries

While many cities developed due to industrial growth during the British era, India's **cottage industries** collapsed because of the rise of large-scale industries, particularly in Britain. Before the arrival of the British, India's cottage industries were thriving. Industries like the production of **Muslin of Dhaka**, **embroidery**, **dyeing**, **printing**, and **combing** were highly developed. The Industrial Revolution led to an increase in the consumption of British goods in India, which directly harmed India's domestic industries. Indian weavers traditionally performed all steps from weaving to dyeing cotton cloth themselves, often using natural dyes like those from the parrot tree (kesuda). They wove cloth on **handlooms**. Sarees like **Bandhni** and **Patola**, made by these weavers, were very popular.

Many other cottage industries also flourished in villages. For instance, pure iron was produced by melting cast iron in furnaces and sold to merchants. These cottage industries contributed to a strong rural economy in India, which unfortunately collapsed due to the impact of the Industrial Revolution and British policies.
