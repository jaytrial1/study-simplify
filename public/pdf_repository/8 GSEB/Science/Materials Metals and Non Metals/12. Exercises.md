(a) All metals are ductile.
(b) All non-metals are ductile.
(c) Generally, metals are ductile.

> **Answer:** (c) Generally, metals are ductile. (Some metals like mercury are not ductile, so (a) is incorrect. Non-metals are generally not ductile, making (b) and (d) incorrect.)

6. Give reasons for the following.
(a) Aluminium foils are used to wrap food items.
(b) Immersion rods for heating liquids are made up of metallic substances.
(c) Copper cannot displace zinc from its salt solution.
(d) Sodium and potassium are stored in kerosene.

> **Answer:**
> (a) Aluminium foils are used to wrap food items because aluminium is a metal, and metals are malleable, meaning they can be beaten into thin sheets. Aluminium is also lightweight and does not react with most food items.
> (b) Immersion rods for heating liquids are made up of metallic substances because metals are good conductors of heat, allowing them to efficiently transfer heat from the heating element to the liquid.
> (c) Copper cannot displace zinc from its salt solution because zinc is more reactive than copper. A less reactive metal cannot displace a more reactive metal from its salt solution.
> (d) Sodium and potassium are stored in kerosene because they are very reactive metals. They react vigorously with oxygen and water present in the air, which can cause fires. Storing them in kerosene prevents their contact with air and moisture.

7. Can you store lemon pickle in an aluminium utensil? Explain.

> **Answer:** No, you should not store lemon pickle in an aluminium utensil. Lemon pickle contains acids (like citric acid). Acids react with metals like aluminium, producing hydrogen gas and metallic salts. This reaction can spoil the pickle and make it unfit for consumption, and it can also damage the utensil.

9. What happens when
(a) Dilute sulphuric acid is poured on a copper plate?
(b) Iron nails are placed in copper sulphate solution? Write word equations of the reactions involved.

> **Answer:**
> (a) When dilute sulphuric acid is poured on a copper plate, a reaction occurs. Copper reacts with sulphuric acid to produce copper sulphate, sulphur dioxide gas, and water. A gas with a pungent smell (sulphur dioxide) is produced, and the copper plate may dissolve slowly.
> Word Equation: Copper + Dilute Sulphuric Acid $\rightarrow$ Copper Sulphate + Sulphur Dioxide + Water
> (b) When iron nails are placed in copper sulphate solution, a displacement reaction occurs. Iron, being more reactive than copper, displaces copper from the copper sulphate solution. The blue colour of the copper sulphate solution fades and turns greenish (due to the formation of iron sulphate), and a reddish-brown deposit of copper is formed on the iron nails.
> Word Equation: Iron + Copper Sulphate $\rightarrow$ Iron Sulphate + Copper

10. Saloni took a piece of burning charcoal and collected the gas evolved in a test tube.
(a) How will she find the nature of the gas?
(b) Write down word equations of all the reactions taking place in this process.

> **Answer:**
> (a) Charcoal is mainly carbon. When carbon burns in air, it reacts with oxygen to form carbon dioxide gas. To find the nature of the gas (whether it is acidic or basic), Saloni can add a small amount of water to the test tube containing the gas, shake it well to dissolve the gas, and then test the resulting solution with litmus paper (both red and blue). If the solution turns blue litmus paper red, the gas is acidic (carbon dioxide forms carbonic acid in water). If it turns red litmus blue, it is basic. Non-metallic oxides are generally acidic, so the gas is expected to be acidic.
> (b) Word equations of the reactions:
> Burning of charcoal (Carbon) in air: Carbon + Oxygen $\rightarrow$ Carbon Dioxide
> Dissolving the gas in water: Carbon Dioxide + Water $\rightarrow$ Carbonic Acid
> Testing with blue litmus: Carbonic Acid turns Blue Litmus $\rightarrow$ Red

11. One day Reeta went to a jeweller's shop with her mother. Her mother gave an old gold jewellery to the goldsmith to polish. Next day when they brought the jewellery back, they found that there was a slight loss in its weight. Can you suggest a reason for the loss in weight?

> **Answer:** Goldsmiths often use a solution called "Aqua Regia" (a mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and nitric acid) to clean or polish gold jewellery. Aqua Regia is a highly corrosive mixture that can dissolve gold. During the polishing process, a thin layer of gold from the surface of the jewellery dissolves in the Aqua Regia. When the jewellery is taken out and cleaned, this dissolved gold is removed, resulting in a slight loss in the weight of the jewellery.
