# Chemical Properties of Metals and Non-metals

## Reaction with Oxygen

You are familiar with the phenomenon of rusting of iron. Recall the reaction by which rust is formed. You had also performed in Class VII an activity of burning a magnesium ribbon in air. You had learnt that in both the processes oxide formation takes place. Complete the following reactions of iron and magnesium with oxygen.

Iron $(\mathrm{Fe})+$ Oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right)+$ Water $\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right) \rightarrow$ ?
> **Answer:** Iron $(\mathrm{Fe})+$ Oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right)+$ Water $\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right) \rightarrow$ Rust (Iron oxide, $\mathrm{Fe}_2\mathrm{O}_3$ with associated water)

Magnesium $(\mathrm{Mg})+$ Oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right) \rightarrow$ ?
> **Answer:** Magnesium $(\mathrm{Mg})+$ Oxygen $\left(\mathrm{O}_{2}\right) \rightarrow$ Magnesium oxide $(\mathrm{MgO})$

Does copper also get rusted? I have seen a greenish deposit on the surface of copper vessels.
> **Answer:** Yes, copper also undergoes a form of corrosion similar to rusting, but it forms a green deposit instead of reddish-brown rust.

When a copper vessel is exposed to moist air for long, it acquires a dull green coating. The green material is a mixture of copper hydroxide $\left(\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{OH})_{2}\right)$ and copper carbonate $\left(\mathrm{CuCO}_{3}\right)$. The following is the reaction:

> **Answer:** The solution is basic. You ascertain this by testing it with litmus paper; red litmus paper turns blue in a basic solution.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Metallic Oxides:** Formed when metals react with oxygen. Generally basic in nature (e.g., Iron oxide - rust, Magnesium oxide).
*   **Non-metallic Oxides:** Formed when non-metals react with oxygen. Generally acidic in nature (e.g., Sulphur dioxide).

The name of the product formed in the reaction of sulphur and oxygen is sulphur dioxide gas. When sulphur dioxide is dissolved in water sulphurous acid is formed. The reaction can be given as follows:
Sulphur dioxide $\left(\mathrm{SO}_{2}\right)+\mathrm{Water}\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{O}\right) \rightarrow$ Sulphurous acid $\left(\mathrm{H}_{2} \mathrm{SO}_{3}\right)$

The sulphurous acid turns blue litmus paper red. Generally, oxides of non-metals are **acidic** in nature.
