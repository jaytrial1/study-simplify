# Astronomy in Ancient India

Astronomy in ancient India is mentioned in the Rig Veda, composed about 4000 years ago. Many Indian scholars contributed, including **Aryabhata**.

Aryabhata's work on astronomy is in his writing 'Aryabhatiya', written in 499 CE at age 23. His estimate of Earth's diameter was close to the modern value. Against the popular view that Earth was immovable, Aryabhata stated Earth is a sphere rotating on its axis. His estimate of Earth's sidereal period (23 hours, 56 minutes, 4.1 seconds) is very close to the modern value.

He correctly explained that the moon and planets shine by reflected sunlight and gave scientific explanations for solar and lunar eclipses:
*   **Lunar eclipse:** Earth's shadow falls on the moon.
*   **Solar eclipse:** The moon's shadow falls on the Earth.

Aryabhata also found the distance between Earth and the moon, close to today's known value.
