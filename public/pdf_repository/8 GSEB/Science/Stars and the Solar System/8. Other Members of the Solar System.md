This section describes other celestial bodies orbiting the Sun, including asteroids, comets, and meteors.

# Some Other Members of the Solar System

Besides planets, other bodies revolve around the Sun and are part of the solar system.

## Asteroids

There's a large gap between the orbits of Mars and Jupiter. This region is occupied by a large number of small objects orbiting the Sun, called **asteroids**. They are only visible through large telescopes.

## Comets

**Comets** are solar system members that orbit the Sun in highly elliptical paths with very long periods of revolution. A comet usually has a bright head and a long tail that grows as it nears the Sun. The tail always points away from the Sun.



---
**Image:**

---


*The image depicts a celestial object with a tail following an elliptical path around a central light source, illustrating the trajectory and tail formation of a comet as it orbits the Sun.*

Many comets appear periodically, like Halley's comet, which appears about every 76 years. It was last seen in 1986.

**Q: Can you tell, when Halley's comet will be visible again?**
> **Answer:** Since Halley's comet appears about every 76 years and was last seen in 1986, it will be visible again around the year 1986 + 76 = 2062.

## Superstitions about the Comets

Some believe comets bring disasters, but this is superstition. Comet appearance is a natural phenomenon with no reason to be feared.

## Meteors and Meteorites

On clear, moonless nights, you might see bright streaks of light called **meteors** (commonly "shooting stars," though not stars). A meteor is a small object entering Earth's atmosphere at high speed. Friction heats it, causing it to glow and quickly evaporate, creating the brief streak.

Some meteors are large enough to reach Earth before fully evaporating. The body that reaches Earth is a **meteorite**. Meteorites help scientists study the material from which the solar system formed.

## Meteor Showers

When Earth crosses a comet's tail, many meteors are seen, called **meteor showers**. Some occur annually at regular times.
