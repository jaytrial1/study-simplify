11. Name two objects other than planets which are members of the solar system.

> **Answer:** Two objects other than planets which are members of the solar system are asteroids and comets (or meteors).

12. Explain how you can locate the Pole Star with the help of Ursa Major.

> **Answer:** To locate the Pole Star using Ursa Major, find the two stars at the end of the bowl of the Ursa Major constellation. Imagine a straight line passing through these two stars and extend this line towards the north direction, about five times the distance between the two stars. This line will lead you to the Pole Star.

13. Do all the stars in the sky move? Explain.

> **Answer:** From Earth, all stars appear to move from east to west because the Earth rotates on its axis. However, the Pole Star appears stationary because it is located very close to the direction of Earth's axis of rotation.

14. Why is the distance between stars expressed in light years? What do you understand by the statement that a star is eight light years away from the Earth?

> **Answer:** The distance between stars is expressed in light years because the distances are so vast that using kilometers results in extremely large and inconvenient numbers. A light year is the distance light travels in one year, which is a much more manageable unit for interstellar distances. The statement that a star is eight light years away from the Earth means that the light from that star takes eight years to reach Earth, and the distance is the total distance covered by light in eight years.

15. The radius of Jupiter is 11 times the radius of the Earth. Calculate the ratio of the volumes of Jupiter and the Earth. How many Earths can Jupiter accommodate?

> **Answer:** Assuming both planets are spheres, the volume of a sphere is given by V = (4/3)πr³.
> Let R_J be the radius of Jupiter and R_E be the radius of Earth.
> We are given R_J = 11 * R_E.
> Volume of Jupiter (V_J) = (4/3)π(R_J)³ = (4/3)π(11 * R_E)³ = (4/3)π * 11³ * (R_E)³ = 1331 * (4/3)π(R_E)³.
> Volume of Earth (V_E) = (4/3)π(R_E)³.
> The ratio of the volumes of Jupiter and Earth is V_J / V_E = [1331 * (4/3)π(R_E)³] / [(4/3)π(R_E)³] = 1331.
> The ratio of the volumes is approximately 1331:1. This means Jupiter's volume is about 1331 times the volume of Earth.
> Therefore, Jupiter can accommodate about 1331 Earths. (Note: The chapter mentions 1300 Earths can be placed inside Jupiter, which is a close approximation to this calculation based on the radius ratio).
