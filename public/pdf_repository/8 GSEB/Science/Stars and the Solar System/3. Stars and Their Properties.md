# The Stars

Many stars are visible in the night sky. They vary in brightness and color. Stars emit their own light. The Sun is also a star.

**Q: Why does it appear so large compared to the other stars?**
> **Answer:** The Sun appears so large compared to other stars because it is much, much closer to the Earth than any other star.

Comparing a football placed near you to one at a distance of 100m, the nearby one appears bigger. Similarly, stars are millions of times farther away than the Sun, making them appear as points.

The Sun is nearly 150 million km away from Earth. The next nearest star, Alpha Centauri, is about 40 trillion km away.

**Q: Can you read this distance in kilometres conveniently?**
> **Answer:** Reading distances like 40,000,000,000,000 km conveniently is difficult due to the large number of zeros.

Such large distances are expressed using a unit called **light year**. A light year is the distance traveled by light in one year. The speed of light is about 300,000 km per second.

The distance of the Sun from Earth is about 8 light minutes. The distance of Alpha Centauri is about 4.3 light years.

**Q: If light from stars takes years to reach us, I wonder if we are looking into the past when we look at stars.**
> **Answer:** Yes, when we look at stars, we are seeing light that left the star years ago, so we are essentially looking into the past.

**Q: I want to know why we do not see the stars during the day. Why are they visible only at night?**
> **Answer:** Stars are present in the sky during the day, but they are not visible because the bright sunlight overwhelms their faint light.

Observe a prominent star or group of stars for a few hours on a dark night. Do their positions change? You will find that stars appear to move from east to west. A star rising in the east in the evening sets in the west in the early morning.

**Q: Why do stars appear to move from east to west?**
> **Answer:** Stars appear to move from east to west because the Earth rotates on its axis from west to east.

## Activity: Apparent Motion

Stand at the center of a large room and rotate. Objects in the room will appear to move in the direction opposite to your motion. This is similar to how nearby trees and buildings appear to move backward when you are in a moving train.

This apparent east-to-west movement of stars is because the Earth rotates from west to east on its axis.

**Q: My grandfather told me that there is one star in the sky which does not move at all. How is it possible?**
> **Answer:** There is one star, the Pole Star, which appears stationary because it is located very close to the direction of the Earth's axis of rotation. As the Earth spins, this star stays in almost the same position in the sky.

## Activity: Demonstrating the Pole Star

Open an umbrella and paste one paper star at the center rod's position and others near the ends of the spokes. Rotate the umbrella by the central rod. The star at the center does not appear to move, while the others move in a circle. If a star were located where the Earth's axis meets the sky, it would also appear stationary.
