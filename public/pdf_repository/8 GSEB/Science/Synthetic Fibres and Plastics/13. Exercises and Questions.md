# Exercises

1. Explain why some fibres are called synthetic.

> **Answer:** Some fibres are called synthetic because they are made by human beings through chemical processes, rather than being obtained directly from natural sources like plants or animals.

2. Mark $(\checkmark)$ the correct answer.

Rayon is different from synthetic fibres because
(a) it has a silk-like appearance.
(b) it is obtained from wood pulp.
(c) its fibres can also be woven like those of natural fibres.

> **Answer:** (b) it is obtained from wood pulp. (Rayon is obtained from a natural source, wood pulp, unlike other fully synthetic fibres like nylon).

(b) Synthetic fibres are synthesised from raw material called $\qquad$ .
(c) Like synthetic fibres, plastic is also a $\qquad$ .

> **Answer:**
> (a) Synthetic fibres are also called **artificial** or **man-made** fibres.
> (b) Synthetic fibres are synthesised from raw material called **petrochemicals**.
> (c) Like synthetic fibres, plastic is also a **polymer**.

4. Give examples which indicate that nylon fibres are very strong.

> **Answer:** Examples that indicate nylon fibres are very strong include their use in making parachutes and ropes for rock climbing. The text also states that a nylon thread is stronger than a steel wire.

5. Explain why plastic containers are favoured for storing food.

> **Answer:** Plastic containers are favoured for storing food because they are light weight, have a lower price, possess good strength, are easy to handle, and are non-reactive, meaning they do not react with the food or chemicals stored in them.

6. Explain the difference between thermoplastic and thermosetting plastics.

> **Answer:** Thermoplastics are plastics that get deformed easily on heating and can be bent easily. They can be softened and moulded repeatedly. Thermosetting plastics, on the other hand, when moulded once, cannot be softened by heating. They retain their shape and structure upon heating.

7. Explain why the following are made of thermosetting plastics.
(a) Saucepan handles
(b) Electric plugs/switches/plug boards

> **Answer:**
> (a) Saucepan handles are made of thermosetting plastics like bakelite because they are poor conductors of heat and can tolerate high temperatures without deforming or melting, making them safe to touch while cooking.
> (b) Electric plugs, switches, and plug boards are made of thermosetting plastics like bakelite because they are poor conductors of electricity, preventing electric shock. They also do not melt or deform easily with the heat generated by electricity.

8. Categorise the materials of the following products into 'can be recycled' and 'cannot be recycled'.

Telephone instruments, plastic toys, cooker handles, carry bags, ball point pens, plastic bowls, plastic covering on electrical wires, plastic chairs, electrical switches.

> **Answer:**
> **Can be recycled:** Plastic toys (often thermoplastic), carry bags (often polythene, a thermoplastic), plastic bowls (often thermoplastic), plastic covering on electrical wires (often PVC, a thermoplastic), plastic chairs (often thermoplastic).
> **Cannot be recycled:** Telephone instruments (complex mixture of materials), cooker handles (thermosetting plastic), ball point pens (mixture of materials), electrical switches (thermosetting plastic).
> (Note: The recyclability of plastic items can vary depending on the specific type of plastic used and local recycling facilities).

9. Rana wants to buy shirts for summer. Should he buy cotton shirts or shirts made from synthetic material? Advise Rana, giving your reason.

> **Answer:** Rana should consider buying cotton shirts for summer. Cotton is a natural fibre that absorbs sweat well and allows air circulation, making it comfortable in hot weather. Synthetic fabrics, while durable and quick-drying, do not absorb sweat as well and can feel uncomfortable and sticky in the heat.

10. Give examples to show that plastics are noncorrosive in nature.

> **Answer:** Plastics are used to store various kinds of materials, including many chemicals, because they do not react with water and air and are not corroded easily. This demonstrates their noncorrosive nature.

11. Should the handle and bristles of a tooth brush be made of the same material? Explain your answer.

> **Answer:** The handle and bristles of a toothbrush should ideally not be made of the same material. The handle is often made of plastic (thermoplastic) for grip, durability, and mouldability. The bristles are made of fibres (often nylon, a strong and flexible synthetic fibre) which need to be flexible yet strong enough to clean teeth effectively without damaging gums.

12. 'Avoid plastics as far as possible'. Comment on this advice.

> **Answer:** The advice 'Avoid plastics as far as possible' is important because plastics are non-biodegradable and their disposal causes significant environmental problems, including pollution, harm to wildlife, and clogging of drains. While plastics have many useful properties, reducing their use, especially single-use plastics, and promoting recycling and responsible disposal are crucial for environmental protection.

14. 'Manufacturing synthetic fibres is actually helping conservation of forests'. Comment.

> **Answer:** Manufacturing synthetic fibres can help in the conservation of forests because it reduces the dependency on natural fibres like cotton (which requires land for cultivation) or potentially rayon (which uses wood pulp, although it's chemically treated). By providing alternative materials for clothing and other products, the demand for plant-based fibres from forests or agricultural land can be reduced, indirectly contributing to forest conservation.

15. Describe an activity to show that thermoplastic is a poor conductor of electricity.

> **Answer:** To show that thermoplastic is a poor conductor of electricity, one could set up a simple circuit with a battery, a bulb, and connecting wires. Instead of completing the circuit with a metal wire, insert a piece of thermoplastic (like a piece of plastic scale or a plastic handle from a screwdriver) in the gap. Observe that the bulb does not light up, indicating that the thermoplastic material does not allow electricity to flow through it, hence it is a poor conductor. (Precaution: Perform this activity under adult supervision and use a low-voltage battery).
