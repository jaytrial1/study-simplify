# Exercises

1.  Fill in the blanks.
    (a) Most liquids that conduct electricity are solutions of $\qquad$ ， $\qquad$ and $\qquad$ .
    > **Answer:** acids, bases, salts
    (b) The passage of an electric current through a solution causes $\qquad$ effects.
    > **Answer:** chemical
    (c) If you pass current through copper sulphate solution, copper gets deposited on the plate connected to the $\qquad$ terminal of the battery.
    > **Answer:** negative
    (d) The process of depositing a layer of any desired metal on another material by means of electricity is called $\qquad$ .
    > **Answer:** electroplating
2.  When the free ends of a tester are dipped into a solution, the magnetic needle shows deflection. Can you explain the reason?
    > **Answer:** The deflection of the magnetic needle shows that electric current is flowing through the solution. The solution must be a conductor of electricity. The current flowing through the wires of the tester creates a magnetic field around them, which causes the compass needle to deflect.

5.  A tester is used to check the conduction of electricity through two liquids, labelled A and B. It is found that the bulb of the tester glows brightly for liquid A while it glows very dimly for liquid B. You would conclude that
    (i) liquid A is a better conductor than liquid B.
    (ii) liquid B is a better conductor than liquid A.
    (iii) both liquids are equally conducting.
    (iv) conducting properties of liquid cannot be compared in this manner.
    > **Answer:** (i) liquid A is a better conductor than liquid B. A brighter glow indicates a stronger current, which means the liquid allows current to pass more easily.
6.  Does pure water conduct electricity? If not, what can we do to make it conducting?
    > **Answer:** Pure water (distilled water) does not conduct electricity. It is a poor conductor. To make it conducting, we can dissolve a substance like salt, an acid (like lemon juice or dilute acid), or a base in it.
7.  In case of a fire, before the firemen use the water hoses, they shut off the main electrical supply for the area. Explain why they do this.
    > **Answer:** Firemen shut off the electrical supply because the water they use for fighting fires, especially tap water or water from hoses, is not pure and contains dissolved salts, making it a good conductor of electricity. Using conductive water on electrical equipment or wires that are still live can lead to electric shock, potentially harming the firemen or bystanders, and can also cause further damage or spread the fire.
8.  A child staying in a coastal region tests the drinking water and also the seawater with his tester. He finds that the compass needle deflects more in the case of seawater. Can you explain the reason?
    > **Answer:** Seawater contains a much larger amount of dissolved salts compared to drinking water. Dissolved salts make water a better conductor of electricity. Since seawater is a better conductor, more electric current flows through the tester when dipped in seawater, resulting in a stronger magnetic field and therefore a greater deflection of the compass needle.
9.  Is it safe for the electrician to carry out electrical repairs outdoors during heavy downpour? Explain.
    > **Answer:** It is not safe for an electrician to carry out electrical repairs outdoors during a heavy downpour. Rainwater, although initially pure, dissolves substances from the air and ground as it falls, making it slightly conductive. Wet conditions also make the electrician's body and tools conductive. This increases the risk of electric shock if they come into contact with live wires or damaged insulation, as water can provide a path for the current to flow through their body to the ground.
10. Paheli had heard that rainwater is as good as distilled water. So she collected some rainwater in a clean glass tumbler and tested it using a tester. To her surprise she found that the compass needle showed deflection. What could be the reasons?
    > **Answer:** Paheli's observation that rainwater caused the compass needle to deflect indicates that the rainwater she collected was conductive. While rainwater is initially close to distilled water, it dissolves impurities like dissolved gases (forming mild acids) and pollutants from the air as it falls. These dissolved substances make the rainwater slightly conductive, allowing a weak current to flow through the tester and cause the magnetic needle to deflect.
11. Prepare a list of objects around you that are electroplated.
    > **Answer:** Objects around us that are commonly electroplated include: bicycle handlebars, wheel rims, bath taps, kitchen gas burners, car parts, jewellery (like imitation gold or silver), tin cans for food storage, and nuts, bolts, and screws (often zinc or chromium plated).
12. The process that you saw in Activity 14.7 is used for purification of copper. A thin plate of pure copper and a thick rod of impure copper are used as electrodes. Copper from impure rod is sought to be transfered to the thin copper plate. Which electrode should be attached to the positive terminal of the battery and why?
    > **Answer:** The thick rod of impure copper should be attached to the positive terminal of the battery. In the process of purification by electroplating (electrolysis), the impure metal is used as the anode (connected to the positive terminal). When current flows, the metal from the impure anode dissolves into the solution as ions. These ions then travel through the solution and are deposited as pure metal onto the cathode (connected to the negative terminal), which is made of pure metal.
