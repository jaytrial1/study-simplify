# Chemical Effects of Current

In Class VII, we have learnt some effects of electric current. Can you list these effects? What effect does the current produce when it flows through a conducting solution? Let us find out.
> **Answer:** Some effects of electric current learned in Class VII include the heating effect, the magnetic effect, and the chemical effect. When current flows through a conducting solution, it produces chemical effects.

## Activity 14.6: Passing Current Through Water

Take out carbon rods carefully from two discarded cells. Clean their metal caps with sand paper. Wrap copper wires around the metal caps of the carbon rods and join them to a battery (Fig. 14.5). We call these two rods **electrodes**. (Instead of carbon rods, you may take two iron nails about 6 cm long.) Pour a cupful of water in a glass/plastic bowl. Add a teaspoonful of salt or a few drops of lemon juice to water to make it more conducting. Now immerse the electrodes in this solution. Make sure that the metal caps of the carbon rods are outside the water. Wait for 3-4 minutes. Observe the electrodes carefully.

Do you notice any gas bubbles near the electrodes? Can we call the change taking place in the solution a chemical change? Recall the definition of a chemical change that you learnt in Class VII.
> **Answer:** Yes, you should notice gas bubbles forming near the electrodes. This change taking place in the solution is a chemical change because new substances (hydrogen and oxygen gases) are being formed. A chemical change involves the formation of new substances.
