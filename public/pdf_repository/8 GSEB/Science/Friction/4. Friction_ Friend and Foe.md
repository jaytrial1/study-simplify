This section discusses the dual nature of friction, highlighting its necessity in many daily activities while also pointing out its disadvantages.

# Friction : A Necessary Evil

Recall now some of your experiences. Is it easier to hold a kulhar (earthen pot) or a glass tumbler? Suppose the outer surface of the tumbler is greasy, or has a thin layer of cooking oil on it; would it become easier or more difficult to hold it? Just think : would it be possible to hold the glass at all if there is no friction?

> **Answer:** It is usually easier to hold a kulhar because its surface is rougher than a glass tumbler, providing more friction for grip. If the glass tumbler is greasy or oily, it would become much more difficult to hold because the oil significantly reduces friction. Without any friction at all, it would be impossible to hold the glass as there would be no force to counteract gravity and keep it from slipping.

Recall also how difficult it is to move on a wet muddy track, or wet marble floor. Can you imagine being able to walk at all if there were no friction?

> **Answer:** It would be impossible to walk at all if there were no friction. Walking requires friction between your feet and the ground to push backward and move forward.

If an object started moving, it would never stop if there were no friction.
