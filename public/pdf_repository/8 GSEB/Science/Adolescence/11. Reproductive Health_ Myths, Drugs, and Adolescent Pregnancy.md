## Myths, Taboos, Do's and Don'ts

You have learnt here and from Chapter 9 the scientific facts related to human reproduction. There are many wrong notions which you should now be able to discard as informed adolescents. For example, there are myths and taboos regarding bodily changes that adolescents experience. Some of these are given below and you can now argue why these are myths and not facts.

1.  A girl becomes pregnant if she looks at boys during menstruation.
2.  The mother is responsible for the sex of her child.
3.  A girl should not be allowed to work in the kitchen during menstruation.

You may come across many other myths and taboos. Discard them.

> **Answer:**
> 1. This is a myth. Pregnancy occurs when a sperm fertilises an egg, which happens through sexual intercourse, not by looking at someone. Menstruation is the shedding of the uterine lining when fertilisation has not occurred.
> 2. This is a myth. As explained in the section on sex determination, the sex of the child is determined by the type of sperm (carrying either an X or a Y chromosome) that fertilises the egg. The egg always carries an X chromosome. Therefore, the father determines the sex of the child.
> 3. This is a myth. Menstruation is a natural biological process and does not affect a girl's ability to perform daily tasks like working in the kitchen. Such restrictions are based on superstition, not facts.

## WHAT YOU HAVE LEARNT

*   Humans become capable of reproduction after puberty sets in. Children between the ages of 11 and 19 years are called adolescents.
*   The onset of puberty brings about growth of the reproductive organs. Hair grow at various places on the body. Breasts develop in girls and facial hair (moustache and beard) appear in boys. Voice of boys becomes hoarse as voice box enlarges during adolescence.
*   Children gain height during adolescence.
*   The onset of puberty and maturity of reproductive parts are controlled by hormones.
*   Hormones are secretions of endocrine glands which pour them directly into the blood stream.
*   Pituitary gland secretes hormones which include growth hormone and hormones that make other glands such as the testes, ovaries, thyroids and adrenals, secrete hormones. Pancreas secretes insulin, thyroid produces thyroxine and adrenals produce adrenalin.
*   Testosterone is the male hormone and estrogen, the female hormone. The uterine wall in females prepares itself to receive the developing fertilised egg. In case there is no fertilisation, the thickened lining of the uterine wall breaks down and goes out of the body along with blood. This is called menstruation.
*   Sex of the unborn child depends on whether the zygote has XX or XY chromosomes.
*   It is important to eat balanced food and maintain personal hygiene during adolescence.

8.  Write notes on-
    (a) Adam's apple.

    > **Answer:** Adam's apple is a protruding part of the throat visible in boys during puberty. It is formed by the growth of the voice box or larynx. The enlargement of the larynx is responsible for the deepening of the voice in boys. It is generally not visible from the outside in girls due to the smaller size of their larynx.

    (b) Secondary sexual characters.

    > **Answer:** Secondary sexual characters are features that help to distinguish males from females but are not directly involved in reproduction. These characters develop at puberty due to the action of sex hormones. Examples include the development of breasts in girls, growth of facial hair (moustache and beard) and chest hair in boys, and the growth of hair under the arms and in the pubic region in both genders.

    (c) Sex determination in the unborn baby.

    > **Answer:** The sex of an unborn baby is determined at the time of fertilisation by the sex chromosomes inherited from the parents. Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes, including two sex chromosomes (X and Y). Females have two X chromosomes (XX), while males have one X and one Y chromosome (XY). The egg cell always carries an X chromosome. The sperm cell can carry either an X or a Y chromosome. If a sperm with an X chromosome fertilises the egg, the zygote will be XX and develop into a female child. If a sperm with a Y chromosome fertilises the egg, the zygote will be XY and develop into a male child. Therefore, the father's sperm determines the sex of the baby.
