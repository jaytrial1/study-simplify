2.  Differentiate between the following.
    (a) Wildlife sanctuary and biosphere reserve
    > **Answer:** A wildlife sanctuary is an area protecting animals from disturbance in their habitat, sometimes allowing limited human activities like grazing. A biosphere reserve is a much larger area for conserving biodiversity, plant/animal resources, and the traditional life of tribals, and it may contain national parks and sanctuaries within it.
    (b) Zoo and wildlife sanctuary
    > **Answer:** A zoo keeps animals in artificial, controlled environments for display and conservation. A wildlife sanctuary protects animals within their natural habitat.
    (c) Endangered and extinct species
    > **Answer:** Endangered species are those whose numbers are diminishing to a level that they face the danger of extinction. Extinct species are those that have completely disappeared from the earth.
    (d) Flora and fauna
    > **Answer:** Flora refers to all the plants found in a particular area. Fauna refers to all the animals found in a particular area.

3.  Discuss the effects of deforestation on the following.
    (a) Wild animals
    > **Answer:** Deforestation destroys animal habitats, leads to food scarcity, increases human-animal conflict, reduces animal populations, and can cause species to become endangered or extinct.
    (b) Environment
    > **Answer:** Deforestation increases temperature and pollution, raises atmospheric carbon dioxide levels leading to global warming, lowers groundwater levels, reduces rainfall, increases soil erosion, decreases soil fertility, and can lead to desertification.
    (c) Villages (Rural areas)
    > **Answer:** People in rural areas often depend on forests for firewood, medicinal plants, and other products; deforestation causes shortages. It can also lead to soil erosion affecting agriculture and increased risk of floods and droughts impacting rural livelihoods.
    (d) Cities (Urban areas)
    > **Answer:** Deforestation contributes to climate change and air pollution which affect urban areas. It can also impact water supply if forests are part of the watershed for cities. Shortages of forest products like wood and paper also affect urban life and industries.
    (e) Earth
    > **Answer:** Deforestation contributes significantly to global warming and climate change by increasing greenhouse gases. It leads to widespread loss of biodiversity, disrupts ecosystems globally, and increases the frequency and intensity of natural disasters like floods and droughts.
    (f) The next generation
    > **Answer:** The next generation will inherit a planet with reduced biodiversity, altered climate patterns, degraded soil and water resources, and shortages of essential forest products. They will face greater environmental challenges and have fewer natural resources available.

4.  What will happen if.
    (a) we go on cutting trees.
    > **Answer:** If we go on cutting trees, it will lead to increased carbon dioxide in the atmosphere, global warming, reduced rainfall, increased droughts and floods, soil erosion, desertification, loss of habitat for wildlife, and ultimately, extinction of many species.
    (b) the habitat of an animal is disturbed.
    > **Answer:** If an animal's habitat is disturbed, the animal may lose its home and food sources, become vulnerable to predators or human conflict, be forced to migrate, or face a decline in its population, potentially leading to endangerment or extinction.
    (c) the top layer of soil is exposed.
    > **Answer:** If the top layer of soil is exposed, it is easily eroded by wind and water (soil erosion). This removes the most fertile part of the soil, leaving behind hard, rocky layers that are less fertile and have reduced water-holding capacity, which can lead to desertification.

5.  Answer in brief.
    (a) Why should we conserve biodiversity?
    > **Answer:** We should conserve biodiversity because the variety of organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms) and their interrelationships are essential for the well-being and survival of mankind. Biodiversity maintains the balance in nature, provides us with resources like food, medicine, and materials, and supports essential ecosystem services.
    (b) Protected forests are also not completely safe for wild animals. Why?
    > **Answer:** Protected forests are not completely safe because people living in the neighbourhood may encroach upon them and destroy parts of the habitat. Illegal poaching, forest fires (sometimes accidental), and other human disturbances can still occur.
    (c) Some tribals depend on the jungle. How?
    > **Answer:** Some tribals depend on the jungle for their traditional way of life. They may collect firewood, medicinal plants, fruits, and other forest products for their sustenance and livelihood. The forest is their home and provides for their basic needs.
    (d) What are the causes and consequences of deforestation?
    > **Answer:** Causes include procuring land for cultivation, building houses/factories, making furniture, using wood as fuel, forest fires, and droughts. Consequences include increased temperature and pollution, increased CO2, lower groundwater, reduced rainfall, droughts, floods, soil erosion, desertification, and loss of biodiversity.
    (e) What is Red Data Book?
    > **Answer:** The Red Data Book is a source book that keeps a record of all endangered animals and plants.
    (f) What do you understand by the term migration?
    > **Answer:** Migration is the phenomenon where a species moves from its natural habitat to another area during a specific time of year, often due to climatic changes, for a particular purpose such as breeding.

6.  In order to meet the ever-increasing demand in factories and for shelter, trees are being continually cut. Is it justified to cut trees for such projects? Discuss and prepare a brief report.
    > **Answer:** This requires discussion and report preparation by the student, presenting arguments for and against. Arguments against justification include the severe environmental consequences (habitat loss, climate change, soil degradation) and loss of biodiversity. Arguments for might mention human needs for development and resources, but this needs to be balanced against long-term environmental sustainability. The conclusion should likely favour finding sustainable alternatives and limiting deforestation.

7.  How can you contribute to the maintenance of green wealth of your locality? Make a list of actions to be taken by you.
    > **Answer:** This requires action and listing by the student. Actions could include planting trees, taking care of existing plants, reducing paper usage, recycling paper, participating in local clean-up drives, conserving water, and educating others about the importance of plants.

8.  Explain how deforestation leads to reduced rainfall.
    > **Answer:** Deforestation leads to reduced rainfall because trees absorb carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas. Cutting trees increases CO2 levels in the atmosphere, trapping heat and causing global warming. Increased temperature disrupts the water cycle, potentially leading to less evaporation from land surfaces and reduced cloud formation, resulting in decreased rainfall and droughts.

10. Why should paper be saved? Prepare a list of ways by which you can save paper.
    > **Answer:** Paper should be saved because it is made from trees, and producing one tonne of paper requires cutting many trees. Saving paper helps conserve forests, saves energy and water used in manufacturing, and reduces the use of harmful chemicals. Ways to save paper include using both sides of the paper, reusing envelopes and packaging, recycling used paper, using digital documents instead of printing, and using cloth bags instead of paper bags.
