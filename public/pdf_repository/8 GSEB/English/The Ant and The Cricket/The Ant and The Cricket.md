# Understanding Fables

A **fable** is a short story, often featuring animals as characters, that teaches a **moral** lesson. This poem about an ant and a cricket contains a significant idea that applies to both animals and humans. The poem suggests that some people, like the cricket, may not prepare for the future.

## The Cricket's Carefree Summer

A young, thoughtless **cricket**, used to spending the warm, sunny months of summer and spring singing happily, found himself in trouble when winter arrived. His cupboard was empty, and there was no food to be found anywhere – not a crumb on the snow-covered ground, no flowers, no leaves. Facing starvation, the cricket worried about his fate.

## Seeking Help from the Ant

Made desperate by hunger and cold, and dripping wet from the weather, the trembling cricket decided to seek help from a thrifty **ant**. He hoped the ant would offer him shelter from the rain and a little bit of grain to keep him alive. He only wished to **borrow** the food, promising to repay it the next day, fearing that otherwise, he would die of starvation and sorrow.

## The Ant's Response and the Moral

The ant replied to the cricket, acknowledging their friendship but stating a fundamental rule: "We ants never **borrow**; we ants never **lend**." The ant then asked the cricket what he did during the warm weather. The cricket admitted that his heart was so light that he sang day and night because nature seemed so cheerful.

The ant's stern reply was, "You sang, Sir, you say? Go then, and **dance the winter away**." With that, the ant quickly opened the door and turned the poor cricket out.

The poem concludes by calling this story a fable and stating that it is true, suggesting that some "crickets" (meaning people) have four legs, and some have two, implying the moral applies to humans as well as the animal characters. The story is adapted from Aesop's Fables.
