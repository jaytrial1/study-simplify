# Budget Expenditure
Budget Expenditure refers to the estimated expenditure of the government during a given fiscal year. It is classified into:
(i) Revenue Expenditure
(ii) Capital Expenditure.

## Revenue Expenditure
Revenue expenditure is government expenditure that *neither* creates any asset *nor* causes reduction in any liability of the government. It is recurring.
*   Incurred on normal government functioning and service provision.
*   Examples: Payment of salaries, pensions, interests, expenditure on administrative/defence/health services, grants to states.

An expenditure is a revenue expenditure if it meets both conditions:
(i) It must not create an asset (e.g., salaries don't create assets, construction does).
(ii) It must not decrease any liability (e.g., repayment of borrowings decreases liability).

*Note: Union Grants to states are treated as revenue expenditure even if used by states for asset creation.*

## Capital Expenditure
Capital expenditure is government expenditure that *either* creates an asset *or* causes a reduction in the liabilities of the government. It is non-recurring.
*   Adds to the economy's capital stock and increases productivity through long-term development projects (Metro, Flyover).
*   Examples: Loans to States/UTs, expenditure on building roads/factories, purchase of machinery, repayment of borrowings.

An expenditure is a capital expenditure if it meets *any one* of the two conditions:
(i) It must create an asset (e.g., constructing Metro creates an asset, paying salaries does not).
(ii) It must cause a decrease in liabilities (e.g., repaying borrowings reduces liability).

*Payment of salary, interest, or pension is not capital expenditure as it neither creates an asset nor reduces a liability.*

### Comparison between Revenue Expenditure and Capital Expenditure

| Basic    | Revenue Expenditure                                          | Capital Expenditure                                            |
| :------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning  | Neither creates asset nor reduces liability.                 | Either creates asset or reduces liability.                     |
| Purpose  | Normal running of government departments and services.       | Acquisition of assets, granting loans and advances.            |
| Nature   | Recurring (day-to-day activities).                           | Non-recurring.                                                 |
| Examples | Salary, pension, interest, administrative/defence costs.     | Repayment of borrowings, expenditure on capital asset acquisition. |

### How to classify Expenditure as Revenue or Capital Expenditure?
*   An expenditure is a capital expenditure if it either creates an asset or reduces a liability.
*   An expenditure is revenue expenditure if it neither creates any asset nor reduces any liability.

## Items categorised as Revenue and Capital Expenditure
*   **Subsidies:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
*   **Defence capital equipments purchased from Germany:** Capital expenditure (increases asset).
*   **Grants given to State Governments:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
*   **Construction of school buildings:** Capital expenditure (increases asset).
*   **Expenditure incurred on administrative and defence services:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
*   **Repayment of loans:** Capital expenditure (reduces liability).
*   **Expenditure on building a bridge:** Capital expenditure (increases asset).
*   **Payment of salaries to staff of government hospitals:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
*   **Purchase of 20 cranes for the construction of flyovers:** Capital expenditure (increases asset).
*   **Amount borrowed from USA repaid:** Capital expenditure (reduces liability).
*   **Expenditure on collection of taxes:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
*   **Expenditure on purchasing computers:** Capital expenditure (increases asset).
*   **Expenditure on scholarships:** Revenue expenditure (neither creates asset nor reduces liability).
