# Balanced, Surplus and Deficit Budget
The relationship between total estimated government receipts and expenditures determines the type of budget:

1.  **Balanced Budget:** Estimated government receipts equal estimated government expenditure.
    *   `Balanced Budget: Estimated Government Receipts = Estimated Government Expenditure`
    *   Indicates financial stability and that the government is not spending wastefully. It's an ideal but often impractical situation, especially during economic fluctuations.

2.  **Surplus Budget:** Estimated government receipts are more than estimated government expenditure.
    *   `Surplus Budget: Estimated Government Receipts > Estimated Government Expenditure`
    *   Reduces aggregate demand as the government takes more money out than it injects. Useful during inflation (excess demand).
    *   Harmful during deflation as it further reduces aggregate demand.

3.  **Deficit Budget:** Estimated government receipts are less than estimated government expenditure.
    *   `Deficit Budget: Estimated Government Receipts < Estimated Government Expenditure`
    *   Common in developing countries needing high expenditure for development.
    *   Increases aggregate demand as the government injects more money than it takes out. Useful during deficient demand to control deflation.
    *   Harmful during excess demand as it exacerbates inflationary pressure.
