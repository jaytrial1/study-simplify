# Objectives of Government Budget
Governments prepare budgets to achieve specific economic, social, and political goals. The primary objectives include:

1.  **Reallocation of Resources (Allocation Function):** The budget helps governments reallocate resources according to economic (profit maximization) and social (public welfare) priorities. This can be done through:
    *   **Tax concessions or subsidies:** Encouraging investment by providing tax breaks or subsidies (e.g., subsidies for 'Khadi products', heavy taxes on harmful goods like liquor).
    *   **Directly producing goods and services:** Undertaking non-profitable activities essential for public interest and welfare, such as water supply, sanitation, law and order, and national defense. These are often referred to as 'Public Goods'.

## Public Goods and Private Goods

*   **Public Goods:** Goods or services consumed collectively. They are characterized by:
    *   **Non-rivalrous:** One person's consumption does not reduce availability for others (e.g., a public park).
    *   **Non-excludable:** It's impossible to prevent anyone from benefiting, even if they don't pay. This leads to the 'free-rider' problem.
*   **Private Goods:** Goods or services whose benefits are limited to those who pay for them (e.g., a chocolate bar).

## Public Provision Vs Public Production

*   **Public provision:** Funding of public goods through the government budget, allowing public use without direct payment.
*   **Public production:** Goods directly produced by the government.

2.  **Reducing inequalities in income and wealth:** Governments use budgetary policy to reduce economic disparities by taxing the rich and increasing spending on the welfare of the poor (e.g., providing free LPG connections or subsidized food grains to BPL families).

3.  **Economic Stability:** Maintaining price stability by controlling fluctuations through taxes and expenditure.
    *   During **inflation** (aggregate demand > aggregate supply), the government can reduce its expenditure.
    *   During **deflation**, the government can increase expenditure and provide tax concessions and subsidies.
    *   A surplus budget during inflation and a deficit budget during deflation helps stabilize prices.

4.  **Management of Public Enterprises:** Budgetary provisions are made for managing and providing financial assistance to public sector industries, especially natural monopolies, which are established for social welfare.

## Natural Monopoly

A situation where a single firm can produce goods/services at a lower cost than multiple competing firms (e.g., railways, post offices, electricity distribution often operate as natural monopolies managed by the government for public welfare).

5.  **Economic Growth:** Promoting sustainable increase in real GDP (volume of goods and services). The budget can foster growth by:
    *   Providing tax rebates and incentives for productive ventures to stimulate savings and investment.
    *   Spending on infrastructure (roads, flyovers) which boosts production activities and generates demand, encouraging private sector growth.
    *   *Note:* Government must consider inflation and potential debt risk when planning such expenditures.

6.  **Reducing regional disparities:** Using taxation and expenditure policies to encourage the establishment of production units in economically backward regions (e.g., establishing Special Economic Zones - SEZs).

7.  **Employment Generation:** Using the budget to create jobs through investments in infrastructure projects (flyovers, bridges) and implementing employment generation schemes (MGNREGA, SJSRY, PMRY).
