# Additional Budgetary Concepts

## Kinds of Direct Tax Systems
Direct tax systems can be classified based on how the tax rate changes with income or economic power:

*   **(i) Proportional Tax System:** The tax rate remains constant regardless of income level.
    *   Example: A flat 10% tax rate means someone earning ₹1,00,000 pays ₹10,000, and someone earning ₹1,25,000 pays ₹12,500. The tax burden is proportionally higher for lower-income individuals relative to their income.
*   **(ii) Progressive Tax System:** The tax rate increases as the income level of the taxpayer increases.
    *   Example: A 10% rate for income between ₹60,000 and ₹1,00,000, and a 20% rate for income between ₹1,00,000 and ₹1,50,000. The tax burden is higher on higher-income individuals. India follows a progressive tax system.
*   **(iii) Regressive Tax System:** The tax rate decreases as the income level of the taxpayer increases.
    *   Example: If tax is ₹1,000 on ₹10,000 income (10% rate), but ₹1,200 on ₹15,000 income (8% average rate). The tax burden falls more heavily on lower-income individuals.

## PLAN AND NON-PLAN EXPENDITURE
Government expenditure can also be classified as Plan and Non-Plan Expenditure.

*   **1. Plan Expenditure:** Expenditure on programs outlined in the current five-year plan (though formal five-year plans have been replaced, the concept broadly refers to development outlays). Examples include expenditure on agriculture, energy, transport, communication, social services, and central assistance to states/UTs for planned projects.
*   **2. Non-Plan Expenditure:** Expenditure outside the scope of planned development programs. Examples include interest payments, defence services, subsidies, and administrative services. This is essential for the routine functioning of the government.

### Plan Expenditure Vs Non-Plan Expenditure
*   (i) Plan expenditure focuses on current development and investment; Non-plan expenditure is for routine government functions.
*   (ii) Plan expenditure arises from specific plans; Non-plan expenditure is essential for any economy and government operations.

### How to classify an expenditure as Plan or Non-Plan Expenditure?
*   An expenditure is a plan expenditure if it arises due to planned proposals.
*   An expenditure is a non-plan expenditure if it is outside the scope of government plans.

## DEVELOPMENTAL AND NON-DEVELOPMENTAL EXPENDITURE
Government expenditure can also be classified based on its contribution to economic development.

*   **1. Developmental Expenditure:** Expenditure directly related to the economic and social development of the country. Examples include spending on agricultural/industrial development, education, health, social welfare, and scientific research. This expenditure adds to the flow of goods and services.
*   **2. Non-Developmental Expenditure:** Expenditure on essential general services of the government. Examples include spending on defence, administrative services, police, and justice. While not directly contributing to economic development, it indirectly supports it by maintaining law and order and providing necessary administrative functions.

### Developmental Expenditure Vs Non-Developmental Expenditure
*   (i) Developmental expenditure directly contributes to economic development; Non-developmental expenditure indirectly supports it by maintaining essential services.
*   (ii) Developmental expenditure is productive, adding to goods and services flow; Non-developmental expenditure is focused on administrative/essential functions, not directly on productivity.

### How to classify an expenditure as Developmental and Non-Developmental Expenditure?
*   An expenditure is a developmental expenditure if it directly adds to the flow of goods and services.
*   An expenditure is a non-developmental expenditure if it indirectly contributes to economic development.
