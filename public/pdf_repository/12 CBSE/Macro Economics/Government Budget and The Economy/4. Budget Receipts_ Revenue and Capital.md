# Budget Receipts
Budget receipts are the estimated money receipts of the government from all sources during a fiscal year. They are classified into:
(i) Revenue receipts
(ii) Capital receipts

# Revenue Receipts
Revenue receipts are those government receipts that *neither* create any liability *nor* cause any reduction in government assets. They are regular and recurring.

A receipt is a revenue receipt if it meets both conditions:
(i) It must not create a liability (e.g., taxes do not create liability, borrowings do).
(ii) It must not decrease assets (e.g., selling public enterprise shares decreases assets).

## Two Sources of Revenue Receipts
Revenue receipts come from:
(i) Tax Revenue
(ii) Non-Tax Revenue

## Tax Revenue
Tax revenue is the total receipts from taxes and duties. A tax is a compulsory payment to the government without direct benefit in return.
*   Tax is compulsory.
*   Tax receipts are used for the common benefit of people, not direct benefit to the taxpayer.

Tax revenue is the main source of regular receipts.

## Tax Revenue Classification:
(i) Direct Taxes
(ii) Indirect Taxes

### Direct Taxes
Direct taxes are imposed on the income and property of individuals and companies. Their burden *cannot* be shifted.
*   Imposed on individuals/companies; burden is on the same person/entity.
*   'Liability to pay' (impact) and 'actual burden' (incidence) are on the same person (e.g., Income Tax).
*   They directly affect income and purchasing power, influencing aggregate demand.
*   Examples: Income Tax, Corporate Tax, Property Tax, Capital Gains Tax.

### Indirect Taxes
Indirect taxes are imposed on goods and services. Their burden *can* be shifted to another person/entity, usually the final consumer.
*   Imposed on goods and services.
*   'Liability to pay' (impact) is on one person (e.g., seller), but 'actual burden' (incidence) is on another (e.g., consumer). Burden can be shifted.
*   Example: Goods and Services Tax (GST). Sellers pay GST to the government but collect it from consumers.
*   Examples: Goods and Services Tax (GST), Basic Customs Duty, Central Excise and VAT on Petroleum Products, Excise on Liquor, Electricity Duties, Stamp Duty, Securities Transaction Tax, Entertainment Tax, etc.
*   Most indirect taxes (except those listed above) are now part of GST.
*   Indirect taxes can sometimes be avoided by not engaging in taxed transactions (e.g., buying tax-exempt items).

### How to classify a Tax as Direct Tax or Indirect Tax?
*   A tax is a direct tax if its burden cannot be shifted (impact and incidence on the same person).
*   A tax is an indirect tax if its burden can be shifted (impact and incidence on different persons).

[^0]: *Wealth Tax is no longer applicable from the Financial Year 2015-16.*

### Comparison between Direct Taxes and Indirect Taxes

| Basis           | Direct Taxes                                                                 | Indirect Taxes                                                                |
| :-------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Impact          | Levied on individuals and companies.                                         | Levied on goods and services.                                                 |
| Shift of burden | Burden cannot be shifted (impact and incidence on the same person).          | Burden can be shifted (impact and incidence on different persons).            |
| Nature          | Generally Progressive (tax rate increases with income).                      | Generally Proportional (tax rate constant with income changes).               |
| Coverage        | Limited reach (do not reach all sections).                                   | Wide coverage (reach all sections of society).                                |

### Items categorised as Direct and Indirect Taxes
*   **Corporation Tax:** Direct tax (impact and incidence on the same entity).
*   **Goods and Services Tax:** Indirect tax (impact on seller, incidence on consumer - burden can be shifted).
*   **Income Tax:** Direct tax (impact and incidence on the same person).
*   **Capital Gains Tax:** Direct tax (impact and incidence on the same person).

## Non-Tax Revenue
Non-Tax revenue includes government receipts from sources other than taxes. Main sources:

1.  **Interest:** Received on loans given to state governments, union territories, enterprises, and the public.
2.  **Profits and Dividends:** Profits from public sector undertakings (like railways, LIC) and dividends from government investments in other companies.
3.  **Fees:** Charges for recurring government services provided in public interest (e.g., court fees, registration fees). Compulsory like taxes for those using the service.
4.  **License Fee:** Payment for government permission (e.g., gun license, commercial vehicle permit).
5.  **Fines and Penalties:** Imposed on law breakers (e.g., traffic fines, tax non-payment penalties). Different from taxes; levied for law/order, not primarily revenue.
6.  **Escheats:** Government claim on property of a person dying without legal heir or will.
7.  **Gifts and Grants:** Received from foreign governments, international organizations, or voluntary contributions from individuals/companies (often during crises). Not a fixed source.
8.  **Forfeitures:** Penalties imposed by courts for non-compliance.
9.  **Special Assessment:** Payment by property owners whose property value increased due to government developmental activities (e.g., near a new Metro station).

### Comparison between Tax Revenue and Non-Tax Revenue

| Basis   | Tax Revenue                                       | Non-Tax Revenue                                                                 |
| :------ | :------------------------------------------------ | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Meaning | Receipts from taxes and other duties.             | Receipts from all sources other than tax receipts.                              |
| Nature  | Compulsory payment.                               | Not compulsory; payable when government services are availed.                   |
| Benefit | No direct benefit received in exchange for tax.   | Paid in exchange for services received (though not always directly proportional). |
| Examples| Income Tax, Corporate Tax, GST.                   | Interest, Profits and Dividends, Fees.                                          |

# Capital Receipts
Capital receipts are those government receipts that *either* create a liability *or* cause a reduction in government assets. They are non-recurring.

A receipt is a capital receipt if it meets *any one* of the two conditions:
(i) It must create a liability (e.g., borrowings increase liability).
(ii) It must cause a decrease in assets (e.g., selling public enterprise shares decreases assets).

## Sources of Capital Receipts
Capital receipts come from three main groups:

1.  **Borrowings:** Funds raised by the government to cover excess expenditure. Sources include:
    *   Open Market (Public)
    *   Reserve Bank of India (RBI)
    *   Foreign governments
    *   International institutions (World Bank, IMF)
    *   *Borrowings are capital receipts because they create a liability.*
2.  **Recovery of Loans:** Repayment of loans granted by the government to states or union territories.
    *   *Recovery of loans is a capital receipt because it reduces the assets (loans given out) of the government.*
3.  **Other Receipts:**
    *   **(a) Disinvestment:** Selling part or all of government shares in public sector undertakings (PSUs).
        *   *Disinvestment proceeds are capital receipts because they reduce government assets (ownership in PSUs).*
    *   **(b) Small Savings:** Funds raised from the public through schemes like Post Office deposits, National Saving Certificates.
        *   *Small savings are capital receipts because they create a liability for the government (obligation to repay).*

## Debt Creating Vs Non-Debt Creating Capital Receipts
Capital Receipts can be classified based on whether they create debt:
1.  **Debt Creating Capital Receipts:** Create debt for the government (e.g., Net Borrowings).
2.  **Non-Debt Creating Capital Receipts:** Do not create debt or liability (e.g., Recovery of loans, Disinvestment).

### Comparison between Revenue Receipts and Capital Receipts

| Basis             | Revenue Receipts                                     | Capital Receipts                                       |
| :---------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning           | Neither create liability nor reduce assets.          | Either create liability or reduce assets.              |
| Nature            | Regular and recurring.                               | Irregular & non-recurring.                             |
| Future obligation | No future obligation to return.                      | For some (like borrowings), future obligation to return. |
| Examples          | Tax Revenue (Income tax, GST), Non-tax revenue (Interest, fees). | Borrowings, Disinvestment, Recovery of loans.          |

### How to classify a receipt as Revenue Receipt or Capital Receipt?
*   A receipt is a capital receipt if it either creates an asset or reduces a liability.
*   A receipt is a revenue receipt if it neither creates any liability nor reduces any asset.

### Items categorised as Revenue and Capital Receipts
*   **Loan from the World Bank / Loan taken by the government from Reserve Bank of India (RBI):** Capital receipt (creates liability).
*   **Corporation tax / Income tax received by government:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Grants received from World Bank:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Profits of public sector undertakings:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Sale of a public sector undertaking / Receipts from sale of shares of a PSU / Disinvestment receipts:** Capital receipt (reduces assets).
*   **Foreign aid against earthquake victims / Financial assistance by the Government of USA for promoting girl education in India:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Dividend received by the government from a Maharatna company:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Borrowings from public:** Capital receipt (creates liability).
*   **Fees of Government College:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Recovery of loans / Debt cleared by Sri Lanka Government to the Indian Government:** Capital receipt (reduces assets).
*   **Interest received on loans:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
*   **Tax receipts:** Revenue receipt (neither creates liability nor reduces asset).
