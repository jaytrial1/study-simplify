# Money Supply

**Money Supply** refers to the total amount of money held by the public in an economy at a specific point in time.

## Features of Money Supply

1.  **Includes Money Held by Public Only:** It counts money held by individuals and businesses (the money-using sector). It excludes money held by the government and the banking system (the money-creating sector) because these balances are not in actual circulation among the public.
2.  **Stock Concept:** Money supply is measured at a particular moment in time, making it a stock variable.

## Components of Money Supply

The two main components are:

1.  **Currency with Public:** Consists of paper notes and coins held by individuals and firms. This is the most liquid asset. Includes various denominations of coins and notes.
    *   **Fiat Money:** Currency money is also called **Fiat Money**. This is money declared legal tender by government order, which must be accepted for all debts by law.
    *   **Legal Tender Money:** Currency is also **Legal Tender Money** as it is legally required to be accepted for payments and obligations.

2.  **Demand Deposits with Banks:** These are deposits held by the public in commercial banks that can be withdrawn or transferred using cheques at any time.
    *   Demand deposits are considered equivalent to currency as they are widely accepted for payments.
    *   **Only Net Demand Deposits are Included:** Money supply measures include only *net* demand deposits (public's deposits), excluding inter-bank deposits (deposits banks hold for other banks) as these are not held by the public.
    *   **Term Deposits are Not Included:** Fixed deposits or other time deposits are not included in the narrow definition (M1) because they cannot be withdrawn by cheque on demand.

These two components are sometimes called **'Transaction Money'** because they are readily usable for transactions.

## Measures of Money Supply

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has evolved four measures of money supply since 1977: M₁, M₂, M₃, and M₄.

    M₃ = M₁ + Net Time Deposits with Banks

*   **M₄:** The broadest measure.
    M₄ = M₃ + Total Deposits with Post Office Saving Bank (Excluding NSC)
    (NSC = National Saving Certificate)

## Important Facts about Money Supply

*   The four measures represent decreasing liquidity, with M₁ being most liquid and M₄ least liquid.
*   M₃ is commonly used as a measure of money supply and is called the 'aggregate monetary resources of the society'.
*   M₁ and M₂ are 'narrow money' concepts; M₃ and M₄ are 'broad money' concepts.
*   RBI is responsible for releasing money supply data in India.

## Example of Money Supply Calculation

Given the following data (₹ in crores):
(i) Currency with public: 84,000
(ii) Demand deposits with banks: 68,000
(iii) Other deposits with RBI: 3,612
(iv) Total deposits with Post office: 22,500
(v) Net time deposits with banks: 2,00,555
(vi) Savings deposits with post office saving bank: 5,528

**Solution:**

M₁ = Currency with public + Demand deposits with banks + Other deposits with RBI
M₁ = 84,000 + 68,000 + 3,612 = ₹ 1,55,612 crores

M₂ = M₁ + Savings deposits with post office saving bank
M₂ = 1,55,612 + 5,528 = ₹ 1,61,140 crores

M₃ = M₁ + Net time deposits with banks
M₃ = 1,55,612 + 2,00,555 = ₹ 3,56,167 crores

M₄ = M₃ + Total deposits with Post office
M₄ = 3,56,167 + 22,500 = ₹ 3,78,667 crores

## What is High Powered Money (H)?

**High Powered Money (H)** is the money directly created by the central bank (RBI) and the government. It comprises:
(i) Currency held by the public
(ii) Cash reserves held by commercial banks

## 'Money (M)' Vs 'High Powered Money (H)'

*   'Money' (specifically M₁) includes currency held by the public and demand deposits with banks.
*   'High Powered Money' includes currency held by the public and cash reserves with banks.
*   'Currency held by the public' is common to both definitions.
*   The key difference is that 'Money' includes demand deposits (created by banks), while 'High Powered Money' includes the cash reserves that banks use as a base to create those demand deposits. This is why 'H' is considered 'high-powered' – it forms the foundation for the larger amount of 'Money' (M).
