## Primary Functions

These are the most important functions that money must perform in any economy.

### Medium of Exchange

Money serves as a **medium of exchange**, meaning it is generally accepted as a means of payment for goods and services. This is its most essential function. Its general acceptability allows all exchanges to occur in terms of money.

*   This function eliminates the need for the double coincidence of wants, a major difficulty in the barter system.
*   It separates the acts of buying and selling, allowing them to happen independently.
*   It facilitates trade and transactions within the economy.
*   While money itself doesn't satisfy wants, it provides the power to purchase things that do.

### Measure of Value (Unit of Value)

Money acts as a **measure of value** or a **unit of value**. It provides a common denominator in which the values of all goods and services are expressed (their price).

*   By assigning a single unit (price) to everything, it simplifies determining exchange ratios and comparing prices.
*   This function is crucial for maintaining business accounts.
*   Money helps calculate relative prices, making it a **'Unit of Account'**. For example, the Rupee (₹) is the unit of account in India, the Pound (£) in England, etc.

## Secondary Functions

These functions are supplementary and derived from the primary functions.

### Standard of Deferred Payments

Money acts as a **standard of deferred payments**, meaning it is used as a 'standard' for payments that will be made in the future (credit transactions). This function is vital for modern economies, facilitating borrowing and lending, capital formation, and economic development.

*   It simplifies borrowing and lending operations.
*   It has enabled the creation of financial institutions.

### Store of Value (Asset Function of Money)

Money functions as a **store of value**, allowing people to transfer purchasing power from the present to the future. It is a convenient way to hold wealth.

*   While other assets can store wealth, money is generally the most economical and convenient.
*   It provides security for unforeseen events and future obligations.
*   Unlike many goods in a barter system, money is durable and storage is easy and inexpensive.
*   Money is available in various denominations, easily portable, and generally accepted, making it readily exchangeable for goods at any time.
*   Storing savings in money is generally more secure than storing goods.

## Transfer of Value

Another secondary function is the **transfer of value**. Money allows the value of durable and immovable assets (like land or machinery) to be transferred from one place to another. One can sell property in one location and use the money to buy property elsewhere, providing mobility to capital assets.

## How Money Overcame Barter Drawbacks

Money effectively solved the major problems of the barter system:

*   **Medium of Exchange:** Removed the need for double coincidence of wants by separating buying and selling.
*   **Measure of Value:** Provided a common unit for expressing and comparing the value of different goods.
*   **Store of Value:** Offered a durable, portable, and easily storable form of wealth, unlike perishable goods.
*   **Standard of Deferred Payments:** Simplified credit transactions and future payments, which were difficult or impossible under barter.
