# Demand for Money

Unlike goods that directly satisfy wants, money's utility comes from its ability to purchase goods and services. People demand money for several reasons or motives:

*   **Transaction Motive:** The need to hold cash for daily transactions (buying goods and services). This applies to consumers needing money for household expenses (income motive) and businesses needing money for operations (business motive). Transaction demand for money is positively related to income level – higher income means more money held for transactions.
*   **Precautionary Motive:** The desire to hold cash balances for unforeseen events or emergencies like sickness or accidents. The amount held depends on individual temperament and economic conditions. Precautionary demand is also positively related to income.

## Transaction Motive Vs Precautionary Motive

1.  Transaction motive relates to planned, ordinary transactions, while precautionary motive relates to unexpected transactions.
2.  Holding money for transactions offers convenience and relative certainty of value. Holding money for precautionary reasons depends on the degree of uncertainty in the environment (more cash might be held during crises).

Cash balances for both transaction and precautionary motives generally increase with income.

*   **Speculative Motive:** The desire to hold cash as an alternative to financial assets like bonds. People decide between holding bonds or cash based on expectations about future interest rates and bond prices. Bond prices move inversely to interest rates. When interest rates are high (and bond prices low), people expect rates to fall (and bond prices to rise), making bonds attractive, so they hold less cash for speculation. When interest rates are low (and bond prices high), people expect rates to rise (and bond prices to fall), making bonds risky, so they hold more cash for speculation. Speculative demand for money is inversely related to the interest rate.
