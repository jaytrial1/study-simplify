This section details how the central bank uses quantitative and qualitative monetary policy tools to increase money supply and credit availability, thereby boosting aggregate demand during periods of deficient demand.

# Monetary Policy Measures to Correct Deficient Demand

During deflationary situations, the central bank aims to ensure easy availability of credit and reduce the cost of borrowing money through its **Monetary Policy**. Two major instruments are:

## Quantitative Instruments

1.  **Decrease in Bank Rate:** Bank rate is the rate at which the central bank lends money to commercial banks to meet their long-term needs. During deficient demand, the central bank reduces the bank rate in order to expand credit. It leads to a fall in the market rate of interest which induces people to borrow more funds. It ultimately leads to an increase in aggregate demand.
2.  **Decrease in Repo Rate:** Repo rate is the rate at which the central bank lends money to commercial banks to meet their short-term needs. During deficient demand, the central bank decreases the repo rate to expand credit. It leads to a fall in the interest rates which induces people to borrow more funds and helps to increase aggregate demand.
3.  **Decrease in Reverse Repo Rate:** Reverse Repo Rate is the rate of interest at which commercial banks can deposit their surplus funds with the Central Bank, for a relatively shorter period of time. To control the situation of deficient demand, this rate may be decreased by the Central Bank. It discourages Commercial Banks from depositing their surplus funds with the Central Bank. It will raise the credit-creating power of Commercial Banks, which will increase the consumption expenditure and investment expenditure, leading to a rise in Aggregate Demand.
4.  **Open Market Operations (Purchase of Securities):** Open market operations refer to the sale and purchase of securities in the open market by the central bank. It directly influences the level of money supply in the economy. During deficient demand, the central bank starts purchasing securities from the open market. The purchase of securities increases the reserves of commercial banks and enhances their credit-creating power. It leads to an increase in the money supply and raises the level of aggregate demand in the economy.
5.  **Decrease in Legal Reserve Requirements (LRR):** Commercial banks are obliged to maintain legal reserves. A decrease in such reserves helps to raise the availability of credit. There are two components of legal reserves:
    *   **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR):** It is the minimum percentage of net demand and time liabilities to be kept by commercial banks with the central bank.
    *   **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR):** It refers to a minimum percentage of net demand and time liabilities which commercial banks are required to maintain with themselves.
    To correct the deficient demand, the central bank decreases CRR or/and SLR. It increases the amount of effective cash resources of commercial banks and enhances their credit-creating power. It will raise the level of borrowings and helps to minimize the deficiency in demand.

## Qualitative Instruments

1.  **Decrease in Margin Requirements:** Margin requirement refers to the difference between the market value of security offered and the value of the amount lent. During deficient demand, the central bank reduces the margin, which enhances the credit-creating power of banks. With a decrease in margin, commercial banks can grant more loans than before, against the same amount of security. It encourages borrowers to borrow more money and raises the level of aggregate demand.
2.  **Moral Suasion (Advise to Encourage Lending):** This is a combination of persuasion and pressure that the Central Bank applies to other banks in order to get them to act in a manner in line with its policy. During deficient demand, the central bank advises, requests, or persuades the commercial banks to encourage credit. It helps to raise the availability of credit and aggregate demand.
3.  **Selective Credit Controls (Withdraw Credit Rationing):** It refers to a method in which the central bank gives directions to other banks to give or not to give credit for certain purposes to particular sectors. During deficient demand, the central bank withdraws rationing of credit and makes efforts to encourage credit.

*## Image Description: Measures to Correct Deficient Demand

The image presents a flow chart outlining measures used to correct deficient demand in an economy. It categorizes these measures under two main policies: Fiscal Policy and Monetary Policy.

**1. Fiscal Policy (Policy of Central Government):**

   *   **Increase in Government Spending:** This indicates that the government can increase its expenditure on various sectors to stimulate demand.

   *   **Decrease in Taxes:** Lowering taxes allows individuals and businesses to have more disposable income, leading to increased spending and investment.

**2. Monetary Policy (Policy of Central Bank):**

   *   **Quantitative Instruments:** These are methods that influence the quantity of money supply in the economy.
        *   **Decrease in Bank Rate:** Reducing the interest rate at which the central bank lends to commercial banks.
        *   **Decrease in Repo Rate:** Lowering the rate at which the central bank repurchases securities from commercial banks.
        *   **Decrease in Reverse Repo Rate:** Reducing the rate at which the central bank borrows from commercial banks.
        *   **Open Market Operations (Purchase of securities):** The central bank buying government securities to inject money into the economy.
        *   **Decrease in Legal Reserve Requirements:** Lowering the portion of deposits that commercial banks must keep as reserves.

   *   **Qualitative Instruments:** These methods aim to control credit flow by specific sectors or purposes.
        *   **Decrease in Margin Requirements:** Reducing the percentage of an asset's value that a borrower needs to pay as down payment for a loan.
        *   **Moral Suasion (Advise to Encourage Lending):** The central bank persuading or advising commercial banks to increase lending.
        *   **Selective Credit Controls (Withdraw Credit Rationing):** Directing credit to specific sectors or industries while restricting it to others.
