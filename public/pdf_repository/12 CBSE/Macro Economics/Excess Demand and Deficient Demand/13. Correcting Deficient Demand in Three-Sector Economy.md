# Remedy for Deficient Demand

Deficient demand can be corrected by increasing aggregate demand by an amount equal to the deflationary gap.

In the figure below, F is the equilibrium attained when the initial AD curve intersects the AS curve. But, point F signifies the underemployment equilibrium, and EG is the resultant deflationary gap. In order to correct this deflationary gap, the government needs to use its fiscal measure of increasing expenditure.

When government expenditure is increased, the new level of aggregate demand is $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$ corresponding to a higher level of government expenditure. $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$ is sufficient to keep the economy at full employment equilibrium (point E).

**Overall Representation:**

The image presents a Keynesian cross diagram (also known as the 45-degree line diagram) used to illustrate the concept of aggregate demand and the determination of equilibrium income and employment in an economy. It demonstrates how changes in aggregate demand can lead to either full employment equilibrium or a deflationary gap.

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis:** Represents Aggregate Demand (AD), which is expressed as the sum of Consumption (C), Investment (I), and Government Spending (G). The labels indicate changes in G can shift the AD curve.
*   **X-axis:** Represents Income, Output, or Employment. This is effectively the real GDP of the economy.

**Key Components and Relationships:**

1.  **45-Degree Line:** The solid black line that starts at the origin and moves at a 45-degree angle. This represents the aggregate supply (AS) curve or the points where aggregate expenditure (AD) equals output (Y). It shows where total spending equals total production.

2.  **Initial Aggregate Demand (AD):** The dashed horizontal line labeled "AD (C + I + G)." This shows the planned spending in the economy for a given level of income.

3.  **Equilibrium Point F:** The intersection of the initial AD curve and the 45-degree line. This indicates the initial equilibrium level of income/output. However, at point F, there is unemployment because the output is below the full employment level.

4.  **Increase in Government Spending (ΔG):** The arrow labeled "ΔG" shows an increase in government spending. This shifts the aggregate demand curve upwards.

5.  **New Aggregate Demand (AD1):** The solid horizontal line labeled "AD1 (C + I + G + ΔG)." This represents the new aggregate demand curve after the increase in government spending.

6.  **New Equilibrium Point E (Full Employment Equilibrium):** The intersection of the new AD1 curve and the 45-degree line. This represents the full employment equilibrium level of income/output. At point E, all available resources (labor) are employed.

7.  **Deflationary Gap (EG):** The vertical distance between the initial AD curve (AD) and the full employment equilibrium (E) on the 45-degree line. This represents the amount by which aggregate demand falls short of the level needed to achieve full employment. The deflationary gap represents the amount of increased aggregate demand needed to reach full employment.
