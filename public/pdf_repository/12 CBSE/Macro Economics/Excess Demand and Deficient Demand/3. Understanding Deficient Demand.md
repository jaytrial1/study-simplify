# Understanding Deficient Demand

**Deficient demand** refers to the situation when aggregate demand (AD) is less than the aggregate supply (AS) corresponding to the full employment level of output in the economy.
The situation of deficient demand arises when planned aggregate expenditure falls short of aggregate supply at the full employment level. It gives rise to a **deflationary gap**. A deflationary gap is the gap by which actual aggregate demand falls short of the aggregate demand required to establish full employment equilibrium.

The image presents a macroeconomic graph that explains the concepts of deficient demand and deflationary gap in an economy.

**Axes:**

-   **Y-axis:** Represents Aggregate Demand (AD).
-   **X-axis:** Represents Income/Output/Employment.

**Key Elements:**

1.  **45-degree Line:** A straight line originating from the origin, representing the equilibrium condition where aggregate demand equals aggregate supply (or income/output).

2.  **AD (C + I) Curve:** This is an upward-sloping curve representing the aggregate demand in the economy, calculated as the sum of consumption (C) and investment (I). This curve is labeled "AD (C + I)".

3.  **AD1 (C + I - ΔI) Curve:** A lower curve, parallel to the AD (C + I) curve, represents a decrease in aggregate demand due to a reduction in investment (ΔI). The curve is labeled "AD1 (C + I - ΔI)".

4.  **Full Employment Equilibrium (Point E):** This point represents the equilibrium where the economy is operating at full employment. It is the intersection of the 45-degree line and the AD (C + I) curve.

5.  **Under Employment Equilibrium (Point F):** This point represents the equilibrium where the economy is operating below full employment. It is the intersection of the 45-degree line and the AD1 (C + I - ΔI) curve.

6.  **Deflationary Gap (EG):** The vertical distance between the two aggregate demand curves at the level of full employment output (represented by line segment EG) illustrates the amount by which aggregate demand needs to increase to achieve full employment equilibrium. It indicates the amount of additional aggregate demand required to reach full employment.

**Insights and Information Conveyed:**

-   The graph illustrates the situation of "deficient demand" where the aggregate demand in the economy is insufficient to achieve full employment.
-   The downward shift in the aggregate demand curve, from AD (C + I) to AD1 (C + I - ΔI), indicates a decrease in demand, potentially due to lower investment.
-   The difference between the two equilibrium points (E and F) shows the level of unemployment caused by deficient demand.
-   The "deflationary gap" (EG) represents the amount of additional demand needed to bring the economy to full employment.
-   The graph provides a visual representation of Keynesian economic theory related to aggregate demand and its impact on output and employment levels.*

As seen in the diagram, income, output, and employment are measured on the X-axis and aggregate demand is measured on the Y-axis. Aggregate demand (AD) and aggregate supply (AS) curves intersect at point E, which indicates the full employment equilibrium. Due to a decrease in investment expenditure ($\Delta \mathrm{I}$), aggregate demand falls from AD to $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$. It denotes the situation of deficient demand, and the gap between them, i.e., EG, is termed as the **deflationary gap**. Point F indicates the underemployment equilibrium.

It may be noted that during deficient demand, equilibrium is determined at a level less than full employment equilibrium. It leads to **underemployment equilibrium**. In this situation, there exists **involuntary unemployment**.
