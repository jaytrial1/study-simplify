This section details the various factors that can lead to excess demand in the economy and its consequences on output, employment, and prices.

# Causes and Impact of Excess Demand

## Reasons for Excess Demand

Excess demand may arise due to several factors. Important among them are:

1.  **Rise in the Propensity to Consume:** Excess demand may arise because of an increase in consumption expenditure due to a rise in the propensity to consume or a fall in the propensity to save.
2.  **Reduction in Taxes:** It may also occur due to an increase in disposable income and consumption demand because of a decrease in taxes.
3.  **Increase in Government Expenditure:** A rise in government demand for goods and services due to an increase in public expenditure will also result in excess demand.
4.  **Increase in Investment:** Excess demand can also arise when there is an increase in investment due to a decrease in the rate of interest or an increase in expected returns.
5.  **Fall in Imports:** A decrease in imports due to higher international prices in comparison to domestic prices may also lead to excess demand.
6.  **Rise in Exports:** Excess demand may also arise when demand for exports increases due to comparatively lower prices of domestic goods or due to a decrease in the exchange rate for domestic currency.
7.  **Deficit Financing:** Excess demand may be caused due to an increase in the money supply caused by deficit financing.

It must be noted that the main reason for excess demand is apparently the increase in the four components of aggregate demand (Consumption, Investment, Government Spending, Net Exports).

## Impact of Excess Demand

Excess demand is not a desired situation because it does not lead to any increase in the level of aggregate supply as the economy is already at the full employment level. Excess demand has the following effect on output, employment, and the general price level:

1.  **Effect on Output:** Excess demand does not affect the level of output because the economy is already at the full employment level and there is no idle capacity in the economy.
2.  **Effect on Employment:** There will be no change in the level of employment as the economy is already operating at full employment equilibrium and there is no involuntary unemployment.
3.  **Effect on General Price Level:** Excess demand leads to a rise in the general price level (known as **inflation**) as aggregate demand is more than aggregate supply.
