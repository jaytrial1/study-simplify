This section introduces the concept of excess demand in an economy, explaining what it means and its relationship to the inflationary gap.

# Understanding Excess Demand

**Excess demand** refers to the situation when **aggregate demand (AD)** is more than the **aggregate supply (AS)** corresponding to the full employment level of output in the economy. It is the excess of anticipated expenditure over the value of full employment output.

Excess demand gives rise to an **inflationary gap**. An inflationary gap refers to the gap by which actual aggregate demand exceeds the aggregate demand required to establish full employment equilibrium. It is called inflationary because this leads to a rise in the general price level of the economy.

*Here's a detailed description of the graph:

**Overall Representation:**

The graph depicts a macroeconomic model focused on Aggregate Demand (AD) and its relationship to income, output, and employment. It illustrates a situation of "Excess Demand and Inflationary Gap."

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis:** Represents Aggregate Demand (AD).
*   **X-axis:** Represents Income/Output/Employment.

**Key Components:**

1.  **45-Degree Line:** A straight line originating from the origin and inclined at 45 degrees. This line represents the point where Aggregate Demand (AD) equals Income (Y).

2.  **AD (C + I) Line:** A horizontal line labeled "AD (C + I)". This line represents the initial level of Aggregate Demand, where "C" denotes Consumption and "I" denotes Investment.

3.  **AD1 (C + I + ΔI) Line:** A dashed horizontal line located above the AD (C + I) line, labeled "AD1 (C + I + ΔI)". This line represents an increased level of Aggregate Demand. "ΔI" signifies the change in investment.

4.  **Point E:** The intersection point between the 45-degree line and the AD (C + I) line. It is labeled as "Full Employment Equilibrium."

5.  **Point F:** The intersection point between the 45-degree line and the AD1 (C + I + ΔI) line.

6.  **Line M:** A straight, vertical line perpendicular to the x-axis.

7.  **Inflationary Gap (EF):** The vertical distance between points E and F. This illustrates the excess of aggregate demand beyond the full employment level.

**Insights and Information:**

*   **Full Employment Equilibrium:** Point E indicates the equilibrium level of income/output/employment that corresponds to the initial level of aggregate demand (AD = C + I). At this point, the economy is operating at full employment.

*   **Increase in Aggregate Demand:** The shift from AD (C + I) to AD1 (C + I + ΔI) represents an increase in aggregate demand, likely driven by an increase in investment (ΔI).

*   **Excess Demand and Inflationary Gap:** The inflationary gap (EF) illustrates the excess of aggregate demand beyond the full employment level of output. Since the economy is already at full employment, this increased demand cannot be met with increased production. Instead, it leads to rising prices, resulting in inflation.

*   **Understanding Inflationary Pressure:** The graph demonstrates how increased aggregate demand, when an economy is already at full employment, leads to an inflationary gap and, consequently, inflationary pressures.*

In the diagram, income, output, and employment are measured on the X-axis and aggregate demand is measured on the Y-axis. Aggregate demand (AD) and aggregate supply (AS) curves intersect at point E, which indicates the full employment equilibrium. Due to an increase in investment expenditure ($\Delta \mathrm{I}$), aggregate demand rises from AD to $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$. It denotes the situation of excess demand, and the gap between them, i.e., EF, is termed as the **inflationary gap**.

It must be noted that the situation of excess demand generates inflationary pressure in the economy. The larger the inflationary gap, the greater will be the inflationary pressure on the economy.
