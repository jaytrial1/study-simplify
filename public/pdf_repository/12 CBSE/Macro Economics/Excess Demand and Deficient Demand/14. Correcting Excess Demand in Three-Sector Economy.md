# Remedy for Excess Demand

To correct the problem of excess demand, aggregate demand has to be reduced by an amount equal to the inflationary gap.

In the figure below, F is the equilibrium attained when the initial AD curve intersects the AS curve. In order to correct this inflationary gap, the government needs to use its fiscal measure of reducing expenditure. A decrease in government expenditure will reduce aggregate demand and remove the inflationary gap.

When government expenditure is reduced by $\Delta \mathrm{G}$, the AD curve will shift downward to $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$. The new level of aggregate demand is $\mathrm{AD}_{1}$, and it is sufficient to keep the economy at full employment equilibrium (point E).

**Title:** Aggregate Demand and the Inflationary Gap

**Description:**

The graph depicts the relationship between Aggregate Demand (AD) and Income/Output/Employment, demonstrating the concept of an inflationary gap in macroeconomics.

**Axes:**

*   **Y-axis:** Represents Aggregate Demand (AD), measured as the sum of Consumption (C), Investment (I), and Government Spending (G) (C + I + G).
*   **X-axis:** Represents Income, Output, and Employment level. It essentially shows the level of economic activity.

**Key Elements:**

1.  **45-degree Line:** This line represents the equilibrium condition where Aggregate Demand equals Aggregate Supply (AD = AS). Any point on this line signifies that total spending in the economy is equal to the total value of goods and services produced.

2.  **AD Curve (AD = C + I + G):** This dashed line represents the initial Aggregate Demand curve. It slopes upward, indicating that as income increases, aggregate demand also increases.

3.  **Equilibrium Point F:** Represents the point where the initial AD curve intersects with the 45-degree line. This represents the equilibrium level of income and output in the economy *before* any policy changes.

4.  **Full Employment Equilibrium Point E:** Point E indicates the level of income/output consistent with full employment in the economy. The vertical line going from M up to E denotes that at income level M, the economy is operating at full employment.

5.  **Inflationary Gap (EG):** The vertical distance between the AD curve at equilibrium (F) and the AD curve at full employment (E) represents the inflationary gap. It signifies that aggregate demand is exceeding the economy's ability to produce at full employment. This excess demand puts upward pressure on prices, leading to inflation.

6.  **AD1 Curve (AD1 = C + I + G - ΔG):** Represents a new, lower level of Aggregate Demand. The "ΔG" indicates a decrease in government spending (or potentially a decrease in any component of Aggregate Demand).

7.  **Downward Arrow (ΔG):** Illustrates the effect of the decrease in government spending, shifting the AD curve downward from the initial AD to AD1.

**Insights:**

*   **Excess Demand and Inflation:** The graph clearly illustrates the link between excess aggregate demand (represented by the inflationary gap) and potential inflationary pressures.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** The image demonstrates how changes in government spending can be used as a fiscal policy tool to address an inflationary gap. By reducing government spending (or increasing taxes), the aggregate demand curve shifts downward, potentially closing the inflationary gap and stabilizing prices.
*   **Full Employment:** The graph emphasizes the importance of maintaining aggregate demand at a level that aligns with the full employment level of output. If demand exceeds this level, it can lead to inflation.

In essence, the graph illustrates a basic Keynesian model, showing how aggregate demand influences the level of output and employment, and how fiscal policy can be used to manage inflationary pressures arising from excess demand.*
