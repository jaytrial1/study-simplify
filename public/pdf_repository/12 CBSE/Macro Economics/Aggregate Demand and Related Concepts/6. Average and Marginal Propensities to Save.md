This section defines and explains the two measures of the propensity to save: Average Propensity to Save (APS) and Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS).

# Types of Propensities to Save

Propensities to save are of two types:

1.  Average Propensity to Save (APS)
2.  Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

## Average Propensity to Save (APS)

**Average propensity to save (APS)** refers to the ratio of saving to the corresponding level of income at a point in time.

$$
\text { APS }=\frac{\text { Saving }(\mathrm{S})}{\text { Income }(\mathrm{Y})}
$$

Example: If saving is ₹30 crores at an income of ₹100 crores, APS = 30/100 = 0.30. This means 30% of the income is saved.

The estimation of APS is illustrated with the help of the following table and figure:

| Income (Y) <br> (₹ Crores) | Saving (S) <br> (₹ Crores) | APS $=\frac{S}{Y}$ |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 0 | -40 | - |
| 100 | -20 | $-0.20\left(=\frac{-20}{100}\right)$ |
| 200 | 0 | $0\left(=\frac{0}{200}\right)$ |
| 300 | 20 | $0.067\left(=\frac{20}{300}\right)$ |
| 400 | 40 | $0.10\left(=\frac{40}{400}\right)$ |

*Table: Average Propensity to Save*

*This graph illustrates Average Propensity to Save (APS). The X-axis represents Income (Y) in ₹ Crores, and the Y-axis represents Saving (S) in ₹ Crores. The line 'SS' is the saving curve, starting from a negative value. At income level OY₁, saving is OR. APS at point A on the saving curve is calculated as the ratio of saving (OR) to income (OY₁), i.e., APS = S/Y = OR/OY₁.*

In the table, APS is -0.20 at an income of ₹100 crores due to negative saving. APS is 0 at an income of ₹200 crores where saving is zero (break-even point).

Important Points about APS:

1.  **APS can never be 1 or more than 1:** Saving can never be equal to or greater than national income.
2.  **APS can be 0:** At the break-even point, saving is zero, so APS = 0 (at income ₹200 crores in the table).
3.  **APS can be negative or less than 1:** At income levels below the break-even point, there are dissavings, so APS is negative.
4.  **APS rises with increase in income:** APS increases as income rises because the proportion of income saved keeps on increasing.

## Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS)

**Marginal propensity to save (MPS)** refers to the ratio of change in saving to the change in total income.

$$
\text { MPS }=\frac{\text { Change in Saving }(\Delta S)}{\text { Change in Income }(\Delta Y)}
$$

Example: If saving increases from ₹30 crores to ₹90 crores when income increases from ₹100 crores to ₹200 crores, then ΔS = 90-30 = 60 and ΔY = 200-100 = 100. MPS = 60/100 = 0.60. This means 60% of the incremental income is saved.

Let's understand MPS through the following schedule and diagram:

| Income (Y) <br> (₹ Crores) | Saving (S) <br> (₹ Crores) | Change in Saving <br> $(\Delta S)$ <br> (₹ Crores) | Change in <br> Income ( $\Delta Y$ ) <br> (₹ Crores) | MPS $=\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$ |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 0 | -40 | - | - | - |
| 100 | -20 | 20 | 100 | $0.20\left(=\frac{20}{100}\right)$ |
| 200 | 0 | 20 | 100 | $0.20\left(= \frac{20}{100}\right)$ |
| 300 | 20 | 20 | 100 | $0.20\left(=\frac{20}{100}\right)$ |
| 400 | 40 | 20 | 100 | $0.20\left(=\frac{20}{100}\right)$ |

*Table: Marginal Propensity to Save*

In the table, MPS is 0.20 when income increases from zero to ₹100 crores and remains constant at 0.20 throughout. Since MPS ($\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$) measures the slope of the saving curve, a constant value of MPS means the saving curve is a straight line (linear saving function).

*This graph illustrates Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS). The X-axis represents Income (Y) in ₹ Crores, and the Y-axis represents Saving (S) in ₹ Crores. The line 'SS' is the saving function. Points B and A are two points on the curve. As income changes from Y₁ to Y₂ (ΔY = Y₁Y₂), saving changes from R to P (ΔS = PR). The MPS between points B and A is calculated as the ratio of the change in saving (ΔS) to the change in income (ΔY), i.e., MPS = ΔS/ΔY = PR/(Y₁Y₂). This represents the slope of the saving curve.*

MPS varies between 0 and 1.
*   If the entire additional income is saved (ΔC=0), then MPS = 1.
*   If the entire additional income is consumed (ΔS=0), then MPS = 0.
In normal situations, the value of MPS varies between 0 and 1.

**Slope of Saving Curve:** The slope is measured as the ratio between ΔS (additional saving) and ΔY (additional income), which is known as MPS. The slope is always positive due to the positive relationship between saving and income. In the table, MPS is constant at 0.20, resulting in a linear saving function (straight-line saving curve).

## Comparison between APS and MPS

| Basis                                   | Average Propensity to <br> Save (APS)                                  | Marginal Propensity to <br> Save (MPS)                                 |
| :-------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning                                 | Ratio of saving (S) to income (Y) at a point.                          | Ratio of change in saving (ΔS) to change in income (ΔY) over a period. |
| Value less than zero                    | Can be less than zero when there are dissavings (consumption > income). | Cannot be less than zero as change in saving cannot be negative (change in consumption cannot be more than change in income). |
| Formula                                 | $A P S=\frac{S}{Y}$                                                    | $M P S=\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$                                      |

**Always Remember:** The slope of the Saving Curve is indicated by MPS, not by APS.
