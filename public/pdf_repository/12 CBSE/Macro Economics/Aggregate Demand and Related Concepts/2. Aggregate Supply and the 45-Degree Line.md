# Aggregate Supply

**Aggregate Supply (AS)** refers to the money value of final goods and services that all producers are willing to supply in an economy in a given time period. It represents the *planned production* or *desired output*.

## Aggregate Supply = National Income

When AS is expressed in physical terms, it's the total output of goods and services in the economy. The value of this total output is distributed to factors of production as rent, wages, interest, and profit. The sum of these factor incomes is National Income (Y). Therefore, aggregate supply (AS) and national income (Y) are considered equal.

## Components of Aggregate Supply (AS) or National Income (Y)

National income is primarily used for consumption of goods and services, with the remainder being saved. Thus, income (Y) is either consumed or saved.
So, **National Income (Y) = Consumption (C) + Saving (S)**
Or, **Y = AS = C + S**

## Diagrammatic Representation of AS

## Significance of the 45° Line

The 45° line represents Aggregate Supply (AS) because AS is equal to National Income (Y), and the line shows all points where Y = C + S. At every point on this line, income is equal to the sum of consumption and savings. This line is crucial in Keynesian economic analysis. Since income and expenditure are measured in the same units on the axes, the 45° line has a slope of one and bisects the 90° angle formed by the axes.
