This section explains how the saving curve can be derived graphically from the consumption curve and vice-versa, highlighting their complementary nature.

# Consumption and Saving Curves are Complementary curves

We know that Y = C + S. This means consumption and saving curves are complementary; they together account for total income. If one function is known, the other can be easily obtained. Thus, we can derive the saving curve from the consumption curve and the consumption curve from the saving curve.

## Derivation of Saving Curve from Consumption Curve

Let's understand the derivation of the saving curve from the consumption curve using the figure below.
The figure shows CC as the consumption curve and the 45° line OY representing the income curve (where Y=C).

*   At zero income, autonomous consumption (c̄) is equal to OC (the intercept on the Y-axis). This means saving at zero income will be negative and equal to -c̄ (OS). So, the saving curve starts from point S on the negative Y-axis.
*   The consumption curve CC intersects the income curve OY at point E. This is the break-even point. At point E, Consumption = Income, and saving is zero. This means the saving curve will intersect the X-axis (where S=0) at the corresponding income level (point R).
*   By joining points S and R and extending the line, we get the saving curve SS.

## Derivation of Consumption Curve from Saving Curve

The consumption curve can also be derived from the saving curve similarly.
*   The starting point of the consumption curve on the Y-axis will be equal to the magnitude of dissaving at zero income level (which is the negative of the saving curve's intercept on the Y-axis).
*   The second key point for the consumption curve will correspond to the point where the saving curve intersects the X-axis (where S=0). At this income level, consumption must equal income (C=Y).
