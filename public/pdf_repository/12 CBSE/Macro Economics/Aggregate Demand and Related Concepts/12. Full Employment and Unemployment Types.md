This section defines full employment in macroeconomics and explains different types of unemployment, including frictional, structural, involuntary, and voluntary unemployment.

# Full Employment and Involuntary Unemployment

## Full Employment

**Full employment** refers to a situation where all individuals who are willing and able to work at the existing wage rate can find work without significant difficulty.
In macroeconomics, full employment does not mean zero unemployment. Some types of unemployment can exist even when the economy is at full employment. This means that the demand for labour is equal to its supply, allowing for some natural level of unemployment.

Under full employment, there can be two types of unemployment:
(i) **Frictional Unemployment:** This is temporary unemployment that occurs when workers are in the process of moving between jobs. It arises from imperfections in the labour market, such as lack of information about job openings or limited worker mobility. Factors like introducing new machines, changes in production processes, or plant breakdowns can also cause frictional unemployment.
(ii) **Structural Unemployment:** This type of unemployment occurs when there is a mismatch between the skills or location of unemployed persons and the requirements or location of available jobs. It is linked to structural changes in the economy, such as technological advancements or shifts in industry composition. For example, workers lacking computer skills may become structurally unemployed due to computerization until they acquire the necessary training.

The presence of these two types of unemployment (frictional and structural) is sometimes referred to as the **'Natural Rate of Unemployment'**.

It's important to note that the concept of Full Employment typically applies to the *labour force* (working-age population willing and able to work), not the total population. Children and elderly persons are generally not included in the labour force.

# Involuntary Unemployment

**Involuntary unemployment** refers to a situation where individuals who are willing and able to work at the existing wage rate cannot find employment. Under involuntary unemployment, people are unemployed against their wishes or due to circumstances beyond their control. Only involuntary unemployment is considered when estimating the total unemployment level in an economy. Full employment implies the absence of involuntary unemployment.

## What is Voluntary Unemployment?

**Voluntary unemployment** occurs when a person is unemployed because they are not willing to work at the existing wage rate. This might be because they are searching for a better job, prefer leisure, or have other reasons for not accepting available work at the prevailing rate. Voluntary unemployment is not counted as part of the overall unemployment problem addressed by macroeconomic policies.
