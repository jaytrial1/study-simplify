This section defines Aggregate Demand and outlines its key components in a multi-sector economy, before focusing on a simplified two-sector model.

# Meaning and Components of Aggregate Demand

**Aggregate Demand (AD)** refers to the total value of final goods and services that all sectors of an economy plan to buy at a given level of income during a period of one accounting year. It represents the aggregate expenditure that different sectors are willing to incur. Thus, AD and aggregate expenditure mean the same thing in this context.

It's important to note that aggregate expenditure here refers to *planned* expenditure, not actual expenditure. AD is the total expenditure that households, firms, government, and the rest of the world are planning to incur. AD is a flow concept, measured over a period like an accounting year.

**Do not confuse Aggregate Demand with Market Demand:** Aggregate demand is the total demand for all goods and services in the entire economy, while market demand is the total demand for one commodity in a specific market.

## Components of Aggregate Demand

The various components of Aggregate demand in a four-sector economy are:

1.  **Private (Household) Consumption Expenditure (C):** This is the total expenditure by households on purchasing goods and services during an accounting year. Consumption expenditure is generally influenced directly by the level of *Disposable Income*. Higher disposable income usually leads to more consumption expenditure, and vice-versa.
    *   *Disposable Income* refers to the income from all sources available to households for spending on consumption and saving.
    *   The consumption expenditure discussed here is *ex-ante*, meaning it's the planned consumption expenditure.
2.  **Investment Expenditure (I):** This is the total expenditure incurred by all private firms on capital goods. It includes additions to the stock of physical capital assets (like machinery, equipment, buildings) and changes in inventory.
    *   For simplicity in this study, Investment Expenditure is assumed to be *autonomous*, meaning investments are not influenced by the level of income.
3.  **Government Expenditure (G):** This refers to the total expenditure incurred by the government on consumer goods and capital goods to satisfy the common needs of the economy. Government incurs both consumption expenditure (for public needs like law and order, education, health, transport, defence) and investment expenditure (for construction of highways, roads, power plants). The level of government expenditure is determined by government policy, often guided by social welfare goals.
4.  **Net Exports (X - M):** Exports represent demand for domestically produced goods by the rest of the world. Imports represent demand by domestic residents for goods produced abroad. The difference between exports and imports is called net exports. The magnitude of net exports depends on factors like exchange rates, relative prices, trade duties, and trade policy.

The equation for Aggregate Demand in a four-sector economy is:
**AD = C + I + G + (X - M)**

## Aggregate Demand in a Two-Sector Model (AD = C + I)

Since the determination of income and employment in this study focuses on the two-sector model (households and firms), the government and rest of the world sectors are excluded for simplicity. In this closed economy model, Aggregate Demand is a function of only consumption expenditure and investment expenditure.
So, in a two-sector model: **AD = C + I**

## Important Points about AD

1.  **AD = C + I:** In the two-sector model, AD is the sum of consumption demand and investment demand.
2.  **Positive consumption, even when income level is zero:** There is always a minimum level of consumption needed for survival, even with zero income. This is *autonomous consumption* (c̄). It is met from past savings, sale of assets, or borrowings. In the table, consumption is ₹40 crores at Y=0. In the figure, the consumption curve starts from above the origin, indicating autonomous consumption.
3.  **Slope of Consumption Curve:** The consumption curve slopes upwards because consumption increases with income. However, the proportionate increase in consumption is less than that of income because people start saving a part of their income as it rises.
4.  **Slope of Autonomous Investment Curve:** Investment expenditure (I) is assumed to be autonomous, meaning it's independent of income. Thus, the investment curve is a straight line parallel to the X-axis, showing a constant level of investment (₹20 crores in the table).
5.  **Starting point of AD curve:** The AD curve starts from a point above the origin. At zero income, AD equals the sum of autonomous consumption and autonomous investment (₹40 + ₹20 = ₹60 crores in the table).
6.  **Slope of AD curve:** The AD curve has a positive slope, indicating that as income increases, AD (aggregate expenditure) also increases.
