# The Saving Function

Like consumption, saving is also a function of income; it depends on the level of income. **Saving** is the excess of income over consumption expenditure. The **Saving function** refers to the functional relationship between saving and national income.

Mathematically, it is expressed as:
**S = f(Y)**
Where:
*   S = Saving
*   Y = National Income
*   f = Functional relationship

The 'Saving Function' or 'Propensity to Save' shows the saving of households at a given level of income during a specific time period. It also shows different levels of saving at different income levels in an economy.

The relationship between saving and income is illustrated in the following schedule and figure:

| 0 | 40 | -40 |
| 100 | 120 | -20 |
| 200 | 200 | 0 |
| 300 | 280 | 20 |
| 400 | 360 | 40 |

## Important Observations from Saving Schedule and Saving Curve

1.  **Starting Point of Saving Curve:** The saving curve starts from a point on the negative Y-axis. This indicates *negative saving* (dissaving) at zero national income, equal in magnitude to autonomous consumption.
    *   *Note:* The saving curve has a negative intercept on the Y-axis equal in magnitude to the positive intercept of the consumption curve because if consumption is positive at zero income, there must be dissavings of the same magnitude.
2.  **Slope of Saving Curve:** The saving curve has a positive slope, indicating a positive relationship between saving and income.
3.  **Break-even point (S=0):** The saving curve crosses the X-axis at the point where saving is zero. This corresponds to the break-even point where consumption equals income. According to the table, this occurs at an income of ₹200 crores.
4.  **Positive Saving:** After the break-even point (to the right of where the curve crosses the X-axis), saving is positive.
