This section defines and explains the two measures of the propensity to consume: Average Propensity to Consume (APC) and Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC).

# Types of Propensities to Consume

There are two technical aspects of Propensity to Consume:

1.  Average Propensity to Consume (APC)
2.  Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

## Average Propensity to Consume (APC)

**Average propensity to consume (APC)** refers to the ratio of total consumption expenditure to the corresponding level of income at a point in time.

$$
\text { APC }=\frac{\text { Consumption }(\mathrm{C})}{\text { Income }(\mathrm{Y})}
$$

Example: If consumption is ₹70 crores at an income of ₹100 crores, APC = 70/100 = 0.70. This means 70% of the income is spent on consumption.

Let's understand APC with the help of the following schedule and diagram:

| Income (Y) <br> (₹ Crores) | Consumption (C) <br> (₹ Crores) | APC $=\frac{C}{Y}$ |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 0 | 40 | - |
| 100 | 120 | $1.20\left(=\frac{120}{100}\right)$ |
| 200 | 200 | $1\left(=\frac{200}{200}\right)$ |
| 300 | 280 | $0.933\left(=\frac{280}{300}\right)$ |
| 400 | 360 | $0.90\left(=\frac{360}{400}\right)$ |

*Table: Average Propensity to Consume*

Important Points about APC:

*   **APC is more than 1:** As long as consumption is greater than national income (before the break-even point), APC > 1.
*   **APC = 1:** At the break-even point, consumption equals national income. So, APC = 1 (at income ₹200 crores in the table).
*   **APC is less than 1:** Beyond the break-even point, consumption is less than national income, resulting in APC < 1.
*   **APC falls with increase in income:** APC continuously falls as income increases because the proportion of income spent on consumption decreases.
*   **APC can never be zero:** APC can only be zero if consumption is zero. However, consumption is never zero; there is always autonomous consumption even at zero income.

## Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC)

**Marginal propensity to consume (MPC)** refers to the ratio of change in consumption expenditure to the change in total income. MPC shows what proportion of an additional unit of income is spent on consumption.

$$
\text { MPC }=\frac{\text { Change in Consumption }(\Delta \mathrm{C})}{\text { Change in Income }(\Delta \mathrm{Y})}
$$

Example: If consumption increases from ₹70 crores to ₹110 crores when income increases from ₹100 crores to ₹200 crores, then ΔC = 110-70 = 40 and ΔY = 200-100 = 100. MPC = 40/100 = 0.40. This means 40% of the incremental income is spent on consumption.

Let's understand MPC with the help of the following schedule and diagram:

| Income <br> (Y) <br> (₹ Crores) | Consumption <br> (C) <br> (₹ Crores) | Change in <br> Consumption ( $\Delta \mathrm{C}$ ) <br> (₹ Crores) | Change in <br> income ( $\Delta \mathrm{Y}$ ) <br> (₹ Crores) | MPC $=\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$ |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 0 | 40 | - | - | - |
| 100 | 120 | 80 | 100 | $0.80\left(=\frac{80}{100}\right)$ |
| 200 | 200 | 80 | 100 | $0.80\left(=\frac{80}{100}\right)$ |
| 300 | 280 | 80 | 100 | $0.80\left(=\frac{80}{100}\right)$ |
| 400 | 360 | 80 | 100 | $0.80\left(=\frac{80}{100}\right)$ |

*Table: Marginal Propensity to Consume*

As seen in the table, MPC is 0.80 when income increases from zero to ₹100 crores and remains constant at 0.80 for subsequent increases. Since MPC ($\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$) measures the slope of the consumption curve, a constant value of MPC indicates that the consumption curve is a straight line (linear consumption function).

*This graph illustrates Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC). The X-axis represents Income (Y), and the Y-axis represents Consumption (C). The line 'C' is the consumption function. Points B and A are two points on the curve. As income changes from Y₁ to Y₂ (ΔY = Y₁Y₂), consumption changes from M to N (ΔC = MN). The MPC between points B and A is calculated as the ratio of the change in consumption (ΔC) to the change in income (ΔY), i.e., MPC = ΔC/ΔY = MN/Y₁Y₂. This represents the slope of the consumption curve.*

Important Points about MPC:

1.  **Value of MPC varies between 0 and 1:** Incremental income is either spent on consumption or saved.
    *   If the entire additional income is consumed (ΔS=0), then MPC = 1.
    *   If the entire additional income is saved (ΔC=0), then MPC = 0.
    *   In normal situations, the value of MPC lies between 0 and 1.
2.  **MPC of poor is more than that of rich:** Poor people tend to spend a larger percentage of any increase in income on consumption because many of their basic needs are unsatisfied. Rich people spend a smaller proportion as they already have a high standard of living. Similarly, developing countries tend to have a higher MPC than developed countries.
3.  **MPC falls with successive increase in income:** As an economy becomes richer, the tendency is to consume a smaller percentage of each increment to income and save more. Hence, at higher income levels, people generally have a lower MPC.

**Slope of Consumption Curve:** The slope is measured as the ratio between ΔC (additional consumption) and ΔY (additional income), which is known as MPC. The slope is always positive due to the positive relationship between consumption and income. In the table, MPC is constant at 0.80, resulting in a linear consumption function (straight-line consumption curve).

## Comparison between APC and MPC

| Basis                  | APC                                                                | MPC                                                                 |
| :--------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Meaning                | Ratio of consumption expenditure (C) to income (Y) at a point.     | Ratio of change in consumption (ΔC) to change in income (ΔY) over a period. |
| Value more than one    | Can be more than one when consumption > income (before break-even). | Cannot be more than one as ΔC cannot be more than ΔY.               |
| Response to change <br> in income | Falls with increasing income, but at a rate less than MPC.       | Falls with increasing income, but at a rate more than APC.          |
| Formula                | $A P C=\frac{C}{Y}$                                                | $M P C=\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$                                   |

**Always Remember:** The slope of the Consumption Curve is indicated by MPC, not by APC.
