This section introduces the concept of the Consumption Function, which describes the relationship between consumption expenditure and national income, and discusses Keynes's Psychological Law of Consumption.

# The Consumption Function

**Consumption expenditure** is the portion of income spent on purchasing goods and services at a given income level. The **Consumption function** refers to the functional relationship between consumption and national income.

Mathematically, it is expressed as:
**C = f(Y)**
Where:
*   C = Consumption
*   Y = National Income
*   f = Functional relationship

Key aspects of the consumption function:
*   It represents households' willingness to purchase goods and services at a given income level during a specific period.
*   It shows the level of consumption at different income levels in an economy.
*   It is considered a psychological concept influenced by subjective factors like consumer preferences and habits.

The relationship between consumption and income is shown in the following schedule and figure:

| Income (Y) <br> (₹ Crores) | Consumption (C) <br> (₹ Crores) |
| :--: | :--: |
| 0 | 40 |
| 100 | 120 |
| 200 | 200 |
| 300 | 280 |
| 400 | 360 |
| 500 | 440 |
| 600 | 520 |

*Table: Consumption Schedule*

Important Observations from the Consumption Schedule and Curve:

1.  **Starting Point of Consumption Curve:** The consumption curve starts from a point above the origin on the Y-axis. This indicates the presence of *autonomous consumption* (c̄), which is the consumption level even when national income is zero.
2.  **Slope of Consumption Curve:** The consumption curve has a positive slope, showing that consumption increases as income rises. However, the increase in consumption is proportionally less than the increase in income, as a portion of the increased income is saved.
3.  **Income is less than Consumption:** At income levels below the break-even point, consumption is greater than income. This difference is covered by *dissaving*, which means utilizing previous savings or borrowing.
4.  **Break-even point (C=Y):** This is the point where consumption equals income, and saving is zero. In the table, this occurs at an income of ₹200 crores.
5.  **Income is more than Consumption:** At income levels beyond the break-even point, income is greater than consumption, leading to positive saving. The gap between the 45° line (Y) and the consumption curve represents this positive saving. The 45° line is significant because it shows where consumption spending is equal to, greater than, or less than the level of income.

## Keynesian Psychological Law of Consumption

The consumption function is based on Keynes's famous Psychological Law of Consumption, which states:
(i) There is a minimum consumption level, known as **autonomous consumption**, even at zero national income, because basic survival requires consumption.
(ii) As income increases, consumption also increases.
(iii) However, income rises at a greater proportion compared to the increase in consumption.

## Do not confuse Consumption with Consumption Function

*   **Consumption** refers to the specific amount of income spent on goods and services at a given income level.
*   **Consumption function** refers to the schedule or equation showing consumption expenditures at various levels of income.
