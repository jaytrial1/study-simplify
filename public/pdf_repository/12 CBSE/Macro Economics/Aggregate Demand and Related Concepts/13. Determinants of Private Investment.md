This section explains the two main factors that influence private investment decisions according to Keynesian theory: Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI) and Rate of Interest (ROI).

# Determinants of Investment

According to Keynes, the decision to invest in a new project (i.e., private investment) depends upon two factors:

1.  Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI)
2.  Rate of Interest (ROI)

## Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI)

**Marginal Efficiency of Investment (MEI)** refers to the expected rate of return from an additional unit of investment. MEI is determined by two factors:
(i) **Supply Price:** This is the cost of producing a new asset of a particular kind. It's the price at which a new capital asset can be supplied or replaced. For example, if replacing an old machine with a new one costs ₹10,000, then ₹10,000 is the supply price.
(ii) **Prospective Yield:** This refers to the net return (after deducting all costs) expected from the capital asset over its entire lifetime. For example, if a machine is expected to yield receipts of ₹1,200 and running expenses are ₹200, the prospective yield is ₹1,200 - ₹200 = ₹1,000.

In the given example, the Marginal Efficiency of Investment will be:
$$
\text { MEI }=\frac{\text { Prospective Yield }}{\text { Supply Price }} \times 100=\frac{1,000}{10,000} \times 100=10 \%
$$

## Rate of Interest (ROI)

**Rate of Interest (ROI)** refers to the cost of borrowing money to finance an investment. There is an inverse relationship between the ROI and the volume of investment. A higher ROI means the cost of borrowing is higher, making investment less attractive, thus leading to less investment spending, and vice-versa.

## Comparison of MEI with ROI

The profitability of an investment project is determined by comparing its MEI with the prevailing ROI. If MEI is greater than ROI (MEI > ROI), the investment is considered profitable. For instance, if an entrepreneur has to pay 12% interest on a loan but expects a 20% return (MEI) from the investment, they will proceed with the investment. Investment will continue until MEI equals ROI (MEI = ROI).
