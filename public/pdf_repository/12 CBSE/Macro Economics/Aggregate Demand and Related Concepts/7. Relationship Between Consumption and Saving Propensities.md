This section explains the complementary relationship between consumption and saving propensities, showing how APC + APS = 1 and MPC + MPS = 1.

# Relationship between APC and APS

The sum of Average Propensity to Consume (APC) and Average Propensity to Save (APS) is equal to one.
We know that income (Y) is either consumed (C) or saved (S):
Y = C + S
Dividing both sides by Y:
$\frac{Y}{Y}=\frac{C}{Y}+\frac{S}{Y}$
$1 = \text{APC} + \text{APS}$
APC + APS = 1
This relationship holds because income is entirely used for consumption or saving.

# Relationship between MPC and MPS

The sum of Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) and Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS) is equal to one.
We know that a change in income (ΔY) leads to a change in consumption (ΔC) and a change in saving (ΔS):
ΔY = ΔC + ΔS
Dividing both sides by ΔY:
$\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta Y}=\frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}+\frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$
$1 = \text{MPC} + \text{MPS}$
MPC + MPS = 1
This relationship holds because the total increment in income is either used for consumption or for saving.
