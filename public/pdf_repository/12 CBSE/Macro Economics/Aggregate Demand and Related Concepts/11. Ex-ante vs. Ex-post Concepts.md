This section defines and differentiates between ex-ante (planned) and ex-post (actual) economic variables, particularly in the context of saving and investment.

# Ex-ante and Ex-post Saving and Investment

**'Ex-ante'** variable is the planned or expected value of a variable, while **'Ex-post'** variable is the actual or realized value. These terms are commonly used for saving and investment.

There are two aspects of saving and investments:
(i) Ex-ante Saving and Ex-ante Investment
(ii) Ex-post Saving and Ex-post Investment

## Ex-ante Saving and Ex-ante Investment

**Ex-ante saving** refers to the amount of saving that households (savers) *plan* to save at different income levels in the economy. It represents the planned savings of an economy and is given by the saving function (propensity to save).

**Ex-ante investment** refers to the amount of investment that firms *plan* to invest at different income levels. The amount of ex-ante or planned investment is determined by the relationship between investment demand and the rate of interest (the investment demand function).

*Ex-ante can also be termed as: (i) Intended; (ii) Planned; (iii) Voluntary; (iv) Desired.*

**Equilibrium occurs when Ex-ante saving = Ex-ante investment.**
In an economy, equilibrium is determined when planned saving equals planned investment. However, ex-ante saving and ex-ante investment are equal *only* at the equilibrium level of income. This is because:
(i) Savers (households) and investors (firms) are different groups with different priorities.
(ii) Saving is often done in small amounts, while investment is typically in large amounts.
(iii) Saving is done to meet future uncertain events or contingencies, whereas investment is made for profit motive.
As a result, ex-ante saving and ex-ante investment are not always equal; they are equal only at the equilibrium level. Ex-ante variables form the basis of the theory of national income determination.

When ex-ante (planned) saving is not equal to ex-ante (planned) investment, output tends to adjust until they become equal again.

## Ex-post Saving and Ex-post Investment

**Ex-post saving** refers to the *actual* or realized saving in an economy during a year. It is the sum total of planned saving and unplanned saving.

**Ex-post investment** refers to the *actual* or realized investment in an economy during a year. It is the sum total of planned investment and unplanned investment.

It's important to note that ex-post saving and ex-post investment are equal at *all* levels of income. This equality is brought about by fluctuations in income and inventory changes.

Planned values are ex-ante measures, while realized values are ex-post measures. The significance of distinguishing between ex-ante and ex-post is that all variables in the Theory of Income Determination are considered ex-ante variables; they form the basis of the theory.
