This section explains the concepts of currency depreciation and appreciation, detailing their effects on a country's exports, imports, and national income.

# Currency Depreciation

*Currency Depreciation* is a decrease in the value of a country's domestic currency relative to a foreign currency. This makes the domestic currency less valuable, requiring more units of it to purchase a unit of foreign currency.

Examples:
*   The Rupee depreciates if the price of \$1 increases from ₹ 81 to ₹ 82.
*   A change from \$5 = £4 to \$4.5 = £4 indicates that the UK pound is depreciating relative to the US dollar.

## Effect of Depreciation of Domestic Currency

1.  **Increase in Exports:** When the domestic currency depreciates, its price falls in terms of a foreign currency (e.g., Rupee price falls in terms of US\$). This means that with the same amount of dollars, more goods can be bought from the domestic country. Consequently, exports become relatively cheaper for foreign buyers, leading to an increase in exports.
2.  **Decrease in Imports:** Depreciation makes foreign goods relatively more expensive for domestic buyers because they need to pay more domestic currency to import foreign goods. This leads to a fall in imports.
3.  **Increase in National Income:** An increase in exports and a decrease in imports due to domestic currency depreciation result in an increase in Net Exports (Exports - Imports). Assuming other factors remain constant (ceteris paribus), this increase in Net Exports contributes to an increase in National Income.

## Understanding Depreciation Terminology

It's important to recognize different ways of stating the same phenomenon regarding domestic currency depreciation. All the following statements describe the same situation where the value of the domestic currency (like Indian Rupees) has decreased in relation to foreign currency (e.g., \$1 rising from ₹ 81 to ₹ 82), meaning more domestic currency is needed to buy foreign currency. This situation leads to a rise in exports, a fall in imports, and an increase in National Income.

*   Domestic or Home Currency has *depreciated*.
*   There is a *fall in the price* of Domestic Currency.
*   Domestic Currency has become *less valuable*.
*   Foreign Currency has become *more valuable*.
*   *Exchange Rate* of Domestic Currency falls.
*   There is a *decrease in Exchange Rate* of Home Currency.
*   Exchange Rate of Domestic Currency has become *weaker*.

# Currency Appreciation

*Currency Appreciation* is an increase in the value of a country's domestic currency relative to a foreign currency. As the domestic currency becomes more valuable, fewer units of it are needed to purchase a unit of foreign currency.

Examples:
*   The Indian Rupee appreciates when the price of \$1 falls from ₹ 82 to ₹ 81.
*   A change from \$4.5 = £4 to \$5 = £4 indicates that the UK pound is appreciating relative to the US dollar.

## Effect of Appreciation of Domestic Currency

1.  **Increase in Imports:** Appreciation of the domestic currency means its price rises in terms of a foreign currency (e.g., Rupee price rises in terms of US\$). Now, one unit of domestic currency can be exchanged for more foreign currency. With the same amount of domestic money, more foreign goods can be purchased. This makes foreign goods relatively cheaper for domestic buyers, leading to an increase in imports.
2.  **Decrease in Exports:** Appreciation leads to a decrease in exports because one unit of foreign currency will now buy fewer domestic goods. This makes domestic goods relatively more expensive for foreign buyers, causing exports to fall.
3.  **Decrease in National Income:** As exports fall and imports rise due to domestic currency appreciation, Net Exports decrease. Assuming other factors remain constant, this reduction in Net Exports will reduce National Income.
