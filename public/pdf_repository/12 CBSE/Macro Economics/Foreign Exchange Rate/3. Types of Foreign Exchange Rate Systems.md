This section describes the three primary systems used for determining foreign exchange rates: fixed, flexible, and managed floating.

# Types of Foreign Exchange Rate Systems

The three main types of foreign exchange rate systems are:

1.  Fixed Exchange Rate System (or Pegged Exchange Rate System)
2.  Flexible Exchange Rate System (or Floating Exchange Rate System)
3.  Managed Floating Rate System

## Fixed Exchange Rate System

A *Fixed Exchange Rate System* is one where the exchange rate for a currency is officially set by the government.

*   The primary goal is to ensure stability in foreign trade and capital movements.
*   To maintain the fixed rate, the government buys foreign currency when there is excess supply and sells it when there is excess demand.
*   This requires the government to hold large reserves of foreign currencies.
*   Under this system, a country fixes its currency's value in terms of an 'External Standard'.
*   This standard could be gold, silver, another precious metal, another country's currency ('Pegging'), or an internationally agreed unit of account.
*   When a currency's value is fixed in terms of another currency or gold, it's known as the *'Parity value'* of the currency.

## Merits of Fixed Exchange Rate System

1.  **Stability in the Exchange Rate:** Provides stability and predictability, which promotes foreign trade by reducing uncertainty.
2.  **Promotes International Investment:** Creates a stable environment conducive to the smooth flow of foreign capital and long-term international investments.
3.  **Promotes International Trade:** Increases confidence among traders that the current rate will persist, facilitating quicker and smoother foreign trade.
4.  **Prevents Speculative Activities:** Since the government fixes the rate, it eliminates opportunities for speculative transactions aimed at profiting from exchange rate fluctuations.
5.  **Co-ordination of Macroeconomic Policies:** Encourages countries to coordinate their macroeconomic policies to maintain fixed rates, preventing arbitrary or competitive currency devaluations that could harm trading partners.

## Demerits of Fixed Exchange Rate System

1.  **Huge Foreign Exchange Reserves Required:** Governments must maintain large reserves of foreign currencies, which can restrict capital movement and potentially hinder international growth.
2.  **Difficulty in Fixing the Exchange Rate:** It is challenging to determine the appropriate level for the fixed rate. Fixing it too low can lead to a balance of payments deficit, while fixing it too high can cause inflationary pressure due to a balance of payments surplus.
3.  **Exchange Rates are not Permanently Fixed:** Despite being 'fixed', pegged rates are often adjusted (devalued or revalued) due to external pressures like fluctuations in commodity prices or balance of payments issues, making them not truly permanent.

## Historical Fixed Rate Systems: Gold Standard and Bretton Woods

Historically, exchange rates were fixed under systems like the *Gold Standard* (1870-1914) and the *Bretton Woods Standard* (1944-1971).

*   **Gold Standard:** Currencies' external values were fixed by defining their price in terms of gold. Central banks would buy and sell gold at a fixed price in their currency. For example, if £1 was 5 gm of gold and \$1 was 2 gm of gold, the exchange rate would be £1 = \$2.5.
*   **Bretton Woods Standard:** Gold was replaced by the US dollar as the central element. All other currencies were pegged to the US dollar at fixed rates. This system led to the establishment of the International Monetary Fund (IMF).

## Devaluation and Revaluation

*Devaluation* refers to the reduction in the value of the domestic currency by the government under a Fixed Exchange Rate System. It occurs when the government increases the fixed exchange rate (e.g., from \$1 = ₹ 81 to \$1 = ₹ 85).

*Revaluation* refers to the increase in the value of the domestic currency by the government under a Fixed Exchange Rate System.

| Basis                     | Devaluation                                                                       | Depreciation                                                                       |
| :------------------------ | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Meaning**               | Reduction in price of domestic currency in terms of all foreign currencies under fixed exchange rate regime by the government. | Fall in market price of domestic currency in terms of a foreign currency under flexible exchange rate regime by market forces. |
| **Occurrence**            | Takes place due to Government action.                                             | Takes place due to market forces of demand and supply.                           |
| **Exchange Rate System**  | Takes place under fixed exchange rate system.                                     | Takes place under flexible exchange rate system.                                   |

## Flexible Exchange Rate System

A *Flexible Exchange Rate System*, also known as *Floating Exchange Rate* or *Free Exchange Rate*, is a system where the exchange rate is determined solely by the forces of demand and supply of different currencies in the foreign exchange market.

*   The currency's value is allowed to fluctuate freely based on changes in demand and supply.
*   There is no intervention by the government or central bank to fix or manage the rate.
*   The exchange rate is determined by the market interactions of buyers and sellers of currency.

## Merits of Flexible Exchange Rate System

1.  **Maintains Equilibrium Level:** It is self-adjusting and automatically corrects imbalances in the Balance of Payments, preventing issues like overvaluation or undervaluation of currencies.
2.  **No Need for Huge Foreign Exchange Reserves:** Governments are not required to hold large foreign exchange reserves, which can facilitate capital movement and promote international growth.
3.  **Optimum Utilisation of Resources:** It can lead to a more efficient allocation and utilization of resources within the economy.

## Demerits of Flexible Exchange Rate System

1.  **Instability in the Exchange Rate:** The value of the domestic currency constantly changes based on market forces, creating uncertainty in foreign exchange receipts and payments, which can hinder foreign trade.
2.  **Speculative Activities:** The system is susceptible to manipulation by speculators, which can cause exchange rates to become excessively high or low, destabilizing the market and discouraging foreign trade and investment.
3.  **Creates Inflationary Situation:** Depreciation of the currency under this system can increase the price of imports, potentially leading to inflationary pressures in the economy.

| Basis                                    | Fixed Exchange Rate                                                                                                                            | Flexible Exchange Rate                                                                                                                                |
| :--------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Determination of Exchange Rate**       | Officially fixed in terms of gold or another currency by the government.                                                                         | Determined by forces of demand and supply of foreign exchange in the market.                                                                          |
| **Government Control**                   | Complete government control; only the government can change it.                                                                              | No government intervention; fluctuates freely based on market conditions.                                                                             |
| **Stability in Exchange Rate**           | Generally remains stable with only small possible variations (unless officially changed).                                                      | Keeps on changing frequently.                                                                                                                         |
| **Foreign Exchange Reserves**            | Government must keep a large stock of foreign exchange reserves to maintain the fixed rate.                                                    | Foreign exchange reserves are not required to be kept under this system as the rate is market-determined.                                             |
| **Increase or Decrease in Value of Currency** | Leads to *Devaluation* (decrease by government) or *Revaluation* (increase by government).                                                | Leads to *Depreciation* (decrease by market forces) or *Appreciation* (increase by market forces).                                                  |

# Managed Floating Rate System

The *Managed Floating Rate System* is a hybrid approach that combines elements of both fixed and flexible exchange rate systems. In this system, the exchange rate is primarily determined by market forces (demand and supply), but the central bank (like the RBI in India) intervenes in the foreign exchange market to influence the rate.

*   It is a mixture of the flexible and fixed systems.
*   Market forces of demand and supply determine the rate, similar to the flexible system.
*   The central bank manages the rate through intervention, similar to the fixed system's goal of stability.
*   The central bank intervenes to limit excessive fluctuations and keep the exchange rate close to a desired target range.
*   Maintaining foreign exchange reserves is necessary for the central bank to intervene effectively.
*   This system is also known as 'Dirty Floating', which contrasts with a 'Clean Float' where the central bank does not intervene at all.

## Example of Managed Floating Rate System

Suppose India uses a managed floating system and the RBI aims to keep the Rupee's value near ₹ 82 per US Dollar, allowing fluctuations only between ₹ 81.75 and ₹ 82.25.

*   If excess demand for Rupees causes the Rupee to appreciate (price of \$1 falls) below ₹ 81.75, the RBI will *increase the supply of Rupees* by selling Rupees for Dollars (buying Dollars) to push the Dollar price back up towards the target range. This intervention increases RBI's Dollar holdings.
*   If excess supply of Rupees causes the Rupee to depreciate (price of \$1 rises) above ₹ 82.25, the RBI will *increase the demand for Rupees* by exchanging Dollars for Rupees (selling Dollars) to push the Dollar price back down. This intervention reduces RBI's Dollar holdings.

## Flexible Exchange Rate Vs Managed Floating Exchange Rate

*Flexible exchange rate* is determined purely by supply and demand forces in the foreign exchange market, with no intervention from the central bank. It is a 'clean float'.
*Managed floating exchange rate* is primarily market-determined but is influenced by central bank intervention to control unfavorable fluctuations and keep the rate within a certain range. It is a 'dirty float'.
