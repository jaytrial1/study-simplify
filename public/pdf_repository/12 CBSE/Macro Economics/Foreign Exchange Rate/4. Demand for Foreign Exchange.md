This section outlines the various reasons why domestic residents demand or require foreign currency, leading to an outflow of foreign exchange.

# Demand for Foreign Exchange

The demand for foreign exchange originates from domestic residents who need foreign currency to make payments in other countries. Reasons for this demand include:

1.  **Imports of Goods and Services:** Foreign exchange is required to pay for goods and services imported from other countries.
2.  **Tourism:** When domestic residents travel abroad, they need foreign currency to cover their expenses.
3.  **Unilateral Transfers Sent Abroad:** Sending gifts, donations, or remittances to individuals or organizations in other countries requires foreign exchange.
4.  **Purchase of Assets in Foreign Countries:** Investing in or purchasing assets like land, shares, or bonds in foreign countries necessitates foreign currency.
5.  **Speculation:** Demand arises from speculators who anticipate an appreciation of a foreign currency and buy it now to sell later for a profit. For example, expecting the US dollar's price to rise, people buy dollars today.
6.  **Repayment of International Loans:** Loans taken from foreign countries are often denominated in foreign currency, so foreign currency is needed for repayment.

## Reasons for 'Rise in Demand' for Foreign Currency

Demand for foreign currency increases in the following situations:

1.  **Fall in Foreign Currency Price:** If the price of a foreign currency falls (e.g., \$1 falls from ₹ 82 to ₹ 81), imports from that country become cheaper. This increases imports, leading to a higher demand for that foreign currency (e.g., US dollars).
2.  **Cheaper Foreign Currency for Tourism:** When a foreign currency becomes cheaper relative to the domestic currency, it makes travel to that country more affordable, promoting tourism and increasing demand for that foreign currency.
3.  **Speculative Gains:** If the price of a foreign currency falls, its demand may rise as speculators buy it anticipating a future rise in price to make a profit.

## Demand Curve of Foreign Exchange

The demand curve for foreign exchange slopes downwards because there is an inverse relationship between the demand for foreign exchange and the foreign exchange rate.

The downward-sloping demand curve (DD) illustrates that at a lower exchange rate (OR₁), a larger quantity of foreign exchange (OQ₁) is demanded, while at a higher exchange rate (OR₂), the demand for US dollars falls to OQ₂.
