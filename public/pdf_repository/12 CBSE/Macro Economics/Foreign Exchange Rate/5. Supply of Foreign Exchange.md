# Supply of Foreign Exchange

The supply of foreign exchange comes from transactions that result in receipts of foreign currency for domestic residents. Sources of supply (inflow) include:

1.  **Exports of Goods and Services:** When a country exports goods and services, foreign buyers pay in foreign currency, increasing the supply of foreign exchange.
2.  **Foreign Investment:** Investments made by foreigners in the domestic country (Foreign Direct Investment or Foreign Portfolio Investment) bring foreign currency into the country.
3.  **Remittances (Unilateral Transfers) from Abroad:** Gifts, donations, and remittances received from individuals or organizations in foreign countries increase the supply of foreign exchange.
4.  **Speculation:** Supply comes from speculators who sell foreign currency expecting its value to fall in the future.
5.  **Loans from Rest of the World:** Borrowings by the domestic country from foreign countries or international institutions provide foreign currency.
6.  **Grants and Donations from Abroad:** Aid and grants received from foreign governments or international bodies contribute to the supply of foreign exchange.

## Reasons for 'Rise in Supply' of Foreign Currency

The supply of foreign currency increases in the following situations:

1.  **Rise in Foreign Currency Price:** If the price of a foreign currency rises (e.g., \$1 rises from ₹ 81 to ₹ 82), domestic goods become relatively cheaper for foreign buyers. This encourages foreigners to increase imports from the domestic country, raising the supply of foreign currency (e.g., US dollars).
2.  **Speculative Gains:** When the price of a foreign currency rises, its supply may increase as speculators sell it to realize profits.
3.  **Increased Foreign Investment:** A rise in the price of a foreign currency increases its purchasing power in the domestic country, potentially leading to increased investment from foreign countries.
4.  **Increased Tourism into the Domestic Country:** When the price of a foreign currency rises, travelling to the domestic country becomes relatively cheaper for foreigners, increasing tourism and thus the supply of foreign currency.

## Supply Curve of Foreign Exchange

The supply curve of foreign exchange slopes upwards due to a positive relationship between the supply of foreign exchange and the foreign exchange rate.
