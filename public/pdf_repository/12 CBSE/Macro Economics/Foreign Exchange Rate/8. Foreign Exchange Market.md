This section defines the foreign exchange market, explains its key functions, and describes the different types of markets based on transaction timing.

# Foreign Exchange Market

The *Foreign Exchange Market* is where foreign currencies are bought and sold. Participants include individuals, firms, foreign exchange brokers, commercial banks, and the central bank. It is a global system rather than a single physical location, facilitating the trading, conversion, and exchange of numerous foreign currencies.

## Functions of Foreign Exchange Market

The foreign exchange market performs three main functions:

1.  **Transfer Function:** It enables the transfer of purchasing power between countries involved in international transactions. This is achieved through various credit instruments like bills of foreign exchange, bank drafts, and electronic transfers.
2.  **Credit Function:** It provides credit necessary for international trade. Bills of exchange with typical maturity periods of three months are often used for international payments. This credit period allows importers time to receive, sell the goods, and obtain funds to settle the bill.
3.  **Hedging Function:** Hedging involves exporters and importers agreeing to future transactions at current prices and exchange rates. The purpose is to protect against potential losses caused by unfavorable fluctuations in exchange rates between the time the contract is made and the transaction is settled.

# Kinds of Foreign Exchange Markets

Foreign exchange markets are categorized based on whether transactions are settled immediately or at a future date. The two main types are:

1.  **Spot Market:**
    *   Refers to the market for transactions where receipts and payments are made almost immediately.
    *   Typically allows a settlement time of two business days.
    *   Deals with current transactions, not future ones.
    *   The exchange rate in the spot market is called the *spot exchange rate* or *current rate of exchange*.
    *   The term 'spot transaction' allowing two days is somewhat misleading; it accounts for the time needed for payment clearing processes like cheques.

2.  **Forward Market:**
    *   Refers to the market where the buying and selling of foreign currency are agreed upon today but settled on a specific future date at a predetermined rate.
    *   The exchange rate agreed upon is known as the *forward exchange rate*.
    *   Forward contracts are useful for international transactions agreed upon now but completed later.
    *   Forward contracts are made for two primary reasons:
        *   To minimize the risk of loss from adverse exchange rate changes (*hedging*).
        *   To potentially make a profit (*speculation*).
