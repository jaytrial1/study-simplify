This section explains that the multiplier effect also works in reverse, causing a multiplied decrease in income when there is an initial decrease in investment or autonomous expenditure.

# Reverse Operation of the Multiplier

The multiplier works in the forward direction (increasing income from increased investment) as well as in the backward direction. If investment decreases, the multiplier operates in the backward direction, leading to a multiplied decrease in income.

A reduction in investment leads to a decrease in income for those directly affected. This decrease in income leads to a reduction in consumption expenditure (based on the MPC). This reduced consumption becomes a decrease in income for others, leading to further reductions in consumption in successive rounds. This process results in a cumulative decline in the income and consumption levels.

The total contraction in income will be the product of the value of the multiplier and the initial decrease in investment ($\Delta Y = k \times \Delta I$).

Let us understand this with the help of an example:
Suppose, investment decreases by ₹ 20 crores ($\Delta I = -₹ 20$ crores). With MPC = 0.5, the multiplier is $k = 1 / (1-MPC) = 1 / (1-0.5) = 1 / 0.5 = 2$.
The total reduction in income will be $\Delta Y = k \times \Delta I = 2 \times (-₹ 20 \text{ crores}) = -₹ 40 \text{ crores}$.
There will be a net reduction in income to the extent of ₹ 40 crores.

Higher the MPC, greater the value of the multiplier, and thus, greater the cumulative decline in income resulting from a decrease in investment. The multiplier is, thus, symmetric and works in both directions (forward and backward), meaning it is a **double-edged weapon**.
