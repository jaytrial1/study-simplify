This section explains how equilibrium output is determined under the assumption of fixed prices, presenting the relevant equations and demonstrating the concept with a diagram and solved examples.

# Determination of Equilibrium Output under Fixed Price Model

Under the 'Fixed Price Model', where aggregate supply is infinitely elastic at the given price, the equilibrium output of final goods is determined solely by aggregate demand (AD).

In a two-sector model, AD is a function of Consumption (C) and Investment (I).

$$
A D=C+I
$$

We also know that the consumption function is given by:

$$
C=\bar{c}+b(Y)
$$

Where:
*   $\bar{c}$ = Autonomous Consumption (consumption independent of income)
*   $b$ = Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC), which is the rate at which consumption increases with income. 'b' represents the slope of the consumption function.
*   $Y$ = Income

Investments are assumed to be autonomous investments, meaning they are not influenced by the level of income.

$$
I=\bar{I} = \text{Autonomous Investment}
$$

Substituting the value of C and I into the AD equation, we get:

$$
A D = (\bar{c}+b(Y)) + \bar{I}
$$

Rearranging terms:

$$
A D = \bar{c}+\bar{I}+b(Y)
$$

Let $\bar{A}$ represent the total autonomous expenditure, which is the sum of autonomous consumption and autonomous investment:

$$
\bar{A} = \bar{c}+\bar{I}
$$

So, the aggregate demand function can be written as:

$$
A D = \bar{A}+b(Y)
$$

Now, the equilibrium level of aggregate demand (AD) or national income (Y) depends on the values of autonomous expenditure ($\bar{A}$) and MPC (or b). Equilibrium occurs where AD = Y (since AS = Y in this model).

$$
Y = \bar{A} + bY
$$

To find the equilibrium income (Y), rearrange the equation:

$$
Y - bY = \bar{A}
$$

$$
Y(1-b) = \bar{A}
$$

$$
Y = \frac{\bar{A}}{1-b}
$$

Equilibrium income is equal to autonomous expenditure multiplied by the multiplier, where the multiplier is $1/(1-b)$.

However, if autonomous expenditure increases from $\bar{A}_{1}$ to $\bar{A}_{2}$, the AD line will shift upwards to AD₁. Due to this increase in autonomous expenditure, at the initial income level OY, there is now excess demand equal to the vertical distance between the new AD₁ and the original AD curve at Y (or the distance EG on the diagram).

The initial point E will no longer represent the equilibrium. The new equilibrium will be determined where the new aggregate demand line, AD₁, intersects the 45° line. That occurs at point E₁. This is the new equilibrium point. The new equilibrium output and aggregate demand will be determined at OY₁ and AD₁ respectively. The increase in income from OY to OY₁ is larger than the initial increase in autonomous expenditure, demonstrating the multiplier effect.

## Example for Better Understanding

Q. 1. Estimate the value of ex-ante AD, when autonomous investment and consumption expenditure (A) is 850 crores and MPS is 0.2 and level of income is 8300 crores.
(CBSE, Sample Paper 2018, Delhi & Al Comptt. 2022 (II))

> **Answer:**
> Given:
> Autonomous Consumption + Autonomous Investment ($\bar{A}$) = ₹ 850 crores
> MPS = 0.2
> Level of Income (Y) = ₹ 8300 crores
>
> We know that MPC = 1 - MPS.
> MPC = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8
>
> In a two-sector model, the aggregate demand function is given by:
> $AD = \bar{A} + MPC \times Y$
>
> Substitute the given values:
> $AD = 850 + 0.8 \times 8300$
> $AD = 850 + 6640$
> $AD = \text{₹ 7490 crores}$
>
> The value of ex-ante AD at an income level of ₹ 8300 crores is ₹ 7490 crores.

Q. 2. On the basis of following information, identify whether the economy is in equilibrium or not:

| Particulars | Value |
| :--: | :--: |
| (i) Autonomous Consumption and Investment Expenditure ( $\bar{A}$ ) | 7500 crores |
| (ii) Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS) | 0.2 |
| (iii) National Income (Y) | 74,000 crores |

> **Answer:**
> The economy is in equilibrium when Aggregate Demand (AD) equals National Income (Y or AS).
>
> Given:
> Autonomous Consumption + Autonomous Investment ($\bar{A}$) = ₹ 7500 crores
> MPS = 0.2
> National Income (Y) = ₹ 74,000 crores
>
> First, calculate MPC:
> MPC = 1 - MPS = 1 - 0.2 = 0.8
>
> Now, calculate the Aggregate Demand (AD) at the given level of National Income using the AD function:
> $AD = \bar{A} + MPC \times Y$
> $AD = 7500 + 0.8 \times 74000$
> $AD = 7500 + 59200$
> $AD = \text{₹ 66700 crores}$
>
> Compare AD with the given National Income (Y):
> AD = ₹ 66700 crores
> Y = ₹ 74000 crores
>
> Since AD (₹ 66700 crores) < Y (₹ 74000 crores), the economy is **not in equilibrium**. There is an excess of aggregate supply over aggregate demand.
