# Saving-Investment Approach (S-I Approach)

According to this approach, the equilibrium level of income in the economy is determined at the level where planned saving (S) is equal to planned investment (I).

Let's understand this with the help of the following schedule and diagram:

| Income <br> (Y) | Consumption <br> (C) | Saving <br> (S) | Investment <br> (I) | Remarks |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 0 | 40 | -40 | 40 | $S<1$ |
| 100 | 120 | -20 | 40 | $S<1$ |
| 200 | 200 | 0 | 40 | $S<i$ |
| 300 | 280 | 20 | 40 | $S<1$ |
| 400 | 360 | 40 | 40 | Equilibrium $(S=I)$ |
| 500 | 440 | 60 | 40 | $S>1$ |
| 600 | 520 | 80 | 40 | $S>I$ |

*Table: Equilibrium by Saving and Investment Approach*

*Figure: Equilibrium by Saving and Investment Approach. The image shows the upward-sloping Saving (S) curve intersecting the horizontal Investment (I) curve at point E, determining the equilibrium level of income Y. The top panel shows the equivalent AD=AS equilibrium.*

In the figure, the Investment curve (I) is parallel to the X-axis because of the autonomous character of investments assumed in this model. The Saving curve (S) slopes upwards, showing that as income rises, saving also rises.

*   The economy is in equilibrium at point 'E' where the saving and investment curves intersect each other.
*   At point 'E', ex-ante (planned) saving is equal to ex-ante (planned) investment.
*   OY is the equilibrium level of output corresponding to point E.
*   In the table, the equilibrium level of income is ₹ 400 crores, where planned saving = planned investment = ₹ 40 crores.

If there is any deviation from the equilibrium level of income, i.e., if planned saving is not equal to planned investment, then a process of readjustment will start which will bring the economy back to the equilibrium level.

## Adjustment When Saving is More Than Investment

If planned saving is more than planned investment (S > I), which occurs after point 'E' in the figure, it means that households are not consuming as much as the firms expected them to. They are saving a larger portion of their income than firms are investing.

As a result, the inventory of unsold goods rises above the desired level.

To clear the unwanted increase in inventory, firms would plan to reduce production. This decrease in production leads to decreased employment and output, which in turn reduces the income level. This process continues until saving and investment become equal to each other again.

## Adjustment When Saving is Less Than Investment

If planned saving is less than planned investment (S < I), which occurs before point 'E' in the figure, it means that households are consuming more and saving less than what the firms expected them to. They are saving a smaller portion of their income than firms are investing.

As a result, planned inventory would fall below the desired level. Firms experience a reduction in their stock of unsold goods.

To bring the inventory back to the desired level, firms would plan to increase production. This increase in production leads to increased employment and output, which in turn raises the income level. This process continues until saving and investment become equal to each other again.
