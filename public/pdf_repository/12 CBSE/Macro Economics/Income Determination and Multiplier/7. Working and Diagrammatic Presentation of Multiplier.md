This section explains the step-by-step process of how the multiplier works through successive rounds of spending and income generation, and presents a diagram illustrating the multiplier effect on equilibrium income.

# Working of Multiplier

The working of the multiplier is based on the fact that 'One person's expenditure is another person's income'. When an additional investment is made, it generates income. A portion of this income is spent on consumption, which becomes income for others, and this process continues, leading to an increase in income that is many times more than the initial increase in investment. Let us understand this with the help of an example.

*   Suppose, an additional investment of ₹ 100 crores ($\Delta I$) is made to construct a flyover. This extra investment directly generates an extra income of ₹ 100 crores in the first round for the workers and suppliers involved. But this is not the end of the story.
*   If the Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC) is assumed to be 0.90, then the recipients of this additional income (₹ 100 crores) will spend 90% of it, i.e., ₹ 90 crores, as consumption expenditure. The remaining 10% (₹ 10 crores) will be saved. This consumption expenditure of ₹ 90 crores becomes additional income for others in the second round (e.g., shopkeepers, service providers).
*   In the next round, the recipients of the ₹ 90 crores additional income will again spend 90% of it on consumption, i.e., 0.90 * ₹ 90 crores = ₹ 81 crores. This ₹ 81 crores becomes additional income for others in the third round. The remaining ₹ 9 crores is saved.
*   This multiplier process will continue, with the consumption expenditure in every round being 0.90 times the additional income received in the previous round. The process generates progressively smaller amounts of additional income in each subsequent round as a portion is saved.

| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| $1^{\text {st }}$ | 100 | 100 | $90 (100 \times 0.9)$ | 10 |
| $2^{\text {nd }}$ | 0 | 90 | $81 (90 \times 0.9)$ | 9 |
| $3^{\text {rd }}$ | 0 | 81 | $72.90 (81 \times 0.9)$ | 8.10 |
| $4^{\text {th }}$ | 0 | 72.90 | $65.61 (72.90 \times 0.9)$ | 7.29 |
| $5^{\text {th }}$ | 0 | 65.61 | $59.05 (65.61 \times 0.9)$ | 6.56 |
| ... | ... | ... | ... | ... |
| **Total** | **100** | **1,000** | **900** | **100** |

*Table: Working of Multiplier (MPC = 0.90)*

Thus, an initial investment of ₹ 100 crores leads to a total cumulative increase of ₹ 1,000 crores in the income across all rounds.

Using the multiplier formula:
$k = \frac{1}{1-MPC} = \frac{1}{1-0.90} = \frac{1}{0.10} = 10$

The total increase in income ($\Delta Y$) is $k \times \Delta I = 10 \times ₹ 100 \text{ crores} = ₹ 1,000 \text{ crores}$.

## One more way to Calculate Total Increase in Income

The total increase in income is the sum of the income generated in each round, which forms a geometric series:

Total $\Delta Y = \Delta I + \Delta I \times MPC + \Delta I \times MPC^2 + \Delta I \times MPC^3 + ...$
Total $\Delta Y = \Delta I (1 + MPC + MPC^2 + MPC^3 + ...)$

The sum of an infinite geometric series $1 + r + r^2 + ...$ where $|r| < 1$ is $\frac{1}{1-r}$. In this case, $r = MPC$.

So, Total $\Delta Y = \Delta I \times \frac{1}{1-MPC}$

This confirms the multiplier formula $k = \frac{1}{1-MPC}$ and $\Delta Y = k \times \Delta I$.

Using the example values:
Total $\Delta Y = ₹ 100 \text{ crores} \times \frac{1}{1-0.90} = ₹ 100 \text{ crores} \times \frac{1}{0.10} = ₹ 100 \text{ crores} \times 10 = ₹ 1,000 \text{ crores}$.

*Figure: Diagrammatic Presentation of the Multiplier. The image shows the initial equilibrium at point E with income OY. An increase in investment shifts the AD curve upwards to AD1, leading to a new equilibrium at point F with a higher income OY1. The diagram illustrates that the increase in income (ΔY = YY1) is greater than the initial increase in investment (ΔI), which is the vertical shift between AD and AD1 at income Y.*

In the figure, income is taken on the X-axis and aggregate demand on the Y-axis. Suppose the initial equilibrium is determined at point E where the initial AD curve intersects the 45° AS line. The initial equilibrium level of income is OY.

Now, suppose that investment increases by $\Delta I$. This increase in autonomous expenditure shifts the aggregate demand curve upwards from AD to AD₁. The new aggregate demand curve AD₁ intersects the aggregate supply curve (AS) at point 'F'. Thus, the new equilibrium level of income is OY₁. The income rises from OY to OY₁, in response to an initial increase in investment ($\Delta I$).

It is clear from the figure that the increase in income (YY₁ or $\Delta Y$) is greater than the initial increase in investment ($\Delta I$), which is represented by the vertical distance between the AD and AD₁ curves. The value of the multiplier is given by the ratio of the increase in income to the increase in investment: $k=\frac{\Delta \mathrm{Y}}{\Delta \mathrm{I}}$.
