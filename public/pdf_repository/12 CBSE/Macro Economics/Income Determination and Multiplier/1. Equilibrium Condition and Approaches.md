This section introduces the concept of macroeconomic equilibrium according to Keynesian theory, explaining the fundamental condition where aggregate demand equals aggregate supply and outlining the two main approaches to determining this equilibrium.

# Equilibrium Condition and Approaches

According to the Keynesian Theory, the equilibrium condition for an economy is generally stated in terms of aggregate demand (AD) and aggregate supply (AS). An economy is in equilibrium when the total planned demand for goods and services equals the total planned supply of goods and services during a period.

The fundamental equilibrium condition is:

$$
A D=A S
$$

We know that Aggregate Demand (AD) is the sum of Consumption (C) and Investment (I):

$$
A D=C+I
$$

Also, Aggregate Supply (AS) is the sum of Consumption (C) and Saving (S):

$$
A S=C+S
$$

Substituting the expressions for AD and AS into the equilibrium condition ($AD=AS$), we get:

$$
C+I = C+S
$$

Subtracting C from both sides gives:

$$
I = S
$$

This shows that, according to Keynes, the equilibrium level of income and employment can also be determined when planned saving equals planned investment.

It's important to note that while Classical theory also addresses equilibrium, the scope here is limited to the Keynesian perspective.

## Two Approaches for Determination of Equilibrium Level

The two primary approaches in Keynesian theory used to determine the equilibrium level of income, output, and employment in the economy are:

1.  **Aggregate Demand-Aggregate Supply Approach (AD-AS Approach)**
2.  **Saving-Investment Approach (S-I Approach)**

It must be kept in mind that AD, AS, Saving, and Investment discussed in this context are all *planned* or *ex-ante* variables, meaning they represent intentions or expectations, not necessarily actual outcomes.

## Assumptions

Before delving into the determination of equilibrium output, let's state the key assumptions made:

*   The analysis is conducted within the context of a **two-sector model**, which includes only households (consumers) and firms (producers). This means there is no government or foreign sector considered.
*   Investment expenditure is assumed to be **autonomous**, meaning it is not influenced by the level of income in the economy.
*   The **price level is assumed to remain constant** in the short run.
*   The determination of equilibrium output is analyzed in the **short-run** context.
