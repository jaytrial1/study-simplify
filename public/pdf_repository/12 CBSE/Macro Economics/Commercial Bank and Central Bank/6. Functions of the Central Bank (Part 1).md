# Functions of Central Bank

The Reserve Bank of India performs several functions as the country's Central Bank.

## Currency Authority (or Bank of Issue)

The Central Bank has the sole right to issue currency in the country. In India, the RBI has the sole right to issue paper currency notes (except one-rupee notes and coins, which are issued by the Ministry of Finance). The one-rupee note is signed by the Finance Secretary, while other notes are signed by the RBI Governor.

Currency issued by the Central Bank is its monetary liability. The Central Bank must back the currency with assets of equal value (like gold, foreign securities, government securities) to build public confidence.

## Advantages of Sole Authority of Note issue with RBI

*   Ensures uniformity in note circulation.
*   Gives the central bank power to influence money supply.
*   Allows government supervision and control over currency issue.
*   Builds public faith in the currency system.
*   Helps stabilize the internal and external value of the currency.

The Central Government can borrow from the Central Bank. When the government has a budget deficit, it borrows by selling treasury bills to the Central Bank. The Central Bank issues new currency when it buys these securities, a process called **'Monetizing the Government's Debt'** or **'Deficit Financing'**.

## Digital Rupee

RBI introduced a concept note on digital rupee in October 2022 and launched the first pilot for retail digital Rupee (e₹-R) in December 2022. The digital rupee (e-rupee or **Central Bank Digital Currency - CBDC**) is an electronic version of physical cash.

*   It is a legal tender issued by the central bank in electronic form.
*   Aims to provide a more secure, government-backed alternative to private digital currencies.
*   Offers advantages like efficient, quicker, and cheaper money transfers, faster settlements, lower transaction costs, and easier handling.

## Banker to the Government

RBI acts as a banker, agent, and financial advisor to the Central and State Governments.

*   **As a banker:** Manages government's banking business, including maintaining accounts, accepting receipts, making payments, and providing temporary loans/advances (e.g., against treasury bills).
*   **As an agent:** Manages public debt.
*   **As a financial advisor:** Provides advice on economic, financial, and monetary matters.

## Banker's Bank and Supervisor

The Central Bank (RBI) regulates and supervises commercial banks. It acts as the banker to other banks, similar to how commercial banks deal with the public.

As the banker to banks, the central bank functions in three capacities:

*   **(i) Custodian of Cash Reserves:** Commercial banks must keep a portion of their deposits (**Cash Reserve Ratio - CRR**) with the central bank. This centralizes cash reserves.
*   **(ii) Lender of the Last Resort:** Provides loans and advances to commercial banks during financial emergencies when they cannot obtain funds from other sources. This role guarantees support and maintains a healthy banking system.
*   **(iii) Clearing House:** Settles inter-bank claims easily because all commercial banks have accounts with the central bank. Debit and credit entries in these accounts facilitate clearing.

As a supervisor, the central bank regulates commercial banks regarding licensing, branch expansion, asset liquidity, management, mergers, winding up, etc. Control is exercised through periodic inspections and analysis of reports submitted by banks.
