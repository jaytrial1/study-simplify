This section details the main operations performed by commercial banks, focusing on accepting deposits and advancing loans.

# Primary Functions of Commercial Banks

Commercial banks perform two primary functions:

1.  Accepting Deposits
2.  Advancing of Loans

## 1. Accepting Deposits

This is a crucial function. Banks accept deposits in various forms to suit different customer needs. The main types of deposits are:

*   **Current Account Deposits:**
    *   These are repayable by the banks on demand.
    *   Generally maintained by businessmen for frequent transactions.
    *   Allow unlimited withdrawals by cheque.
    *   Banks typically do not pay interest on these accounts; instead, they may charge service fees.

*   **Fixed Deposits or Time Deposits or Term Deposits:**
    *   Amount is deposited for a fixed period.
    *   Do not have cheque facility.
    *   Carry a high rate of interest, which increases with the deposit period.

| Basis                     | Current Deposits                                     | Fixed Deposits                                                              |
| :------------------------ | :--------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Cheque facility           | They are chequable deposits.                         | They are non-chequable deposits.                                            |
| Interest payments         | They do not carry any interest.                      | They carry interest which varies directly with the period of time.          |
| Number of transactions    | The depositor can make any number of transactions.   | Depositor generally makes only two transactions: Deposit and Withdrawal.    |

*   **Saving Deposits:**
    *   Combine features of both current and fixed deposits.
    *   Cheque facility is available, but often with restrictions on the number and amount of withdrawals.
    *   Carry a rate of interest lower than fixed deposits.

**Note:** Current Account and Saving deposits are chequable, while Fixed Deposits are non-chequable.

## 2. Advancing of Loans

Banks do not keep all deposits idle. After maintaining mandatory cash reserves, the remaining funds are lent to borrowers, and interest is charged. The interest rate on loans is higher than the interest rate on deposits, which is the bank's main income source. Different types of loans and advances include:

*   **Cash Credit:**
    *   A loan given against current assets (shares, stocks, bonds, etc.).
    *   A credit limit is sanctioned, and the amount is credited to the borrower's account.
    *   Interest is charged only on the amount actually withdrawn within the limit.

*   **Demand Loans:**
    *   Loans that can be recalled by the bank on demand at any time.
    *   The entire loan sum is credited to the borrower's account.
    *   Interest is payable on the entire sanctioned sum.

*   **Short-term Loans:**
    *   Given as personal loans, often against collateral security.
    *   Money is credited to the borrower's account.
    *   Interest is payable on the entire sanctioned loan amount.

## Conditions for a Financial Institution to become a Bank

For a financial institution to be considered a banking institution, it must perform **both** the functions of **'Accepting Deposits'** and **'Advancing Loans'**. For example, LIC is not a bank because it does not accept chequable deposits from the public, even though it provides loans.
