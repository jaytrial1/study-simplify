This section explains the process by which commercial banks expand the money supply through lending, based on fractional reserve banking.

# Money Creation or Credit Creation

Money creation is a key activity of commercial banks, allowing them to create credit significantly exceeding initial deposits. The process relies on two assumptions:

1.  The entire commercial banking system acts as a single unit ('Banks').
2.  All transactions (receipts and payments) are routed through the banks (payments by cheque, receipts deposited).

Banks use deposits to give loans. A loan is granted by opening a deposit account for the borrower and crediting the loan amount. This is called a **Secondary Deposit**.

Banks cannot lend the entire deposit amount. They are legally required to hold a minimum fraction of their deposits as reserves, known as the **Legal Reserve Ratio (LRR)**, **Reserve Deposit Ratio**, or **Reserve Ratio (RR)**, fixed by the central bank. Banks do not hold 100% reserves.

## Primary Deposits Vs Secondary Deposits

*   **Primary Deposits** are cash deposits made by people into various accounts (saving, current, term deposit).
*   **Secondary or Derivative Deposits** are deposits created when banks grant loans. The loan amount is credited to the borrower's account, creating a new deposit.

*   Total Demand Deposits = Primary Deposits + Secondary Deposits.
*   Primary Deposits represent savings, while Secondary Deposits represent borrowings.
*   Banks can create secondary deposits many times larger than their cash reserves.

## Why only Fraction of deposits are kept as Cash Reserves?

Banks keep only a fraction of deposits as reserves because:

1.  Not all depositors withdraw money simultaneously, nor do they withdraw the full amount at once.
2.  There is a continuous inflow of new deposits.

Experience shows that keeping a fraction (e.g., 20%) is sufficient to meet daily withdrawal demands.

Let's understand Money Creation with an example:
Assume initial deposit is ₹ 1,000 and LRR is 20%.

*   Banks keep ₹ 200 (20% of 1,000) as reserve and lend ₹ 800.
*   The ₹ 800 lent is credited to borrowers' accounts (Secondary Deposits).
*   Borrowers withdraw and spend ₹ 800.
*   This ₹ 800 is received by others and deposited back into the banking system, increasing deposits by ₹ 800 (Round I).
*   With the new ₹ 800 deposit, banks keep ₹ 160 (20% of 800) as reserve and lend ₹ 640.
*   This ₹ 640 is deposited back (Round II).
*   The process continues.

|       | Deposits (₹) | Loans (₹) | Cash Reserves (₹) (LRR = 20%) |
| :---- | :----------- | :-------- | :---------------------------- |
| Initial Deposit | 1,000        | 800       | 200                           |
| Round I | 800          | 640       | 160                           |
| Round II | 640          | 512       | 128                           |
| -     | -            | -         | -                             |
| -     | -            | -         | -                             |
| -     | -            | -         | -                             |
| **Total** | **5,000**    | **4,000** | **1,000**                     |

Starting with ₹ 1,000, banks created total deposits of ₹ 5,000. This is 'five times' the initial deposit. The factor 'five' is the **Money Multiplier**.

## Money Multiplier or Credit Multiplier or Deposit Multiplier

The **Money Multiplier** is the number by which total deposits can increase from an initial deposit. It is inversely related to the LRR. It's the process by which commercial banks create credit based on the reserve ratio and initial deposits.

$$
\text{Money Multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{LRR}}
$$

In the example, LRR = 20% or 0.2.
Money Multiplier = 1 / 0.2 = 5.

This means for every unit of money kept as reserves, banks can create 5 units of money. A higher LRR leads to a lower money multiplier and less money creation.

**For Better Clarification**
Example: Calculate total credit creation if initial deposit is ₹ 1,000 crores and LRR is 12.5%.
Solution: Given, LRR = 12.5% or 0.125.
Money Multiplier = 1 / 0.125 = 8.
Initial Deposit = ₹ 1,000 crores.
Total Credit Creation = Initial Deposits × Money Multiplier = 1,000 × 8 = ₹ 8,000 crores.
This is the maximum credit banks can create given the LRR.

## FORMULAE ASSOCIATED WITH CREDIT CREATION

*   Money Multiplier = $\frac{1}{\text{LRR}}$
*   Total Credit Creation (Total Deposits Created) = Initial Deposits × Money Multiplier
    OR
    Total Credit Creation = Initial Deposits × $\frac{1}{\text{LRR}}$
    OR
    Initial Deposits = $\frac{\text{Total Deposits}}{\text{Money Multiplier}}$
    OR
    Money Multiplier = $\frac{\text{Total Deposits}}{\text{Initial Deposits}}$
*   Total Demand Deposits of Commercial Banks = Primary Deposits + Secondary Deposits

Money creation by commercial banks increases National Income because banks lend primarily to investors. Increased investment leads to a rise in National Income through the multiplier effect.
