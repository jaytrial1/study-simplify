Commercial banks in India are broadly classified into two categories:

Image Description: Diagram classifying Commercial Banks into Scheduled Commercial Banks and Non-Scheduled Commercial Banks. Scheduled Commercial Banks are further divided into Private Sector Banks, Public Sector Banks (including SBI and its Subsidiaries, and Other Nationalized Banks), and Foreign Sector Banks.

1.  **Scheduled Commercial Banks:**
    *   Banks included in the Second Schedule of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934.
    *   Types:
        *   **(i) Private Sector Banks:** Owned and controlled by private entities, focused on profit (e.g., ICICI Bank, HDFC Bank, Kotak Bank).
        *   **(ii) Public Sector Banks:** Owned and controlled by the government, primarily focused on service (e.g., State Bank of India, Bank of Baroda, Punjab National Bank, Canara Bank). Public sector banks include SBI and its subsidiaries, and other nationalized banks.
        *   **(iii) Foreign Sector Banks:** Owned and controlled by foreign promoters. Their presence increased significantly after economic liberalization in 1991 (e.g., Bank of America, American Express Bank, Standard Chartered Bank).

2.  **Non-Scheduled Commercial Banks:**
    *   Banks not included in the Second Schedule of the Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934.
