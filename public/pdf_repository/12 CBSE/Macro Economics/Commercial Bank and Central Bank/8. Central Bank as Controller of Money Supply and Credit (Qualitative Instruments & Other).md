6.  **Margin Requirements:**
    *   The difference between the loan amount and the market value of the security offered by the borrower.
    *   If the margin is 40%, a bank can lend only up to 60% of the security's value.
    *   **Increasing margin:** Reduces borrowing capacity $\rightarrow$ Decreases money supply.
    *   **Decreasing margin:** Encourages borrowing $\rightarrow$ Increases money supply.
    *   RBI can set different margins for different types of borrowers or securities.
    *   Margins are necessary to protect banks from losses if the security value falls.

## Other Instruments of Credit Control

1.  **Moral Suasion:**
    *   A combination of persuasion and pressure by the Central Bank to encourage other banks to align with its policy.
    *   Exercised through discussions, letters, speeches, and hints.
    *   RBI announces policy positions and urges cooperation from banks.
    *   Can be used for both quantitative and qualitative control.
    *   Usually effective as the central bank is the lender of last resort, though no punitive action is taken for non-compliance.

2.  **Selective Credit Controls:**
    *   RBI gives directions to banks to channel credit to specific sectors or restrict it for certain purposes.
    *   **Positive manner:** Directing credit to **priority sectors** (e.g., small-scale industry, agriculture, exports).
    *   **Negative manner:** Restricting credit flow to particular sectors.

## Credit Control Instruments of RBI: Quantitative Vs Qualitative Instruments

RBI controls credit using 'Quantitative' and 'Qualitative' methods:

*   **Quantitative methods:** Include repo rate, bank rate, reverse repo rate, open market operations, and varying reserve requirements (CRR, SLR). These are general and affect all sectors using bank credit. They regulate the **total volume** of credit. Also known as traditional methods.
*   **Qualitative methods:** Include margin requirements, moral suasion, and selective credit controls. These are specific and affect the **direction** or specific use of credit. Also known as selective methods.

## Custodian of Foreign Exchange Reserves

The central bank holds the country's gold stock and foreign exchange reserves. This allows it to control foreign exchange transactions (which must be routed through RBI) and serves two objectives:

*   Stabilizing the external value of the currency.
*   Implementing a coordinated policy regarding the country's balance of payments.
