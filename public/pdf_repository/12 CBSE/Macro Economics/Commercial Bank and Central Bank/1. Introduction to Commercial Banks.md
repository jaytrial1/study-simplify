# Introduction to Commercial Banks

A **commercial bank** is a primary unit of the Indian Banking System. Like other businesses, commercial banks aim to earn profits by offering a variety of services to their customers.

A commercial bank is defined as an institution which performs the functions of accepting deposits, granting loans and making investments, with the aim of earning profits. Examples in India include State Bank of India (SBI), Punjab National Bank (PNB), Bank of Baroda, and Canara Bank.

## Commercial Banks - Backbone of an Economy

*   The household sector saves a part of its income, which commercial banks collect.
*   Commercial banks act as **financial intermediaries** by collecting savings from the household sector and lending it to the productive sector for investment.
*   The rate of interest paid by banks on deposits is the **borrowing rate**.
*   The rate of interest charged by banks on loans is the **lending rate**.
*   The difference between the lending rate and the borrowing rate is known as the **spread**, which is the profit earned by the banks.
