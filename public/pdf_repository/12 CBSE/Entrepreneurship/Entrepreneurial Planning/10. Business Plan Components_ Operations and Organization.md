# Business Plan Components: Operations and Organization

## IV. Operational plan

Operational plan is a system achieving a smooth and coordinated flow of work within the factory, planning and controlling productive operations so the final product is completed according to plans. - Wright

Where the production plan aims at "plan your work", operations plan ensures "work your plan". It's a blueprint prepared in advance of actual operations:
i) Ensuring orderly flow of materials from raw state to finished products.
ii) Facilitating continuous production, lesser work-in-progress, and minimization of wastage.
iii) Co-ordinating work of engineering, purchasing, production, selling, and inventory management.
iv) Describing flow of goods/services from production to consumers.
v) Introducing a proper quality control system.
vi) Undertaking best and most economic production policies and methods.

The operational plan organizes movement of material, performance of machines, and operations of labour into a defined direction, co-ordinating for desired manufacturing results in terms of:
a) Quantity
b) Quality
c) Time
d) Place and
e) Cost

## Elements of operational plan

The operation plan involves planning:
i) For production in advance of operations.
ii) Establishing the exact route of each item/part/assembly.
iii) Setting starting and finishing dates for each assignment/work.
iv) Regulating orderly movement of goods through the entire manufacturing cycle (procurement to shipping).

To achieve these, entrepreneur pays attention to:
1) **Routing:** Determining exact route/path a product/service follows from raw material to finished product.
2) **Scheduling:** Fixing time, day, date for each operation to start and complete; determining required time for each operation.
3) **Dispatching:** Initiating production according to plan, issuing necessary orders, instructions, guidelines to give practical shape to the plan.
4) **Follow-Up:** Evaluating and appraising work performed. Helps in detecting errors/defects. Helps entrepreneurs in:
    a) Reviewing situation regarding materials, work-in-progress, finished goods.
    b) Expediting performance of lagging departments.
    c) Removing production obstacles by suggesting remedial measures.
5) **Inspection:** Comparing materials, product, or performance with established standards. Helps set up labs or strategies to ensure predetermined quality.
6) **Shipping:** Describes flow of goods/services from production to consumers, beyond manufacturing.

This part details chronological steps to complete a business transaction efficiently and profitably. Operational plan is affected by:
a) Nature of venture
b) Type of product/service
c) Scale of operation, and
d) Technology involved

## V. Organizational plan

Businesses fall into four basic categories:
a) **Manufacturing:** Makes a tangible product.
b) **Wholesale:** Buys in bulk from manufacturers, sells in smaller lots to retailers.
c) **Retail:** Sells directly to final consumer.
d) **Service:** Sells intangibles like time or expertise.

The organizational plan describes the proposed venture's form of ownership. Each business type differs significantly in:
a) Commencement procedures
b) Legal constraints
c) Financial requirement
d) Accounting methods
e) Marketing and promotional strategies

It's important to categorize the business and carefully choose a legal structure. As discussed earlier, entrepreneurs can choose from:
a) Sole proprietorship
b) Partnership
c) Joint Hindu family
d) Cooperative, or
e) Corporation

Each form has implications on taxes, liability, continuity, financing, and ownership. No form is the best; the entrepreneur decides based on multiple factors.

The organizational plan describes the chosen form of ownership, adequately mentioning:
i) Terms and conditions associated with the form.
ii) Lines of authority and responsibility of members.
iii) Names, designation, addresses, and resumes of members.
iv) Stake of members in the organization.
v) Roles and responsibilities of each member.
vi) Procedure for solving conflicts/disputes.
vii) Forms of payment for members.
viii) Voting rights, managerial, and controlling rights.

This information gives potential investors a clear understanding of control and member interaction in management. Starting with a strong management team committed to goals is important. The organizational plan helps evaluate and decide the legal structure affecting long-term effectiveness and profitability.

It benefits both the entrepreneur and investors by:
i) Specifying needed skills and roles (formal organization).
ii) Representing attitudes, behaviors, dress, communication styles (informal organization or culture).

### Organization Structure

*   What is the form of ownership of the organization?
*   In a partnership, who are the partners and what are the terms of agreement?
*   If incorporated, who are the principal shareholders and how much stock do they own?
*   How many and what type of shares of voting or non-voting stock have been issued?
*   Who are the members of the board of directors? (Give names, addresses, and resumes)
*   Who has the check-signing authority or control?
*   Who are the members of the management team and what are their backgrounds?
*   What are the role and responsibilities of each member of the management team?
*   What are the salaries, bonuses, or other forms of payment for each member of the management team?
