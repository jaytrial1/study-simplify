# Forms of Business Organization

Setting-up a business is not an easy task. An enterprise is a separate and distinct unit, institutionally arranged to conduct any type of business activity. It needs to combine necessary resources like materials, tools, equipment, working space and manage them systematically to achieve objectives.

Every business entity needs to select an appropriate legal structure or framework. This structure determines the extent of ownership and responsibility of proprietor(s). The appropriate form strongly influences success and future prospects. Once selected, changing the 'form' is quite complex, time consuming and costly.

## Forms of Enterprises

From the point of view of ownership and management, business enterprises may be broadly classified under three categories:

1.  Private sector enterprises
2.  Public sector enterprises
3.  Joint sector

### Private sector enterprises

Enterprises owned, controlled, and managed by private individuals, with the main objective of earning profit. Private individuals could start a venture as:
a) Sole-proprietorship
b) Partnership
c) Joint Hindu family business
d) Co-operative
e) Company

### Public sector enterprises

Business enterprises owned, controlled and operated by public authorities, with welfare as primary and profit as secondary goals. Either the whole or most of the investment is done by the Government(s). These enterprises have the following forms:
a) Departmental undertaking
b) Public corporations
c) Government companies

### Joint sector enterprises

A form of partnership between the private sector and the government where management is generally in the hands of the private sector, with government representation on the Board of Directors. Resources are mostly shared equally.

One of the first decisions for an entrepreneur is structuring the business. This legal configuration defines:
a) Control
b) Personal liability
c) Rights and liabilities of participants
d) Life span
e) Financial structure

This decision has long-term implications. An entrepreneur may consult experts to select the right form, considering:
1) Vision regarding size and nature of business.
2) Level of control desired.
3) Level of "structure" willing to deal with.
4) Business vulnerability to lawsuits.
5) Tax implications.
6) Expected profit (or loss).

From the entrepreneur's point of view, the most commonly opted forms for a new venture are:
1) Sole proprietorship
2) Partnership
3) Company

As entrepreneurs are strongly motivated by monetary gains and independence, these three forms fulfill their basic requirements well.
