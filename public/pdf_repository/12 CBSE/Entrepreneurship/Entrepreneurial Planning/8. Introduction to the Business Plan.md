This section introduces the concept of a business plan as a crucial roadmap for entrepreneurs, defining what it is, who should write it, and highlighting its significant importance for various stakeholders.

# Introduction to the Business Plan

## "WHAT TO DO? HOW TO DO? WHO IS TO DO?" IS BASIC TO ANY ACTIVITY

Planning is "thinking in advance, what is to be done, when it is to be done, how it is to be done and by whom it should be done."

Like a vigilant traveler planning a trip (considering external factors like weather, hotel availability, political conditions, and internal factors like money, time, companions, routes), an entrepreneur must make decisions and gather information about external and internal factors for their proposed venture. Planning is an intellectual process with organized foresight, a vision based on facts and experience, essential for intelligent action. Planning bridges the gap between the current state and desired future state.

"Entrepreneurship is personal. It is what you can do almost by yourself." - John H. Johnson, American publisher of Ebony and Jet magazines.

This is possible only if the entrepreneur can effectively plan their current position, desired position, and how to get there. A 'game plan' or 'road map' is needed for the venture to evolve. An entrepreneur needs to detail all relevant external and internal elements for starting and successfully running a new venture. This road map is called a "Business Plan".

## What is the business plan

The business plan is a comprehensively written down document prepared by the entrepreneur describing formally all the relevant external and internal elements involved in starting a new venture. It's a formal statement of business goals, reasons for their attainability, the plan to reach them, and background information about the organization/team.

A business plan is a comprehensive project report that encompasses planned activities and also:
a) helps to understand the feasibility and viability of the proposed venture.
b) facilitates assessing and making provisions for bottlenecks in progress and implementation.
c) discusses potential for success and risk factors.

Business plans are decision-making tools:
1) Describing all necessary inputs for the enterprise.
2) Explaining the mode of utilization of resources.
3) Detailing strategies for project execution.
4) Outlining desired goals.
5) Assessing market sensitivity and profitability.

The content and format represent all aspects of business planning.

## Who should write the plan?

A business plan represents all aspects of planning, including vision, strategy, and sub-plans for:
*   Marketing
*   Finance
*   Operations
*   Human resources
*   Legal compliance
*   Intellectual property rights, etc.

Preparing a business plan requires knowledge from various disciplines. The entrepreneur should consult experts like lawyers, accountants, marketing consultants, engineers, banks, financial institutions, agencies promoting entrepreneurship, friends, relatives, and mentors. The entrepreneur should prepare the plan but assess their own skills to determine where assistance is needed.

## Importance of the business plan

The business plan is valuable to the entrepreneur, potential investors, venture capitalists, banks, financial institutions, new personnel, suppliers, customers, advisors, and others interested in the venture, its goals, and objectives. The business plan:
a) helps determine the viability of the venture in a designated market.
b) provides guidance in organizing planning activities, such as identifying required resources, obtaining licenses, and complying with legal requirements.
c) helps satisfy concerns, queries, and issues of interested parties.
d) provides room for self-assessment and self-evaluation, requiring the entrepreneur to think through scenarios and plan to avoid obstacles.
e) at times, helps realize unavoidable/unbeatable obstacles, suggesting termination of the venture before further investment.
f) serves as an important tool for funds procurement as investors/lenders focus on the four Cs of credit (character, cash flow, collateral, and equity contribution), which the plan reflects.

Hence, a business plan gives adequate clarity to the entrepreneur, investor(s), and government on:
*   What an entrepreneur is doing
*   Why he/she is doing it
*   How he/she will do that

It's well said that "writing a good business plan can't guarantee success, but it can go a long way toward reducing the odds of failure."

## Formats of a business plan

The depth and detail depend on the size and scope of the venture. There's no fixed content; it varies by entrepreneur's goals and audience. Start-ups often use three or four formats:
i) **Elevator pitch:** A three-minute summary of the executive summary, used as a teaser for potential funders, customers, or partners.
ii) **A pitch deck with oral narrative:** An engaging slideshow and narrative to spark discussion and interest investors in reading the written presentation (executive summary, key financial graphs).
iii) **A written presentation for external stakeholders:** A detailed, well-written, and formatted plan for external parties.
iv) **An internal operational plan:** A detailed plan for management, potentially not of interest to external stakeholders.

Depending on experience, knowledge, and purpose, a business plan generally outlines the following components sequentially:
1) Introductory cover page/general introduction
2) Business venture
3) Organisational plan
4) Production plan
5) Operational plan
6) Human resource plan
7) Marketing plan
8) Financial plan
9) Miscellaneous/appendix
