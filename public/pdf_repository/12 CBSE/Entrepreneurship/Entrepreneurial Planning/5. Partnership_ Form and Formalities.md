## Meaning

A partnership is an association of two or more persons to carry on, as co-owners, a business and to share its profits and losses. Two or more persons may form a partnership by making a written or oral agreement to carry a business jointly and share its proceeds.

## To Quote Authors....

*   John A. Shubin: "Two or more individuals may form partnership by making written or oral agreement that they will jointly assume full responsibility for the conduct of business."
*   L.H. Haney: "The relationship between persons who agree to carry on a business in common with a view to private gain is partnership."
*   Indian Partnership Act, 1932: "Partnership is a relationship between persons who have agreed to share the profits of a business carried on by all, or any of them acting for all."

## Characteristics of Partnership

The essential features of partnership are as follows:

1.  **Two or more persons:** At least 2 persons are needed. Minors cannot form a partnership but can be admitted to the benefits of a running firm. Maximum number is 10 for banking business, 20 for other businesses.
2.  **Agreement:** Arises from contract, not status. Oral agreements are acceptable, but written (Partnership Deed) is advisable to avoid disputes.
3.  **Profit sharing:** Objective is to make and distribute profits among partners. Charity work is not partnership.
4.  **Unlimited liability:** Mostly, partners' liability is unlimited. Personal properties can be used for firm's debts. Creditors can claim from any or all partners (individually and collectively liable).
5.  **Implied authority:** Any partner can act on behalf of the firm; the firm is bound by their acts.
6.  **Mutual agency:** Business can be carried on by all or any one acting for all. Every partner is both principal and agent of others and the firm. Each partner is liable for others' acts and can bind others/firm by acts in ordinary course of business.
7.  **Utmost good faith:** Partners are expected to act honestly and provide proper accounts. Mutual faith and confidence are the main strength.
8.  **Restriction on transfer of shares:** No partner can transfer their share without consent of others. A partner can discontinue by giving notice for dissolution.
9.  **Continuity:** Continues as long as all partners desire. Legally, dissolves on retirement, death, bankruptcy, lunacy, or disability of a partner, unless stated otherwise in the deed.

## Suitability

Partnership is an ideal choice if capital and managerial requirements are higher than a sole proprietorship, the enterprise is small to medium scale, and direct contact with customers is essential. It suits businesses needing more investment and expert management.

## Consequences for non-registration of a partnership firm

Partnership firms in India are governed by the Indian Partnership Act, 1932. Registration is not compulsory, with no penalties for non-registration, but it is advisable because non-registered firms are denied the following rights:

1.  A partner cannot file a suit against the firm or other partners for enforcing rights from a contract or the Act.
2.  A right from a contract cannot be enforced in any Court by or on behalf of the firm against any third party.
3.  The firm or its partners cannot claim a set-off or other proceedings in a dispute with a third party.

## Drafting of partnership deed

Partnership is an agreement, oral or written. Written agreement (Partnership Deed) is desirable to avoid misunderstandings and litigation. It must be signed by partners, stamped, and registered. Alterations require mutual consent.

A partnership deed generally contains:
1.  Name of the firm.
2.  Nature of the business.
3.  Name of partners.
4.  Place of the business.
5.  Amount of capital contributed by each partner.
6.  Profit sharing ratio.
7.  Loans and advances from partners and interest rate.
8.  Drawings allowed and interest rate.
9.  Salary and commission payable to partners.
10. Duties, powers, and obligations of partners.
11. Maintenance of accounts and audit arrangement.
12. Mode of goodwill valuation (admission, retirement, death).
13. Settlement of accounts (dissolution).
14. Arbitration of disputes.
15. Arrangements if a partner becomes insolvent.

## Registration procedure

A partnership firm can be registered at formation or later. An application is filed with the Registrar of Firms in the relevant area.

### Step: 1

Application should include:
1) Name of the firm
2) Place of business
3) Other places of business
4) Date of partners joining
5) Full name and permanent address of partners.
6) Duration of the firm

### Step: 2

Every partner verifies and signs the application. Enclose:
a) Application in prescribed form-I.
b) Duly filled specimen of affidavit
c) Certified copy of the partnership deed
d) Proof of ownership or rental/lease agreement of business place.

The firm name should not imply government approval or patronage unless consent is given. Once satisfied, the Registrar records the statement and issues a Certificate of Registration.
