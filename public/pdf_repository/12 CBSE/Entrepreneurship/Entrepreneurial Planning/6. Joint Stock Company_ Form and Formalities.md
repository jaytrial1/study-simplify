This section covers the Joint Stock Company form, tracing its historical development, explaining its meaning and characteristics, discussing the choice between private and public companies, and outlining the legal formalities for incorporation in India.

# Joint Stock Company: Form and Formalities

## ONCE "ILLEGAL .... TODAY LEGALLY MOST DESIRABLE!!!

Joint stock company organisation started in Italy in the thirteenth century. It developed in England under Royal Charter or Acts of Parliament in the 17th and 18th centuries. It faced a setback with the "BUBBLES ACT OF 1720" which declared promotion of companies illegal in England. The form regained status with THE JOINT STOCK COMPANIES ACT IN 1844, and limited liability was granted in 1855.

The first Companies Act in India was passed in 1850; limited liability was introduced in 1857. A comprehensive bill passed in 1956 defined companies incorporated under it.

Resources and lifespan of sole proprietorship and partnership are limited, with unlimited liabilities. Growing needs demanded:
1) Capital
2) Managerial talent and skills
3) Limited liability

Thus, the joint stock company emerged as a modern form to meet the requirements of large-sized business.

## Meaning

In common prevalence, a company means a voluntary association of persons formed for some common object with capital divisible into units of equal value called 'shares' and with limited liability. A company is a creation of law (an artificial human being) and can only be terminated by law.

## Definitions Well Known

*   Prof. L.H. Haney: "A joint stock company is a voluntary association of individuals for profit, having a capital divided into transferable shares, the ownership of which is the condition of membership."
*   James Stephenson: "A company is an association of many persons who contribute money or money's worth to a common stock and employ it is some trade or business, and, who share the profit and loss arising there from."
*   Section 3 of Indian companies act, 1956: "A company means a company formed and registered under this act or any previous act."

Thus, a company is an association of persons who contribute money in the shape of shares. The company gets a legal entity and enjoys a permanent existence.

## Characteristics of a Company

A company is distinctive from other forms due to:

1.  **Voluntary association:** Minimum two persons for a private company, seven for a public company.
2.  **Artificial person:** Created by law, exists as a distinct person though without body or conscience. Can buy, sell, own property, sue, and be sued.
3.  **Separate legal entity:** Independent status, distinct from its members. Not liable for members' actions and vice-versa.
4.  **Common seal:** Being artificial, uses a common seal with its name engraved, equivalent to a signature.
5.  **Limited liability:** Liability of shareholders is normally limited to the amount of shares held or guarantee given.
6.  **Transferability of shares:** Shares are freely transferable (subject to conditions). Private companies may restrict transfer.
7.  **Diffusion of ownership and management:** Separation of ownership (shareholders) from management (directors). Shareholders give directors the right to manage.
8.  **Number of members:**
    *   Private company: Minimum 2, Maximum 50 (excluding employees).
    *   Public company: Minimum 7, No maximum limit.
9.  **Limitation of action:** Scope determined by the Indian Companies Act, Memorandum of Association, and Articles of Association. Actions beyond these documents can lead to winding up.
10. **Winding up:** Termination is as per the Companies Act.

## Choice to be made

An entrepreneur can incorporate an enterprise as either a:
a) Private company or,
b) Public company

### A) Private company

A private company:
1) has a minimum of two and a maximum of fifty members (excluding past and present employees).
2) restricts the right of its members to transfer shares.
3) prohibits public invitation to subscribe for shares, debentures, or accept deposits (except from directors, members, or relatives).
4) has a minimum paid up capital of one lakh rupees (subject to change).
5) uses the word 'Pvt. Ltd.' at the end of its name.

### B) Public company

Under Section 3(i)(ii) of the Companies Act, a public company is one which is not a private company. Implications:
1) has minimum seven people to commence with no upper limit to membership.
2) does not restrict any transfer of shares.
3) invites public to subscribe for its shares, debentures and public deposits.
4) has a minimum paid up capital of five lakh rupees.
5) uses the word 'Ltd.' at the end of its name.

## Why private company is more desirable

Despite restrictions, a private company enjoys privileges under the Companies Act, making it preferred by many entrepreneurs:
1) Only two members required to form.
2) Only two directors required for quorum in meetings.
3) Can file a statement in lieu of prospectus with the Registrar.
4) Can commence business immediately after incorporation.
5) Holding of statutory meeting or filing statutory report is not required.
6) Non-members cannot inspect copies of profit and loss A/c filed with Registrar.
7) Limit on maximum managerial remuneration does not apply.
8) Restrictions on appointment/reappointment of managing director do not apply.
9) Maintaining index of members is not required.
10) Directors need not have qualification shares.

The company form has seen phenomenal growth globally.

## Case Study-II: Quest for Independence effects Form's Selection

William C. Durant, founder of General Motors, had various entrepreneurial ventures that didn't succeed. He noticed a friend's 'buggy' rode smoothly due to new spring design by Coldwater Road Cart Company. Durant immediately offered to buy the company for \$1500, borrowing \$2000. He made two sample buggies, sold them, got orders for 600, and the business took off. In 1904, BUICK MANUFACTURING COMPANY offered Durant partnership, needing capital and his selling abilities. Durant agreed only if he had absolute managerial control, eventually taking over BUICK COMPANY.

## Suitability

Comparing advantages and limitations, the company form is suggested if the entrepreneur's venture is:
1) A heavy and basic industry type.
2) Involves large-scale operations.
3) Requires huge funds.
4) Involves heavy risks.
5) Technologically complex and sophisticated, relying heavily upon experts and professionals.
