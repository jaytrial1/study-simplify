This section lists the essential legal and administrative formalities an entrepreneur needs to comply with when starting a business in India, including taxes, registrations, and permissions.

# Key Formalities for Starting a Business

## Legal formalities expected to be complied by the entrepreneur:

A heavy responsibility rests on the entrepreneur in translating his idea into a working reality. The sphere of activities done during this stage is very wide encompassing:

## 1. Obtain PAN number from Income Tax Department

**What is PAN?** Permanent Account Number (PAN) is a ten-digit alphanumeric number issued by the Income Tax Department. It links all financial transactions with the department (tax payments, TDS/TCS credits, returns, specified transactions, correspondence). PAN acts as an identifier. It is mandatory to quote PAN in all financial transactions.

**Who must have PAN?**
i) All existing assesses or taxpayers or persons required to furnish a return of income.
ii) Any person carrying on business/profession whose total sales/turnover/gross receipts are likely to exceed five lakh rupees in any previous year.
iii) Any person intending to enter financial transactions where quoting PAN is mandatory.
iv) The Assessing Officer may allot PAN to any person.

## 2. Open a current account

**Who can open a current account?**
i) Any person competent to contract and satisfactorily introduced to the Bank. May not open more than one such account in their own name. Accounts may be opened in names of two or more persons, payable as specified.
ii) Accounts can be opened for sole proprietorships, partnerships, private/public limited companies, Joint Hindu families, trusts, clubs, associations etc. satisfactorily introduced and fulfilling procedures/credentials.
iii) Minors 14+ years, able to read/write, can open accounts in their sole names.

**Basic common documentation:**
*   Proof of Identity: PAN Card, Voter Id Card, Passport, Driving License
*   Proof of Address: Latest Telephone Bill or Electricity Bill

**Public or private limited companies documentation:**
*   Certificate of incorporation and commencement of business
*   Memorandum and Articles of Association
*   Board resolution authorising account opening/operation
*   PAN or GIR No. or completed Form 60
*   List of directors with residential addresses

## 3. Register your company (Pvt. Ltd/Public limited Company)

Steps involved in incorporating a private or public company in India:
1.  Name of the business entity.
2.  Register for e-filing at MCA (Ministry of Corporate Affairs) portal.
3.  Apply for Director Identification Number (DIN).
4.  Obtain Digital Signature Certificate (DSC).
5.  Register DSC at MCA website.
6.  Apply for approval of the company name.
7.  Formulate Memorandum of Association.
8.  Formulate Articles of Association.
9.  Verify, stamp, and sign Articles of Association.
10. Verify the various forms required for incorporation.

## 4. Register for service tax

Service tax is a tax on Services, levied on specified services by the Central Government under the Finance Act, 1994. It is an indirect tax; service provider pays and recovers from recipient.

## 5. Register for VAT/sales tax

**Value added tax (VAT):** A multi-point destination-based taxation system on value addition at each stage of production/distribution. 'Value addition' is the increase in value at each stage. VAT is a tax on final consumption, borne by the consumer.

Tax Payer's Identification Number (TIN) is used for dealer identification/registration, an 11-digit numeral (first two characters are State Code).

**Sales tax:** Levied on the first sale of a commodity (produced or imported). Subsequent sales without further processing may be exempt. Paid by dealer on sale in interstate trade/commerce, even if not taxable under state laws.

**Sales tax ID number:** A business version of PAN, under which tax is collected and paid for taxable services/products in the state.

## 6. Excise duty (If applicable)

Tax on manufacture or production of goods. State Excise duty (alcohol, narcotics) collected by State Government. Central Excise duty (rest of goods) collected by Central Government (Section 3, Central Excise Act, 1944). Different from sales tax (tax on sale vs. tax on manufacture).

**Categories required to register with Central Excise:**
i) Every manufacturer of dutiable excisable goods.
ii) First/second stage dealers or importers issuing Cenvatable invoices.
iii) Persons holding bonded warehouses for non-duty paid goods.
iv) Persons obtaining excisable goods for end-use based exemption.

**Procedure for obtaining registration:** Apply to nearest central excise division office in form A. 1 with self-attested copy of PAN. After verification, a regular registration certificate (form RC) is normally issued immediately.

## 7. Customs duty

Indirect tax on goods imported into India and on goods exported from India. Taxable event is import into or export from India. Import means bringing goods to India from outside (includes territorial waters up to 12 nautical miles). Export means taking goods out of India to a place outside.

## 8. File entrepreneurship memorandum at DIC (optional)

Not mandatory, but filing Part I at the District Industries Centre may be necessary for claiming incentives/subsidies and certain state-level formalities.

## 9. Apply for TAN

Tax Deduction and Collection Account Number (TAN) is a 10-digit alphanumeric number required by persons responsible for deducting or collecting tax. Mandatory to quote TAN in TDS/TCS returns, challans, and certificates.

**Who must apply for TAN?** All persons required to deduct tax at source or collect tax at source on behalf of Income Tax Department.

## 10. Permissions required at the construction stage

Some government permissions required:
*   Application for plot/shed, offer letter, EMD payment.
*   Allotment of plot/shed, balance occupancy price payment, possession.
*   Application for NOC/SSI registration.
*   Execution of lease agreement.
*   Application for construction water connection.
*   Application for construction power connection.

## Post construction clearances

*   Building completion/drainage completion/tree plantation certificate.
*   Permission for mortgage.
*   NOC from Pollution Control Board.
*   Final fire clearance.
*   NOC from environment department.
*   Industrial safety permit.
*   Sanction of permanent power.
*   Sanction of permanent water and sewerage connection.

## 11. Employee's Provident Fund (EPF)

Applicable for establishments employing 20 or more persons in industry.

## 12. Employee's State Insurance (ESI) scheme

Applicable to non-seasonal factories employing 10 or more persons. Extended to shops, hotels, restaurants, cinemas, road-motor transport, and newspaper establishments employing 20 or more persons.
