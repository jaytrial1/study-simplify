This section details the financial plan, crucial for assessing funding needs and economic feasibility, and the human resources plan (manpower planning), essential for ensuring the availability of qualified personnel.

# Business Plan Components: Finance and Human Resources

## VI. Financial plan

Finance is a most important prerequisite to establish an enterprise, facilitating the entrepreneur to bring together resources (men, material, machines, methods) to produce goods/services. Timely availability of funds in right volume is key. Entrepreneur should develop a sound financial plan discussing:
a) Financial requirements
b) Sources of raising funds
c) Exact assessment of revenue, cost, profits, cash flow, inventory, loans, etc.

### Troublesome Money

King C. Gillette's Gillette Company, a multibillion business today, was \$12,500 in debt in 1901. Gillette recalled being "backed upto the wall with our creditors lined up in front waiting for the signal to fired." He secured financing from a Boston Millionaire and saved his company. THUS, AN ENTREPRENEUR MAY FACE EQUALLY INTENSE FINANCIAL CHALLENGES MANY TIMES.

Financial plan is a projection of key financial data about:
a) Potential investment commitment needed.
b) Economic feasibility of the enterprise.

To simplify, the financial plan clarifies for the entrepreneur and investors:
a) How much funds are required?
b) Where will funds come from?
c) How are they disbursed?
d) Amount of cash available.
e) General financial well-being (probable revenue forecast).

Generally, three years of projected financial data are needed to satisfy outside investors. HENRY FORD went bankrupt twice before the FORD MOTOR COMPANY succeeded.

## Components of financial plan

Major financial items include:
a) Proforma investment decisions
b) Proforma financing decisions
c) Proforma income statements
d) Proforma cash flow
e) Proforma balance sheet
f) Break-even analysis
g) Economic and social variables

### A) Proforma investment decisions

Relates to how funds are invested in assets for highest possible returns. Estimate of capital components (fixed assets, working capital) should be mentioned:
i) Land and building
ii) Machinery and plant
iii) Installation cost
iv) Preliminary expenses
v) Margin for working capital
vi) Expenses on research and development
vii) Investment in short-term assets (raw material, cash level)

This part helps understand total finance required. Inadequate or excess funds can severely damage financial fortune, so these decisions require utmost care.

### B) Proforma financing decisions

Summarizes projected sources of funds (owner's funds, borrowed funds). Entrepreneur selects the best mix to minimize capital cost and financial risk, and maximize return on investment and profitability.

### C) Proforma income statement

Projected net profit (projected revenue minus projected costs/expenses). Summarizes profit data for the first year. 'Sales by month' must be calculated first, using forecasting techniques like:
1) Marketing research
2) Industry sales
3) Survey of buyers' intentions
4) Expert opinions
5) Financial data on similar start-ups
6) Trial experience of self or others.

When calculating projected sales and expenses, being conservative for initial planning lends credibility.

### D) Proforma cash flow

Reflects projected cash available (projected cash receipts minus projected cash disbursements). Cash flows when actual payments are received or made, not just from sales (which might be credit) or incurring bills (not immediately paid). Many entrepreneurs use "Cash in LESS cash out" for internal monitoring. Projecting cash flows is difficult; a conservative approach with assumptions helps maintain funds for negative cash months.

### E) Proforma balance sheet

Reflects business position at the end of the first year. Summarizes projected assets, liabilities, and net worth.

### F) Break-even point

Level of production volume where the firm neither profits nor loses (total revenue equals total cost). Calculating BEP is useful for assessing:
1) Minimum output level.
2) Effect of output change on profits.
3) Selling price.
4) Profitable options in production line.

Break-even analysis helps determine units/sales volume needed to cover total variable and fixed expenses.

### G) Economic and social variables

In view of social responsibility, abatement costs (cost of controlling environmental damage) should be stated. Socio-economic benefits expected from investment should be mentioned:
1) Employment generation
2) Import substitution
3) Ancillarisation
4) Export promotion
5) Local resource utilization
6) Development of the area

If quantification isn't possible, analyze and emphasize importance.

## VII. Manpower planning

Every organisation exists when people join hands to achieve goals set by the entrepreneur. Human resource is paramount for success. "The people working in a firm make it what it is." - Hicks and Gullet. Organization's performance/productivity is proportional to quantity/quality of manpower. Ensuring loyal, efficient personnel requires proper human resource planning.

Manpower planning ensures the right number and kind of people with appropriate skills, at the right place and time, for economically suitable work.

Manpower planning helps assess:
1) What kind of people are required?
2) How many people are required?
3) How will they be selected?

### 1) What kind of people are required?

Organisation needs personnel with necessary qualifications, skills, knowledge, experience, and aptitude. Entrepreneur must state: (a) kind/type of person to hire, (b) as positions have different duties/responsibilities, clearly work out range from managers to unskilled labour, (c) nature of business helps decide type of manpower.

### 2) How many people are required?

Deals with quantity. Number needed for various positions is affected by:
a) Total work to be done.
b) Work an average person does in a period.
c) Expected absenteeism level.
d) Rate of labour turnover.
e) Present number of employees.
f) Future expansion/diversification plans.

### 3) How to procure personnel?

Entrepreneur mentions strategies, methods, policies, rules for:
*   Recruitment
*   Selection
*   Training.

Objective: "right person, at right job, at right time".
