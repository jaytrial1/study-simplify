This section details two additional forms of business organization prevalent in India: the Joint Hindu Family (HUF) and Co-operative Societies, outlining their unique characteristics, formation, and suitability.

# Other Forms: Joint Hindu Family and Co-operative

Though the most commonly opted out forms of business had already been discussed, yet there are two more forms prevalent in India, to start venture. They are:

## Joint Hindu Family / Firm (HUFs)

Joint Hindu family or Hindu Undivided Family Business is a unique form of business organisation prevailing only in India. It is governed by Hindu law and represents a form which is owned, managed and controlled by the male members of a joint Hindu family.

### Meaning of HUFs

HUFs are defined under Hindu law "as a family, which consists of male lineally descended from a common ancestor and included their wives and unmarried daughters." The relation arises from status, not legal contracts. Creating HUFs can be a way to save taxes.

### Schools of law under HUF

Two schools of law for creating a HUF:
a) **Dayabhaga:** Prevalent in West Bengal and Assam. Son acquires right in family property only after father's death.
b) **Mitaakshara:** Prevalent in rest of India. Son acquires right in family property from birth.

Two conditions for existence of HUFs:
1) Minimum two members in the family.
2) Existence of some ancestral property.

## Characteristics of Hindu undivided family

Main features:
1) **Creation:** Arises by status or Hindu Law operation.
2) **Membership:** Male member becomes member by birth. Adopted outsider can be admitted but not otherwise.
3) **Management:** Senior most male member ('Karta') manages affairs, having unlimited powers. Other male members ('Coparceners') cannot deal with outsiders or inspect accounts.
4) **Liability:** Karta's liability is unlimited; coparceners' liability is limited to their share in joint property. Self-acquired property of any member cannot be taken for business liabilities.
5) **Right of Accounts:** Members other than Karta cannot inspect/copy account books.
6) **Minor as member:** Male becomes member from birth.
7) **Dissolution:** Continues forever as death/birth of members doesn't dissolve it. Can be dissolved if all members mutually agree.
8) **Implied Authority:** Only the Karta has implied authority to bind the HUF by acts in ordinary course of business, hence his unlimited liability.

## Legal formalities involved

### Steps involved in creation of HUFs

To operate as an HUF, comply with:
1) **Capital and members:** Major requirements are capital (ancestral property, gifts, will) and persons (minimum two).
2) **Select a suitable name:** Ensure name doesn't violate laws or have negative impact.
3) **Form a Deed:** Creation should be embodied in a legal deed/agreement, disclosing Karta's name, coparceners, address, and source of funds.
4) **Apply for PAN:** Mandatory Permanent Account Number for the HUF, obtained by the Karta after executing the deed. All financial transactions require PAN.
5) **Open a Bank Account:** Karta opens a bank account in the HUF's name. Stationery for official communication is advisable. Only money related to HUF business is invested in this account. Karta invests in tax-saving instruments and files tax returns for the HUF.

## Some Interesting Information

1.  A daughter ceases to be a member of her father's HUF after marriage, becoming a member of her husband's HUF.
2.  Adopted child can become a member but not a coparcener.
3.  The HUF continues to exist in the hands of female members after the death of the male member.
4.  A widow cannot be the Karta as she is not a coparcener.
5.  HUF is a separate entity for income tax purposes under Section 2(31) of the Income Tax Act.

## Co-operative Organisations

A co-operative is a different form of business enterprise where the main motive is mutual help, not profit. It works on the principle of "each for all and all for each."

*   H.C. Calvert: "Co-operation is a form of organisation wherein persons voluntarily associate together as human beings on the basis of equality for the promotion of the economic interest of themselves."
*   Indian Co-operative Societies Act, 1912 (Section 4): "Society which has its objectives as the promotion of economic interests of its members in accordance with co-operative principle."

## Features

Co-operative is a self-help and mutual help system:
1.  **Voluntary organisation:** Voluntary association of persons with a common objective. Members can join and leave freely.
2.  **Democratic management:** Management is vested in a managing committee elected by members based on 'one member-one vote'.
3.  **Service motive:** Primary objective is rendering services to members, not earning profit.
4.  **Capital and return thereon:** Capital from members' share capital (max 10% of total or Rs. 1,000, whichever is higher). Shares cannot be transferred but surrendered. Dividend rate restricted to 9% as per Act.
5.  **Government control:** Regulated by Co-operative Societies Act and State Acts. Required to submit annual report and accounts to Registrar.
6.  **Distribution of surplus:** After dividends, surplus profit is distributed among members based on goods purchased from the society.

An entrepreneur does not find this form very desirable for a new venture due to their quest for excellence, drive for independence, project champion tendency, strong desire to succeed and earn profit, creative/innovative nature, "Must be own boss" attitude, love for being a 'Leader', and zeal to capture market share, which align less with the mutual help motive.

## Amazing Similarities!

*   Ray Kroc, founder of McDonald's, was selling milkshake machines when inspired to turn the McDonald brothers' restaurant into an international operation.
*   William C. Durant, founder of General Motors, began as a buggy salesman.
*   King C. Gillette was a traveling salesman when he invented the safety razor.

No form is without merits and limitations. No form is the best form. Selection is difficult, affected by:
1) Capital requirement
2) Risk and liability
3) Managerial control desired
4) Scale of operations
5) Continuity and stability demanded
6) Government regulations
7) Tax burden
8) Nature of business
9) Confidentiality required
10) Flexibility desirable.

No single factor decides the form; it's a summative effect of factors.
