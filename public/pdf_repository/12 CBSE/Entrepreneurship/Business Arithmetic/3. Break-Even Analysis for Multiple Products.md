This section explains how to calculate the break-even point for businesses selling multiple products or services, using the concept of weighted average contribution margin.

# Break-Even Analysis (Multiple Products or Services)

**Break-even point** (BEP) is the sales level where total revenue equals total expenses, resulting in neither profit nor loss.

For a single product:
Total Revenue = Total Expenses
Quantity × Unit Price = (Quantity × Unit Cost) + Fixed Expenses
Quantity × (Unit Price - Unit Cost) = Fixed Expenses
Quantity × Gross Margin per Unit = Fixed Expenses

**BEP Quantity (Single Product)** = Fixed Expenses / (Selling Price per unit - Variable Cost per unit)

For multiple products, the concept is similar, but we use weighted averages:
**BEP Quantity (Multiple Products)** = Fixed Expenses / (Weighted average selling price per unit - Weighted Average variable cost per unit)
*Note: Gross margin and gross profit are the same.*

Break-even analysis is useful for setting profit goals and sales targets. In manufacturing, it helps identify products that aren't contributing enough to cover fixed expenses, aiding decisions about their future.

## Sales Mix Break-Even Point Calculation

**Sales mix** is the proportion in which different products are sold. To calculate BEP for a sales mix, certain assumptions are made:

*   The proportion of the sales mix is predetermined and constant within the relevant period.
*   All costs are categorized as variable or fixed.
*   Sales price per unit, variable cost per unit, and total fixed costs are constant.
*   All units produced are sold.

The calculation uses the contribution approach, overcoming varying contribution margins by calculating a **weighted average contribution margin** per unit or ratio.

## Example: Formulas and Calculation Procedure

Given the following information for products A, B, and C:

| Product             | A        | B        | C        |
| :------------------ | :------- | :------- | :------- |
| Sales price per unit | ₹ 15/-   | ₹ 21/-   | ₹ 36/-   |
| Variable cost per unit | ₹ 9/-    | ₹ 14/-   | ₹ 19/-   |
| Sales mix percentage | 20%      | 20%      | 60%      |
| Total fixed cost    | ₹ 40,000/- |          |          |

Calculate the break-even point in units and in rupees.

### Calculation:

**Step 1: Calculate the contribution margin per unit for each product:**

| Product                  | A      | B      | C      |
| :----------------------- | :----- | :----- | :----- |
| Sales price per unit     | ₹ 15/- | ₹ 21/- | ₹ 36/- |
| - Variable cost per unit | ₹ 9/-  | ₹ 14/- | ₹ 19/- |
| **Contribution margin per unit** | **₹ 6/-** | **₹ 7/-** | **₹ 17/-** |

**Step 2: Calculate the weighted-average contribution margin per unit:**

Weighted average unit contribution margin = (Product A CM per Unit × Product A sales mix percentage) + (Product B CM per unit × Product B sales mix percentage) + (Product C CM per unit × Product C sales mix percentage)

| Product                      | A      | B      | C      |
| :--------------------------- | :----- | :----- | :----- |
| Contribution margin per unit | ₹ 6/-  | ₹ 7/-  | ₹ 17/- |
| × Sales mix percentage       | 20%    | 20%    | 60%    |
| **Weighted CM per unit**     | **₹ 1.2/-** | **₹ 1.4/-** | **₹ 10.2/-** |

Sum: Weighted average CM per unit = ₹ 1.2 + ₹ 1.4 + ₹ 10.2 = ₹ 12.80/-

**Step 3: Calculate total units of sales mix required to break-even:**

Break-even point in units of sales mix = Total fixed cost / Weighted average CM per unit
Break-even point in units = ₹ 40,000 / ₹ 12.80 = 3,125 units

**Step 4: Calculate number units of product A, B and C at break-even point:**

| Product                         | A     | B     | C     |
| :------------------------------ | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Sales mix ratio                 | 20%   | 20%   | 60%   |
| × Total break-even units        | 3,125 | 3,125 | 3,125 |
| **Product units at break-even** | **625** | **625** | **1,875** |

**Step 5: Calculate break-even point in rupees:**

| Product                       | A       | B        | C         |
| :---------------------------- | :------ | :------- | :-------- |
| Product units at break-even   | 625     | 625      | 1,875     |
| × Price per unit              | ₹ 15/-  | ₹ 21/-   | ₹ 36/-    |
| **Product sales in rupees**   | **₹ 9,375/-** | **₹ 13,125/-** | **₹ 67,500/-** |

Sum: Break-even point in rupees = ₹ 9,375 + ₹ 13,125 + ₹ 67,500 = ₹ 90,000/-

BE analysis aids product-related decisions. The calculation shows how weighted average sales price and variable cost are used to find the break-even point.

## Multiple-Product Break-Even Analysis

Toy Craft produces toy alligators and toy dolphins. Fixed costs are ₹ 12,90,000 per year. Sales revenue and variable costs per unit are:

|                | Alligators | Dolphins |
| :------------- | :--------- | :------- |
| Sales Price    | ₹ 20/-     | ₹ 25/-   |
| Variable Costs | ₹ 8/-      | ₹ 10/-   |

### Questions:

A. Suppose the company currently sells 140,000 alligators per year and 60,000 dolphins per year (Sales Mix Percentage 14:6). Assuming the sales mix stays constant how many alligators and Dolphins must the company sell to break even?
B. Suppose the company currently sells 60,000 alligators per year and 140,000 dolphins per year (Sales mix percentage 6:14). Assuming the sales mix stays constant, how many alligators and dolphins must the company sell to break even per year?
C. Explain why the total number of toys needed to break even in (a) is the same as or different from the number in (b).

> **Answer:**
> **A. Sales Mix Percentage 14:6 (or 7:3)**
> Total current units = 140,000 + 60,000 = 200,000
>
> Contribution Margin per unit:
> Alligators: ₹ 20 - ₹ 8 = ₹ 12
> Dolphins: ₹ 25 - ₹ 10 = ₹ 15
>
> Weighted Average Contribution Margin per unit based on 14:6 mix:
> (₹ 12 * 14/20) + (₹ 15 * 6/20)
> = (₹ 12 * 0.7) + (₹ 15 * 0.3)
> = ₹ 8.40 + ₹ 4.50 = ₹ 12.90
>
> Break-even Point in units = Total Fixed Costs / Weighted Average Contribution Margin per unit
> BEP units = ₹ 12,90,000 / ₹ 12.90 = 100,000 units
>
> Allocating BEP units based on the 14:6 mix:
> Alligators: 100,000 * (14/20) = 100,000 * 0.7 = 70,000 units
> Dolphins: 100,000 * (6/20) = 100,000 * 0.3 = 30,000 units
>
> Toy Crafts must sell **70,000 alligators** and **30,000 dolphins** to break even under this sales mix.
>
> **B. Sales Mix Percentage 6:14 (or 3:7)**
> Total current units = 60,000 + 140,000 = 200,000
>
> Contribution Margin per unit (same as above):
> Alligators: ₹ 12
> Dolphins: ₹ 15
>
> Weighted Average Contribution Margin per unit based on 6:14 mix:
> (₹ 12 * 6/20) + (₹ 15 * 14/20)
> = (₹ 12 * 0.3) + (₹ 15 * 0.7)
> = ₹ 3.60 + ₹ 10.50 = ₹ 14.10
>
> Break-even Point in units = Total Fixed Costs / Weighted Average Contribution Margin per unit
> BEP units = ₹ 12,90,000 / ₹ 14.10 ≈ 91,489.36 units. Rounding up to the nearest whole unit for practical purposes: 91,490 units.
>
> Allocating BEP units based on the 6:14 mix:
> Alligators: 91,490 * (6/20) ≈ 91,490 * 0.3 = 27,447 units (rounding up)
> Dolphins: 91,490 * (14/20) ≈ 91,490 * 0.7 = 64,043 units (rounding up)
> *Note: Total units 27,447 + 64,043 = 91,490*
>
> Toy Crafts must sell approximately **27,447 alligators** and **64,043 dolphins** to break even under this sales mix.
>
> **C. Explanation for different BEP units:**
> The total number of units needed to break even is different in (a) and (b). The break-even point in units is lower in scenario (b) (91,490 units) compared to scenario (a) (100,000 units). This is because the sales mix in scenario (b) includes a higher proportion of dolphins (70% vs 30% in scenario a). Dolphins have a higher contribution margin per unit (₹ 15) than alligators (₹ 12). A sales mix that includes more products with higher contribution margins results in a higher weighted average contribution margin. With a higher weighted average contribution margin, fewer total units need to be sold to cover the fixed costs, thus lowering the break-even point in units.

## More Examples for Practice

Consider a manufacturing plant producing four types of machine tools (A, B, C, D) with allocated fixed costs.

|                       | A         | B         | C           | D           |
| :-------------------- | :-------- | :-------- | :---------- | :---------- |
| Unit Selling Price    | ₹ 100,000 | ₹ 50,000  | ₹ 70,000    | ₹ 200,000   |
| Unit Variable Cost    | ₹ 30,000  | ₹ 25,000  | ₹ 30,000    | ₹ 100,000   |
| Gross Profit per Unit | ₹ 70,000  | ₹ 25,000  | ₹ 40,000    | ₹ 100,000   |
| Fixed Exp Per Month   | ₹ 350,000 | ₹ 250,000 | ₹ 1,000,000 | ₹ 1,500,000 |
| **BE Qty per Month**  | **5**     | **10**    | **25**      | **15**      |

*Calculation of BE Qty per Month:* Fixed Expenses / Gross Profit per Unit
*   A: ₹ 350,000 / ₹ 70,000 = 5
*   B: ₹ 250,000 / ₹ 25,000 = 10
*   C: ₹ 1,000,000 / ₹ 40,000 = 25
*   D: ₹ 1,500,000 / ₹ 100,000 = 15

Each product has its own BE level. If a product consistently fails to reach its BE level, it warrants management discussion regarding increasing sales, reducing costs, or potentially dropping the product. BE analysis supports these product-level decisions.

## Exercise for Practice

### Illustration 1: Textile Shop

Calculate the break-even point for a textile shop selling garments: silk saris, cotton saris, suit materials, kids wear.

| Garment        | Selling Price (₹/unit) | Variable Cost (₹/unit) | Allocated Fixed Expenses (₹) |
| :------------- | :--------------------- | :--------------------- | :--------------------------- |
| Silk saris     | 3,500                  | 1,050                  | 2,45,000                     |
| Cotton saris   | 900                    | 270                    | 15,7500                      |
| Suit materials | 1,800                  | 540                    | 63,000                       |
| Kids wear      | 450                    | 135                    | 70,875                       |

> **Answer:**
> First, calculate the Gross Profit per Unit for each garment:
> *   Silk saris: ₹ 3,500 - ₹ 1,050 = ₹ 2,450
> *   Cotton saris: ₹ 900 - ₹ 270 = ₹ 630
> *   Suit materials: ₹ 1,800 - ₹ 540 = ₹ 1,260
> *   Kids wear: ₹ 450 - ₹ 135 = ₹ 315
>
> Now, calculate the Break-Even Quantity for each garment using the formula: BE Qty = Allocated Fixed Expenses / Gross Profit per Unit
> *   Silk saris: ₹ 2,45,000 / ₹ 2,450 = 100 saris
> *   Cotton saris: ₹ 15,7500 / ₹ 630 = 250 saris
> *   Suit materials: ₹ 63,000 / ₹ 1,260 = 50 suits
> *   Kids wear: ₹ 70,875 / ₹ 315 = 225 wears
>
> The break-even points for the textile shop are:
> *   Silk saris: **100 saris**
> *   Cotton saris: **250 saris**
> *   Suit materials: **50 suits**
> *   Kids wear: **225 wears**
