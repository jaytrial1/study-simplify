This section explains how to determine the unit of sale, unit price, and unit cost in businesses that offer multiple products or services, often using averages.

# Unit of Sale, Unit Price and Unit Cost (Multiple Products or Services)

Understanding the **unit of sale** is essential for analyzing business economics in a standardized way, tracking progress, comparing performance, and taking corrective actions. It serves as a management tool.

In businesses selling a single item (like milk packets or mangoes), the unit of sale is simple, perhaps a quantity (one, a dozen) or weight (kilograms). This unit is used for pricing, calculating cost per unit, gross profit per unit, tracking sales trends, and setting sales targets.

However, most businesses sell many items (e.g., grocery stores, restaurants, beauty parlors). Even with hundreds or thousands of items, business owners need quick ways to check progress. The solution lies in defining the unit of sale, unit price, and unit cost in a meaningful way, often using averages.

Consider a grocery store. Each customer's bill is different. To understand overall performance, the business can use averages.

| Number of Customers | Per Customer Billed Amount (₹) | Total Billed Amount (₹) |
| :------------------ | :----------------------------- | :---------------------- |
| 5                   | 500                            | 2,500                   |
| 10                  | 700                            | 7,000                   |
| 15                  | 1,000                          | 15,000                  |
| 10                  | 1,450                          | 14,500                  |
| 25                  | 2,000                          | 50,000                  |
| 5                   | 2,500                          | 12,500                  |
| 10                  | 3,100                          | 31,000                  |
| 15                  | 3,500                          | 52,500                  |
| 5                   | 5,000                          | 25,000                  |
| **Total**           | **Weighted Average**           | **Total**               |
| **100**             | **2,100**                      | **2,10,000**            |

In this example, 100 customers resulted in total sales of ₹ 2,10,000, averaging ₹ 2,100 per customer. Here, the **"Customer"** is the **"unit of sale"**, and the average purchase amount per customer (Total billed amount / Total number of customers) is the **"Unit Price"**. In this case, the Unit Price is ₹ 2,100.

For a restaurant, the **"Diner"** is the **"unit of sale"**, and the average amount billed per diner is the **"Unit Price"**. Similarly, for a beauty parlor, the **"Customer"** is the **"Unit of Sale"**, and the average billing per customer is the **"Unit Price"**.

## Calculating Unit Cost in Multi-Product Businesses

**Unit cost** refers to **variable cost** (also known as cost of goods sold). For a grocery store (a trading business), the purchase price of sold items is known. If the purchase price of all items sold for ₹ 2,10,000 was ₹ 1,70,000, and there were 100 units of sale (customers), the unit cost is ₹ 1,700 (₹ 1,70,000 / 100).

Alternatively, if the purchase price is a known percentage of the selling price (e.g., 80%), the unit cost would be 80% of the unit price (80% of ₹ 2,100 = ₹ 1,680).

**Gross Profit** per unit of sale is the Unit Price minus the Unit Cost.
*   Case 1: ₹ 2,100 - ₹ 1,700 = ₹ 400
*   Case 2: ₹ 2,100 - ₹ 1,680 = ₹ 420

The relationship between Unit Price and Unit Cost often follows industry-specific thumb rules (e.g., FMCG, food industry, electronics). While thumb rules can be helpful, computing actual costs is more accurate.

## Some Formulas

1.  **Unit Price per customer** = Total Billed Amount / Number of Customers
2.  **Unit Cost per product** = Total Sale / Number of Unit Sold (Note: This formula seems misaligned with the multi-product context discussed. A more relevant formula for multi-product unit cost average would be Total Cost of Goods Sold / Total Units of Sale)
3.  **Gross Profit** = Selling price per unit - cost price per unit (Note: In the multi-product context, this would be Unit Price - Unit Cost)

## An Exercise

A stationery store sold the following items in one day:

| Products                 | Price per unit (₹) | Quantity sold |
| :----------------------- | :----------------- | :------------ |
| Student note book        | 40                 | 35            |
| Reynolds pens            | 40                 | 40            |
| Erasers                  | 5                  | 5             |
| Scale (12" plastic)      | 15                 | 10            |
| Flip chart               | 10                 | 5             |
| Sketch pens (one DOZEN)  | 25                 | 3             |

The shopkeeper issued 50 bills (customers). If customer is the unit of sale, what is the "Unit Price"? If the cost of each stationery item is 75% of its selling price, calculate the "Unit Cost" and the "Gross Margin" per unit of sale.

> **Answer:**
> First, calculate the total sales revenue:
> (40 * 35) + (40 * 40) + (5 * 5) + (15 * 10) + (10 * 5) + (25 * 3)
> = 1400 + 1600 + 25 + 150 + 50 + 75
> = ₹ 3300
>
> The number of customers (units of sale) is 50.
>
> **Unit Price** = Total Sales Revenue / Number of Customers
> Unit Price = ₹ 3300 / 50 = ₹ 66
>
> The cost of each item is 75% of its selling price. Therefore, the total cost of goods sold is 75% of the total sales revenue.
> Total Cost of Goods Sold = 75% of ₹ 3300 = 0.75 * 3300 = ₹ 2475
>
> **Unit Cost** = Total Cost of Goods Sold / Number of Customers
> Unit Cost = ₹ 2475 / 50 = ₹ 49.50
>
> **Gross Margin (Gross Profit) per Unit of Sale** = Unit Price - Unit Cost
> Gross Margin = ₹ 66 - ₹ 49.50 = ₹ 16.50

## Exercises for Practice

### Illustration 1: Beauty Parlor

A beauty parlor had varying numbers of customers and total weekly billing over 5 weeks:

| Week # | No. of Customers | Total Amount Billed (₹) | Average Amount Billed (₹) per Customer |
| :----- | :--------------- | :---------------------- | :------------------------------------- |
| Week 1 | 10               | 1,000                   | 100                                    |
| Week 2 | 17               | 1,445                   | 85                                     |
| Week 3 | 13               | 923                     | 71                                     |
| Week 4 | 22               | 5,082                   | 231                                    |
| Week 5 | 18               | 3,150                   | 175                                    |
| **Total** | **80**          | **11,600**              |                                        |

What are the "Unit of sale" and the "Unit Price" in this case? If the cost of goods sold or variable cost is 60% of the sale price, calculate the "Unit Cost" and the "Gross Margin" per Unit of Sale.

> **Answer:**
> The **Unit of Sale** in this case is the **Customer**.
>
> The total number of customers over 5 weeks is 80.
> The total amount billed over 5 weeks is ₹ 11,600.
>
> **Unit Price** = Total Amount Billed / Total Number of Customers
> Unit Price = ₹ 11,600 / 80 = ₹ 145
>
> The variable cost is 60% of the sale price.
> **Unit Cost** = 60% of Unit Price
> Unit Cost = 0.60 * ₹ 145 = ₹ 87
>
> **Gross Margin per Unit of Sale** = Unit Price - Unit Cost
> Gross Margin = ₹ 145 - ₹ 87 = ₹ 58

### Illustration 2: Restaurant

Number of people taking meals and total billing for each of 5 weeks:

| Weeks | No. of People Taking Meals | Total Amount Billed (₹) | Average Amount Billed (₹) |
| :---- | :------------------------- | :---------------------- | :------------------------ |
| Week 1 | 120                        | 18,000                  | 150                       |
| Week 2 | 60                         | 12,300                  | 205                       |
| Week 3 | 70                         | 10,220                  | 146                       |
| Week 4 | 80                         | 17,680                  | 221                       |
| Week 5 | 90                         | 21,600                  | 240                       |
| **Total** | **420**                   | **79,800**              |                           |

What are the "Unit of sale" and the "Unit Price" in this case? If the variable cost is 50% of the sale price, calculate the "Unit Cost" and the "Gross Margin" per Unit of Sale.

> **Answer:**
> The **Unit of Sale** in this case is the **Person Taking Meals (Diner)**.
>
> The total number of people taking meals over 5 weeks is 420.
> The total amount billed over 5 weeks is ₹ 79,800.
>
> **Unit Price** = Total Amount Billed / Total Number of People Taking Meals
> Unit Price = ₹ 79,800 / 420 = ₹ 190
>
> The variable cost is 50% of the sale price.
> **Unit Cost** = 50% of Unit Price
> Unit Cost = 0.50 * ₹ 190 = ₹ 95
>
> **Gross Margin per Unit of Sale** = Unit Price - Unit Cost
> Gross Margin = ₹ 190 - ₹ 95 = ₹ 95
