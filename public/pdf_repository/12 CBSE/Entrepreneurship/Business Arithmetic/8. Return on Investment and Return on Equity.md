This section defines and explains key profitability measures: Return on Investment (ROI) and Return on Equity (ROE), and introduces EBITDA as a measure of operational efficiency.

# Return on Investment and Return on Equity

## Measure of Profitability

Profit is the goal of economic activity. Comparing profitability between different activities requires common yardsticks. **Return on Investment (ROI)** and **Return on Equity (ROE)** are critical profitability ratios used for individual projects or entire businesses.

# Return on Investment (ROI)

**Return on Investment (ROI)** measures the profitability relative to the total capital invested.
**ROI** = (Net Profit / Total Capital Invested) × 100

Example: Investing ₹ 100,000 in a grocery shop yields ₹ 20,000 net profit in one year.
ROI = (₹ 20,000 / ₹ 100,000) × 100 = 20%
Total Capital Invested includes both owner's money (equity) and borrowed funds (loan). If the owner invested ₹ 40,000 (equity) and borrowed ₹ 60,000 (loan), the total investment is ₹ 100,000.

## Return on Equity (ROE)

**Return on Equity (ROE)** measures profitability relative to the owner's equity investment.
**ROE** = (Net Income / Equity) × 100

Using the grocery shop example: Owner's equity = ₹ 40,000. Loan = ₹ 60,000 at 10% interest. Annual interest = ₹ 6,000. Net profit before interest = ₹ 20,000. Net income after interest = ₹ 20,000 - ₹ 6,000 = ₹ 14,000.
ROE = (₹ 14,000 / ₹ 40,000) × 100 = 35%

*   ROE of 35% means for every Rupee of own money invested, the owner made 35 paisa net income.
*   ROI of 20% means for every Rupee of total capital (equity + loan) invested, the business yielded 20 paisa average profit.

## Which one to use? ROI or ROE:

*   **ROE** is a good indicator of how effectively "own money" is used.
*   **ROI** indicates how effectively the "total money" (equity + loan) is used.

# EBITDA

**EBITDA** stands for **Earnings Before Interest, Taxes, Depreciation and Amortization**. It's calculated to assess operational efficiency by removing factors influenced by financing decisions (Interest), government policies (Taxes), and accounting practices (Depreciation & Amortization).

*   **Interest:** Adding back interest removes the effect of funding decisions (using debt vs. equity).
*   **Taxes:** Adding back taxes removes the effect of varying tax rates or treatments beyond management control.
*   **Depreciation & Amortization:** Adding back D&A removes distortions caused by different accounting methods for writing off assets.

EBITDA measures a business's ability to generate cash from operations and is widely used for evaluating businesses (e.g., for acquisition). Business value is often quoted as a multiple of EBITDA, reflecting the expected future cash flows.

*Note: Valuation using EBITDA as a multiple assumes future cash flows. Concepts like Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) or Internal Rate of Return (IRR) are more advanced valuation techniques.*

## Illustration 1: Beauty Parlor

Calculate return on investment and return on equity.
Investment = ₹ 1,50,000. Equity = ₹ 70,000. Loan = ₹ 80,000. Interest rate = 7% per annum.
Monthly sales revenue = ₹ 80,000. Monthly cost of goods sold = ₹ 30,000.
Monthly fixed expenses: salary ₹ 20,000, rent/utility ₹ 10,000. Total fixed expenses = ₹ 30,000.
Monthly depreciation = ₹ 3,000. Tax rate = 14%.

> **Answer:**
> First, calculate annual figures:
> Annual Sales Revenue = ₹ 80,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 9,60,000
> Annual Cost of Goods Sold = ₹ 30,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 3,60,000
> Annual Fixed Expenses (excluding interest & depreciation) = ₹ 30,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 3,60,000
> Annual Depreciation = ₹ 3,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 36,000
> Annual Interest on loan = ₹ 80,000 * 7% = ₹ 5,600
> Total Capital Invested = ₹ 1,50,000
> Equity = ₹ 70,000
>
> Calculate Profit Before Tax (PBT):
> Sales Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold - Fixed Expenses - Depreciation - Interest
> ₹ 9,60,000 - ₹ 3,60,000 - ₹ 3,60,000 - ₹ 36,000 - ₹ 5,600 = ₹ 1,98,400
>
> Calculate Tax:
> Tax = 14% of PBT = 0.14 * ₹ 1,98,400 = ₹ 27,776
>
> Calculate Net Profit (Profit After Tax):
> PBT - Tax = ₹ 1,98,400 - ₹ 27,776 = ₹ 1,70,624
>
> **Return on Investment (ROI):**
> ROI = (Net Profit / Total Capital Invested) * 100
> ROI = (₹ 1,70,624 / ₹ 1,50,000) * 100 ≈ **113.75%**
>
> **Return on Equity (ROE):**
> ROE = (Net Profit / Equity) * 100
> ROE = (₹ 1,70,624 / ₹ 70,000) * 100 ≈ **243.75%**

## Illustration 2: Restaurant

Calculate return on investment and return on equity.
Investment = ₹ 10,00,000. Equity = ₹ 4,00,000. Loan = ₹ 6,00,000. Interest rate = 10% per annum.
Monthly sales revenue = ₹ 6,00,000. Monthly cost of goods sold = ₹ 3,00,000.
Monthly fixed expenses: salary ₹ 1,50,000, rent/utility ₹ 50,000. Total fixed expenses = ₹ 2,00,000.
Monthly depreciation = ₹ 10,000. Tax rate = 20%.

> **Answer:**
> First, calculate annual figures:
> Annual Sales Revenue = ₹ 6,00,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 72,00,000
> Annual Cost of Goods Sold = ₹ 3,00,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 36,00,000
> Annual Fixed Expenses (excluding interest & depreciation) = ₹ 2,00,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 24,00,000
> Annual Depreciation = ₹ 10,000/month * 12 months = ₹ 1,20,000
> Annual Interest on loan = ₹ 6,00,000 * 10% = ₹ 60,000
> Total Capital Invested = ₹ 10,00,000
> Equity = ₹ 4,00,000
>
> Calculate Profit Before Tax (PBT):
> Sales Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold - Fixed Expenses - Depreciation - Interest
> ₹ 72,00,000 - ₹ 36,00,000 - ₹ 24,00,000 - ₹ 1,20,000 - ₹ 60,000 = ₹ 10,20,000
>
> Calculate Tax:
> Tax = 20% of PBT = 0.20 * ₹ 10,20,000 = ₹ 2,04,000
>
> Calculate Net Profit (Profit After Tax):
> PBT - Tax = ₹ 10,20,000 - ₹ 2,04,000 = ₹ 8,16,000
>
> **Return on Investment (ROI):**
> ROI = (Net Profit / Total Capital Invested) * 100
> ROI = (₹ 8,16,000 / ₹ 10,00,000) * 100 = **81.6%**
>
> **Return on Equity (ROE):**
> ROE = (Net Profit / Equity) * 100
> ROE = (₹ 8,16,000 / ₹ 4,00,000) * 100 = **204%**
