# Franchising

Franchising began back in the 1850's when Isaac Singer invented the sewing machine. In order to distribute his machines outside of his geographical area, and also provide training to customers, Singer began selling licenses to entrepreneurs in different parts of the country. In 1955 Ray Kroc took over a small chain of food franchises and built it into today's most successful fast food franchise in the world, now known as McDonald's. McDonald's currently has the most franchise units worldwide of any franchise system.

Today, franchising is helping thousands of individuals be their own boss and own and operate their own business. Franchising allows entrepreneurs to be in business for themselves, but not by themselves. There is usually a much higher likelihood of success when an individual opens a franchise as opposed to a mom and pop business, since a proven business formula is in place. The products, services, and business operations have already been established.

Franchising opportunities have often evolved from changes in the environment as well as important social trends.

Franchising is defined as "an arrangement whereby the manufacturer or sole distributor of a trademarked product or service gives exclusive rights of local distribution to independent retailers in return for their payment of royalties and conformance to standardized operating procedures". The person offering the franchise is known as the **franchisor**. The **franchisee** is the person who purchases the franchise and is given the opportunity to enter a new business with a better chance to success than if he or she were to start a new business from scratch. The foundation of this relationship is the **Franchise Agreement**.

A franchise agreement is the legal document that binds the franchisor and franchisee together. This document explains what the franchisor expects from the franchisee in running the business. The agreement is designed to assure that all of the franchisees within an organization are treated equitably. The expectations must be uniform throughout the system.

## Main Ingredients of a Franchise Agreement

*   **Contract Explanation**: Outlines the type of relationship a franchisee is entering into with the franchisor.
*   **Operations Manual**: Details the guidelines the franchisee must legally follow in operating the business. Contents are confidential and subject to amendments.
*   **Proprietary Statements**: Outline how the franchise name is to be used, marketing and advertising procedures, and the franchisee's contribution to national advertising.
*   **Ongoing Site Maintenance**: Details types and timeframes for maintenance and upgrades required at the franchisee's location.

As in any legal document, it is recommended that you have your attorney review the franchise agreement before signing.

## Big Brands Head Towards Franchising

Big corporate houses opt for the franchise route as an easy mode of expansion, leveraging the commitment levels of both franchisor and franchisees. It's an ideal way for companies lacking capital, manpower, or time to build a network of company-owned outlets.

Examples of big brands using franchising:

*   **Raymond Ltd**: Used the franchise model for expanding in smaller towns, leveraging local entrepreneurs' knowledge.
*   **NIIT**: Built an extensive global franchising network for IT education, reaching remote areas and fostering entrepreneurship.
*   **JSW Steel Ltd**: Operates through JSW Shoppe outlets based on the franchise model for distribution.
*   **Mahindra & Mahindra Ltd**: Expanded its multibrand car-servicing subsidiary, Mahindra First Choice, through franchising.

## Types of Franchising

Before searching for a franchise opportunity, it's important to understand the four different types:

*   **Product Franchise Business Opportunity**: Manufacturer grants a retailer the right to distribute products using the manufacturer's name/trademark, in return for a fee or minimum inventory purchase. Example: Some tire stores.
*   **Manufacturing Franchise Opportunity**: Organization is given the right to manufacture a product using the franchisor's name/trademark and sell it. Common in food/beverage (e.g., soft drink bottlers).
*   **Business Franchise Opportunity Ventures**: Business owner purchases and distributes products for one specific company, which provides customers/accounts. Owner pays a fee or consideration. Examples: Vending machine routes, distributorships.
*   **Business Format Franchise Opportunity**: The most popular form. Company provides a proven method for operating a business using its name/trademark. Significant assistance is usually provided. Owner pays a fee/royalty and may be required to purchase supplies from the company.

## How Franchising Helps Start-ups

1.  **Established Brand Name**: Start-ups use an already recognized product name, saving time and money on establishing credibility. They pay royalty for this benefit.
2.  **Training and Knowledge**: Franchisors provide training to make franchisees fully conversant with the product/services. A fee is charged for this.
3.  **Faster Growth**: Start-ups can grow quickly without increasing labor, operating costs, or blocking running expenses, as customers often seek out the established brand.
4.  **Mutual Support**: Franchisor and franchisee work together for mutual benefit. Franchisors have a sincere interest in boosting their franchises' success.

## Advantages of Franchising

One of the most important advantages of buying a franchise is that the entrepreneur does not have to incur all the risks associated with creating a new business. Risks related to product acceptance, management expertise, capital requirements, market knowledge, and operating controls are minimized.

### Advantages to the Franchisee

1.  **Product Acceptance**: The franchisee enters a business with an accepted name, product, or service (e.g., Subway). Credibility and a favorable image already exist due to years of operation and advertising.
2.  **Management Expertise**: Franchisors provide managerial assistance, often including mandatory training programs covering operations, accounting, personnel, marketing, and production. On-the-job training may also be required. Ongoing support and advice are available.
3.  **Capital Requirements**: Franchises offer up-front support that can save time and capital. Franchisors may conduct location analysis, market research, and sometimes even finance the initial investment. Savings are also realized through layout design, inventory control, and collective buying power. Pooled advertising funds allow for local and national campaigns.
4.  **Knowledge of the Market**: Established franchises offer years of experience and market knowledge, providing a plan detailing target customers and strategies. They offer advice on regional differences and keep franchisees informed of new developments through publications.
5.  **Operating and Structural Controls**: Franchisors help maintain quality control by identifying approved suppliers (sometimes providing supplies directly) and establishing standardized operating procedures outlined in manuals. Administrative controls cover financial decisions (costs, inventory, cash flow) and personnel issues (hiring, scheduling, training) to ensure consistent service.

### Advantages of Franchising to the Franchisor

Advantages for the franchisor relate to expansion speed, reduced capital requirements, and cost advantages from collective buying power. Franchising allows rapid growth that would be difficult otherwise.

*   **Quick Expansion**: Allows the venture to expand nationally and internationally quickly with less capital than required for company-owned outlets.
*   **Lower Operating Costs**: Requires fewer employees at headquarters and regional offices, primarily needed to support franchisees, minimizing payroll and personnel issues.
*   **Cost Advantages**: Size of the franchise system allows purchasing supplies in large quantities, achieving economies of scale. Franchisors can produce items and sell them to franchisees, who are often required to purchase them, benefiting from lower prices.
*   **Advertising Pool**: Franchisees contribute a percentage of sales to a pool, allowing for large-scale advertising campaigns across wide geographic areas, which would be too costly for a single operation.

## Disadvantages of Franchising to the Franchisee

Despite advantages, disadvantages exist, often stemming from differences or unfulfilled commitments between franchisor and franchisee.

*   **Limited Freedom**: The franchise contract limits the franchisee's freedom, imposing a high degree of control and potentially restricting innovation.
*   **Continuing Cost Implications**: Beyond the initial fee and royalties, a percentage of revenue is perpetually shared. Additional charges may apply for services like advertising and training. Well-known franchisors can be expensive due to potential exploitation.
*   **Risk of Franchisor Failure or Acquisition**: Franchisees face problems if the franchisor fails or is bought out, potentially losing support services as experienced by Vicent Niagra with Window Works franchises.
*   **Inability to Provide Services**: Disadvantages centre on the franchisor failing to provide promised services like advertising or location analysis, leaving the franchisee unsupported (e.g., Curtis Bean with Checkers of America Inc.).

## Disadvantage to the Franchisor

*   **Difficulty in Identifying Quality Franchisees**: Finding suitable partners is challenging. Poor management by a franchisee can lead to failure, negatively impacting the entire franchise system.

## Case Study III: Indian Franchise Boom

For small and first-time entrepreneurs in India, taking up a franchise of a well-established brand is a good proposition. It offers an alternative to starting from scratch or a faster way to scale an existing business.

Examples of successful Indian franchises:

*   **Ferns N Petals**: A leading floral boutique brand that grew through franchising, offering flowers and related gift items.
*   **Haldiram Franchise**: A renowned sweets and namkeens brand with decades of history, using franchising for expansion in the food and beverage industry, both domestically and internationally.
*   **Hotel Saravana Bhavan**: The largest vegetarian restaurant chain globally, expanding through franchising, offering South Indian cuisine.
*   **Jumboking**: A pioneer in the Indian QSR segment, branding the Vada Pav and expanding rapidly through franchising with a goal of providing hygienic, affordable food.

Other examples taking India by storm include Chi Kitchen & Bar, Mahindra First Choice Wheels, Van Heusen, EduKart.com, Koutons Retail, The Yellow Chilli, and VLCC.
