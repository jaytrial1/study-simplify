# Moving Up the Value Chain

A **value chain** is the whole series of activities that create and build value at every step. The total value delivered by the company is the sum total of value built up throughout the company. Michael Porter developed this concept in his 1980 book 'Competitive Advantage'.

In simple words, a 'Value Chain' is a high-level model of how businesses receive raw materials, add value through processes, and sell finished products to customers. 'Value Chain' analysis looks at every step from raw materials to end users to deliver maximum value.

Michael Porter introduced the value chain analysis concept in his 1985 book 'The Competitive Advantage'. Porter suggested that activities within an organisation add value to the service and products produced, and all activities should run optimally for competitive advantage. If run efficiently, value obtained should exceed costs, leading customers to return. Porter split organization activities into 'primary activities' and 'support activities'.

## Primary Activities

These are the core activities involved in creating and delivering the product/service:

*   **Inbound Logistics**: Receiving, storing, and distributing inputs from suppliers.
*   **Operations**: Transforming raw materials/inputs into the final product. Value is added here.
*   **Outbound Logistics**: Distributing finished products to customers (distribution centers, wholesalers, retailers, or direct).
*   **Marketing and Sales**: Activities to promote and sell the product/service, targeting the correct customer group using the marketing mix.
*   **Services**: After-sales support provided to customers (training, guarantees, warranties).

Any or a combination of these activities are essential for a firm to develop the "competitive advantage" Porter discusses.

## Support Activities

These activities assist the primary activities in achieving competitive advantage:

*   **Procurement**: Sourcing raw materials and inputs at the best possible price and quality.
*   **Technological Development**: Using technology to gain competitive advantage in production (cost reduction), R&D (new products), and customer access (online platforms).
*   **Human Resource Management**: Recruiting, training, developing, motivating, and retaining employees. Crucial in service sectors where employees are the primary point of value delivery.
*   **Firm Infrastructure**: Ensuring efficient finance, legal structure, and management structure to support the organization and avoid issues.

## Six Requirements for Value Chain Management

Value chain managers constantly seek ways to improve processes. Key requirements include:

1.  **Coordination and Collaboration**: Essential for efficiency, avoiding duplicated efforts, and achieving common goals by working together.
2.  **Technology Investment**: Crucial for manufacturing and distribution efficiency; outdated technology weakens competitiveness.
3.  **Organizational Process**: Identifying every aspect of the process and improving it through better technology and procedural knowledge.
4.  **Leadership**: Strong leaders are crucial for success, earning respect through sound practices, conflict management, motivation, and direction.
5.  **Employee/Human Resources**: A central hub for information and support regarding benefits, policies, hiring, and conflict management. HR acts as a liaison and ensures employees have a voice.
6.  **Organizational Culture and Attitudes**: Fostering a strong cultural identity and positive attitudes attracts and retains top employees, boosting productivity.
