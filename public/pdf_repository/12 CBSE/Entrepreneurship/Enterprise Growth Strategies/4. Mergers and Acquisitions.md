# Growth Through Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)

Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A) is a potential strategy for ensuring the accelerated growth of a business. Firms choose M&A over internal expansion because it accelerates the growth process by acquiring an existing company with production capacity, distribution network, and clientele. This saves time and investment. M&A can also be less expensive than internal expansion, especially if the replacement cost of assets exceeds the target's market value.

## Mergers

A **merger** is a combination of two companies into one larger company. This involves stock swap or cash payment. The acquiring company takes over the assets and liabilities of the merged company. All combining companies are dissolved, and only the new entity continues to operate. When firms of similar size combine, it's often called **consolidation**. When sizes differ significantly, the term **merger** is used.

Mergers commonly take two forms:

1.  **Amalgamation**: Two entities combine to form an entirely new entity, dissolving both original entities (A + B = C).
2.  **Absorption**: One entity is absorbed into another; the latter retains its identity (A + B = A, where B is merged into A).

Usually, mergers are consensual, with executives from both sides agreeing after due diligence. Boards of directors agree and seek stockholder approval (often requiring at least 50% from both firms). The target firm ceases to exist.

*   Digital Computers was absorbed by Compaq (Absorption).
*   TOMCO Ltd. merged with HLL (Absorption).
*   Citi Group was created from the consolidation of Citicorp and Travelers Insurance Group (Amalgamation/Consolidation).

## Types of Mergers

The term describing a merger depends on the economic function, purpose, and relationship between the merging companies.

1.  **Conglomerate Merger**: Between firms in totally unrelated business activities.
    *   *Pure*: Firms with nothing in common.
    *   *Mixed*: Firms seeking product or market extensions.
    *   Example: A leading athletic shoe manufacturer merges with a soft drink firm (Walt Disney Company and American Broadcasting Company).
2.  **Horizontal Merger**: Between companies in the same industry, often competitors offering the same goods/services. Goal is usually increased market share and potential cost reduction through joined operations.
    *   Example: A merger between Coca-Cola and the Pepsi beverage division.
3.  **Market Extension Merger**: Between two companies dealing in the same products but in separate markets. Purpose is access to a bigger market and client base.
    *   Example: Acquisition of Eagle Bancshares Inc. by RBC Centura, allowing RBC access to the Atlanta financial market.
4.  **Product Extension Merger**: Between companies dealing in related products and operating in the same market. Allows grouping products for a bigger consumer base and higher profits.
    *   Example: Acquisition of Mobilink Telecom Inc. by Broadcom, combining related wireless technologies.
5.  **Vertical Merger**: Between two companies producing different goods/services for one specific finished product, operating at different stages of the supply chain. Logic is often increased synergies and efficiency.
    *   Example: An automobile company merging with a parts supplier.

Synergy, the idea that the combined value/performance is greater than the sum of parts, is a key reason for mergers.

## Acquisitions

**Acquisition** is a more general term, involving a company buying most, if not all, of a target company's ownership stakes to assume control. Acquisitions are often part of a growth strategy, being more beneficial than expanding alone. They are paid for in cash, stock, or a combination. An acquisition is also known as a **takeover**.

## Types of Acquisitions

There are four types of acquisitions:

1.  **Friendly Acquisition**: Both companies approve under cordial terms; no forceful takeover.
2.  **Reverse Acquisition**: A private company takes over a public company.
3.  **Back Flip Acquisition**: The purchasing company becomes a subsidiary of the purchased company (rare).
4.  **Hostile Acquisition**: Done by force, either by driving the target company into a state where it must accept or by buying a majority of its shares.
