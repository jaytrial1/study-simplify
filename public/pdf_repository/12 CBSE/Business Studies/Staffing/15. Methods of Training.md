This section describes the different approaches organizations use to impart training, categorized into methods conducted at the workplace and those conducted away from it.

# Methods of Training

The important methods of training are broadly categorised into two groups:

*   (i) On-the-Job Training Methods
*   (ii) Off-the-Job Training Methods

## On-the-Job Training Methods

On-the-Job training methods refer to the methods that are applied to the workplace, while the employee is actually working. Examples include **Induction Training, Apprenticeship Programmes, Internship Training**, etc.

These methods are more suitable for technical jobs as employees can learn the practical aspects while working on the job. However, in case of expensive and sophisticated machinery, these methods should be avoided.

## Off-the-Job Training Methods

Off-the-Job training methods refer to the methods in which training is provided to the employee away from his job. This method involves 'learning before doing'. The trainee is separated from the job and his attention is focused on learning the material related to his future job performance. This method is more suitable for managerial jobs as employees are trained by professionals in the conferences and seminars.

## Difference between On-the-Job and Off-the-Job Training Methods

| Basis           | On-the-Job Training Methods                                      | Off-the-Job Training Methods                                    |
| :-------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Meaning**     | Training is provided at the workplace.                           | Training is provided away from the job.                         |
| **Principle adopted** | Principle of learning while doing is adopted.                    | Principle of learning before doing is adopted.                  |
| **Place of training** | Employees are trained at their workplace.                        | Employees are trained away from their actual workplace.         |
| **Suitability** | More suitable for technical jobs.                                | Suitable for managerial jobs.                                   |
| **Trainers**    | Employees are trained by experienced workers and qualified instructors. | Employees are trained by academicians, professionals and technicians. |
| **Examples**    | Apprenticeship Programmes, Internship Training, Induction Training. | Vestibule Training, Lectures, Conferences, Seminars.            |

## On-the-Job Methods of Training

1.  **Induction Training (or Orientation Training)**

Induction training is a type of training given to help a new employee in settling down quickly into the job by becoming familiar with the people, the surroundings, the job and the business.

*   The duration of induction training may be from a few hours to a few days.
*   The induction provides a good opportunity to socialize and brief the new employee with the company's overall strategy and performance standards.
*   Such training may be imparted with the help of film shows, lectures and tours of various departments.
*   Induction training is very important for industrial workers, who are to work on sophisticated or technically advanced machines. It improves effectiveness and efficiency of the new employee, which helps in saving time and cost.

2.  **Apprenticeship Programmes**

Under this method, trainee (apprentice) works under the guidance of a master worker, who guides and helps him in learning the job.

*   These are designed to acquire a higher level of skill.
*   A uniform period of training is offered to trainees and slow learners may be given additional training.
*   The trainees spend a prescribed amount of time working with an experienced guide or trainer and are paid only nominal remuneration in the form of stipend during the apprenticeship period.
*   The trainee is usually absorbed in regular post on completion of his apprenticeship period.
*   This method is common in skilled trades such as electricians, plumbers, carpenters, etc.

3.  **Internship Training**

It is a joint programme of training between the educational institutions and business firms, wherein students of educational institutions are sent to business firms to gain practical work experience.

*   Selected candidates carry on regular studies for the prescribed period and they also work in some organisation to acquire practical knowledge and skills.
*   Students studying in educational institutions are sent to business organisations to gain practical knowledge and employees working in business organisations are sent to educational institutions to get latest theoretical knowledge. The basic objective is to achieve a balance between theoretical and practical knowledge.
*   For example, engineering students are sent to business enterprises and medical students are sent to hospitals to get practical knowledge.

## Off-the-Job Method of Training

## Vestibule Training

It is an off-the-job method of training in which training is imparted away from the actual work area, with the help of machines and equipments, which are identical to those at the work place.

*   Actual work environment is created in a class room, i.e. a duplicate model of workshop is prepared and instead of using original equipments, employees are trained on the dummy model.
*   This method is extremely useful when employees are required to handle expensive and sophisticated equipments.
