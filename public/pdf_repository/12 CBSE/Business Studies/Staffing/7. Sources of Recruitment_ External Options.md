This section explores the various ways organizations can find suitable candidates from outside their existing pool of employees.

## External Sources

External recruitment refers to searching for the employees from the sources outside the organisation. Every enterprise has to use external sources as all the vacancies cannot be filled from internal sources. External sources provide a wide choice of candidates with diverse qualifications.

Various sources of external recruitment are:

1.  **Direct Recruitment**

Under this method, a notice is placed on the notice-board of the enterprise specifying the details of the jobs available.

*   Job-seekers assemble outside the premises on the specified date and selection is done on the spot.
*   This method is suitable for unskilled or semi-skilled jobs.
*   The workers appointed under this method are known as casual or badli workers and are paid remuneration on daily wage basis.
*   It is an economical method as it does not involve cost of advertising the vacancies.
*   It is suitable for filling casual vacancies when there is a rush of work or when some permanent workers are absent.

2.  **Casual Callers**

Companies generally receive unsolicited applications from job seekers at various points of time. Companies keep a database of such applications as they can provide a valuable source of recruitment. When a suitable vacancy arises, such casual callers can be considered for employment. It is an economical method as there is no cost of advertising the vacancy.

3.  **Advertisement**

Under this method, the advertisement is given in newspapers, trade and professional journals along with full description about the job and its requirements like nature, qualifications, remuneration, etc.

*   It gives wider choice and most of the senior positions are filled by this method.
*   The advantage of advertising is that more information about the organisation and job can be given and management gets a wider range of candidates for selection. Advertisements may be placed in leading newspapers.
*   However, advertisement may provide flood of response, and many times, from quite unsuitable candidates.

4.  **Employment Exchange**

Under this method, employment exchanges run by government are used for filling unskilled and semiskilled jobs.

*   Unemployed persons get themselves registered with these exchanges.
*   Employment exchanges keep in touch with the employers. At the employer's request, they provide a list of candidates fulfilling the required qualifications.
*   In certain cases, it has been made obligatory for the business concerns to notify their vacancies to the employment exchange.
*   Thus, employment exchange serves as a link between job-seekers and employers.
*   However, at times, records of employment exchange are not up to date and many of the candidates referred by them may not be found suitable.

5.  **Placement Agencies and Management Consultants**

Under this method, services of placement agencies and management consultants (like ABC Consultants) are used, who undertake total functions of recruiting and selecting personnel on behalf of various organisations.

*   They provide a nationwide service in matching personnel demand and supply.
*   These agencies compile resume (or curriculum vitae) of large number of candidates and recommend suitable names to the employers on demand.
*   They charge fees for this purpose and are useful where extensive screening is required.
*   They help the organisations to recruit technical, professional and managerial personnel. They specialise in middle level and top level executive placements.
*   These professional recruiters can also entice (i.e. attract) the needed top executives from other companies by making the right offers.
*   They maintain data bank of persons with different qualifications and skills and even advertise the jobs on behalf of their clients to recruit right type of personnel.

6.  **Campus Recruitment**

In this method, organisations conduct interviews in campuses of various institutes, universities and colleges, to search prospective candidate. In short, it involves recruitment from educational institutions.

*   It has become a popular source of recruitment for technical, professional and managerial jobs.
*   Many big organisations maintain a close liaison with the universities, vocational schools and management institutes to recruit qualified personnel for various jobs.
*   Recruitment from educational institutions is a well-established practice of businesses. For example, Human Resource Managers of various companies visit institutes like IIT and IIM to recruit young talented candidates through campus recruitment.

7.  **Recommendations of Employees**

Under this method, existing employees are encouraged to recommend suitable persons among their friends and relatives for employment.

*   Such applicants are likely to be good employees because their background is sufficiently known.
*   In this method, a type of preliminary screening also takes place as the present employees know both the company and the candidates and they would try to satisfy both.

8.  **Labour Contractors**

Under this method, intermediaries known as agents or labour contractors are used for hiring unskilled and semi-skilled workers.

*   The contractors keep themselves in touch with the labour and can provide required number of unskilled workers at short notice.
*   Workers are recruited through labour contractors who are themselves employees of the organisation.
*   However, if the contractor himself decides to leave the organisation, all the workers employed through him will follow suit.

9.  **Advertising on Television**

Recruitment through advertisement of vacant posts over television is gaining importance these days. The detailed job requirements and qualities required to do it are publicised along with the profile of the organisation where vacancy exists.

10. **Web Publishing**

Internet is becoming a common source of recruitment these days.

*   Certain websites are specifically designed to provide information about both job seekers and job opening.
*   For example, websites such as naukri.com, shine.com, indeed.com, etc. are commonly visited, both by the prospective employees and the organisations searching for suitable people.
