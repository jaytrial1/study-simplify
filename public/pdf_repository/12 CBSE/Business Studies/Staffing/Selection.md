# SELECTION 

Selection is the process of identifying and choosing the best person out of a number of prospective candidates for a job.
In selection process, various steps are performed to eliminate the unsuitable candidates and to select the most suitable candidate.

## Definitions of Selection 

- Selection is the process of choosing from among the candidates from within the organisation or from the outside, the most suitable person for the current position or for the future position. -Dale Yoder
- Selection is a managerial decision making process as to predict which job applicants will be successful if hired.
- Selection is the process of differentiating between applicants in order to identify and hire those with a greater likelihood of success in a job.

The process of selection may continue even after the offer of employment, acceptance and joining of the candidate because it involves judgment about the performance potential of the candidate.

## Selection Process

The steps involved in the selection process are as follows:

## 1. Preliminary Screening

On the basis of preliminary screening of application form, unqualified candidates are eliminated.

- Other suitable applicants are called for the first round of interview, known as preliminary interview.
- It helps to reject the candidates for reasons, which did not appear in the application forms.


## 2. Selection Tests

An employment test is a mechanism (either a paper and pencil test or an exercise) which aims to measure certain characteristics of individuals.

- Selection tests give information about their aptitude, interest, intelligence, personality, etc., which cannot be known by the application forms.
- Some of the Important Tests used for selection of employees are:
(a) Intelligence Test: It is one of the important psychological test, which measures the level of intelligence or the intelligence quotient (I.Q.) of the candidate. It indicates a person's learning ability or the ability to make decisions and judgments.
(b) Aptitude Test: Aptitude test is conducted to determine potential of an individual in learning new skills. This test indicates the capacity of the candidate to develop. It also helps to know how he will perform in future.
(c) Personality Test: Personality tests are conducted to measure the personal characteristics such as values, beliefs, attitudes, emotional stability, capacity to get along, maturity, etc. It aims to explore qualities of the personality as a whole. These tests are difficult to design and implement.
(d) Trade Test: Trade test is conducted to determine the existing skills of the individual. It measures the level of knowledge and proficiency in the area of professions or technical training. For example, a typing test may be conducted for the job of typist.
Trade Test is different from Aptitude Test as former is concerned with what one has accomplished in the past and the latter measures the capacity to learn in future.
Typing Test for the Typist Job helps to determine typing speed of the applicant.

(e) Interest Test: Interest test is conducted to determine a person's area of interest and the kind of jobs that will satisfy him. This test aims to identify areas in which candidate has special liking, fascination and involvement.

## 3. Employment Interview 

All those applicants who qualify the tests are called for an Interview. Interview is a formal, indepth conversation conducted to evaluate the applicant's suitability for the job.

- The role of interviewer is to seek information and that of the interviewee is to provide the same.
- Interview also gives a chance to the interviewee (candidate) to get information regarding salary, working conditions, posting, etc.


## 4. Reference and Background Checks

A candidate is generally required to give names, addresses and telephone numbers of references for the purpose of verifying information and gaining additional information about him. The aim is to check the character background and social relations of the candidate. The candidates successful in the interview are generally investigated on the basis of references supplied by them. References can be of previous employers, known persons, teachers, university professors, etc.
However, references may be biased as the names are given by the candidate himself.

## 5. Selection Decision

The candidates who cross all the hurdles are included in the final list of selection. Before making the final decision about selection, the views and opinion of the concerned manager (manager responsible for the performance of the new employee) is considered.

## 6. Medical Examination

Before making the job offer, the candidate is required to undergo a medical test. The job offer is given to the candidate who is declared fit after the medical examination.

## 7. Job Offer

The candidates, who have passed all the previous hurdles, are issued job offer. Job offer is made through a letter of appointment to confirm his acceptance.

- The letter contains terms and conditions of employment, pay scale and other benefits associated with the job.
- Such a letter generally contains a date by which the appointee must report on duty.
- The appointee must be given reasonable time for reporting.

## 8. Contract of Employment 

After the candidate accepts the job offer, a written contract of employment is made between the employer and the candidate.

- Candidate is also required to provide certain vital details in a form, duly authenticated and attested by him. Such attestation form is kept by the company as a valid record for future reference.
- The contract of employment consist of necessary information regarding job title, duties, responsibilities, date of joining, pay and allowances, hours of work, leave rules, disciplinary rules, termination procedure, etc.


## Difference between Recruitment and Selection

| Basis | Recruitment | Selection |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1. Purpose | It aims to create a large pool of applicants. | It aims to ensure that competent candidate <br> is employed. |
| 2. Nature | It is a positive process as it encourages people <br> to apply for job. | It is a negative process as it rejects more <br> proportion of candidates. |
| 3. Process | It is a simple process as candidates are not <br> required to cross many hurdles. | It is a complex process as candidates are <br> required to cross several hurdles. |
| 4. Stage | It precedes selection process. | It starts after recruitment. |
