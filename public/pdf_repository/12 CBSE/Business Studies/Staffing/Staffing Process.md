# STAFFING PROCESS

The main concern of the staffing function in an organisation is the timely fulfillment of manpower requirements. The need for manpower arises in case of starting a new business or expanding the existing one or at the time of replacing those who quit, retire or are transferred or promoted. The various steps involved in the Staffing Process are as follows:

## 1. Estimating the Manpower Requirements 

Manpower requirements involve forecasting and determining future manpower needs of the organisation.

- It aims to determine the number and type of employees required. Type refers to specific qualifications, skills or experience to be possessed by the employees.
- While assessing the type of manpower required, company should also make policy to appoint women, persons from backward communities and persons with special abilities (such as physically challenged, visually and hearing impaired).
- Manpower requirements should be translated into specific job description and the desirable profile of its occupant: the desired qualifications, experience, personality characteristics and so on. This information becomes the base for looking for potential employees.
The manpower requirements involve two kinds of analysis:

1. Workload Analysis: It involves determining number and types of human resources required to perform various jobs and to achieve organisational objectives.
2. Workforce Analysis: It shows the number and types of human resources working with the organisation.
Such analysis would reveal whether we are understaffed, overstaffed or optimally staffed. A situation of overstaffing would require removal or transfer of employees and a situation of understaffing would necessitate the starting of recruitment process. The aim is to have right number and right kind of people in the right place and at right time.
Overstaffing is an undesirable situation as it leads to more pay and less work. So, every company needs proper estimation of manpower requirements.

## 2. Recruitment

Recruitment refers to a process of searching for prospective employees and encouraging them to apply for jobs in the organisation.

- The information generated in the process of writing the job description and the candidate profile may be used for developing the 'situations vacant' advertisement. The advertisement may be displayed on the office gate or it may be got published.
- This step involves locating the potential candidate or determining the sources of potential candidates.
- The aim of recruitment is to create a pool of applicants by large number of qualified candidates.
- Both internal sources (promotion or transfer) and external sources (placement consultants, advertisements, campus recruitment, etc.) may be used for recruitment.
- Internal sources give limited choice, whereas, external sources give wider choice and fresh talent.


## 3. Selection

Selection involves choosing the best person out of the pool of prospective candidates, developed through recruitment.

- Selection is done through a process, which involves steps like preliminary screening, tests, etc.
- The rigorous selection process serves two important purposes:

(i) It ensures that the organisation gets the most suitable candidate out of various applicants;
(ii) It enhances the self-esteem and prestige of those selected and conveys to them the seriousness with which the things are done in the organisation.

- Applicants, who cross all the hurdles of selection process, are offered an employment contract mentioning terms, conditions and date of joining.


## 4. Placement and Orientation 

After selection, candidate is assigned a suitable job.

- Placement refers to assigning specific jobs to the candidates, selected for appointment. After placement, the employees need induction or orientation.
- Orientation refers to introducing the selected employee to other employees and familiarising him with the rules and policies of the organisation. The employee is given a brief presentation about the company and is introduced to his superiors, subordinates and the colleagues.


## 5. Training and Development

This step involves improving the job knowledge and competence of employees in order to enable them to perform their jobs efficiently.

- Training and development not only improves the knowledge and skills for the present jobs, but also improve their competence for career growth.
- It motivates the employees to contribute more to organisational effectiveness and efficiency.
- Organisations have either in-house training centers or have tie up with some institutions to provide training to their employees.
By offering opportunities for career advancement, organisations are not only able to attract but also retain its talented people.
In certain small firms, there is no separate Human Resource Department. In such cases, line manager is also required to perform following three more steps.


## 6. Performance Appraisal

After the training of employees and performing the job for some time, there is a need to valuate their performance. Performance appraisal refers to systematic evaluation of employee's current and/or past performance against predetermined standards and their potential for development.

- The standards are made known to the employees in advance and superior is to provide feedback on their performance.
- The aim is to determine potential of an employee for better assignments, promotion and transfers.
- All organisations have some formal or informal means of appraising their employee's performance.
- The process of performance appraisal will include defining the job, appraising performance and providing feedback.


## 7. Promotion and Career Planning

Organisations must encourage the employees to grow and realise their full potential. It becomes necessary for all organisations to address career related issues and promotional avenues to serve

refers to the advancement of an employee in the management hierarchy. Promotion is generally accompanied by greater job responsibilities, more pay, higher status and greater prestige.
## 8. Compensation

Financial benefits play an important role in attracting and retaining a competent group of employees. So, managers should develop a sound compensation policy. Basically, the price of the job needs to be determined. Compensation refers to all forms of pay or rewards given to the employees.

- Compensation may be in the form of:
(a) Direct Financial Payments like wages, salaries, incentives, commissions and bonuses. They can be either time based (i.e. daily, weekly, monthly or annually) or performance based (i.e. according to number of units produced or sold).
(b) Indirect Payments can be in the form of rent free home, free car, etc.
- Certain pay plans can be created which are a combination of time based pay plus incentives for higher performance. Managers may formulate various plans of compensation, but all of them should be adequate and equitable.
- The compensation policy is significantly influenced by company's financial strength, salary offered by competing firms, rules framed by government (like labour laws), bargaining power of trade unions and company policy.
