# RECRUITMENT 

Recruitment refers to a process of searching for prospective employees and encouraging them to apply for jobs in the organisation.

- Recruitment is a positive process as it stimulates people to apply for jobs.
- It precedes the selection process and aims to create a pool of candidates so that management can select the most suitable candidate. For this, organisation communicates the vacant job position through different sources of recruitment.
- Recruitment aims to attract potential employees with the necessary qualifications, in the adequate number for the jobs available.


## Recruitment process involves following activities:

(i) Identify different sources of labour supply, from which applications of prospective employees may be obtained.

(ii) Evaluate validity of different sources.
(iii) Choose the most suitable source or sources.
(iv) Attract and motivate prospective candidates to apply for the vacant jobs.

## Sources of Recruitment 

There are two sources of recruitment: Internal and External.

## Internal Sources

Internal sources refers to making use of existing staff to fill up vacant jobs. Employees are recruited from among those who are already working in the organisation. There are two main sources in this category: (i) Promotion; and (ii) Transfer.

## 1. Promotion

Promotion refers to shifting of employee from a lower level position to a higher level position, carrying higher responsibilities, facilities, status and pay. For example, the vacant post of General Manager can be filled through promoting branch manager to the position of General Manager.

- Promotion is a vertical shifting of employees.
- It helps to improve the motivation, loyalty and satisfaction level of employees.
- It has a great psychological impact over the employees because a promotion at the higher level may lead to a chain of promotions at lower levels in the organisation.


## 2. Transfer

Transfer refers to shifting of an employee from one job to another, one department to another or from basically shifting of employees from one job to another at the same level of authority.

- For example, transfer of an accountant from sales department to purchase department.
- Transfer may lead to changes in duties and responsibilities, working condition etc., but not necessarily salary. It is practically a horizontal movement of employees.

- Transfer is a good source to fill departmental vacancies with employees from overstaffed departments.
- Job transfers are also helpful in avoiding termination and in removing individual problems and grievances.
- Shortage of suitable personnel in one branch may be filled through transfer from other branch or department.
- During transfer, it must be ensured that employee to be transferred is capable of performing the other job. Transfers also help to train employees for different jobs.


## Merits of Internal Sources 

1. It motivates the employees to improve their performance.

- A promotion at a higher level may lead to a chain of promotion at lower levels. This motivates the employees to improve their performance through learning and practice.
- Employees work with their commitment and loyalty and remain satisfied within jobs and peace prevails in the enterprise because of promotional avenues.

2. It simplifies the process of selection and placement as the candidates are already known to the organisation and can be evaluated more accurately.
3. Through transfer, employees get training, which prepares them for higher jobs. There is no need for induction training as employees are already familiar with the organisation.
4. Transfer has the benefit of shifting surplus workforce to those departments where there is shortage of staff.
5. This process is economical or cheaper as compared to getting candidates from external sources as no money needs to be spent on searching for sources of recruitment and no time is wasted in long selection process.

## Demerits of Internal Sources

1. It prevents induction of fresh talent. By stopping 'infusion of new blood' into the organisation, it may also lead to inbreeding, i.e. existing employees may continue to work and behave in old ways even after promotion and transfer.
2. The employees may become lethargic if they know that they will automatically get timebound promotions.
3. A new company cannot use internal sources of recruitment. Also, no organisation can fill all its vacancies from internal sources.
4. It reduces the spirit of competition among the employees as there is no competition from the outside world.
5. Frequent transfers of employees may often reduce the productivity of the organisation.

## External Sources

External recruitment refers to searching for the employees from the sources outside the organisation. Every enterprise has to use external sources as all the vacancies cannot be filled.

Various sources of external recruitment are:

## 1. Direct Recruitment 

Under this method, a notice is placed on the notice-board of the enterprise specifying the details of the jobs available.

- Job-seekers assemble outside the premises on the specified date and selection is done on the spot.
- This method is suitable for unskilled or semi-skilled jobs.
- The workers appointed under this method are known as casual or badli workers and are paid remuneration on daily wage basis.
- It is an economical method as it does not involve cost of advertising the vacancies.
- It is suitable for filling casual vacancies when there is a rush of work or when some permanent workers are absent.


## 2. Casual Callers

Companies generally receive unsolicited applications from job seekers at various points of time. Companies keep a database of such applications as they can provide a valuable source of recruitment. When a suitable vacancy arises, such casual callers can be considered for employment. It is an economical method as there is no cost of advertising the vacancy.

## 3. Advertisement

Under this method, the advertisement is given in newspapers, trade and professional journals along with full description about the job and its requirements like nature, qualifications, remuneration, etc.

- It gives wider choice and most of the senior positions are filled by this method.
- The advantage of advertising is that more information about the organisation and job can be given and management gets a wider range of candidates for selection. Advertisements may be placed in leading newspapers.
- However, advertisement may provide flood of response, and many times, from quite unsuitable candidates.


## 4. Employment Exchange

Under this method, employment exchanges run by government are used for filling unskilled and semiskilled jobs.

- Unemployed persons get themselves registered with these exchanges.
- Employment exchanges keep in touch with the employers. At the employer's request, they provide a list of candidates fulfilling the required qualifications.
- In certain cases, it has been made obligatory for the business concerns to notify their vacancies to the employment exchange.
- Thus, employment exchange serves as a link between job-seekers and employers.
- However, at times, records of employment exchange are not up to date and many of the candidates referred by them may not be found suitable.

## 5. Placement Agencies and Management Consultants 

Under this method, services of placement agencies and management consultants (like ABC Consultants) are used, who undertake total functions of recruiting and selecting personnel on behalf of various organisations.

- They provide a nationwide service in matching personnel demand and supply.
- These agencies compile resume (or curriculum vitae) of large number of candidates and recommend suitable names to the employers on demand.
- They charge fees for this purpose and are useful where extensive screening is required.
- They help the organisations to recruit technical, professional and managerial personnel. They specialise in middle level and top level executive placements.
- These professional recruiters can also entice (i.e. attract) the needed top executives from other companies by making the right offers.
- They maintain data bank of persons with different qualifications and skills and even advertise the jobs on behalf of their clients to recruit right type of personnel.


## 6. Campus Recruitment

In this method, organisations conduct interviews in campuses of various institutes, universities and colleges, to search prospective candidate. In short, it involves recruitment from educational institutions.

- It has become a popular source of recruitment for technical, professional and managerial jobs.
- Many big organisations maintain a close liaison with the universities, vocational schools and management institutes to recruit qualified personnel for various jobs.
- Recruitment from educational institutions is a well-established practice of businesses. For example, Human Resource Managers of various companies visit institutes like IIT and IIM to recruit young talented candidates through campus recruitment.


## 7. Recommendations of Employees

Under this method, existing employees are encouraged to recommend suitable persons among their friends and relatives for employment.

- Such applicants are likely to be good employees because their background is sufficiently known.
- In this method, a type of preliminary screening also takes place as the present employees know both the company and the candidates and they would try to satisfy both.


## 8. Labour Contractors

Under this method, intermediaries known as agents or labour contractors are used for hiring unskilled and semi-skilled workers.

- The contractors keep themselves in touch with the labour and can provide required number of unskilled workers at short notice.
- Workers are recruited through labour contractors who are themselves employees of the organisation.
- However, if the contractor himself decides to leave the organisation, all the workers employed through him will follow suit.

## 9. Advertising on Television 

Recruitment through advertisement of vacant posts over television is gaining importance these days. The detailed job requirements and qualities required to do it are publicised along with the profile of the organisation where vacancy exists.

## 10. Web Publishing

Internet is becoming a common source of recruitment these days.

- Certain websites are specifically designed to provide information about both job seekers and job opening.
- For example, websites such as naukri.com, shine.com, indeed.com, etc. are commonly visited, both by the prospective employees and the organisations searching for suitable people.


## Merits of External Sources

1. Qualified Personnel: External sources help management to attract qualified and trained people to apply for vacant jobs in the organisation.
2. Wider Choice: External sources provide wide choice and help the enterprise to make right choice of candidates.
3. Fresh Talent: The present employees may be insufficient or they may not fulfil the specifications of the jobs to be filled. Through external recruitment, enterprise can expect to get fresh, talented candidates from outside. This means infusion of new blood and new ideas into the enterprise. However, it is expensive and time-consuming.
4. Competitive Spirit: External candidates are expected to be trained and talented. As internal candidates have to compete with them, it induces the existing employees to work harder to show better performance.

## Demerits or Limitations of External Sources

1. Dissatisfaction among Existing Staff: Recruitment from outside may cause dissatisfaction and frustration among the existing employees. They may feel that their chances of promotion are reduced.
2. Lengthy Process: External recruitment is a time consuming process as vacancies are publicised and enterprise has to wait for the response of prospective candidates.
3. Costly Process: It is very costly to recruit staff from external sources as lot of money is spent on advertising and processing the applications.
