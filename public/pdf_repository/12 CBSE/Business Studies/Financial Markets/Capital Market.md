# CAPITAL MARKET

Capital Market refers to whole network of all organisations, institutions and instruments that provide medium and long-term funds.

- Capital market consists of a series of channels through which savings of the community are made available for industrial and commercial enterprises and for the public in general.
- It encourages people to invest their small savings into the most productive use. It leads to growth and development of the economy.
- Capital Market consists of development banks, commercial banks and stock exchanges.
- Long-term funds are raised and invested in the form of both debt and equity. The instruments used in capital market are: Shares, Debentures, Bonds, Public deposits, etc.
- The process of economic development is facilitated by the existence of a well functioning capital market. In fact, development of the financial system is seen as a necessary condition for economic growth.
It must be noted that capital market does not include instruments or institutions that provide shortterm finance upto one year.


## Requisites of Capital Market

A Capital Market is ideal when:

1. Finance is available at reasonable cost.
2. Financial institutions are sufficiently developed.
3. Market operations are fair, competitive and transparent.
4. Sufficient information is provided to the investors.
5. Transaction costs are minimised.
6. Capital is most productively allocated.

## Nature or Features of Capital Market

1. Duration of Investments: It deals in medium and long-term securities, whose maturity period is more than one year.
2. Financial Instruments: In the capital market, the flow of capital takes place through instruments like shares, debentures, bonds, public deposits, etc.
3. Segments: Capital market has 2 segments: Primary Market (i.e. market meant for issue of fresh capital) and Secondary Market (i.e. market for sale and purchase of previously

4. Use of Intermediaries: Capital market functions through several types of intermediaries, like underwriters, bankers, stock brokers, etc.
5. Helps in Capital Formation: Capital market offers attractive investment opportunities to those who can invest surplus funds. As a result, it facilitates flow of capital from investors to the borrowers. This process leads to capital formation in an economy.
6. Provides Liquidity: Capital market provides liquidity as securities can be bought and sold in the stock exchange as per requirement.
Difference between Capital Market and Money Market

| Basis | Capital Market | Money Market |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 1. Participants | The participants are financial institutions, banks, corporate entities, foreign investors and ordinary retail investors from members of the public. | The participants are institutional participants such as RBI, banks, financial institutions and finance companies. Although individual investors are permitted to transact in the secondary money market, but they normally do not transact. |
| 2. Instruments | Equity Shares, Debentures, Bonds, Preference Shares, etc. | Treasury Bills, Call Money, Commercial Paper, etc. |
| 3. Investment Outlay | It requires less investment as value of securities is generally low, i.e. ₹ $10, ₹ 100$ with minimum trading lot of $5,50,100$ or so. This helps individuals with small savings to subscribe to these securities. | It requires huge financial investment as instruments of money market are quite expensive. |
| 4. Duration | It deals in medium and long-term securities. | It deals in short-term securities having maximum tenure of 1 year and may even be issued for a single day. |
| 5. Liquidity | Securities are liquid as they are marketable on the stock exchange. However, a share may not be actively traded, i.e. it may not easily find a buyer. | Money market instruments enjoy a higher degree of liquidity as Discount Finance House of India (DFHI) has been established for the specific objective of providing a ready market for them. |
| 6. Safety | Capital market instruments are riskier both with respect to returns \& principal repayment as issuing companies may fail to perform as per projections \& promoters may defraud investors. | Money market instruments are generally safe due to shorter duration of investment and financial soundness of the issuers. |
| 7. Expected Return | The expected return is high as there are chances of earning capital gains in equity shares. Moreover, in the long run, prosperity of a company is shared by shareholders by way of high dividends and bonus issues. | The expected return is less due to short duration. |
