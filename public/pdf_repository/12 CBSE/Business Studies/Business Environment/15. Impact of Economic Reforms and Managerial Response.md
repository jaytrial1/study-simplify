This section covers the effects of the 1991 economic reforms on Indian businesses and how managers have responded to the changing environment.

# Impact of Economic Reforms and Managerial Response

The policy of liberalisation, privatisation, and globalisation (LPG policy) significantly impacted the functioning of businesses and industries in India.

The impact of changes in government policy can be analyzed as follows:

1.  **Increasing Competition:** Following the reforms, delicensing and the liberalized entry of foreign firms intensified competition for Indian firms, particularly in service sectors like telecommunications, airlines, banking, and insurance, which were previously reserved for the public sector. For example, banking reforms increased competition for nationalized banks like SBI from domestic private banks (like ICICI) and MNC banks (like HSBC).
2.  **More Demanding Customers:** Customers are now more informed and demanding. Increased market competition offers customers wider choices and better quality goods and services, creating a buyer's market.
3.  **Rapidly changing Technological Environment:** Increased competition necessitates that Indian firms adopt new methods to survive and grow. Using advanced technology provides a competitive edge and helps improve processes, products, and services. However, rapid technological change poses challenges for smaller firms with limited funds. For example, Tata invested heavily in R&D for the 'Nano' car to adapt to the changing technological environment.
4.  **Necessity for Change:** Before the reforms, the business environment was relatively stable, and long-term plans and policies were common. After the reforms, market forces became turbulent, requiring enterprises to continuously modify operations and policies. For example, firms like LG, Samsung, and Bajaj constantly update their products to remain competitive.
5.  **Need for developing human resources:** Indian firms faced challenges due to a shortage of trained personnel. The new market conditions demand employees with higher competence and commitment. Consequently, firms recognized the need to develop human resources, often through regular on-the-job and off-the-job training.
6.  **Market Orientation:** Previously, firms often followed a production-oriented approach (produce first, then sell). With increased competition and consumerism, firms shifted to a market-oriented approach, analyzing market needs first before producing goods to satisfy buyers effectively.
7.  **Loss of Budgetary Support to the public sector:** The role of the public sector declined after the reforms, leading the government to reduce financial support to PSUs. PSUs realized they must improve performance and generate their own resources to survive and grow in the competitive environment.

In conclusion, the economic reforms positively affected Indian business and industry. Indian enterprises developed strategies and adopted new processes to meet competitive challenges, becoming more customer-oriented and focusing on customer relationships and satisfaction.

## Managerial Response to Changes in Business Environment

A business enterprise's survival depends on its ability to cope with environmental uncertainties. Some ways managers respond to changes in the business environment include:

1.  **Diversification:** Many Indian companies are diversifying by entering new product and service areas where they lacked prior experience. For example, Reliance Industries entered mobile communication. Other companies have also diversified into related and unrelated business fields.
2.  **Joint Ventures with MNCs:** Many Indian companies form joint ventures with MNCs to strengthen themselves and better face environmental pressures and uncertainties. Examples include the joint venture between Jaguar and Tata Motors, and Mahindra and Mahindra with Ford.
3.  **Acquisitions and Mergers:** There has been an increase in acquisitions and mergers in Indian industry since the economic reforms. Examples: The merger of Vodafone India and Idea Cellular; Flipkart's acquisition of Myntra.
4.  **Use of Modern Technology:** Most businesses are adopting the latest technology to improve quality and reduce costs. Upgrading technology is crucial for all companies. For example, cellular companies continuously develop new mobile models with the latest processors.
5.  **Brand Building:** Companies are investing substantially in building and popularizing brands through extensive promotional activities. For instance, consumer durable companies like Samsung and LG spend heavily on advertising for brand building.
6.  **Customer Orientation:** Companies now prioritize customer needs and convenience. They manufacture products based on customer requirements and emphasize customer satisfaction. Most companies have introduced toll-free customer care numbers to quickly address customer queries and grievances.
7.  **Higher Level of Compensation:** To compete with salaries offered by MNCs, Indian companies have significantly increased pay packages for managerial and technical roles. Starting salaries for engineering and management graduates have also risen. Companies are implementing performance-linked incentive schemes like Employee Stock Option Plans (ESOP).
