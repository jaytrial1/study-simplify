This section defines globalisation and explains how the New Economic Policy aimed to integrate the Indian economy with the world economy.

# Globalisation

**Globalisation** means integrating the various economies of the world, leading to the emergence of a cohesive global economy.

It involves integrating the national economy with the world economy by removing barriers to international trade and capital movements. Before 1991, the Indian Government strictly regulated imports through licensing, tariffs, and quantitative restrictions.

However, the New Economic Policy sought to liberalize foreign trade (through import liberalization and export promotion) by rationalizing the tariff structure and reforming foreign exchange regulations. This aimed to prevent the country from remaining isolated from the rest of the world.

Globalisation has increased interaction and interdependence among global economies. Businesses can now more easily serve customers in distant markets, facilitated by rapid technological advancements and liberal government trade policies.

For Example, 'Startup20', initiated under the G20 Indian presidency in 2023, is an official engagement group aiming to be the voice of the global startup ecosystem and bring stakeholders together on a common platform. Startup20 seeks \$1 trillion in investment for the global startup ecosystem.

# Which Economy is Truly Globalised?

An economy is considered truly global when there is:

1.  Free flow of goods and services across nations.
2.  Free flow of capital across nations.
3.  Free flow of information and technology.
4.  Free movement of people across borders.
5.  A commonly accepted mechanism for settling disputes.
6.  A global governance perspective.
