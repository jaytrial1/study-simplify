This section introduces the economic reforms initiated in India in 1991, including their main aim and broad features.

# New Industrial Policy (New Economic Policy)

In 1991, the Indian economy faced significant challenges, including a severe foreign exchange crisis, a high government deficit, and rising prices despite good agricultural output. To address these issues, the Indian Government announced a **New Industrial Policy** in July 1991 as part of broader economic reforms. The primary goal was to foster a more competitive economic environment and remove barriers hindering the entry and growth of firms.

The broad features of this policy included:

1.  **Delicensing:** Industrial licensing was abolished for most projects, except for six specific industries.
2.  **Decreased role of Public Sector:** The role of the public sector was restricted to only four industries deemed strategically important, opening the remaining sectors to the private sector.
3.  **Disinvestment:** This involved selling part or all shares of public sector undertakings (PSUs). Disinvestment was carried out in many public sector enterprises.
4.  **Liberalisation of Foreign Capital:** The allowed share of foreign equity participation was increased, and 100% Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) was permitted in many activities.
5.  **Liberal policy for technical collaboration:** Automatic permission was granted for technology agreements with foreign companies.
6.  **Setting up of Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB):** FIPB was established to promote and channel foreign investment into India.
7.  **De-reservation under small-scale industries:** Many goods previously reserved for production by small-scale industries were de-reserved.

In essence, the New Industrial Policy aimed to free industry from the licensing system (**Liberalisation**), significantly reduce the public sector's role (**Privatisation**), and encourage foreign private participation in India's industrial development (**Globalisation**). These three components constitute the core of the New Industrial Policy or New Economic Policy.
