This section covers the influence of political conditions and government attitudes on business.

# Political Environment

**Political Environment** includes political conditions such as general stability and peace in the country, as well as the specific attitudes of elected government representatives towards business.

Factors like the ruling government, its policies towards different groups, etc., are part of the Political Environment. Political factors significantly shape the environment in which businesses operate, requiring them to adapt to political changes.

The significance of political conditions for business success lies in the predictability of business activities under stable conditions. Political unrest and threats to law and order can create uncertainty. A stable government fosters confidence among businesses to invest in long-term projects, while instability erodes this confidence.

The attitude of government officials towards business can have positive or negative impacts. For example, even after India's economic reforms in 1991, foreign companies faced significant bureaucratic hurdles ("red tape") in obtaining permits.

**Examples:**

*   The 'Har Ghar Tiranga' campaign initiated by the Government generated substantial business, with over 35 crore flags sold in 2023 according to CAIT.
*   In 1977, the Janta Government's strict policy against MNCs led companies like Coca-Cola and IBM to leave India.
*   Changes in government policies in 1991 allowed Pepsi and other MNCs to enter the Indian market.

## Some Components of Political Environment

1.  Constitution of the country.
2.  Prevailing political system.
3.  Political ideology and practices of the ruling party.
4.  Extent and nature of government intervention in business.
5.  Nature of relationship with foreign countries.
6.  Nature and profile of political leadership and thinking of political personalities.
