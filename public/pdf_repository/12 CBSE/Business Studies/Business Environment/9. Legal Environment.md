This section examines the impact of laws, regulations, and court decisions on business operations.

# Legal Environment

**Legal Environment** comprises various legislations passed by the Government, administrative orders issued by government authorities, court judgments, and decisions by various commissions and agencies at all levels of government.

Essentially, the legal environment is the framework of laws and regulations within which business transactions must occur. It is mandatory for every enterprise to comply with these rules and laws. Adequate knowledge of relevant laws and regulations is crucial for better business performance; non-compliance can lead to legal issues.

To operate successfully in India, businesses must have working knowledge of laws such as:

*   Companies Act, 2013
*   Industries (Development and Regulations) Act, 1951
*   Foreign Exchange Management Act and the Imports and Exports (Control) Act 1947
*   Factories Act, 1948
*   Trade Union Act, 1926
*   Workmen's Compensation Act, 1923
*   Industrial Disputes Act, 1947
*   Consumer Protection Act, 2019
*   Competition Act, 2002

Besides these, numerous other laws regulate product standards, packaging, promotion, ethics, etc. For example, in India, cigarette packaging must include the statutory warning: "Cigarette smoking is injurious to health".

**Examples:**

*   Advertising of alcoholic beverages is prohibited on Television.
*   Companies producing hazardous goods must comply with provisions of the Bureau of Indian Standards Act, 1986.
*   Advertisements for baby food products must state that mother's milk is the best.
