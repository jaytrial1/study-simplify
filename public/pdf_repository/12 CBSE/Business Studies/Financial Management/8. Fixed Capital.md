This section defines fixed capital, explains its management, and lists the factors that influence a firm's requirement for fixed capital.

# Fixed Capital

## Meaning of Fixed Capital

**Fixed assets** refer to those assets, which remain in the business for a period more than one year. For example, plant and machinery, furniture, land and building, vehicles, etc.
*   **Fixed Capital** is the money invested in fixed assets, which is to be used over a long period of time.
*   Fixed Capital is meant for meeting permanent or long-term needs of the business.
*   Decision to invest in fixed assets must be taken very carefully as the investment is usually quite large. Such decisions once taken are irrevocable except at a huge loss.

## Management of Fixed Capital

Management of fixed capital involves allocation of firm's capital to different projects or assets with long-term implications for the business. The decisions taken to invest in fixed assets are known as **Investment Decisions** or **Capital Budgeting Decisions**. Investment in these assets would also include expenditure on acquisition, expansion, modernisation and their replacement.

It must be remembered that fixed assets should always be financed through long-term sources (shares, debentures, long-term loans, etc) and never from short-term sources.

## Factors Affecting Requirement of Fixed Capital

The amount of fixed capital required varies from business to business. Various factors which influence the requirements of fixed capital are:

1.  **Nature of Business:** The nature of business determines the amount of fixed capital to a large extent.
    *   In case of **Manufacturing Business**, heavy investment is required in fixed assets like land, building, plant, machinery, etc. So, there is a need for large amount of fixed capital.
    *   On the other hand, a **Trading Business**, in which finished goods are bought and sold, requires relatively less amount of fixed capital.
2.  **Scale of Operations:** Firms which operate at large-scale require larger amount of fixed capital as they need heavy and bigger machinery and equipments. However, firms operating at small-scale need relatively lesser fixed capital.
3.  **Choice of Technique:** Production techniques adopted by business also influences the requirements of fixed capital.
    *   Companies using **capital-intensive techniques** require more fixed capital as larger investment is needed in plant and machinery as it relies less on manual labour.
    *   However, **labour-intensive enterprises** need lesser fixed capital as they require more of labour than capital consuming equipments.
4.  **Technology Upgradation:** Companies, in which assets become obsolete sooner, require higher fixed capital to purchase such assets. For example, computer based companies need more fixed capital due to continuous advancement in computer technology. However, if companies use assets which are less affected by technological upgradation (like in case of furniture based companies), then they need lesser fixed capital.
5.  **Growth Prospects:** Companies with growth plans in future, need more fixed capital as more investment in plant and machinery is needed to increase the production capacity in order to meet the anticipated higher demand. However, companies with less or no possibility of growth, need less fixed capital.
6.  **Diversification:** Diversification of operations also governs the fixed capital requirements. If a company chooses to diversify its operations, then it certainly needs more fixed capital. For example, if a textile company starts electronics business, then it will require additional fixed capital to invest in land, building, machinery, etc.
7.  **Financing Alternatives:** Fixed assets can be either purchased outrightly (cash basis) or can be acquired on lease or hire basis. A developed financial market may provide leasing facilities as an alternative to outright purchase.
    *   When a company acquires an asset on lease, then it pays lease rentals and uses it. Thus, availability of leasing facilities reduces the fixed capital requirements. Such a strategy is specially suitable in high risk lines of business.
    *   On the other hand, a company which purchases assets on cash basis, requires more fixed capital.
8.  **Level of Collaboration:** If an organisation collaborates with another organisation, then both the organisations can share each other's facilities.
    *   This is feasible if the scale of operations of each one of them is not sufficient to make full use of the facility.
    *   Such collaboration or joint venture will reduce the need of fixed capital for each one of the participating organisation.
    *   For example, some banks jointly establish ATMs at different locations.
