# FINANCIAL MANAGEMENT 

Since finance is the lifeblood of any business, its management requires special attention. Financial Management is concerned with management of flow of funds and involves decisions relating to procurement of funds, investment of funds in long-term and short-term assets and distribution of earnings to the owners.
Financial Management is concerned with optimal procurement as well as the usage of finance. Financial Management is basically concerned with two aspects:

1. Procurement of Funds: For optimal procurement, different available sources of finance are identified and compared in terms of their costs and associated risks.
2. Usage of Finance: Funds procured needs to be profitably invested, i.e. returns from investment must be more than cost at which funds have been procured.
While dealing with these two aspects, Financial Management aims:

- To reduce the cost of funds procured.
- To keep the risk under control.
- To achieve effective investment of such funds.
- To ensure availability of enough funds as and when required.
- To avoid idle finance.

Overall financial success of a business is determined by the quality of its financial management.

## Role of Financial Management

Financial Management influences the financial health of a business. The financial statements (i.e. Balance Sheet and Profit and Loss Account) reflect a firm's financial position and its financial health. Almost all items in the financial statements are affected directly or indirectly through some financial management decisions.
Some prominent examples of the aspects being affected by financial management are:

1. Size as well as Composition of Fixed Assets: Financial Management plays a significant role in determining the size and composition of fixed assets of the business. For example, capital budgeting decision to invest $₹ 100$ crores in fixed assets would raise the size of fixed assets block by this amount.
2. Quantum of Current Assets: The quantum of current assets is also influenced by financial management decisions. It helps to determine amount of current assets as well as its breakup into cash, inventories and receivables.

- With an increase in the investment in fixed assets, there is a proportionate increase in the working capital requirement.
- Decisions about credit policy and inventory management affect the amount of debtors and inventory, which in turn affect the total current assets as well as their composition.

3. Amount of long-term and short-term financing: Financial management takes decision regarding the proportion of long and short-term finance. An organisation raises more amount on long-term basis if it wants to remain more liquid. However, there is a choice between liquidity* and profitability. Long-term debts adversely affect profitability as they are costly as compared to short-term debts (current liabilities).
*Liquidity means firm's ability to meet its current obligations or short-term liabilities.
4. Break-up of long-term financing into debt and equity: Firm can raise long-term finance through issue of equity shares as well as by issue of debentures and other borrowed funds. Financial Management decision also helps in fixing this ratio between debt and equity.
5. All items in the Profit and Loss Account: Financial management decisions affect all the items in the profit and loss account of the business. For example, use of more debt means higher interest expense in future and use of more equity involves higher dividend payment. Similarly, an expansion of business, which is a result of capital budgeting decision, is likely to affect all items in the Profit and Loss Account of the business.
Thus, it can be stated that the financial statements of a business are largely determined by financial management decisions taken earlier. Similarly, future financial statements would depend upon past as well as current financial decisions. Effective financial management helps in mobilisation of financial resources at a lower cost and deployment of these in the most profitable activities.

## Objective of Financial Management 

The primary and most important objective of financial management is to maximise wealth of equity shareholders, which means maximising the market price of equity shares.

- This is because a company funds belong to the shareholders and the manner in which they are invested and the return earned by them determines their market value or price.
- Market price of equity shares increase if the benefits derived from a decision exceed the cost involved.
- So, all the financial decisions, major or minor, are made with the objective of making some value addition, so that market price of equity shares is maximised.
For example, while taking decision to invest in a new machine, financial management aims to ensure that benefits from such investment exceed the cost, so that some value addition takes place for betterment of market price of shares. Similarly, when finance is procured, the aim is to reduce the cost so that the value addition is even higher.
- All those avenues of investment, modes of financing, ways of handling various components of working capital must be identified which will ultimately lead to an increase in the price of equity share.
Continuous increase in prices of shares and higher dividends help to achieve the Financial Management's basic objective of 'Wealth Maximisation'.

- The objective of wealth maximisation helps in achieving the objectives of: (i) Profit Maximisation; (ii) Maintaining liquid assets; (iii) Financial reserves for growth; (iv) Optimum utilisation of funds.
- It can happen through efficient decision making. Decision-making is efficient if best alternative is selected out of various available alternatives.
- This objective of wealth maximization also implies that all other stakeholders (like suppliers, employees, lenders, creditors, etc.) are also gaining as equity shareholders get residual share, i.e. they are given dividend only after claims of all other stakeholders.
