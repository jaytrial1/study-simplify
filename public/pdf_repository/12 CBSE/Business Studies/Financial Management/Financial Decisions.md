# FINANCIAL DECISIONS 

Financial decisions refer to the decisions concerning financial matters of a business firm. There are various financial decisions that the firm makes to maximise shareholders' wealth.
Financial decision-making is concerned with three broad decisions which are as follows:

## Investment Decision

A firm's resources are scarce in comparison to the requirements. So, the firm should invest them in a manner which generates highest possible return for their investors. Investment Decision is concerned with investment of firm's funds in different assets.
Investment Decisions can be categorised as:

1. Capital Budgeting Decisions or Long-term Investment Decisions: These decisions relate to the investment in fixed assets. Such decisions involve commitment for long-term.

## Examples:

(i) Purchase of a new Fixed Asset like Plant and Machinery, Land, Building, etc.;
(ii) Opening a new Branch;
(iii) Launch of a new Product Line;
(iv) Investing in Advanced Techniques of Production.
(v) Expenditure on Research and Development (R\&D) Programme.

2. Working Capital Decisions or Short-term Investment Decisions: These decisions relate to investment in current assets (like cash, inventories, debtors, etc.). These decisions affect the day-to-day working of the enterprise.

- Essential ingredients of sound working capital management are:
(i) Efficient Cash Management
(ii) Efficient Inventory Management
(iii) Efficient Receivables Management
- For this, an adequate working capital (i.e. neither excess nor deficient amount) should be with the business at all the times.


## Importance of Capital Budgeting Decisions 

Capital Budgeting decisions are among the most crucial and critical business decisions as a bad decision may lead to adverse financial consequences. Their importance will be clear from the following points:

1. Long-term Growth and effects: These decisions have long-term implications for the firm. The funds invested in long-term assets are likely to yield returns in the future. Therefore, such decisions considerably affect the growth and future prospects of the business.
2. Large amount of funds involved: These decisions result in a substantial portion of capital funds being blocked in the long-term projects.

- So, such decisions are very crucial \& firm must carefully plan its investment programme.
- This may involve decisions like where to procure funds from and at what rate of interest.

3. Risk involved: Capital budgeting decisions involve huge funds. It affects the returns of the firm as a whole in the long-term. So, these decisions influence the overall business risk complexion of the firm.
4. Irreversible Decisions: Most of the capital budgeting decisions cannot be reversed or reverted without incurring heavy losses. Abandoning a project in midway involves adverse financial consequences. Therefore, they should be taken after evaluating each and every minute detail, otherwise firm may have to incur heavy losses.

## Factors Affecting Capital Budgeting Decision

A number of projects are often available to a business to invest in. In such a case, each project needs to be evaluated carefully and, depending upon the returns, a particular project is either selected or rejected. If there is only one project, its viability is seen in terms of rate of return and is compared with the industry's average.
There are certain factors which affect capital budgeting decisions:
5. Cash Flows of the Project: When a firm takes an investment decision involving huge amount, it expects to generate some cash flows over a period.

- These cash flows are in the form of a series of cash receipts and payments over the life of an investment.
- Such cash flows should be carefully analysed before making a capital budgeting decision.

2. Rate of Return: The firm should compare the rate of return expected and risk involved in different investment projects. For example, If there are two projects: A and B (with same risk involved), with a rate of return of $10 \%$ and $12 \%$ respectively, then under normal circumstance, firm will definitely select B.
3. Investment Criteria: The decision to invest in a particular project involves a number of calculations regarding the amount of investment, interest rate, cash flows and rate of return. There are different techniques to evaluate investment proposals which are known as capital budgeting techniques. These techniques are applied to each proposal before selecting a particular project.

## Financing Decision 

Financing decision is about the quantum of finance to be raised from various long-term sources. It deals with determination of various sources of finance, amount to be raised from each source and the cost of each source of finance.
The main sources of finance are:
(i) Shareholders' or Owners' Funds: They consist of equity capital and retained earnings.
(ii) Borrowed Funds: They refer to finance raised as debentures, loans or other forms of debt. Financing decision is concerned with deciding how much to raise from owners' funds and how much from borrowed funds. Both the sources have their own peculiar merits and demerits.

- Borrowed Funds are more risky as they involve commitment of fixed interest payment (whether or not firm has earned profit) \& return of principal amount. On the other hand, owners' funds are less risky as there is no such commitment regarding payment of dividend or repayment of capital.
'Financial Risk' is the risk that a firm would fail to meet its payment obligations. It means, there is some amount of financial risk in debt financing. The overall financial risk depends upon the proportion of debt in the total capital.
- Financing decision involves analysing the risk and cost associated with each source of finance in order to decide a judicious mix of both debt and equity.
- Borrowed funds are considered the cheapest source of finance as interest paid is a deductible expense for computation of tax liability. The cost of raising finance, known as 'Floatation Cost' is also considered.
Thus, financing decision is concerned with deciding how much to be raised from each source. This decision determines the overall cost of capital and the financial risk of the enterprise.


## Factors Affecting Financing Decision

The various factors which affect the financing decisions are as follows:

1. Cost: The cost of raising funds from different sources is different. A prudent financial manager generally prefers the cheapest source of finance.
2. Risk: The risk associated with different sources of finance is different. More risk is associated with borrowed funds as compared to owners' funds. Finance Manager considers the risk involved with each source before taking any financing decision.

## Business Risk and Financial Risk 

Every business faces two kinds of risks:

1. Business or Operating Risk, i.e. risk of inability to meet fixed operating costs (rent, salary, insurance premium, etc).
2. Financial Risk, i.e. risk of inability to meet fixed financial charges (interest payment, preference dividend and repayment obligations).
The total risk depends on both the business risk and the financial risk. If a firm's business risk is lower, then company may face financial risk, i.e. more debt capital can be used. However, in case of higher business risk, financial risk should be reduced by using less debt.
3. Floatation Costs: Floatation Cost refers to the cost involved in issue of securities. For example, underwriting commission, brokerage, stamp duty, listing charges, etc. Firms prefer securities with least floatation cost.
4. Cash Flow Position: A company with stronger cash flow position can take the advantage of debt (cheaper finance) as interest payment and repayment of principal amount can be easily made. However, equity should be preferred by companies with shortage of cash.
5. Fixed Operating Costs: Firms with high fixed operating costs (like rent, insurance premium, salaries, etc.) must reduce fixed financing costs, like interest payment on debt. In such case, lower debt financing is better. Similarly, if fixed operating cost is less, more of debt financing may be preferred.
6. Control Considerations: Issues of more equity shares may lead to dilution of management's control over the business. Debt financing has no such implication.

- Companies which are afraid of a takeover bid would prefer debt to equity. In other words, if existing shareholders want to retain complete control of the company, then debt should be preferred.
- However, if they do not mind loss of control, then the company may go for equity.

7. State of Capital Markets: The conditions in the stock market also help in making choice of source of finance.

- In the boom period (i.e. when stock market is rising), it is easier to raise funds through issue of shares as more people invest in equity.
- However, depressed capital market may make issue of equity shares difficult for any company.


## Dividend Decision

Dividend refers to that part of profits, which is distributed amongst its shareholders. Dividend Decision (or Disposal of Surplus Decision) involves deciding how much of the profit earned by company (after paying tax) is to be distributed to the shareholders as dividend and how much of it should be retained in the business.

- Dividend constitutes current income and its re-investment as retained earnings helps in increasing future earning capacity of the firm.

- Generally, shareholders want the maximum dividend and management want that the maximum amount be kept as retained earnings. The finance manager has to strike a balance between the two.
- Decision regarding dividend should be taken keeping in view the overall objective of maximising shareholders' wealth.


## Factors Affecting Dividend Decision 

The decision of payment of dividend depends on the following factors:

1. Amount of Earnings: Dividends are paid out of the earnings of current and previous years. So, more earnings will ensure greater dividend, whereas, less earnings will lead to declaration of low rate of dividend.
2. Stability of Earnings: Company with stable and regular earnings can afford to declare higher dividends as compared to company, which does not have such stability in its earnings.
3. Stability of Dividends: Some companies follow the policy of paying stable and steady dividends as it satisfies the shareholders and helps in building company's reputation.

- The increase in dividend is made only when the company is confident that there is an increase in their earning potential and not just the earnings of the current year.
- It means, dividend per share is not changed if the change in earnings is small or seen to be temporary in nature.

4. Growth Opportunities: Companies with growth opportunities prefer to retain more money out of their earnings to finance the required investment. So, companies having growth prospects in near future, declare lesser dividend as compared to companies, which do not have any growth plans.
5. Cash Flow Position: Payment of dividend involves an outflow of cash. A company may be profitable but short on cash.

- In case of surplus cash, firm can pay more dividends.
- However, during shortage of cash, companies declare no or very low dividend.

6. Shareholders' Preference: The preferences of shareholders are usually kept in mind while declaring dividend.

- If majority of the shareholders are those who invest with the purpose of getting some regular income (like retired, old and middle class shareholders), then the company will declare more dividend and keep aside less in the form of retained earnings.
- However, in case of young and wealthy shareholders, company may declare low dividend as such shareholders prefer capital gains by reinvesting the profits of company.

7. Taxation Policy: The rate of dividend also depends on the taxation policy of the government. In the present taxation policy, dividend income is tax free income in the hands of shareholders. So, they prefer higher dividends. However, dividend distribution tax is levied on companies.

- In case of higher dividend distribution tax rate, company prefers to pay lesser dividend.
- However, if tax rate is low, then company may declare higher dividend.

8. Stock Market Reaction: Rate of dividend and market value of the shares are directly related to each other.

- A higher rate of dividend has a positive impact on the market price of shares, whereas, low rate of dividend may have a negative impact on the share prices in the stock market.
- So, management should consider the effect on the price of equity shares while determining the rate of dividend.

9. Access to Capital Market: If a firm has easy access to the capital markets (either because it is financially strong or large in size), then it may depend less on retained earnings to finance their growth and follow a liberal dividend policy. Such firms tend to pay higher dividends. However, a firm with limited access to capital market, is likely to declare low dividend.
10. Legal Constraints: Every company has to comply with provisions of the Companies Act while declaring the dividend. Such provisions must be adhered to while declaring the dividends.
For example, according to Section 123 of Companies Act, 2013, company has to provide for present and past depreciation on assets and create statutory reserves before declaring dividend.
11. Contractual Constraints: When a company borrows loan, then the lender may put certain restrictions on the company on payment of dividends in future. The companies are required to ensure that the payment of dividend does not violate the terms and conditions of the lender.
