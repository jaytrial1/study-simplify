This section focuses on the dividend decision, which determines how much of a company's profit is distributed to shareholders and how much is retained for reinvestment.

# The Dividend Decision

**Dividend** refers to that part of profits, which is distributed amongst its shareholders. **Dividend Decision** (or Disposal of Surplus Decision) involves deciding how much of the profit earned by company (after paying tax) is to be distributed to the shareholders as dividend and how much of it should be retained in the business.
*   Dividend constitutes current income and its re-investment as retained earnings helps in increasing future earning capacity of the firm.
*   Generally, shareholders want the maximum dividend and management want that the maximum amount be kept as retained earnings. The finance manager has to strike a balance between the two.
*   Decision regarding dividend should be taken keeping in view the overall objective of maximising shareholders' wealth.

## Factors Affecting Dividend Decision

The decision of payment of dividend depends on the following factors:

1.  **Amount of Earnings:** Dividends are paid out of the earnings of current and previous years. So, more earnings will ensure greater dividend, whereas, less earnings will lead to declaration of low rate of dividend.
2.  **Stability of Earnings:** Company with stable and regular earnings can afford to declare higher dividends as compared to company, which does not have such stability in its earnings.
3.  **Stability of Dividends:** Some companies follow the policy of paying stable and steady dividends as it satisfies the shareholders and helps in building company's reputation.
    *   The increase in dividend is made only when the company is confident that there is an increase in their earning potential and not just the earnings of the current year.
    *   It means, dividend per share is not changed if the change in earnings is small or seen to be temporary in nature.
4.  **Growth Opportunities:** Companies with growth opportunities prefer to retain more money out of their earnings to finance the required investment. So, companies having growth prospects in near future, declare lesser dividend as compared to companies, which do not have any growth plans.
5.  **Cash Flow Position:** Payment of dividend involves an outflow of cash. A company may be profitable but short on cash.
    *   In case of surplus cash, firm can pay more dividends.
    *   However, during shortage of cash, companies declare no or very low dividend.
6.  **Shareholders' Preference:** The preferences of shareholders are usually kept in mind while declaring dividend.
    *   If majority of the shareholders are those who invest with the purpose of getting some regular income (like retired, old and middle class shareholders), then the company will declare more dividend and keep aside less in the form of retained earnings.
    *   However, in case of young and wealthy shareholders, company may declare low dividend as such shareholders prefer capital gains by reinvesting the profits of company.
7.  **Taxation Policy:** The rate of dividend also depends on the taxation policy of the government. In the present taxation policy, dividend income is tax free income in the hands of shareholders. So, they prefer higher dividends. However, dividend distribution tax is levied on companies.
    *   In case of higher dividend distribution tax rate, company prefers to pay lesser dividend.
    *   However, if tax rate is low, then company may declare higher dividend.
8.  **Stock Market Reaction:** Rate of dividend and market value of the shares are directly related to each other.
    *   A higher rate of dividend has a positive impact on the market price of shares, whereas, low rate of dividend may have a negative impact on the share prices in the stock market.
    *   So, management should consider the effect on the price of equity shares while determining the rate of dividend.
9.  **Access to Capital Market:** If a firm has easy access to the capital markets (either because it is financially strong or large in size), then it may depend less on retained earnings to finance their growth and follow a liberal dividend policy. Such firms tend to pay higher dividends. However, a firm with limited access to capital market, is likely to declare low dividend.
10. **Legal Constraints:** Every company has to comply with provisions of the Companies Act while declaring the dividend. Such provisions must be adhered to while declaring the dividends.
    *   For example, according to Section 123 of Companies Act, 2013, company has to provide for present and past depreciation on assets and create statutory reserves before declaring dividend.
11. **Contractual Constraints:** When a company borrows loan, then the lender may put certain restrictions on the company on payment of dividends in future. The companies are required to ensure that the payment of dividend does not violate the terms and conditions of the lender.
