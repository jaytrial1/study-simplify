This section explains the financing decision, which involves determining the mix of debt and equity used to fund a business, and discusses the factors influencing this decision.

# The Financing Decision

**Financing decision** is about the quantum of finance to be raised from various long-term sources. It deals with determination of various sources of finance, amount to be raised from each source and the cost of each source of finance.
The main sources of finance are:
(i) **Shareholders' or Owners' Funds:** They consist of equity capital and retained earnings.
(ii) **Borrowed Funds:** They refer to finance raised as debentures, loans or other forms of debt.

Financing decision is concerned with deciding how much to raise from owners' funds and how much from borrowed funds. Both the sources have their own peculiar merits and demerits.
*   Borrowed Funds are more risky as they involve commitment of fixed interest payment (whether or not firm has earned profit) & return of principal amount. On the other hand, owners' funds are less risky as there is no such commitment regarding payment of dividend or repayment of capital.
    *   **'Financial Risk'** is the risk that a firm would fail to meet its payment obligations. It means, there is some amount of financial risk in debt financing. The overall financial risk depends upon the proportion of debt in the total capital.
*   Financing decision involves analysing the risk and cost associated with each source of finance in order to decide a judicious mix of both debt and equity.
*   Borrowed funds are considered the cheapest source of finance as interest paid is a deductible expense for computation of tax liability. The cost of raising finance, known as **'Floatation Cost'** is also considered.

Thus, financing decision is concerned with deciding how much to be raised from each source. This decision determines the overall cost of capital and the financial risk of the enterprise.

## Factors Affecting Financing Decision

The various factors which affect the financing decisions are as follows:

1.  **Cost:** The cost of raising funds from different sources is different. A prudent financial manager generally prefers the cheapest source of finance.
2.  **Risk:** The risk associated with different sources of finance is different. More risk is associated with borrowed funds as compared to owners' funds. Finance Manager considers the risk involved with each source before taking any financing decision.

### Business Risk and Financial Risk

Every business faces two kinds of risks:
1.  **Business or Operating Risk:** risk of inability to meet fixed operating costs (rent, salary, insurance premium, etc).
2.  **Financial Risk:** risk of inability to meet fixed financial charges (interest payment, preference dividend and repayment obligations).

The total risk depends on both the business risk and the financial risk. If a firm's business risk is lower, then company may face financial risk, i.e. more debt capital can be used. However, in case of higher business risk, financial risk should be reduced by using less debt.

3.  **Floatation Costs:** Floatation Cost refers to the cost involved in issue of securities. For example, underwriting commission, brokerage, stamp duty, listing charges, etc. Firms prefer securities with least floatation cost.
4.  **Cash Flow Position:** A company with stronger cash flow position can take the advantage of debt (cheaper finance) as interest payment and repayment of principal amount can be easily made. However, equity should be preferred by companies with shortage of cash.
5.  **Fixed Operating Costs:** Firms with high fixed operating costs (like rent, insurance premium, salaries, etc.) must reduce fixed financing costs, like interest payment on debt. In such case, lower debt financing is better. Similarly, if fixed operating cost is less, more of debt financing may be preferred.
6.  **Control Considerations:** Issues of more equity shares may lead to dilution of management's control over the business. Debt financing has no such implication.
    *   Companies which are afraid of a takeover bid would prefer debt to equity. In other words, if existing shareholders want to retain complete control of the company, then debt should be preferred.
    *   However, if they do not mind loss of control, then the company may go for equity.
7.  **State of Capital Markets:** The conditions in the stock market also help in making choice of source of finance.
    *   In the boom period (i.e. when stock market is rising), it is easier to raise funds through issue of shares as more people invest in equity.
    *   However, depressed capital market may make issue of equity shares difficult for any company.
