# **MEANING OF MANAGEMENT**

Management is an activity which is necessary wherever there is a group of people working in an organisation. People perform diverse tasks in the organisations, but they all are working towards the same goal. Management aims at guiding their efforts towards achieving a common objective: a 'Goal'. So, management has to ensure that tasks are completed and goals are achieved (ie., Effectiveness) with the least amount of resources at a minimum cost (i.e., Efficiency). Thus, Management can be defined as a "Process' of getting things done with the aim of achieving goals 'Effectively' and 'Efficiently'.

Definitions of Management

Management is the creation of an internal environment where individuals working in a group can perform effectively and efficiently for the achievement of organisational goal.

\-Koontz and Donnell

Management is the process of designing and maintaining an environment in which individuals, working together in groups, efficiently accomplish selected aims. \-Koontz and Weihrich

Management is defined as the process of planning, organising, actuating and controlling an organisation's operations in order to achieve coordination of the human and material resources essential in the effective and efficient attainment of objectives. \-Trewelly and Newport

The concept of management insists on three essential elements:

1\. Management is a 'Process': Management is a process as it involves a series of inter-related functions, such as planning, organising, staffing, directing and controlling. All managers perform these functions to achieve goals.

2\. Management requires 'Effective' performance: Effectiveness in management is concerned with doing the right task and achieving goals on time. It aims at end result. For example, if a firm achieves its target production within time, then the firm is said to be effective.  
Being effective or doing work effectively basically means finishing the given task, irrespective of the fact how they are achieved.

3\. Management needs 'Efficiency': Efficiency means doing the task correctly and with minimum cost. It is not enough to just complete the tasks.

Efficiency involves cost-benefit analysis and relationship between inputs and outputs. It aims to achieve optimum utilisation of resources, i.e. maximum benefits (i.e., the output) by using less resources or inputs like money, materials, equipment and persons. Efficiency also increases when for the same output, fewer resources are used and less costs are incurred. Management aims at efficient use of resources as it reduces cost and ultimately leads to higher profits.

Relation between Effectiveness and Efficiency

The two terms 'Effectiveness' and 'Efficiency' mean different but they are interrelated.

"Effectiveness aims to achieve the goals within time.

"Efficiency' focuses on optimum use of resources to achieve the goals.

Effectiveness and Efficiency are two sides of the same coin. But, these two aspects need to be balanced and management at times, has to compromise with efficiency. For instance, it is easier to be effective if one ignores efficiency. For example, Suppose an organisation has a target to produce 5,000 units each month at a cost of 400 per unit. If the organisation is able to achieve the target production of 5,000 units, but at a higher cost of 450 per unit (say, due to double shifts of workers or due to power failure), then the organisation is Effective (as target is achieved on time), but Inefficient (as higher cost is spent). On the other hand, if cost of production comes out to 400 per unit, but only 4,600 units could be produced, than the organisation is efficient but not effective. Both the situations are not good for the organisation.

Usually, high efficiency is associated with high effectiveness, which is the aim of all managers. However, undue emphasis on high efficiency without being effective is also not desirable. Poor management is due to both inefficiency and ineffectiveness. So, Management should be both effective and efficient.
