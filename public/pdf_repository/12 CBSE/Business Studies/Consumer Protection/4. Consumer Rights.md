This section outlines the six fundamental rights granted to consumers under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019, explaining each right with examples.

# Consumer Rights

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 has provided six rights to consumers:

## 1. Right to Safety

Consumer has the right to be protected against goods and services hazardous to life, health, and property. This right is important for a safe and secure life.
*   For instance, electrical appliances manufactured with substandard products or not conforming to safety norms might cause serious injury.
*   Consumers have the right to safety against loss caused by such products.
*   Consumers are educated to use ISI marked electrical appliances as the ISI mark assures quality specifications.

## 2. Right to be Informed

Consumer has the right to complete information about the product they intend to buy, including ingredients, date of manufacture, price, quantity, directions for use, etc. Producers are legally obligated to provide this information on the package and label.
Such information protects consumers from deceptive advertising, misleading labelling, products with expired life, etc.

## 3. Right to Choose (or Right to be Assured)

Every consumer has the right to choose from a variety of goods and services at competitive prices. Marketers should offer a wide variety of products in terms of quality, brand, prices, size, etc., and allow the consumer to make a choice. Marketers should not force customers to buy a particular brand.

## 4. Right to be Heard

Consumer has the right to file a complaint and be heard in case of dissatisfaction with a good or service.
*   Many firms have set up consumer service and grievance cells.
*   Many consumer organisations help consumers with grievance redressal.
This right is crucial as the other rights are relevant only when a consumer can file a complaint.

## 5. Right to seek Redressal

Consumer has the right to get relief if the product or service falls short of expectations. The Act provides several reliefs, including replacement, removal of defect, compensation for loss or injury, etc.
For example, if a product doesn't conform to specified quantity or quality, the consumer can claim compensation. Consumer courts and redressal forums are set up for this purpose.

## 6. Right to Consumer Education

Consumer has the right to acquire knowledge and skills to be an informed consumer.
*   Consumers must know their rights and available reliefs.
*   Many consumer organisations and businesses educate consumers.
*   Government campaigns like 'Jago Grahak Jago' and inclusion of consumer education in curricula promote awareness.
