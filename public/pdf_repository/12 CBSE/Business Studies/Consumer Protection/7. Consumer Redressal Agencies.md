This section describes the three-tier quasi-judicial machinery established under the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 for handling consumer complaints: District, State, and National Commissions.

# Redressal Agencies

For the redressal of consumer grievances, the Consumer Protection Act, 2019 has set up a three-tier machinery:

## District Commission (or District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)

Established by the State Government in each district (possibly more than one).

*   **Composition:** A President and at least two other members (appointed by the state government).
*   **Jurisdiction:** To entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration does not exceed ₹1 crore.
*   **Settlement through Mediation:** The Commission may suggest mediation if a settlement seems possible. If mediation fails, the complaint proceeds.
*   **Procedure:** Refers complaint to the opposite party, sends goods for testing if needed, passes order after considering reports and facts.
*   **Appeal:** To the State Commission within 45 days of the order. 50% of the ordered amount must be deposited to appeal if payment is required.

## State Commission (or State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)

Established by the State Government in the State.

*   **Composition:** A President and at least four other members (appointed by the state government).
*   **Jurisdiction:**
    *   To entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds ₹1 crore but does not exceed ₹10 crore.
    *   To hear appeals against the orders of any District Commission within the State.
*   **Procedure:** Refers complaint to the opposite party, sends goods for testing if needed, passes order after considering reports and facts.

*   **Appeal:** To the National Commission within 30 days of the order. 50% of the ordered amount must be deposited to appeal if payment is required.

## National Commission (or National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission)

Established by the Central Government.

*   **Composition:** A President and at least four other members (appointed by the central government).
*   **Jurisdiction:**
    *   To entertain complaints where the value of goods or services paid as consideration exceeds ₹10 crore.
    *   To hear appeals against the orders of any State Commission.
*   **Procedure:** Refers complaint to the opposite party, sends goods for testing if needed, passes order after considering reports and facts.
*   **Appeal:** To the Supreme Court within 30 days of the order. 50% of the ordered amount must be deposited to appeal if payment is required.

**Important Note:** Only cases where the value of goods or services exceeds ₹10 crore and the aggrieved party is dissatisfied with the National Commission's order can be appealed in the Supreme Court. An appeal from a District Commission order goes to the State Commission, then to the National Commission, but no further to the Supreme Court unless it's a case of original jurisdiction for the National Commission (exceeding ₹10 crore).

**Author's Note:** The Act allows State Commission, National Commission, and Supreme Court to entertain appeals after the prescribed period if there were sufficient reasons for delay. However, standard time limits for appeals are:
*   To State Commission (from District Commission): 45 Days
*   To National Commission (from State Commission): 30 Days
*   To Supreme Court (from National Commission): 30 Days
