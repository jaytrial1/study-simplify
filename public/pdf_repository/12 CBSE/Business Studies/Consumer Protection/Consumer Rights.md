# CONSUMER RIGHTS

The Consumer Protection Act, 2019 has provided six rights to consumers:

## 1. Right to Safety

According to this right, consumer has a right to be protected against goods and services which are hazardous to life, health and property. Right to safety is important for safe and secure life.

- For instance, electrical appliances which are manufactured with substandard products or do not conform to the safety norms, might cause serious injury.
- Consumer has the right to safety against any loss caused by such products.
- Consumers are educated that they should use ISI marked electrical appliances as ISI mark gives an assurance of such products meeting quality specifications.


## 2. Right to be Informed

According to this right, consumer has a right to have complete information about the product he intends to buy, including its ingredients, date of manufacture, price, quantity, directions for use, etc. In India, producers are under legal obligation to provide such information on the package and label of the product.
Such information protects the consumers from deceptive advertising, misleading labelling, products with expired life, etc.

## 3. Right to Choose (or Right to be Assured) 

According to this right, every consumer has the right to choose from a variety of goods and services at competitive prices. This implies that marketers should offer wide variety of products in terms of quality, brand, prices, size, etc. and allow the consumer to make a choice from amongst these. Marketer should not force the customer to buy a particular brand.

## 4. Right to be Heard

According to this right, consumer has the right to file a complaint and to be heard in case of dissatisfaction with a good or a service.

- Many firms have also set up their own consumer service and grievance cells.
- Many consumer organisations are also working towards this direction and helping consumers in redressal of their grievances.
This right is very important because, all the earlier three rights are relevant only when consumer has a right to file the complaint.


## 5. Right to seek Redressal

According to this right, consumer has the right to get relief in case the product or service falls short of his expectations.
The Consumer Protection Act provides a number of reliefs to the consumers including replacement of the product, removal of defect in the product, compensation paid for any loss or injury suffered by the consumer, etc.
For example, if a product does not confirm to the specified quantity or quality, then consumer has the right to claim compensation. A number of consumer courts \& redressal forums have been set up for this purpose.

## 6. Right to Consumer Education

According to this right, consumer has the right to acquire knowledge and skills to be an informed consumer.

- Consumer must know the rights and reliefs available to him in case a product falls short of his expectations.
- Many consumer organisations and business firms are also taking an active part in educating consumers.
- For example, to promote awareness, Government has taken several measures, such as campaign of 'Jago Grahak Jago' or inclusion of consumer education in school and university courses.

The Consumer Protection Act by conferring these rights on the Consumers empowers them to fight against any unscrupulous, exploitative and unfair trade practices adopted by sellers.
Government is continuously encouraging campaign of 'Jago Grahak Jago' to promote awareness among consumers.


## 12.5 CONSUMER RESPONSIBILITIES 

In addition to rights, consumer has certain responsibilities also. Rights and responsibilities are two sides of the same coin. Unless a consumer fulfills these responsibilities, it may not be possible for him to enforce his rights. So, A consumer should keep in mind the following responsibilities while purchasing, using and consuming goods and services:

1. Consumer should have knowledge about market: Consumer should be aware about various goods and services available in the market so that an intelligent and wise choice can be made.
2. Consumer must be Quality Conscious: Consumer should buy only standardised goods as they provide quality assurance. While purchasing goods or services, consumer must look for following quality marks:
3. Use the Products Safely: Consumer should learn about the risks associated with products or services, follow manufacturer's instructions and use the products safely.
4. Consumer must refer labels carefully: While purchasing a product, consumer must read etc.
5. Insist on Cash Memo: Consumer must ask for a cash memo on purchase of goods or services as it serves as a proof of purchase at the time of filing a complaint.

6. Consumer must be Honest: Consumer should be honest in his dealings. He should buy only legal goods and services and discourage malpractices like black-marketing, hoarding, etc.
7. Consumer must file a complaint: Consumer should file a complaint in an appropriate consumer forum in case of any defect in the product or deficiency in the service. Sometimes, consumer ignores the loss he has suffered on purchase of a defective product and does not file the complaint. It encourages the corrupt businessman to supply defective or low standard products. So, the complaint must be filed even for a small loss. Such awareness of consumer will make the businessman more conscious to supply quality product.
8. Form Consumer Organisations: Consumers should form consumer societies which could play an active part in educating them and safeguarding their interests.
9. Respect the Environment: Consumer must save the environment by avoiding waste, littering and contribution to pollution.
10. Assert yourself to ensure that you get a fair deal.

Rights and Responsibilities go simultaneously. If we ask for our rights, we should fulfil the responsibilities first.
