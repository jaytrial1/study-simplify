# Dematerialisation, Depository, and Trading Procedure

## Dematerialisation

**Dematerialisation** is the process where physical securities held by an investor are cancelled and replaced with an electronic entry or balance in an account. This allows securities to be held and traded in electronic form.

*   To hold securities in dematerialised form, an investor must open a **demat account** with a **depository**.
*   Currently, all Initial Public Offers (IPOs) are issued in dematerialised form, and the vast majority of trading turnover is settled electronically.
*   SEBI has mandated settlement in demat form for certain securities.
*   Holding shares in demat form is convenient, similar to a bank account, eliminating risks of loss, theft, or forgery.
*   Physical shares can be converted to electronic form (dematerialisation), and electronic holdings can be converted back to physical certificates (rematerialisation).
*   Dematerialisation allows shares to be transferred electronically between accounts and facilitates settlement of trades through a single account.
*   Demat securities can also be pledged or hypothecated to obtain loans.
*   The broker is responsible for crediting the correct number of shares to the investor's demat account.

## Depository

A **Depository** is an institution or organisation that holds securities (like shares, debentures, bonds) of investors in **electronic form**.

*   It functions like a bank, keeping securities electronically on behalf of the investor.
*   Investors can open a securities account with a depository, deposit shares, withdraw or sell them, and give instructions for delivery or receipt of shares.
*   It is a technology-driven electronic storage system.
*   Eliminates paperwork, leading to faster and more efficient transaction settlement.

In India, there are two main depositories:
1.  **National Securities Depositories Limited (NSDL):** The first and largest operational depository, promoted as a joint venture by IDBI, UTI, and the National Stock Exchange.
2.  **Central Depository Services Limited (CDSL):** The second depository, promoted by the Bombay Stock Exchange and the Bank of India.

Both depositories operate through intermediaries called **Depository Participants (DPs)**.

*   A **Depository Participant (DP)** acts as an intermediary between the investor and the Depository (NSDL or CDSL). DPs are authorised to maintain accounts of dematerialised shares.
*   Financial institutions, banks, clearing corporations, stock brokers, and non-banking finance corporations can become Depository Participants.
*   When an investor buys or sells securities through a broker, bank, or NBFC, that entity often acts as the DP, completing the necessary formalities.

## Settlement of Transactions

*   Historically, stock exchange positions were settled on weekly or fortnightly cycles, increasing trading volume and liquidity but also leading to speculation, sudden price changes, and broker defaults.
*   Since 2003, a **'Rolling Settlement System'** was introduced, initially on a T+2 basis (settlement within 2 days after trade date). This means a trade on Monday must be settled by Wednesday.
*   **As of October 1st, 2023, all shares are covered under the Rolling Settlement System on a T+1 basis.** This means securities must be settled on the first business day after a trade is executed.

## Trading Procedure on a Stock Exchange (Steps)

The electronic screen-based trading system involves the following steps for buying and selling securities:

1.  **Selection of a Broker:** The investor chooses a registered broker or sub-broker and enters into a formal agreement (broker-client agreement and client registration form). The investor provides mandatory details like PAN, date of birth, address, occupation, bank account details, and demat account details. The broker then opens a trading account for the investor.

2.  **Opening Demat Account with Depository:** The investor opens a 'Demat' or 'Beneficial Owner' (BO) account with a depository participant to hold securities electronically. A bank account is also needed for cash transactions.
3.  **Placing the Order:** The investor instructs the broker to buy or sell shares, specifying the number of shares and desired price. The broker issues an order confirmation slip.
4.  **Matching Share and Price:** The broker connects online to the stock exchange system to find a match for the order based on available shares and prices.
5.  **Executing the Order:** When a match is found at the specified price, the order is executed electronically. The broker receives confirmation and issues a trade confirmation slip to the investor.
6.  **Issue of Contract Note:** Within 24 hours of execution, the broker issues a **Contract Note**. This legal document contains transaction details (shares, price, date, time, brokerage) and is crucial for settling disputes. Each transaction is assigned a Unique Order Code number by the exchange, printed on the note.
7.  **Delivery of Shares or Making Payment:** The investor must deliver shares (if selling) or pay cash (if buying). This is done immediately after receiving the contract note or before the **Pay-in Day**.
8.  **Settlement Cycle (Pay-in Day):** Cash is paid or securities are delivered on the pay-in day, which is before the final settlement day (T+1 as of Oct 1, 2023).
9.  **Pay-out Day Working:** On the settlement day (T+1), the exchange delivers shares or makes payment to the respective brokers. This is the Pay-out Day. The broker then has to make payment to the investor within 24 hours of receiving funds from the exchange.
10. **Delivery of Shares in Demat Form:** Shares are delivered electronically directly to the investor's demat account. The investor provides demat account details and instructs their DP to receive the securities.
