This section defines the capital market, its role in long-term finance, its essential requirements, and key characteristics.

# The Capital Market

The **Capital Market** refers to the network of organisations, institutions, and instruments that provide **medium and long-term funds**.

*   It serves as a channel through which community savings are made available to industrial and commercial enterprises and the public.
*   It encourages individuals to invest small savings productively, contributing to economic growth and development.
*   Key components include development banks, commercial banks, and stock exchanges.
*   Long-term funds are raised and invested as both **debt** (Debentures, Bonds, Public deposits) and **equity** (Shares).
*   A well-functioning capital market is essential for economic development.

It's important to note that the capital market **excludes** instruments or institutions providing short-term finance (up to one year), which fall under the money market.

## Requisites of an Ideal Capital Market

An ideal capital market functions best when:
*   Finance is available at a reasonable cost.
*   Financial institutions are well-developed.
*   Market operations are fair, competitive, and transparent.
*   Sufficient information is provided to investors.
*   Transaction costs are minimised.
*   Capital is allocated to its most productive uses.

## Nature or Features of Capital Market

1.  **Duration of Investments:** Deals with medium and long-term securities maturing in **more than one year**.
2.  **Financial Instruments:** Capital flows through instruments like **shares, debentures, bonds, public deposits**, etc.
3.  **Segments:** Composed of two main segments: the **Primary Market** (for new issues) and the **Secondary Market** (for trading existing securities).
4.  **Use of Intermediaries:** Functions through various intermediaries like underwriters, bankers, and stock brokers.
5.  **Helps in Capital Formation:** Facilitates the flow of surplus funds from investors to borrowers (entrepreneurs), leading to **capital formation**.
6.  **Provides Liquidity:** Offers liquidity as securities can be bought and sold on the stock exchange.
