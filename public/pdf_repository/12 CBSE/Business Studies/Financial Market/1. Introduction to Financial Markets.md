# Introduction to Financial Markets

A business operates within an economic system comprising households (savers) and business firms (investors). The financial market serves as a crucial **intermediary** or **link** between these two sectors.

It enables savers to invest their surplus funds and allows investors to borrow funds for their requirements. In doing so, the financial market performs an **allocative function**.

The allocative function of the financial market involves mobilising funds between savers and investors, directing available funds towards the most productive investment opportunities. This process leads to two key outcomes:
*   A higher rate of return is offered to households for their savings.
*   Scarce resources are efficiently allocated to firms that demonstrate the highest productivity for the economy.

Funds can be mobilised from households to business firms through two primary mechanisms:
1.  **Banks:** Households deposit funds with banks, which then lend these funds to businesses.
2.  **Financial Markets:** Households directly provide funds to businesses by purchasing securities from the financial market.

The process of allocating funds through these mechanisms is termed **Financial Intermediation**. Both banks and financial markets offer various options for savers to channel their savings into investments. This chapter focuses specifically on aspects of the Financial Market.
