This section presents a comparison between the capital market and the money market based on various parameters.

# Comparing Capital Market and Money Market

| Basis              | Capital Market                                                                                                                               | Money Market                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| :----------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Participants** | Includes financial institutions, banks, corporate entities, foreign investors, and ordinary retail investors from the public.              | Primarily institutional participants such as RBI, banks, financial institutions, and finance companies. Individual investors typically do not transact in the secondary money market.                         |
| **2. Instruments** | Equity Shares, Debentures, Bonds, Preference Shares, etc.                                                                                    | Treasury Bills, Call Money, Commercial Paper, etc.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **3. Investment**  | Often requires less investment outlay per security (e.g., ₹ 10, ₹ 100 face value) with minimum trading lots, making it accessible to small savers. | Requires huge financial investment as instruments are typically quite expensive.                                                                                                                            |
| **4. Duration**    | Deals in medium and long-term securities (maturity > 1 year).                                                                                | Deals in short-term securities (maximum tenure of 1 year, can be for a single day).                                                                                                                         |
| **5. Liquidity**   | Securities are liquid as they are marketable on the stock exchange. However, liquidity can vary depending on how actively a specific security is traded. | Instruments generally have a higher degree of liquidity, partly facilitated by institutions like DFHI providing a ready market.                                                                             |
| **6. Safety**      | Instruments are riskier regarding returns and principal repayment due to potential company performance issues or fraudulent actions by promoters. | Instruments are generally safer due to their shorter duration and the financial soundness of the typical issuers (like RBI, large banks, and creditworthy corporations).                                       |
| **7. Expected Return** | Expected return is potentially high, with chances of capital gains in equity shares and sharing company prosperity through dividends and bonus issues in the long run. | Expected return is generally less due to the short investment duration.                                                                                                                                     |
