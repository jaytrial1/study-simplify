This section details the various ways companies can issue new securities in the primary market.

# Methods of Floating New Issues

Companies can use several methods to issue new securities in the primary market:

## Offer Through Prospectus

*   The most popular method for public companies to raise funds.
*   The company invites the public to subscribe to its securities by issuing a **prospectus**, which is advertised in media.
*   May involve services of underwriters or brokers.
*   The issue must be listed on at least one stock exchange.
*   Prospectus content must comply with the Companies Act and SEBI guidelines for disclosure and investor protection.

## Offer for Sale

*   Securities are initially issued to intermediaries (like issue houses, stock brokers) at a fixed price, **not directly to the public**.
*   These intermediaries then **resell** the securities to the investing public at a higher price.
*   This method helps the company avoid the formalities and complexities of a direct public issue.

## Private Placement

*   Securities are sold to a **select group of institutional investors and individuals**, rather than the general public.
*   Allows for quicker capital raising compared to public issues.
*   More economical as it reduces mandatory and non-mandatory expenses associated with public issues.
*   Often preferred by companies that cannot afford a full public issue, selling privately to entities like UTI, LIC, GIC, etc.

## Rights Issue

*   Applicable only to **existing companies**.
*   New shares are offered to the company's **existing shareholders** in proportion to the shares they already hold.
*   Provides existing shareholders a **pre-emptive right** to subscribe to the new issue.
*   If existing shareholders do not subscribe within the specified time, the offer can be opened to the general public.
*   This is a compulsory requirement under the Companies Act for existing companies issuing new shares.

## E-IPOs

*   Companies issue capital to the public through the **online system of a stock exchange**.
*   An Initial Public Offer (IPO) conducted electronically is called an E-IPO.
*   The issuing company must agree with a stock exchange and appoint SEBI-registered brokers to accept applications and place orders.
*   A registrar with electronic connectivity to the stock exchange is also appointed.
*   Securities must be listed on an exchange other than the one used for the electronic offer.
*   A lead manager coordinates all activities of intermediaries involved in the issue.
