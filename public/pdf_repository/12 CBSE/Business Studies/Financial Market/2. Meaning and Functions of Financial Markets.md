This section defines what a financial market is and outlines its essential functions in facilitating economic activity.

# Meaning and Functions of Financial Markets

A **Financial Market** encompasses the entire network of organisations and institutions involved in providing short, medium, and long-term funds. Fundamentally, it is a market for the **creation and exchange of financial assets**.

Financial markets exist wherever financial transactions take place. These transactions can involve the *creation* of new financial assets (like the initial issue of securities by a company) or the *exchange* of existing financial assets (like buying and selling previously issued securities).

## Functions of Financial Market

Financial markets perform four crucial functions in the allocation of scarce resources within an economy:

1.  **Mobilising Savings and Channelising into Productive Uses (Allocative Function):** The financial market facilitates the transfer of savings from savers to investors. It offers various investment options to savers, helping them direct their surplus funds towards the most productive uses.
2.  **Facilitating Price Discovery:** Prices of financial assets are determined by the interaction of demand (from business firms) and supply (from households) within the financial market. This interaction establishes a market price for the assets being traded.
3.  **Providing Liquidity to Financial Assets:** Financial markets enable the easy purchase and sale of financial assets. This provides **liquidity**, meaning assets can be readily converted into cash when needed. Holders of assets can easily sell them through the market mechanism.
4.  **Reducing the Cost of Transactions:** Financial markets provide valuable information about traded securities, saving buyers and sellers time, effort, and money they would otherwise spend searching for each other. They offer a common platform for fulfilling individual needs.
