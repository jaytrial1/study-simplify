# Detailed Money Market Instruments

Here are descriptions of the common instruments traded in the money market:

## Treasury Bill (T-bill)

*   A short-term borrowing instrument issued by the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** on behalf of the Indian Government.
*   Also known as **'Zero Coupon Bonds'**. They are issued to the public or financial institutions as Promissory Notes.
*   Highly liquid, offering assured yield and negligible risk of default due to the issuer (RBI/Government).
*   Available for a minimum amount of ₹ 25,000 and in multiples thereof.
*   Maturity period ranges from **14 to 364 days**.
*   Issued at a price **less than face value** (discount) and redeemed at face value (par) on maturity. The difference is the interest earned (discount).
*   *Example:* Purchasing a T-bill with a face value of ₹ 1,00,000 for ₹ 96,000 yields ₹ 4,000 interest upon redemption at ₹ 1,00,000.

## Commercial Paper

*   A short-term, **unsecured, negotiable promissory note** with a fixed maturity period.
*   Issued by large and creditworthy companies to raise short-term funds, often at lower interest rates than bank loans.
*   **Negotiable**, meaning freely transferable by endorsement and delivery.
*   Typically has a maturity period of **15 days to one year**.
*   Sold at a **discount** and redeemed at par.
*   Used for short-term funding needs like seasonal or working capital requirements.
*   Also used for **'Bridge Financing'**: raising funds to cover floatation costs (brokerage, prospectus costs, etc.) incurred when preparing to raise long-term finance through shares or debentures.
*   Issued for investments of at least ₹ 5 lakhs and in multiples thereof.

## Call Money

*   Short-term finance repayable on **demand**.
*   Maturity period varies from **1 day to 15 days**.
*   Primarily used for **inter-bank transactions**.
*   Banks use call money to maintain their **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** with the RBI.
*   The interest rate is called the **'Call Rate'**, which is highly volatile, changing frequently.
*   There is an inverse relationship between the call rate and rates on other short-term instruments like Certificates of Deposit and Commercial Paper. A high call rate makes other sources cheaper, encouraging banks to borrow elsewhere.

## Certificate of Deposit

*   An **unsecured, negotiable, short-term instrument in bearer form**.
*   Issued by **commercial banks and development financial institutions** against deposits by companies and institutions.
*   Can be issued to individuals and corporations, particularly during periods of tight liquidity when bank deposit growth is slow but credit demand is high.
*   Helps in raising large sums for short periods.
*   Maturity period ranges from **91 days to one year**.

## Commercial Bill

*   A **bill of exchange** used by businesses to finance working capital needs.
*   A short-term, **negotiable, self-liquidating instrument** used to finance credit sales.
*   When goods are sold on credit, the seller can draw a bill of exchange on the buyer.
*   Once accepted by the buyer, it becomes a **trade bill**.
*   If the trade bill is accepted by a commercial bank, it is known as a **commercial bill**.
*   Can be **discounted with a bank** before the due date if the seller needs funds early. The bank pays the seller the bill amount minus a discount charge.
