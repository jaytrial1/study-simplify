# Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)

**SEBI** was initially established by the Indian Government in 1988 under the Finance Ministry. It gained statutory status with perpetual succession and a common seal under the **Securities and Exchange Board of India Act, 1992**. SEBI was created to promote the orderly and healthy growth of the securities market and protect investors.

## Reasons for the Establishment of SEBI

In the 1980s, the capital market experienced significant growth with increasing public participation. However, this growth was accompanied by various **malpractices** by companies, brokers, and merchant bankers, including:
*   Unofficial private placements.
*   **Price Rigging:** Manipulating security prices to inflate or depress them.
*   **Insider Trading:** Using sensitive, non-public information for personal profit by company insiders (Directors, Promoters, etc.).
*   Charging unofficial premiums on new issues.
*   Non-adherence to Companies Act provisions.
*   Violation of Stock Exchange rules and regulations.
*   Delays in delivery of shares.

These malpractices eroded investor confidence and increased grievances. Existing mechanisms (Government and stock exchanges) were insufficient due to a lack of proper penal provisions. Consequently, the Government of India established SEBI as a dedicated regulatory body.

## Purpose and Role of SEBI

SEBI's fundamental purpose is to facilitate the efficient mobilisation and allocation of resources through the securities market. It aims to stimulate competition and encourage innovation within the market environment, which includes rules, institutions, instruments, practices, infrastructure, and policy framework.

SEBI primarily aims to meet the needs of three key groups in the securities market:

1.  **Issuers:** To provide a market where they can confidently raise finance easily, fairly, and efficiently.
2.  **Investors:** To protect their rights and interests by ensuring adequate, accurate, and continuous disclosure of information.
3.  **Intermediaries:** To offer a competitive, professional, and expanding market with sufficient infrastructure, enabling them to provide better service to investors and issuers.

## Objectives of SEBI

SEBI's overall objective is to protect investor interests and promote and regulate the securities market. Its specific objectives include:
1.  Regulating stock exchanges and the securities industry for orderly functioning.
2.  Protecting investor rights and interests, and providing guidance and education.
3.  Preventing trading malpractices and balancing self-regulation by the industry with statutory regulation.
4.  Regulating and developing a code of conduct for intermediaries (brokers, merchant bankers, etc.) to enhance their competitiveness and professionalism.
