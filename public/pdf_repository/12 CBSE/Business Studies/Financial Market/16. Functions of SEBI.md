## 1. Regulatory Functions

These functions are aimed at regulating the securities market's operations:
*   Registering brokers, sub-brokers, and other market participants.
*   Registering collective investment schemes and Mutual Funds.
*   Regulating stock bankers, portfolio exchanges, underwriters, merchant bankers, and business activities on stock exchanges and other securities markets.
*   Regulating takeover bids by companies.
*   Requesting information, undertaking inspections, conducting enquiries, and auditing stock exchanges and intermediaries.
*   Levying fees and other charges as per the Act's purpose.
*   Exercising powers delegated by the Government of India under the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act 1956.

## 2. Development Functions

These functions are focused on promoting the growth and development of the securities market:
*   Providing training for intermediaries in the securities market.
*   Conducting research and publishing information beneficial to market participants.
*   Taking measures to develop capital markets through a flexible approach.

## 3. Protective Functions

These functions are designed to safeguard the interests of investors:
*   Prohibiting fraudulent and unfair trade practices, such as misleading statements, manipulations, and Price Rigging.
*   Controlling Insider Trading and imposing penalties for such practices.
*   Undertaking steps specifically for investor protection.
*   Promoting fair practices and a code of conduct in the securities market.
