This section compares and contrasts the contributions of F.W. Taylor and Henri Fayol to the field of management, highlighting their differences in perspective, focus, and applicability, as well as their similarities.

# Comparison: Taylor vs Fayol

Both Taylor and Fayol made significant contributions to management knowledge, forming the basis for modern management practices. Here are some key points of difference:

| Basis                 | F.W. Taylor                                               | Henri Fayol                                                 |
| :-------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Perspective        | Principles and techniques focused on the lower level (shop floor). | Principles developed from the perspective of top level management. |
| 2. Unity of Command   | Did not strictly follow; advocated functional foremanship (multiple superiors). | Strictly followed this principle (one superior).            |
| 3. Applicability      | Techniques are less flexible, applied in specialised production situations. | Principles are universally applicable and more flexible.    |
| 4. Basis of formation | Work based on observations and experiments.               | Principles based on personal experiences and observations.  |
| 5. Focus              | Focused on increasing worker productivity and efficiency.   | Focused on improving overall administration of the organisation. |
| 6. Personality        | A scientist, known as 'Father of Scientific Management'.  | A practitioner, known as 'Father of General Management'.    |
| 7. Expression         | Contribution expressed as scientific management.            | Contribution expressed as general theory of administration. |
| 8. Human Element      | Gave more importance to production increase than human element. | Gave importance to human element (principles like equity, initiative, stability). |
| 9. Emphasis           | Laid greater emphasis on standardisation of work and tools. | Emphasised principles and theory of general administration and managerial functions. |

## Similarities between Taylor and Fayol

1.  Both stressed mutual cooperation between employers and employees.
2.  Both recognised the importance of personnel and their management at all levels.
3.  Both were engineers and developed their ideas through practical experience.
4.  Both suggested the principle of division of work for specialisation.
5.  Both aimed to secure maximum output from available resources.
