# F. W. TAYLOR AND SCIENTIFIC MANAGEMENT 

## About F. W. Taylor

Fredrick Winslow Taylor (March 20, 1856 - March 21, 1915) was an American Mechanical Engineer.

- In 1874, he became an apprentice mechanist and rose to the position of chief engineer in Bethlehem Steel Company, USA. During his career of 26 years, he experimented with his ideas and made significant contribution to the management theory.
- He was one of the intellectual leaders of the efficiency movement and was highly influential in reshaping the factory system of production.


## Contributions of F. W. Taylor

- Taylor gave a concrete shape to the theory of scientific management. He was the first one to emphasise the importance of scientific approach managing an enterprise instead of hit and trial method.
- Taylor's major contribution was his two important books: 'Shop Management' and 'Principles of Scientific Management', which were published in 1903 and 1911 respectively.
- For his contributions, he is well known as the 'Father of the Scientific Management'.


## Scientific Management

## Introduction

In the earlier days of Industrial Revolution, managers relied on personal judgment in attending to the problems they confronted in the course of managing their work. This is referred to as 'rule of thumb'. This 'rule of thumb' method suffered from the limitation of Trial and Error approach. Taylor insisted on scientific management as opposed to rule of thumb. He divided human activity into small parts and found out that it could be done effectively in less time and with increased productivity. It implies conducting business activities according to standardised tools, methods and trained personnel in order to increase the output, improve its quality and reduce costs and wastage.

## Meaning of Scientific Management

Scientific Management refers to use of scientific, systematic, objective and logical principles and techniques to various managerial functions. It stresses that there is always 'one best way' of doing a work. Scientific Management can also be defined as 'application of science for each and ever element of management'.

## Definitions of Scientific Management 

- Scientific Management means knowing exactly what you want men to do and seeing that they do it in the best and the cheapest way.
-F. W. Taylor
- It is the organised study of work, the analysis of work into its simplest elements and the systematic improvement of the worker's performance of each element.
—Peter F. Drucker

The Bethlehem Steel company where Taylor himself worked achieved three-fold increase in productivity by application of scientific management principles. So, it would be in order to discuss these principles.
