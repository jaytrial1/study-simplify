# TAYLOR VS FAYOL - A COMPARISON 

Both, Taylor and Fayol have contributed immensely to the knowledge of management, which has formed a basis for further practice by managers. Let us discuss some of the points of difference between their contributions.

| Basis | F. W. Taylor | Henri Fayol |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 1. Perspective | Taylor gave the principles and techniques keeping in mind lower level. | Fayol's principles were developed from the perspective of top level. |
| 2. Unity of Command | Taylor did not follow this principle and insisted on functional foremanship. | This principle is strictly followed. |
| 3. Applicability | Taylor's techniques are less flexible and can be applied in specialised situations. | Fayol's principles are universally applicable as they are more flexible. |
| 4. Basis of formation | Taylor's work is based on observations and experiments. | Fayol's principles are based on personal experiences. |
| 5. Focus | Taylor focused on increasing productivity of workers. | Fayol focused on improving overall administration of the organisation. |
| 6. Personality | Taylor was a scientist and is well known as the 'Father of the Scientific Management'. | Fayol was the practitioner and is known as the 'Father of General Management'. |
| 7. Expression | Taylor's contribution is expressed as scientific management. | Fayol's contribution is expressed as general theory of administration. |

| 8. Human <br> Element | Taylor gave more importance to increase in <br> production as compared to human element. | Fayol gave importance to human element in <br> principles like equity, initiative and stability <br> of tenure. |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 9. Emphasis | Taylor laid greater emphasis on <br> standardisation of work and tools. | Fayol emphasised on principles and theory of <br> general administration and also the functions <br> of managers. |

## Similarities between Taylor and Fayol 

1. Both of them stressed on mutual cooperation between employers and employees.
2. Both observed the importance of personnel and its management at all levels.
3. Both were engineers by profession and developed their ideas through practical experience.
4. Both of them suggested the principle of division of work for specialisation.
5. The aim of both of them was to secure maximum from available resources.
