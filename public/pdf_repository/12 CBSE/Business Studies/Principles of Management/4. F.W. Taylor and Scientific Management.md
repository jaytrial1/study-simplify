# F.W. Taylor and Scientific Management

## About F. W. Taylor

Fredrick Winslow Taylor (March 20, 1856 - March 21, 1915) was an American Mechanical Engineer.

*   He started as an apprentice machinist in 1874 and rose to Chief Engineer at Bethlehem Steel Company, USA. Over 26 years, he experimented with his ideas, significantly contributing to management theory.
*   He was a leading figure in the efficiency movement, influential in reshaping factory production systems.

## Scientific Management

### Introduction

In the early days of the Industrial Revolution, managers often relied on personal judgment or 'rule of thumb' to handle problems. This approach was limited by trial and error. Taylor advocated for scientific management as an alternative to the rule of thumb. He proposed dividing human activity into small parts to find ways to perform them effectively, in less time, and with increased productivity. Scientific management involves conducting business activities using standardised tools, methods, and trained personnel to increase output, improve quality, and reduce costs and wastage.

### Meaning of Scientific Management

**Scientific Management** refers to the use of scientific, systematic, objective, and logical principles and techniques in various managerial functions. It posits that there is always 'one best way' of doing a work. It can also be defined as the 'application of science for each and every element of management'.

## Definitions of Scientific Management

*   Scientific Management means knowing exactly what you want men to do and seeing that they do it in the best and the cheapest way. - F. W. Taylor
*   It is the organised study of work, the analysis of work into its simplest elements and the systematic improvement of the worker's performance of each element. - Peter F. Drucker

The application of scientific management principles at Bethlehem Steel company, where Taylor worked, reportedly led to a three-fold increase in productivity.
