This section defines management principles, explains how they are derived through observation and experimentation, and outlines their key characteristics or nature.

# Meaning of Principles of Management

**Principles of Management** are broad and general guidelines for decision making and behaviour of managers.

They serve as general practical guidelines for managers in making decisions, taking action, and solving various problems systematically. They are *not* rigid rules, but rather a guide to action.

These principles are applicable to various types of organisations, including businesses, hospitals, clubs, and educational institutions.

*   Management Principles have been derived and developed on the basis of observation, experimentation, and personal experience of management thinkers.

## Derivation of Management Principles

Principles of Management are developed through observation. The guidelines derived from observation are repeatedly experimented with to test their validity in different organisations and environments. If they consistently provide the same result under various conditions, they are then designated as management principles.

For example, observing that subordinates receiving orders from only one boss (principle of 'Unity of Command') reduces confusion and conflict is then tested in different organisations. If this consistently yields the same positive result, the guideline is accepted as a management principle.

*   These principles contribute to the development of management as both a science and an art. The derivation of principles can be seen as a matter of science, while their creative application is an art.
*   These principles are guidelines for action. While management functions (*Planning, Organising, Staffing, Directing, Controlling*) are the actions themselves, principles help managers make decisions *while* performing these functions.

## Management Principles Vs Pure Science Principles

Management principles are not as exact as principles found in Physics or Chemistry.

| Basis                  | Management Principles                                     | Pure Science Principles                                  |
| :--------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Flexibility         | They are flexible.                                        | They are rigid in nature.                                |
| 2. Application         | They have to be applied creatively as they deal with unpredictable human behaviour. | They are applied in a static or absolute manner.         |
| 3. Relation with Environment | They have to keep pace with the changing environment as human behaviour is never static. | They are not required to keep pace with the changing environment as they do not change with time. |

## Management Principles Vs Management Techniques

Principles of management should not be confused with techniques of management.

| Basis          | Management Principles                                     | Management Techniques                                   |
| :------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Meaning     | They are guidelines to take decisions or actions while practicing techniques. | They are procedures, which involve a series of steps to achieve desired goals. |
| 2. Flexibility | They are flexible.                                        | They are not so flexible.                               |

## Management Principles Vs Values

While practicing principles of management, values cannot be neglected as businesses must fulfil social and ethical responsibilities towards society. Management principles are different from values.

| Basis      | Management Principles                              | Values                                                |
| :--------- | :------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Meaning | They are guidelines to take decisions.             | Values are general rules for behaviour of individuals in the society. |
| 2. Formation | They are formed after research in work situations. | They are formed through common practice.              |
| 3. Nature  | They are technical in nature.                      | They are ethical in nature.                           |

# Nature of Management Principles

The following characteristics summarise the basic nature of principles of management:

1.  **Universal Applicability:** Management principles can be applied to all kinds of organisations, regardless of size (small or large) and nature (business, government, or private). However, the extent of application varies depending on the organisation's nature and size.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Division of Work', which advocates dividing work into small tasks and training employees for specialisation, applies to all organisation types. The *extent* of division may differ. In a bank, tasks like cash collection and payments are done by different people, leading to specialisation. In a company, separate departments for production, finance, and marketing exist for the same reason.

2.  **General Guidelines:** Management principles serve as guidelines for action but are not rigid, tailor-made solutions. Real business situations are complex and dynamic, so principles cannot be applied as strictly as those in physical sciences.
    *   *Example:* If a conflict arises between two departments, the principle of 'Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest' guides the manager to prioritize the organisation's overall goals. This principle offers guidance but not a specific, ready-made solution for every conflict.

3.  **Formed by Practice and Experimentation:** Management principles are evolutionary, developed through observations, experimentation, and the experience of management thinkers.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Discipline' is based on the common experience that discipline is essential for achieving any purpose. Experiments might also be conducted, such as studying the effect of improved physical conditions on reducing worker fatigue.

4.  **Flexible:** Management principles are not rigid like pure science principles. They are flexible and can be modified by the manager according to the specific situation. They should not be applied blindly, even in seemingly identical situations. Principles are like tools, and the manager must decide which principle to use in each circumstance.
    *   *Example:* The degree of centralisation or decentralisation of authority depends on the specific situation and circumstances of each enterprise, demonstrating flexibility.

5.  **Mainly Behavioural:** Principles of management primarily aim to influence human behaviour, as work is done by human beings. Human behaviour is complex and dynamic. Management principles seek to influence this unpredictable behaviour to achieve organisational goals.
    *   They also help in understanding the relationship between human and material resources in achieving organisational purposes.
    *   *Example:* When planning a factory layout, the principle of 'Order' would require aligning workflows with the flow of materials and movement of people, focusing on human interaction with resources.

6.  **Cause and Effect Relationships:** Management principles aim to establish cause and effect relationships, allowing them to be used to predict outcomes of managerial actions in similar situations.
    *   However, precise cause and effect relationships can be difficult to establish perfectly because they apply mainly to human behaviour, and real-life situations are rarely identical. Nevertheless, principles are useful as they assist managers in establishing these relationships to some extent.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Unity of Command' states that having a single superior avoids confusion. Here, the cause is the presence of unity of command, and the effect is the avoidance of confusion.

7.  **Contingent:** The application of management principles is dependent or contingent upon the prevailing conditions at a particular point in time. They must be adjusted according to the situation.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Division of Work' might not be applicable in situations or organisations where personal skills are paramount and cannot be easily divided.
