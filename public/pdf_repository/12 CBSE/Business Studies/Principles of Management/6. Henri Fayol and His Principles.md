# Henri Fayol and His Principles

## About Henri Fayol

Henri Fayol (1841-1925) was a French management theorist. He graduated as a Mining Engineer in 1860.

*   He joined a French Mining Company in 1860 as an engineer and became its Managing Director in 1888.
*   He successfully turned the company around from near bankruptcy using his extensive administrative experience.
*   When he retired in 1918, the company was in excellent condition.

## Contributions of Henri Fayol

*   Fayol identified six main activities in an industrial organisation: Technical, Commercial, Financial, Security, Accounting, and Managerial. He clearly distinguished between technical and managerial skills.
*   He was the first to identify the four core functions of management: Planning, Organising, Directing, and Controlling.
*   Fayol established management as a distinct field and developed 14 principles to guide managers.
*   He is known as the '**Father of General Management**'.

## Management Principles Developed by Henri Fayol

Henri Fayol recognised the need for principles and teaching in management, leading him to identify fourteen principles:

### 1. Division of Work

According to this principle, the total work should be divided into small tasks or jobs, and each job should be assigned to a trained specialist competent to perform it.

*   Fayol recommended dividing and allocating work based on competence, qualification, and experience. The goal is to produce more and better work with the same effort.
*   Division makes tasks simpler and repetitive performance builds speed, accuracy, and specialisation, which is the most efficient use of human effort.
*   This principle applies to both managerial and technical work.
*   *Example:* Large companies have separate departments (finance, marketing, production, HR) headed by specialists to achieve targets. A bank divides operations like cash collection and payments among different persons to improve efficiency and specialisation.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                   | Consequences of Violation                                    |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leads to specialisation as work is divided as per qualifications. | There will be lack of efficiency and specialisation.         |
| Simplifies work and increases efficiency through repetition.     | Waste of time and efforts due to switching between tasks.    |

### 2. Authority and Responsibility

This principle states there should be a proper balance between authority and responsibility.

*   **Authority** is the right to give orders and obtain obedience. **Responsibility** is the duty a subordinate performs based on their position. Authority and responsibility are intertwined.

*   Managers need authority commensurate with their responsibility to carry out duties effectively and on time.
*   Organisations should have safeguards against the abuse of managerial power.
*   A manager should have the right to penalize subordinates for wilfully disobeying legitimate orders, but only after the subordinate has had a chance to present their case.
*   *Example 1:* A foreman responsible for producing 500 units needs authority to draw raw materials. If not given, they can't be blamed for failing the target (imbalance). If given authority but they draw *excess* material, it's misuse of authority (needs safeguards).
*   *Example 2:* A sales manager needs to negotiate a deal. If company policy limits credit period (authority) below what's needed to clinch the deal (responsibility), there's imbalance. If given free hand and they offer excessive credit (misuse), action is needed after giving them a chance to explain.

| Helps in fixing responsibility.                              | If authority exceeds responsibility, subordinate may misuse it. |
| Increases confidence of the employees.                       | Work can be delayed due to mismatch.                       |

### 3. Discipline

Discipline means obedience to organisational rules and regulations, essential for systematic working.

*   Fayol stated discipline requires good supervisors at all levels, clear and fair agreements on rules, and judicious application of penalties.
*   Discipline is necessary for all enterprises and at all levels.
*   *Example:* If a manager promises increments for overtime, employees must work effectively and efficiently, and the manager must fulfil the promise.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                               | Consequences of Violation                                   |
| :----------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Smooth and systematic functioning (better efficiency).       | Disobedience of rules, leading to work delays.              |
| Better relations between management and employees.           | Relations may not be cordial.                               |
| Disciplined organisation earns a better reputation.          | Absence of discipline leads to poor reputation and wastage. |

### 4. Unity of Command

This principle states that each subordinate should receive orders from and be accountable to one and only one superior.

*   Receiving orders from two superiors simultaneously violates this principle and should be avoided to prevent confusion and conflict between superiors.
*   Violating this principle undermines authority, jeopardises discipline, disturbs order, and threatens stability. It resembles military organisation structure.

### 5. Unity of Direction

Unity of direction means there should be one head and one plan for a group of activities with the same objectives.

*   All units of an organisation should work towards the same objectives through coordinated efforts, ensuring unity of action and avoiding unnecessary duplication.
*   *Example:* In team sports like hockey, all players coordinate towards a common goal. Similarly, in management, all employees' efforts should be directed towards the organisational goal. If a company makes motorcycles and cars, it should have separate divisions, each with its own incharge, plans, and resources.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                   | Consequences of Violation                                 |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| Emphasises common goals and helps in achieving them.             | Unnecessary duplication of efforts and wastage of resources. |
| Ensures unity of action and facilitates coordination.            | There will be lack of coordination.                       |

**Difference between Unity of Command and Unity of Direction**

| Basis           | Unity of Command                                             | Unity of Direction                                          |
| :-------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Meaning      | Each subordinate receives orders from and is accountable to one superior. | One head and one plan for activities with the same objectives. |
| 2. Aim          | Prevents dual subordination.                                 | Prevents overlapping of activities.                         |
| 3. Implications | Affects an individual employee.                              | Affects the entire organisation.                            |
| 4. Significance | Improves efficiency, brings harmony and discipline.          | Facilitates unity of action and coordination of efforts.    |

### 6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest

This principle states that the interest of the organisation as a whole must take precedence over the interests of individuals or employees.

*   Every individual has personal interests, and the company has its objectives. This principle requires individuals to sacrifice personal interests for company objectives.
*   The company's interest always supersedes individual interests because the larger interests of stakeholders (owners, shareholders, customers, society, etc.) cannot be sacrificed for one or a few individuals.
*   If individual and organisational interests differ, management should try to reconcile them. If not possible, the general interest must prevail. Employees should remember that their rise is linked to the organisation's rise.
*   Managers demonstrate this principle through their behaviour, e.g., not misusing power for personal gain at the expense of general interest. This encourages similar behaviour from workers.
*   *Example:* If a manager wants a higher salary but the company is in a financial crisis and needs to cut expenses, the manager must sacrifice their interest. The manager's objective can be met when the organisation recovers.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                   | Consequences of Violation                                |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- |
| Helps in achieving goals of the organisation.                    | The firm will not be able to achieve its goals.          |
| Brings coordination between individual and organisational objectives. | Lack of coordination between individual and organisational objectives. |

### 7. Remuneration of Employees

This principle suggests that overall pay and compensation should be fair to both employees and the organisation.

*   Remuneration should be fair to everyone, providing employees with at least a reasonable standard of living.
*   It must also be within the company's paying capacity.
*   Fair remuneration fosters a congenial atmosphere and good relations between workers and management, leading to smooth operations.
*   *Example:* If a company earns higher profits, sharing some with employees as a bonus can be very satisfying and fair.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                 | Consequences of Violation                                    |
| :------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| High motivation and dedication from fair pay.                  | Lack of motivation and dedication.                           |
| Cordial relations between employees and management.            | Unhealthy relations, potentially leading to dishonesty.      |
| Improvement in commitment of employees towards organisation. | Increase in labour turnover.                                 |

### 8. Centralisation and Decentralisation

This principle advocates for a proper balance between centralisation (concentration of decision-making authority) and decentralisation (dispersal of authority).

*   There's a need to balance subordinate involvement (through decentralisation) with managers retaining final authority (through centralisation).
*   The degree of centralisation/decentralisation depends on factors like superior experience, subordinate ability, and organisation size. Large organisations usually have more decentralisation.
*   Excessive centralisation delays work; complete decentralisation leads to loss of top management control. An optimum balance is needed based on organisational needs.
*   *Example:* Giving more powers to Panchayats in India to decide and spend government funds for village welfare is decentralisation at the national level.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                 | Consequences of Violation                                   |
| :------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Leads to quick decisions at middle and operational levels.     | Slow decision-making in case of complete centralisation.    |
| Decreases workload of superiors.                               | Risk of misuse of authority in case of complete decentralisation. |
| Increases encouragement and motivation of subordinates.        | Decreases encouragement in case of complete centralisation.   |

### 9. Scalar Chain

According to this principle, organisations should have a clear chain of authority and communication from top to bottom, followed by managers and subordinates.

*   The formal lines of authority from highest to lowest ranks form the **scalar chain**.
*   It defines superior-subordinate relationships and authority levels.
*   The scalar chain is crucial for unity of command and effective communication.
*   Normally, this chain should not be violated for formal communication. In the figure, communication from D to A must go through C and B.

| Unity of command due to no confusion of dual orders.       | Lack of unity of command.                                 |

### 10. Order

This principle states that there should be a place for everything and everyone.

*   People and materials must be in suitable places at the appropriate time for maximum efficiency.
*   Having a fixed place for everything and everyone ensures orderliness and avoids work obstruction.
*   This leads to increased productivity and efficiency.
*   *Example:* A specific workshop or office for the foreman ensures they can be easily contacted.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                   | Consequences of Violation                                 |
| :--------------------------------------------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------------- |
| Efficient use of resources as no time is wasted in tracing them. | Inefficient use of resources due to time/energy lost in search. |
| Smooth and systematic working with better discipline.            | Lack of discipline in the organisation.                   |

**Fayol Emphasised Two Types of Orders:**
*   **Material Order:** A certain place for everything, and everything must be in its proper place.
*   **Social Order:** An appointed place for every employee, and every employee must be in their appointed place.

### 11. Equity

This principle states there should be no discrimination among employees based on religion, language, caste, sex, belief, or nationality.

*   Fayol believed 'Good sense and experience are needed to ensure fairness to all employees, who should be treated as fairly as possible.'
*   This principle promotes kindness and justice in managers' behaviour towards workers, fostering loyalty, devotion, and cordial relations.
*   Partial or biased management leads to employee dissatisfaction and reduced potential contribution.
*   Fayol did not rule out strict treatment for lazy personnel to show everyone is treated equally.
*   Modern multinational corporations often embody this principle with diverse workforces in discrimination-free environments.
*   *Example:* If two Head Clerks with the same qualifications and job profile are paid differently based on one being a manager's relative, the principle of Equity is violated. Employees performing similar jobs should receive the same salary.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                 | Consequences of Violation                                   |
| :------------------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Improves satisfaction and motivation level among employees.    | Decrease in satisfaction and motivation.                    |
| Cordial relations between employees and management.            | Unhealthy relations.                                        |

### 12. Stability of Personnel

This principle emphasises proper efforts to achieve stability and continuity of employee tenure.

*   Fayol believed employees should be selected rigorously and kept in their positions for a minimum period, given reasonable time to show results.
*   Employee turnover should be minimised to maintain organisational efficiency.
*   Frequent changes create instability and insecurity, causing employees to leave, increasing recruitment, selection, and training costs.
*   It is beneficial for the enterprise to retain trusted, experienced, and trained employees.
*   Changes are unavoidable in cases like illness, retirement, or death.
*   *Example:* A newly appointed employee should be given time to settle down and adapt to the new environment and conditions.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                  | Consequences of Violation                                    |
| :-------------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| Increase in efficiency due to feeling of job security.          | Unnecessary labour turnover creates disbelief.               |
| Better reputation and goodwill of the company.                  | Decline in company reputation.                               |
| Less expenses on recruitment, selection, and training.          | Increase in cost of recruitment, selection, and training.    |
| No wastage of time and resources.                               | Wastage of considerable time in learning a new job.          |

### 13. Initiative

This principle states that workers should be encouraged and given opportunities to take initiative in making and executing plans.

*   Fayol encouraged workers to develop and implement their plans for improvements.
*   Initiative is taking the first step with self-motivation and is a source of strength. It should be encouraged but not involve going against established practices or discipline.
*   Organisations should have employee suggestion systems and reward useful suggestions.
*   *Example:* A sales manager developing a sales plan should welcome suggestions from the sales team to ensure maximum participation. Once the plan is made, everyone should follow it.

| Advantages or Positive Effects                                 | Consequences of Violation                                |
| :------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------- |
| Increase in mental growth and feeling of belongingness.        | Leads to frustration among employees.                    |
| Increases commitment through maximum participation.            | Lack of initiative may create non-cooperation.           |

### 14. Espirit De Corps

This principle states that management should promote team spirit, unity, and harmony among employees.

*   Promoting team spirit is crucial, especially in large organisations, to achieve objectives and ensure coordination.
*   Production is **TEAM** (Together Everyone Achieves More) work, requiring full support and cooperation.
*   A manager should use 'We' instead of 'I' to foster team spirit, developing mutual trust and belongingness and reducing the need for penalties.

Fayol's 14 principles are widely applicable to managerial problems and have significantly impacted modern management thinking.

## Fayol: Traditional (THEN) and Modern Implications (NOW)

The interpretation of Fayol's principles has evolved with changes in the business environment.

| Principle                                          | Traditional Implication                               | Modern Implication                                      |
| :------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Division of Work                                | Specialisation in worker's job design.                | Generalisation in worker's job design.                  |
| 2. Authority and Responsibility                    | Managers are empowered.                               | Employees are empowered.                                |
| 3. Discipline                                      | Formal Controls.                                      | Informal Controls.                                      |
| 4. Unity of Command                                | Subordinates report to only one boss.                 | Subordinates report to multiple bosses.                 |
| 5. Unity of Direction                              | Functions have only one plan and one boss.            | Functions have multiple plans and multiple bosses.      |
| 6. Subordination of individual interest to general interest | Employees are committed to the organisation.          | Organisation is committed to employees and vice-versa.  |
| 7. Remuneration of Employees                       | Reasonable Pay reward system.                         | Performance based reward system.                        |
| 8. Centralisation and decentralisation             | Top level retains power.                              | Dispersal of authority at all levels.                   |
| 9. Scalar Chain                                    | Formal communication channel.                         | Less formal communication channel.                      |
| 10. Order                                          | Material and social order for control.                | Material and social order for coordination.             |
| 11. Equity                                         | Commitment obtained through kindness.                 | Commitment obtained through a sense of ownership.       |
| 12. Stability of Personnel                         | Train employees and encourage them to remain.         | Continuous employee training and development.           |
| 13. Initiative                                     | Managers conceive and implement new ideas.            | Workers conceive and implement new ideas.               |
| 14. Espirit De Corps                               | Maintaining high morale among employees is essential. | Maintaining high morale among employees is not necessary. |
