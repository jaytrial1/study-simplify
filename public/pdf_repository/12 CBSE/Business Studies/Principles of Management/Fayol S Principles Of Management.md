# FAYOL'S PRINCIPLES OF MANAGEMENT 

## About Henri Fayol

Henri Fayol (1841-1925) was a French management theorist. He graduated from the Mining Academy of St. Etienne in 1860 in Mining Engineering.

- He joined a French Mining Company in 1860 as an engineer and rose to the position of its managing director in 1888.
- At that time, the company was at the verge of bankruptcy. With rich and broad administrative experience, Fayol contributed a lot in turning the fortune of the company.
- When he retired in 1918, the company was in excellent condition.


## Contributions of Henri Fayol

- Fayol found that activities of an industrial organisation could be divided into six groups: Technical; Commercial; Financial; Security; Accounting and Managerial. He made clear distinction between technical and managerial skill.
- Fayol was the first to identify four functions of management: Planning, Organising, Directing and Controlling.
- Fayol not only identified management as a separate field of endeavour, but also developed 14 principles, which act as guidelines for managers.
For his contributions, he is well known as the 'Father of General Management'.


## Management Principles Developed by Henri Fayol

Henri Fayol recognised a widespread need for principles and management teaching. So, he identified fourteen such principles. Let us discuss these principles.

## 1. Division of Work

According to this principle, whole work must be divided into small tasks or jobs and each job must be assigned to a trained specialist who is competent to perform it.

- Fayol recommended that work of all kinds must be divided and allocated as per competence, qualification and experience. The intent of division of work is to produce more and better work for the same effort.
- Division makes each task simpler and repetitive performance helps in gaining speed, accuracy and specialisation. Specialisation is the most efficient way to use human effort.
- This principle applies to both managerial as well as technical work.
- Due to this reason, big companies have separate departments for finance, marketing, production and human resources. All of them are headed by specialised persons and they collectively achieve production and sales targets of the company.
Example: A bank involves several operations like collection of cash, payments, issue of cheque books, etc. All these activities are performed by different persons. It improves their efficiency and makes them specialised in their respective fields.

| :-- | :-- |
| Leads to specialisation as work is divided as per <br> qualifications. | There will be lack of efficiency and specialisation. |
| It simplifies the work and increases the efficiency as same <br> work is performed repeatedly. | There will be waste of time and efforts due to changes <br> from one work to another. |

## 2. Authority and Responsibility 

According to this principle, there should be a proper balance between authority and responsibility.

- Authority is the right to give orders and obtain obedience and responsibility is the duty, which a subordinate is expected to perform by virtue of his position in organisation. Authority and responsibility go hand in hand.
- Authority is of two types: (i) Official Authority,
which is the authority to command; and (ii) Personal Authority, which is the authority of the individual manager. Authority is both formal and informal.
- Managers require authority commensurate with their responsibility, i.e. a manager should have necessary authority to carry out his / her responsibility. Parity between authority and responsibility helps in meeting responsibilities on time.
- At the same time, the organisation should build safeguards against abuse of managerial power to prevent misuse of authority.
- A manager should have the right to punish a subordinate for wilfully not obeying a legitimate order but only after sufficient opportunity has been given to a subordinate for presenting his case.
Example 1. Suppose a foreman is given responsibility to produce 500 units per day. To achieve this target, he must be given requisite authority.
Case 1: If the foreman is not given required authority to draw raw material from stores, then he cannot be blamed for not meeting the target.
Case 2: However, if the foreman has been given the authority to draw raw material from stores as and when required and he draws excess raw material, then it is misuse of authority.
In Case 1, the foreman should be given required authority so that he can achieve the target. In Case 2, the organisation should build safeguards to prevent the misuse of authority.
Example 2. Suppose, a sales manager has to negotiate a deal with a buyer. As per the old practices, it has been observed that a credit period of 60 days is required to be given to clinch the deal.
Case 1: If the company gives power to the sales manager to offer a credit period of only 40 days, then there is an imbalance in authority and responsibility and manager was unable to clinch the deal.

Case 2: However, if the manager is given free hand to offer any credit period, the manager offers a credit period of 100 days, than it is misuse of authority.
In Case 1, the manager should be granted authority of offering credit period of 60 days in company's interest. In Case 2, strict action should be taken against the manager for wilfully not obeying a legitimate order but only after sufficient opportunity has been given to him for presenting his case.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| It prevents misuse of authority. | If responsibility exceeds authority, then subordinate cannot perform <br> his duties. |
| It helps in fixing responsibility. | If authority exceeds responsibility, subordinate may misuse it. |
| It increases confidence of the employees. | Work can be delayed due to mismatch in authority and responsibility. |

## 3. Discipline 

Discipline refers to obedience to rules and regulations of the organisation, which is necessary for systematic working of the organisation.

- According to Fayol, discipline requires good supervisor at all levels, clear and fair agreement regarding rules and judicious application of penalties.
- Discipline is must for all the enterprises and for all the levels.
Example: If a manager has agreed to give increments in salary to employees in lieu of overtime worked by them, then the employees must be disciplined to work effectively and efficiently and manager must also be disciplined to meet the promise of increments.

Lack of Discipline results in Chaos and leads to disruption in systematic working of the organisation.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Smooth and systematic functioning of the business, i.e. <br> better efficiency. | There may be disobedience of rules and regulations by <br> employees, which may lead to delay in work. |
| Better relations between management and employees. | Relations between management and employees may <br> not be so cordial. |
| Disciplined organisation earns a better reputation. | Absence of discipline leads to poor reputation of the <br> organisation and wastage of resources. |

## 4. Unity of Command

According to this principle, each subordinate should receive orders and be accountable to one and only one superior.

- If an employee gets orders from two superiors at the same time, then the principle of unity of command is violated.
- Dual subordination should be avoided as it creates confusion to the subordinate and conflict between the superiors. So, in order to avoid confusion, conflicts, disharmony and many other unpleasant situations, the employee should receive orders from one boss only.

- According to Fayol, if this principle is violated, then 'authority is undermined, discipline is in jeopardy, order disturbed and stability threatened'. The principle resembles military organisation.
- In Fig. 2.2, principle of unity of command is followed as subordinate receive orders and is accountable to only one superior. However, in Fig. 2.3, the principle of unity of command is violated as subordinate get orders from two superiors.

## Examples: 

(i) Suppose, Marketing Manager of a company allows the sales executive to grant $10 \%$ discount, whereas, finance manager instructs not to offer more than $5 \%$ discount. In the given case, there is lack of unity of command. This can be avoided if there is coordination between various departments.
(ii) The production foreman of a company gets instructions from the Production Manager to increase the production due to over purchase of cloth, while sales manager orders the foreman to slow down the production due to unpredictable trend of market demand. There is lack of unity of command in the given case.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| There will be no confusion in the mind of subordinates. | It creates confusion for subordinate as to whose order <br> to be complied. |
| Responsibility can be easily fixed. | It will be difficult to fix responsibility. |
| Better performance as subordinates receive orders from <br> one superior only. | Lack of Discipline. |
| Avoidance of Ego Clashes. | Chances of Ego clashes and overlapping of orders. |

## 5. Unity of Direction

Unity of Direction implies that there should be one head and one plan for group of activities having same objectives.

- According to this principle, all the units of an organisation should be moving towards the same objectives through coordinated and focused efforts.
- It ensures unity of action and coordination and avoids unnecessary duplication of efforts.
- In the game of hockey or football, the eleven players coordinate their actions to move to a common goal. Similarly, in management, efforts of all employees should be directed towards organisational goal.

Example: if a company is manufacturing motorcycles as well as cars, then it should have two separate divisions for both of them. Each division should have its own incharge, plans and resources to execute.

| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| It emphasise importance of common goals and helps in <br> achieving them. |  | Unnecessary duplication of efforts and wastage of <br> resources. |
| It ensures unity of action and facilitates coordination. | There will be lack of coordination. |  |

## 6. Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest

According to this principle, interest of the organisation as a whole must prevail over the interest of the individuals or employees.

- In an organisation, every individual has his own interests and company has got its own objectives. This principle demands that each individual should sacrifice his personal interest for achieving company's objectives.
- In all the situations, company's interest will supersede the interest of any one individual. This is so because larger interests of various stakeholders (like owners, shareholders, creditors, debtors, tax authorities, customers, society, etc.) cannot be sacrificed for one individual or a small group of individuals.
- In case, individual interest and organisational interest differ, it is the duty of the management to reconcile them. However, if reconciliation is not possible, then general interest must supersede individual interest. It must be remembered that one rises when his organisation rises.
- A manager can ensure this principle through his behaviour. For example, he should not misuse his powers for his own benefit at the cost of general interest of workers or company. This will ensure same behaviour by the workers.
Example: If a manager's objective is to earn more salary and company is going through financial crisis and wants to cut down expenses, then manager must sacrifice his interest. Manager can achieve his objective, when organisation recovers from financial crisis.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| It helps in achieving goals of the organisation. | The firm will not be able to achieve its goals. |
| It brings coordination between individual and <br> organisational objectives. | Lack of coordination between individual and <br> organisational objectives. |

## 7. Remuneration of Employees 

According to this principle, overall pay and compensation should be fair to both employees and organisation.

- The remuneration should be fair to everybody.
- Employees should be paid fair wages, which should give them at least a reasonable standard of living.
- At the same time it should be within the paying capacity of the company.
- It will ensure congenial atmosphere and good relations between workers and management and consequently, working of the company would be smooth.
Example: If a company has earned higher profits, then it must share some of its part with the employees in the form of bonus, which would be very satisfying to them.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| High level of motivation as they are paid fully for services <br> rendered. | Lack of motivation and dedication among employees. |
| Cordial relations between employees and management. | Unhealthy relations between employees and <br> management and it may also lead to dishonesty. |
| Improvement in commitment of employees towards <br> organisation. | Increase in labour turnover. |

## 8. Centralisation and Decentralisation

According to this principle, there should be proper balance between centralisation and decentralisation in the organisation.

- Centralisation refers to concentration of decision-making authority, whereas decentralisation refers to evenly dispersal of authority among more than one person.
- There is a need to balance subordinate involvement through decentralisation and managers' retention of final authority through centralisation.
- The degree of centralisation and decentralisation depends on various factors, such as experience of superior, ability of subordinates, size of the organisation, etc. Generally, large organisations have more decentralisation than small organisations.
- Too much centralisation leads to delay in work and complete decentralisation leads to loss of control of top management. So, an optimum balance should be maintained according to the needs of organisation.
Example: Panchayats in our country have been given more powers to decide and spend funds granted to them by the government for the welfare of villages. This is decentralisation at the national level.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :--: | :--: |
| It leads to quick decisions at middle and operational level. | Slow decision-making in case of complete centralisation. |
| It decreases work-load of superiors. | Risk of misuse of authority in case of complete decentralisation. |
| Increase in the encouragement and motivation level of the subordinates. | Decrease in the encouragement in case of complete centralisation. |

## 9. Scalar Chain 

According to this principle, organisations should have a chain of authority and communication that runs from top to bottom and should be followed by managers and the subordinates

- The formal lines of authority from highest to lowest ranks are known as scalar chain.
- It states superior-subordinate relationship and authority of superiors in relation to subordinates at various levels.
- Scalar chain is crucial to ensure unity of command and effective communication in the organisation.
- According to Fayol, this chain should not be violated in the normal course of formal communication. In Fig. 2.4, if D wants to communicate some reports to A , then as per scalar chain, information will first flow to C , who will pass it to B and B will communicate it to A.
- However, in case of urgency, Fayol permitted a short cut in the chain, known as 'Gang Plank'.

Gang Plank allows direct communication between two employees of the same level, without following the scalar chain.

- In the given figure, if D wants to talk to H , then they can directly communicate with each other after informing their respective superiors. Gang Plank is a shorter route and has been provided so that communication is not delayed.
- A worker cannot directly contact CEO of the company. He/she has to pass through all the formal levels i.e., foreman, superintendent, manager, director, etc. However, in case of an emergency, it is possible that a worker (or lower level employee) can contact CEO (or top level) directly.
Example: Harish (supervisor) has to pass on some important details of work to Karan (Director) and to Mahesh (supervisor of another department). Now, Harish cannot directly contact Karan and he will have to follow the scalar chain. However, he can talk to Mahesh (through gang plank) to avoid delays.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Orderly and smooth flow of information. | Information may not flow in orderly direction. |
| Clear lines of authority and responsibility relationship. | Authority and responsibility relationship will not be clear. |
| Better relations between subordinate and superior due <br> to absolute respect for authority. | Unhealthy relations between subordinate and superior. |
| Unity of command due to no confusion of dual order. | Lack of unity of command. |

## 10. Order

According to this principle, there should be a place for everything and everyone.

- According to Fayol, People and materials must be in suitable places at appropriate time for maximum efficiency.

- If there is a fixed place for everything (everyone) and it (he/she) is present there, then there will be no obstruction in work. Essentially, it means orderliness.
- It will lead to increased productivity and efficiency.

Example: There should be a specific place or workshop for the foreman so that he can be easily contacted whenever necessary.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Efficient use of resources as no time is wasted in tracing <br> the resources. | Inefficient use of resources due to loss of time and energy <br> in search of resources. |
| Smooth and systematic working of organisation, with <br> better discipline. | Lack of discipline in the organisation. |

## Fayol Emphasised Two Types of Orders 

- Material Order: There must be a certain place for everything and everything must be at its proper place.
- Social Order: There must be an appointed place for every employee and every employee must be in his or her appointed place.


## 11. Equity

According to this principle, there should not be any discrimination among the employees on the basis of religion, language, caste, sex, belief or nationality.

- According to Fayol, 'Good sense and experience are needed to ensure fairness to all employees, who should be treated as fairly as possible.'
- This principle emphasises kindliness and justice in the behaviour of managers towards workers. This will ensure loyalty, devotion and cordial relations between superiors and subordinates.
- If the management is partial or biased in dealing with employees, then employees will get dissatisfied and will not contribute to their maximum potential.
- It must be noted that Fayol does not rule out use of force sometimes. He also advocates the use of strict treatment for lazy personnel in order to send the message that everyone is equal in the eyes of the management.
- Now-a-days, in MNCs, people of various nationalities work together in a discrimination free environment.
Example: Ankit and Mahesh work in a company at the position of Head Clerk and both of them have same educational qualification. With the same job profile and nature of work, Ankit gets a salary of $₹ 20,000$ per month, while Mahesh gets $₹ 23,000$ per month as he a relative of manager. In the given case, principle of Equity is violated. As per Equity, employees performing similar jobs should be paid same salary.

| :-- | :-- |
| Improves satisfaction \& motivation level among <br> employees. | Decrease in satisfaction \& motivation level of employee. |
| Cordial relations between employees and management. | Unhealthy relations between employees and <br> management. |

## 12. Stability of Personnel 

According to this principle, there should be proper efforts to achieve stability and continuity of tenure of personnel.

- Fayol was of the view that employees should be selected and appointed after due and rigorous procedure. Once selected, they should be kept at their position for a minimum period and must be given reasonable time to show results.
- In short, employee turnover should be minimised to maintain organisational efficiency.
- Continuous changes will create instability and insecurity among employees and they would tend to leave the organisation. It will also raise the cost of recruitment, selection and training.
- So, it is always in the interest of the enterprise that its trusted, experienced and trained employees do not leave the organisation.
- However, changes may be made when it is unavoidable, like in case of prolonged illness, retirement or death of an employee.
Example: If a new employee is appointed, then the employee must be given time to settle down and to adapt to the new environment and working conditions.

| Advantages or Positive Effects | Consequences of Violation |
| :-- | :-- |
| Increase in efficiency of employees due to feeling of job <br> security. | Unnecessary labour turnover creates an atmosphere of <br> disbelief. |
| Better reputation and goodwill of the company. | Decline in the reputation of the company. |
| Less expenses on recruitment, selection and training. | Increase in cost of recruitment, selection and training. |
| No wastage of time and resources. | Wastage of considerable time in learning new job. |

## 13. Initiative

According to this principle, workers should be encouraged and given an opportunity to take some initiative in making and executing the plans.

- According to Fayol, workers should be encouraged to develop and carry out their plans for improvements.
- Initiative means taking the first step with self-motivation and a source of strength for the organisation. It is one of the traits of an intelligent person.
- Initiative should be encouraged. However, it does not mean going against the established practices of the company for the sake of being different or doing something against the discipline.
- An organisation must have an employee suggestion system and employees with useful suggestions should be rewarded.
Example: Suppose, sales manager of Newtech Ltd. wants to develop a plan to increase sales of their wireless routers. Now, the manager must welcome the suggestions and ideas of its sales team in making the plan so as to have their maximum participation. However, once the plan is made, every employee should follow the plan.

Advantages or Positive Effects
Increase in mental growth and feeling of belongingness in employees.

It increases commitment of employees towards organisation through maximum participation

## Consequences of Violation

Leads to frustration among employees.

Lack of initiative may create an atmosphere of noncooperation.

## 14. Espirit De Corps 

According to this principle, management should promote a team spirit of unity and harmony among employees.

- Management should promote feeling of team spirit among employees, especially in the large organisations because otherwise objectives would be difficult to realise and there will be loss of coordination.
- Production is TEAM (Together Everyone Achieves More) work and requires full support and cooperation of all members.
- A manager should replace 'I' with 'We' in his conversations to give rise to a spirit of team spirit. It will develop an atmosphere of mutual trust and belongingness among team members and will minimise the need for using penalties. Example: Suppose, sales manager assigned a target of selling 50 units to a group of 10 sales executives. They mutually decided to sell 5 units each. Now, it is the duty of each sales executive to ensure that target of 50 units is achieved on time, even if any member is unable to fulfil his/her individual target. It means 'Espirit De Corps' helps in achievement of group goals.
that employees should not concentrate only on achieving their individual targets but on achieving group target.

| :-- | :-- |
| Develops an atmosphere of mutual trust. | Lack of team spirit and employees may not cooperate <br> with each other. |
| Helps in achievement of group goals. | Group goals may not be achieved. |
| Leads to cordial relations between management and <br> workers. | Unhealthy relations between management and workers. |

Finally, it can be concluded that Fayol's 14 principles of management are widely applicable to managerial problems and have cast a profound impact on management thinking today.

## Fayol: Traditional (THEN) and Modern Implications (NOW)

With the change of environment in which business is done, the interpretation of these principles has changed. Let us compare the traditional and modern implications of Fayol's principles:

| 1. Division of Work | Specialisation in worker's job design. | Generalisation in worker's job design. |
| 2. Authority and Responsibility | Managers are empowered. | Employees are empowered. |

| 4. Unity of Command | Subordinates report to only one boss. | Subordinates report to multiple bosses. |
| 5. Unity of Direction | Functions have only one plan and one <br> boss. | Functions have multiple plans and <br> multiple bosses. |
| 6. Subordination of individual <br> interest to general interest | Employees are committed to the <br> organisation. | Organisation is committed to <br> employees and vice-versa. |
| 7. Remuneration of Employees | Reasonable Pay reward system. | Performance based reward system. |
| 8. Centralisation and <br> decentralisation | Top level retains power. | Dispersal of authority at all levels. |
| 9. Scalar Chain | Formal communication channel. | Less formal communication channel. |
| 10. Order | Material and social order for control. | Material and social order for <br> coordination. |
| 11. Equity | Commitment obtained through <br> kindness. | Commitment obtained through a sense <br> of ownership. |
