# Importance of Management Principles

The principles of management offer valuable insights into managerial behaviour and influence practical management approaches. They guide managers in making and implementing decisions.

The importance of management principles is evident from the following points:

1.  **Providing Managers with Useful Insights into Reality:** Management principles give managers useful insights into real-world situations. Applying these principles enhances their knowledge, ability, and understanding of managerial contexts. They offer timely guidance, boosting managerial efficiency. Principles help managers learn from past errors and save time by quickly resolving recurring issues.

2.  **Optimum Utilisation of Resources and Effective Administration:** Organisations have limited human and material resources that must be used optimally.
    *   Optimum use means employing resources to achieve maximum benefit at minimum cost.
    *   Principles help managers foresee the cause-and-effect relationships of their decisions, reducing wastage and inefficiency from trial-and-error methods.
    *   *Example:* Principles like division of work, unity of direction, and order facilitate better use of human and physical resources. Principles also enhance administration effectiveness by discouraging personal biases and promoting an objective approach. The principle of 'Subordination of Individual Interest to General Interest', for instance, prioritizes organisational objectives.

3.  **Scientific Decisions:** Management decisions should be based on facts, justifiable by their intended purpose, timely, realistic, and measurable.
    *   Decisions guided by management principles are free from bias and prejudice.
    *   They are based on objective assessment and logic, not blind faith.
    *   *Example:* Taylor's principle of 'Science not Rule of Thumb' emphasizes the scientific analysis of every work element.

4.  **Meeting Changing Environment Requirements:** For survival and growth, an organisation must constantly adapt to the dynamic business environment.
    *   Although principles are general guidelines, they are flexible and can be modified to suit the changing environment.
    *   Management principles provide effective and dynamic leadership, helping the organisation implement changes.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Division of Work' leads to specialisation. Consequently, many companies now specialise in core competencies and outsource non-core activities to adapt to the environment.

5.  **Fulfilling Social Responsibility:** As businesses are part of society, fulfilling social responsibilities is in their long-term interest. Management principles guide managers not only towards organisational goals but also towards fulfilling social duties.
    *   *Example:* The principle of 'Equity' promotes fair treatment of all workers, helping fulfil responsibility towards them.

6.  **Management Training, Education and Research:** Principles contribute to knowledge and form the basis for management training, education, and research.
    *   They are included in academic courses (like B.Com, BBA, MBA) for this reason.
    *   These principles refine management practices and facilitate the development of new techniques.

Q. 2. Anish and Devansh are two friends and both of them are part of top level management of Sigma Ltd. Anish is of the opinion that decisions of the management should be based on logical and rational decision-making rather than on the basis of bias and prejudice. Devansh believes that organisation must continuously adapt itself as per the dynamic business environment.
The given case highlights the importance of principles of management. Identify and explain them.
> **Answer:** The case highlights two aspects of the importance of management principles. Anish's opinion that decisions should be based on logic and rationality rather than bias relates to the importance of **Scientific Decisions**. Devansh's belief that the organisation must adapt to the dynamic environment relates to the importance of **Meeting Changing Environment Requirements**.
