This section describes the second step of the controlling process, which involves assessing what has actually been achieved.
# Step 2: Measurement of Actual Performance

Once the standards have been established, the second step is to measure the **actual performance**.

*   Performance should be measured in an objective and reliable manner using techniques like personal observation, sample checking, or performance reports.
*   To make comparison easier, performance should be measured in the same units as the standards.
*   Measurement is generally done after the task, but if possible, it should be done during the performance (e.g., checking parts before assembly).
*   Measuring employee performance may require a performance report from their superior.
*   Performance can also be measured through calculation of ratios (e.g., Gross Profit Ratio, Return on Investment) at periodic intervals.
*   Progress in marketing might be measured by units sold, and production by units produced and defective pieces.
*   In small organisations, every piece might be checked for quality, while in large organisations, sample checking is common.
