This section details the first step of the controlling process, which involves establishing benchmarks for performance evaluation.
# Step 1: Setting Performance Standards

The first step in the controlling process is the establishment of **standards of performance**. Standards are the criteria against which actual performance is measured. Thus, standards serve as benchmarks or yardsticks towards which an organisation strives to work.

Standards can be set in both **Quantitative** as well as **Qualitative** terms.

*   **Quantitative Standards:** These are standards that can be shown in figures, such as cost to be incurred, revenue to be earned, units to be produced and sold, time to be spent, etc. Managers should try to set standards in precise quantitative terms to make comparison easier (e.g., reduction of defects from 10 to 5 per 1,000 pieces). Standards should ideally be set for the short-term for early detection of errors.
*   **Qualitative Standards:** These are standards that cannot be shown in figures, such as improving goodwill or employee motivation level. When setting qualitative standards, efforts should be made to define them in a manner that facilitates measurement (e.g., time taken to serve a customer to measure customer satisfaction).

Standards should be flexible so that they can be modified as per changes in the business environment.

## Standards Used in Functional Areas

Standards of performance can be fixed in various functional areas:

*   **Production Department:**
    *   Quantity (e.g., target output units)
    *   Quality (e.g., rejection ratio)
    *   Cost (e.g., total cost or cost per unit)
    *   Individual Job (e.g., units produced per worker)
*   **Marketing Department:**
    *   Sales Volume (e.g., target units sold)
    *   Sales Expense (e.g., expenditure limit)
    *   Advertising Expenditure (e.g., budget limit)
    *   Sales Person's Performance (e.g., minimum sales volume per person)
*   **Human Resource Management:**
    *   Labour Relations
    *   Labour Turnover
    *   Labour Absenteeism
*   **Finance and Accounting:**
    *   Capital Expenditure
    *   Flow of Capital
    *   Inventories
    *   Liquidity
