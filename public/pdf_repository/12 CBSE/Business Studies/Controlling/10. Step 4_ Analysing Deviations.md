# Step 4: Analysing Deviations

Some deviation in performance is expected. The next step is to analyse these deviations.

*   An **acceptable range of deviation** must be fixed, as significant deviations need more attention than minor ones.
*   Deviations in **key areas** of business require more urgent attention than those in insignificant areas.

Managers use 'Critical Point Control' and 'Management by Exception' in this regard.

## Critical Point Control

According to this principle, the control system should focus on **Key Result Areas (KRAs)**, which are critical to the overall performance of an organisation. If something goes wrong at these critical points, the entire organisation suffers. Attention must be paid to such critical points because it is neither economical nor easy to check every single activity. For example, a 5% increase in labour cost might be more critical than a 15% increase in postal charges for a manufacturing firm.

## Management by Exception (Control by Exception)

According to this principle, managers should give attention only to **significant deviations** that go beyond the permissible limit. This is based on the idea that "if you try to control everything, you may end up controlling nothing". A range of acceptable deviations should be established, and attention should be focused only when performance falls outside this range. For example, if labour cost is within an acceptable 2% deviation, it might be ignored, but a 15% deviation requires immediate attention.

After identifying major deviations, their causes need analysis. Causes might include unrealistic standards, defective processes, lack of resources, structural drawbacks, organisational constraints, or external factors (like changes in prices or government policies). Identifying the exact cause is crucial for taking appropriate corrective action.

## Advantages of Critical Point Control and Management by Exception

1.  Saves time and effort by focusing only on significant deviations.
2.  Ensures better use of managerial talent by focusing attention on important areas.
3.  Facilitates delegation of authority and improves employee morale as routine problems are handled by subordinates.
4.  Helps identify critical problems requiring immediate action.

## Critical Point Control Vs Management by Exception

Under **Critical Point Control**, the manager focuses on selected strategic or key activities (KRAs). Under **Management by Exception**, the manager is concerned with major deviations, regardless of where they occur, provided they fall outside the acceptable range.
