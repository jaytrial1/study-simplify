This section details the final step in the controlling process, which involves implementing measures to correct deviations and improve performance.
# Step 5: Taking Corrective Action

The final step in the controlling process is taking **corrective action**. If deviations are within acceptable limits, no corrective action is needed.

However, if deviations go beyond the acceptable range, especially in important areas, immediate managerial attention is required to prevent recurrence and ensure standards are accomplished.

For example:
*   If a production target is missed due to lack of skills, corrective action might involve **training employees**.
*   If a project is behind schedule, corrective action might involve **assigning additional workers and equipment**.
*   If sales are declining, the company might **implement a budget system** to set sales and profit targets.

In cases where deviation cannot be corrected by managerial action (e.g., due to external factors), the standards themselves may need to be revised.

## Some Examples of Corrective Actions

| Causes of Deviation         | Possible Corrective Action                          |
| :-------------------------- | :-------------------------------------------------- |
| Defective material          | Change quality specification of material.           |
| Defective machinery         | Repair or replace the existing machinery.           |
| Obsolete machinery          | Undertake technological upgradation of machinery. |
| Defective process           | Modify the existing process.                        |
| Poor working conditions     | Improve physical conditions of work.                |
| Lack of motivation          | Providing suitable incentives.                      |
