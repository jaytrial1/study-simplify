# IMPORTANCE OF DIRECTING

The importance of directing can be understood by the fact that every action in the organisation is initiated through directing only.
The various points of importance of directing are discussed below:

1. Initiates Action: Directing helps to initiate action by people in the organisation towards achieving it desired objectives.
For example, if a supervisor guides his subordinates and clarifies their doubts in performing a task, it will help the worker to achieve work targets given to him.

2. Integrates Group Activity: The organisational objectives can be achieved only when the individual efforts are integrated. Directing integrates employees efforts in such a way that every individual effort contributes to the organisational performance.
For example, a manager with good leadership abilities can convince his subordinates that individual efforts and team effort will lead to achievement of organisational goals.
3. Means of Motivation: Directing guides employees to fully realise their potential by motivating them and providing effective leadership. A good leader can always identify the potential and capacity of his employees and motivate them to work up to their full potential.
4. Directing Facilitate Changes: An organisation operates in a changing environment and employees generally have a tendency to resist changes. Effective directing through motivation, communication and leadership helps to reduce such resistance and develop required cooperation in introducing changes in the organisation.
For example, if a manager wants to introduce new accounting system, then there may be initial resistance from accounting staff. But, if manager explains the purpose, provides training and motivates them with additional rewards, the staff may accept change and cooperate with manager.
5. Provides Stability and Balance in the organisation: Effective direction promotes cooperation and commitment amongst the people and it helps to bring stability and balance in the organisation.

- Employees working at different levels may develop different attitudes and may have different objectives. Directing helps to settle down these clashes and creates a balance in the organisation.
For example, employees work in an organisation to fulfil their objective of higher salary, while the organisation aims to earn higher profits. Through direction, managers try to bring balance in the objectives of both by convincing the employees that their objectives can be fulfilled while achieving organisational objectives.
