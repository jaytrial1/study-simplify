# LEADERSHIP 

Leadership is the process of influencing the behaviour of people at work towards achievement of organisational goals.

- Leadership indicates the ability of an individual to maintain good interpersonal relations with followers and motivate them to contribute for achieving organisational objectives.
- An individual, who possess attributes of leadership is known as 'Leader'. The leader plays a key role in the success of an organisation.
In the words of George Terry, Leadership is the activity of influencing people to strive willingly for group objectives.
In the words of Koontz and Weihrich, Leadership is the art or process of influencing people so that they will strive willingly and enthusiastically towards the achievement of group goals.
Leadership helps in influencing the behaviour of people towards achieving organisational goals.

## Features of Leadership 

The various features of leadership are as follows:

1. Leadership indicates ability of an individual to influence others.
2. It aims to bring change in the behaviour of others.
3. It indicates interpersonal relations between leader and followers.
4. It is exercised to achieve common goals of the organisation.
5. It is a continuous process.

Both leader and followers play key role in Leadership Process
Many times, the success of an organisation is attributed to the leader, but due credit is not given to his followers. Many followers related factors like their skills, knowledge, commitment, willingness to cooperate, team spirit etc., make a person an effective leader. A person can be a successful leader only through willing cooperation and team spirit of followers. So, it is to be recognised that both leader and follower play key role in leadership process.

## Difference between Managers and Leaders

The terms 'manager' and 'leader' are often used interchangeably. However, it is important to distinguish them for purposes of management analysis. The difference between the two will be clear from the following comparison:

| Basis | Manager | Leader |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1. Focus | Manager focuses on influencing the <br> behaviour of employees to achieve goals of <br> the organisation. | Leaders focus to satisfy behaviour of each <br> and every member of group and realisation <br> of group goals. |
| 2. Scope | A manager is always a leader as he has to <br> influence behaviour of his subordinates. | A leader is not always a manager. Informal <br> groups may be lead by non-managers. |
| 3. Formal or Informal <br> organisation | A manager exists only informal organisation. | A leader may exist in formal as well as <br> informal organisation. |
| 4. Authority | Managers have formal authority to influence <br> behaviour of the employees. | Leaders have only informal authority arising <br> due to trust \& faith shown by group members. |

Finally, we can conclude that leadership is a part of management. It is rightly said, "All managers are leaders, but all leaders are not managers".

## Leadership Styles

There are number of theories of leadership behaviour and styles. As per the research studies, a leader might possess certain traits and qualities. However, it is not conclusive as many people may possess these qualities but may not be leaders.
Leadership style refers to behaviour pattern, which the leader reflects in his role as a leader.

- A Leadership style is the result of philosophy, personality, experience \& value system of leader.
- It also depends upon the type of followers and the atmosphere prevailing in the organisation.
- There are several bases for classifying leadership styles. The most popular classification of leadership styles is based on the use of authority.

- Depending on the use of authority, there are three basic styles of leadership:
(i) Autocratic or Authoritative Leadership
(ii) Democratic or Participative Leadership
(iii) Laissez Faire or Free Rein Leadership

A leader may use all the styles over a period of time. However, one style tends to predominate as his normal way of using power.

Autocratic or Authoritative Leadership (Leader-Centered Style)
An autocratic leader gives orders and expects his subordinates to obey those orders.

- Under this style, the leader centralises all decision-making powers and exercises complete control over his subordinates, i.e. there is no delegation of authority.
- Communication is only one-way where subordinate has to act according to the command given by the manager.
- Autocratic leader is dogmatic, i.e. he does not change or wish to be contradicted. According to him, both rewards and punishment can be given depending upon the result.
- This leadership style is effective in getting productivity in many situations like in a factory where the supervisor is responsible for production on time and has to ensure labour productivity. Quick decision-making is also facilitated.
- This style of leadership is also known as 'Directive style of Leadership'.

Boss Centered Leadership

An Autocratic Leader gives orders and insists that they are obeyed.
Such leaders have little or no concern for the well-being of employees. The leader compels
subordinates to follow his orders under threat of penalties. As a result, employees suffer from frustration and low morale. Subordinates become 'Yes Men', avoid responsibility, lack initiative and remain frustrated.

| No. | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 1. | Quick decision-making. | Leads to low morale and frustration among subordinates. |
| 2. | Necessary for less competent employees. | Reduces initiative and creativity of subordinates. |
| 3. | Strongly motivates and satisfies the leader. | Subordinates do not get an opportunity for development <br> and growth. |

## Suitability 

Autocratic leader is suitable in the following cases:

1. Where the supervisor is responsible for production on time and has to ensure labour productivity.
2. When there is a need for quick decision-making or when leader prefers to be dominant in decision-making.
3. When subordinates are illiterate, unskilled, inexperienced or incompetent.
4. When leader is the most knowledgeable member of the group.

Examples: Adolf Hitler, Father Junipero Serra, Genghis Khan, King Henry III, Napoleon Bonaparte, and Queen Elizabeth I are just some people in the world's political history who demonstrated autocratic leadership.

## Democratic or Participative Leadership (Group-Centered Style)

A democratic leader develops action plans and makes decisions in consultation with his subordinates.

- He encourages them to participate in decision-making and to give suggestions in setting goals.
- This style of leadership is more common now-a-days as leaders also recognise that people perform best if they have set their own objectives.
- The democratic leader needs to respect the other's opinion and support subordinates in performing their duties and achieving organisational objectives.
- Such leaders exercise more control by using forces within the group.
- This style of leadership wins greater confidence, cooperation, loyalty and initiative of the group. It raises morale of the subordinates.

This style is suitable in the following cases:

## Laissez Faire or Free Rein Leadership 

Laissez faire or Free-rein leader does not believe in the use of power unless it is absolutely essential.

- The followers are given a high degree of independence and freedom to formulate their own objectives and ways to achieve them
- The group members work on their own tasks and resolve the issues themselves.
- The leader is there only to support them and supply them the required information to complete the task assigned and subordinates assume the responsibility for the work to be performed.
- This style of leadership is known as "laissez faire" which means no interference in the affairs of others. (The phrase laissez-faire is French and literally means "let them do").
This style is more a non-leadership style than a leadership style. The leader acts as an umpire and delegates the entire authority of decision-making in the hands of subordinates. This style is effective only when leaders are leading a team of highly motivated and skilled people, who have produced excellent work in the past.

## Suitability

This style is suitable in the following cases:

1. When subordinates are highly skilled, competent, experienced and educated.
2. When subordinates are self motivated.
3. When subordinates are ready to assume responsibility.

Example: Warren Buffett is known for great success, surrounding himself with people he trusts. He uses this leadership style to make sure the people he works with can do their jobs efficiently so he doesn't have to worry about it, only intervening when it's absolutely necessary.

## Comparison between Different Styles of Leadership 

| Basis | Authoritative Style | Democratic Style | Laissez Faire Style |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 1. Decisionmaking | Leader centralises decisionmaking power. | Leader consults subordinates in the decision-making process. | Leader delegates entire authority of decisionmaking to subordinates. |
| 2. Delegation of Authority | No Delegation. | Delegation of authority to some extent. | Complete delegation of authority. |
| 3. Degree of Supervision | Close supervision. | Moderate supervision. | No supervision. |
| 4. Communication | One Way <br> (Downward Communication) | Two Way Communication | Free flow of Communication |
| 5. Employees <br> Morale | Low | High | Leader does not promote employees morale. |
| 6. Effectiveness | This style may be effective in short-run. | This style is usually effective in both short and long-run. | This style may or may not be effective. |
| 7. Scope of initiative | No Scope | Considerable Scope | Full Scope |
| 8. Positive or negative approach | The leader makes use of negative approach (like penalties) to get work done. | The leader makes use of positive approach (like rewards) to get work done. | The leader neither uses positive nor negative methods. |
| 9. Discipline | Subordinates are obedient and there is full discipline. | Exchange of ideas between subordinates and leader | Self-discipline |

A leader may choose to exercise a combination of these styles depending on the situation. In an emergency situation, even a laissez faire leader would have certain rules to be followed while doing work and a democratic leader may have to take his own decision. It all depends on the circumstances.
