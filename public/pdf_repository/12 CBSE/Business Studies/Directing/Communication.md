# COMMUNICATION 

Communication plays key role in the success of a manager. The professional knowledge and intelligence possessed by a manager becomes material only when he is able to communicate
effectively with his subordinates and create understanding in them. Directing abilities of a

manager mainly depend upon his communication skills. Therefore, organisation emphasise on improving communication skills of managers as well as employees.
The term communication is derived from the Latin word 'communis' which means common, which consequently implies common understanding. Communication refers to a process of exchange of ideas, views, facts, feelings, etc. between two or more persons to reach common understanding.

## Definitions of Communication 

- Communication is the sum of all things, a person does when he wants to create an understanding in the mind of another. It involves a systematic and continuous process of telling, listening and understanding.
-Louis Allen
- Communication is transfer of information from the sender to the receiver with the information being understood by the receiver.
-Koontz and Weihrich
- Communication is a process by which people create and share information with one another in order to reach common understanding.
-Rogers


## Elements of Communication Process

Communication has been defined as a process. The following elements are involved in the communication process:

1. Sender: Sender is the person who conveys his thoughts, message or ideas to the receiver. Sender represents the source of communication.
2. Message: Message may be regarded as a subject matter of communication. It is the content of ideas, feelings, suggestions, order etc., which sender wants to convey to the receiver.
3. Encoding: Encoding is the process of converting message into communication symbols such as words, pictures, gestures, symbols, etc., which may be understood by the receiver. The purpose of encoding is to translate internal thought of sender into a language, so that receiver of the message could understand it.
4. Media: It is the path, channel or medium, through which encoded message is transmitted to the receiver. It may be in written form, face-to-face talk, telephone, letter, internet, etc.
5. Decoding: Decoding is the process of translating the encoded message of the sender into the effective language, which may be understood by the receiver.
6. Receiver: Receiver is a person who receives the message of sender.
7. Feedback: To complete the process of communication, feedback is required. Feedback includes all those actions of receiver which indicates that he has received and understood message of sender.
8. Noise: Noise is any obstruction or hindrance, which hampers the communication process. This hindrance may be caused to sender, message or receiver.
Some examples of noise are:
(i) Ambiguous symbols that lead to faulty encoding.
(ii) Poor telephone connection.
(iii) Inattentive receiver.

(iv) Faulty decoding (attaching wrong meanings to message).
(v) Prejudices obstructing the poor understanding of message.
(vi) Improper Gestures and postures that may distort the message.
(vii) Loss of letters or documents on the way.

Noise acts as a barrier to effective communication and message may be interpreted differently than is the intention of the sender.

## Features of Communication 

1. It is a social process as it involves two or more persons and they exchange ideas, information and knowledge.
2. It is a two-way process, in which sender sends the information to the receiver who receives it, understands it and starts acting upon it and gives a feedback to the sender.
3. It is all pervasive function as information is exchanged at each and every level of the organisation.
4. It is a continuous process.
5. It aims at creating understanding between sender and receiver.
