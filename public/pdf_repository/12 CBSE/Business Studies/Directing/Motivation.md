# MOTIVATION 

Motivation refers to a process of stimulating and inspiring people at work to accomplish desired goals. It is an inner psychological force, which activates and compels the person to behave in a desired manner.

## Definitions of Motivation

- Motivation means a process of stimulating people to action to accomplish desired goals.
-William G. Scout
- Motivation refers to the way, in which urges, drives, desires, aspirations, strivings or needs, direct, control and explain the behaviour of human beings.
-McFarland
- Motivation is the complex forces starting and keeping a person at work in an organisation. Motivation is something which moves the person to action and continues him in the course of action already initiated.
-Robert Dubin
- Motivation is a process which begins with a physiological or psychological need or deficiency which triggers behaviour or a drive that is aimed at a goal or incentive.
-Fred Luthans


## Reason for Motivation

Every manager has a responsibility to get things done by subordinates. To discharge this responsibility, manager has to find out:
(i) What makes people to do things?
(ii) What makes people to behave the way they do?
(iii) Why some people are reluctant to work though they have the required ability?
'Lack of Motivation' often leads to loss of interest in the job.
(iv) What should be done to make people work effectively?

The process of motivation provides answer to all these questions. It helps the manager to analyse and understand the needs of subordinates and to determine how the subordinate can be inspired to work for achievement of organisational goals.

## Motive, Motivation and Motivators

While discussing about motivation, we need to understand three related terms:

1. Motive: Motive refers to inner state or desire, which activates, compels and directs the individual to behave in the direction of achievement of goals.

- The behaviour of an individual consists of some needs, desires and urges, which give rise to some drives and motives.
- When a person recognises some motive, it prompts him to perform some action to reduce such restlessness.
For example, the need for food causes hunger (motive) and it induces a person to search for food. Some examples of motives are: thirst, security, need for comfort, recognition, etc.
2. Motivation: Motivation is a process of stimulating and inspiring people at work to accomplish desired goals. It depends upon satisfying needs of people.
3. Motivator: Motivator is the technique or incentives used to motivate people in an organisation. Managers use diverse motivators like pay hike, bonus, promotion, recognition, responsibility, etc. to influence people to contribute their best.
Motivators are broadly classified into two categories: Financial Incentives and Non-financial incentives. Both the categories of motivators are discussed later in this chapter.

## Process of Motivation 

Motivation process is based on human needs. An Unsatisfied Need of an individual Creates Tension which Stimulates his Drives. These drives generate a Search Behaviour to satisfy such need. If such Need is Satisfied, the individual is Relieved of Tension.
For example, if a person is hungry, then the need for food will create tension and he will start thinking ways to satisfy his need. It will stimulate his drives and will search for different options (bread, biscuits, etc.) to satisfy such need. If the need is satisfied (through bread), then the individual is relieved of tension.

Motivation is a continuous process and it does not end by satisfaction of a particular need. So, after fulfillment of a particular need, another need develops and the process continues.

## Features / Nature / Characteristics of Motivation

The various features of motivation are discussed in the following points:

1. Motivation is an Internal Feeling: Motivation is a psychological concept and cannot be forced on employees. The internal feelings like urges, drives, desires, aspirations, striving or needs influence human behaviour to act in a particular manner.
For example, desire or urge to own a new house or new car. These urges are internal to an individual.
2. Motivation Produces Goal Directed Behaviour: Motivation influences employees' behaviour in such a manner so that they can achieve their goals.
For example, if a person has desire to get promoted, then he will work efficiently to achieve this goal.
3. Motivation can be either positive or negative: Motivation can be either positive or negative.

- Positive Motivation provides positive rewards in the form of additional pay, incentives, promotion, recognition, etc.
- Negative Motivation means making use of negative motivators like punishment, threat of demotion, etc. in order to influence employee's behaviour to act in a desired manner.

4. Motivation is a Complex Process: Motivation is a complex process as the individuals are heterogeneous in their expectations, perceptions and reactions. A particular type of motivation may not have uniform effect on all the members as some get motivated with monetary gain and some with non-monetary.

## Maslow's Need Hierarchy Theory 

An organisation can succeed only when it has highly motivated and committed team of employees. Since motivation is a complex process, various researches have given different theories. These theories help to develop understanding about motivation phenomenon.
Among the various theories, Maslow's theory is considered very significant as it highlights the needs of the people. Abraham Maslow, an eminent U.S. psychologist, outlined the elements of an overall theory of motivation, in a classic paper published in 1943.
Maslow's theory was based on human needs. He felt that within every human being, there exists a hierarchy of five needs. These are:

1. Basic Physiological Needs: Physiological needs are basic for human survival and include need for food, shelter, clothing and other basic necessities of life. These needs form the foundation of hierarchy and basic salary help to satisfy these needs.
2. Safety or Security Needs: These needs provide security and protection from physical and emotional harm. Motivators like benefits of pension plans, job security, stability of income, etc. help to satisfy these needs. Safety or Security needs are concerned with two kinds of securities: (i) Physical Security, i.e. security against death, injury, illness and other bodily threats; and (ii) Financial Security, i.e. security of job and congenial working conditions.

Once the people satisfy their physiological and safety needs then the third need, i.e.'Social Needs' become more active.
3. Social or Affiliation or Belonging Needs: These needs include the need for affection, love, sense of belongingness, acceptance, companionship and friendship. For example, employees often form informal groups to satisfy their social needs.
4. Esteem Needs: These needs include the need for self-respect, autonomy status, recognition and attention. Generally, higher level employees are motivated by such needs.
5. Self-Actualisation Needs: These are the needs of becoming what one really wants to become. It is the highest level of need in the hierarchy. These needs include growth, self-

Motivators like challenging jobs, opportunities for innovation, etc. help to satisfy self-actualisation needs of an individual. People with strong self-actualisation needs do not wait for things to happen; they in fact, make things happen.


## Assumptions of Maslow's Theory 

Maslow's Need Hierarchy Theory is based on following assumptions:

1. People's behaviour is based on their needs. Satisfaction of such needs influences their behaviour.
2. People's needs are in hierarchical order, starting from basic needs to other higher level needs.
3. People are motivated by unfulfilled needs and once a particular need is satisfied, it ceases to be a motivating factor.
4. A person moves to the next higher level of the hierarchy only when the lower need is satisfied.

## Conclusion of Maslow's Theory

Maslow's Theory has been widely recognised and appreciated. It helps managers to realise that need level of employee should be identified to provide motivation to them. However, Maslow's theory has been criticised by some writers. It is said that the need hierarchy may not follow the sequence stated by Maslow, i.e. hierarchy of needs may be different for different kinds of people. For example, in case of creative people like singers, painters, etc., their self-actualisation needs may become the dominant motivation force even before their lower order needs are satisfied. But, despite such criticism, the theory is still relevant because needs (irrespective of their classification) are important to understand the human behaviour.

## Financial and Non-Financial Incentives 

Incentive refers to all measures, which are used to motivate people to contribute more efficiently. The needs of individuals are varied and different. Some of them can be satisfied by money, while others cannot be satisfied by money alone. On the basis of this, the incentives may be broadly classified as:

1. Financial Incentives
2. Non-Financial Incentives
Financial and Non-Financial Incentives

## Financial Incentives

Financial or Monetary Incentives refer to incentives or rewards, which are in direct monetary form or can be calculated in terms of money and serve to motivate people for better performance.

- Money not only satisfies the lower level needs, but also satisfies the higher level needs of social status and power.
- Since, money has the purchasing power, it becomes very important incentive for every individual.
- These incentives may be provided on individual or group basis.

Some of the common Financial Incentives are:

1. Pay and Allowances: Salary is the basic monetary incentive for every employee. It includes basic pay, dearness allowance, house rent allowance and other allowances.

- In some organisations, regular increments in the pay and allowances are directly linked with the performance of employees.
- The scope of increments induces the employees to improve their performance.

2. Productivity Linked Wage Incentives: Some companies have adopted wage plans in which wages are paid according to performance of employees. Such incentive plans aim to improve productivity of employees by paying higher wages to efficient workers as compared to inefficient workers.
For example, in case of differential piece wage system, efficient and inefficient workers are paid at different wage rates.

3. Bonus: Bonus is an incentive offered over and above the wages/salary to the employees. Employees are often given extra amount in the form of bonus when they achieve or exceed their target. Bonus is also given in the form of paid vacations or during the festival time (Diwali, Christmas, etc.)
4. Profit Sharing: Profit sharing is an agreement between employer and employees, in which employees are given a share in profits, if profits exceed a pre-determined level. It motivates the employees to improve their performance and contribute to increase in profits.
5. Co-Partnership/Stock Option: Under this scheme, employees are given an option to purchase shares of the company at a price less than market price.

- Sometimes, in lieu of cash incentives, workers are given option to buy shares of the company. As a result of co-partnership, employees get dividend as well as participation in management in addition to their regular salaries.
- The allotment of shares creates a feeling of ownership in the employees and it induces them to contribute for the growth of the organisation.
For example, Infosys offers the scheme of stock option as a part of managerial compensation.

6. Retirement Benefits: Some companies offer various retirement benefits such as provident fund, pension and gratuity, to provide financial security to employees after their retirement. This act as an incentive when they are in service in the organisation.
Such incentives help in satisfying safety or security needs of the employees.
7. Perquisites: In many companies, perquisites and fringe benefits are offered in addition to basic salary and wages. Such benefits motivate the employees for better performance.
For example, perks like rent free accommodation, car allowance, medical aid, education to children, etc.

## Non-Financial Incentives 

Non-Financial or Non-Monetary incentives refer to incentives or rewards which cannot be measured in terms of money.
All the needs of individuals are not satisfied by money alone. Psychological, social and emotional factors also play an important role in providing motivation. Non-financial incentives mainly focus on these needs.
At times, monetary aspect may also be involved in non-financial incentives, but emphasis is to provide psychological and emotional satisfaction, rather than monetary satisfaction. For example, if an individual gets promotion in the organisation, it satisfies him psychologically more as he gets increase in status, increase in authority, challenge in the job, etc. Though promotion also involves payment of extra money, non-monetary aspects supersede monetary aspects.

2. Organisational Climate: It refers to the characteristics which describe an organisation and distinguish one organisation from the other.

- These characteristics include individual autonomy, reward orientation, open communication, risk-tasking, etc.
- Such characteristics directly influence the behaviour of individuals in the organisation.
- Positive measures by the management helps to create better organisational climate and employees feel motivated in the healthy environment.

3. Career Advancement Opportunity: Every individual aspires for growth and development in his life. Managers should provide opportunity to employees to improve their skills and be promoted to the higher level jobs.

- Appropriate skill development programmes and sound promotion policy will help them to achieve promotions.
- Opportunities of promotion and development work as a tonic and encourage employees to exhibit improved performance.

4. Job Enrichment: Job Enrichment is concerned with designing jobs that include greater variety of work content, require higher level of knowledge and skill.

- It gives more autonomy and responsibility to the workers and provide the opportunity for personal growth and a meaningful work experience.
- Instead of doing the routine jobs, employees enjoy doing jobs which offer them variety and opportunity to show their skill. Enriched and challenging jobs become a source of motivation to the individual.

5. Employee Recognition Programmes: When an employee performs well, he naturally wants it to be praised and recognised by his superiors and fellow-employees. Recognition means acknowledgment with a show of appreciation. Such appreciation satisfies the ego of the employee and he feels motivated to perform much better in future. Superior must try to praise the good efforts of the employee in presence of other employees and in case of mistake committed by him, it should be discussed in private.
Some Examples of Employee Recognition are:
Employee Recognition Programmes

- Congratulating or Rewarding the employee for good performance.
- Displaying of Employee's Achievement on the notice board or in the company news letter.
- Installing award or certificate for best performance.
- Distributing mementus.

6. Job Security: Employees want their job to be secure and want to be sure about their future income and job continuity. Security of job improves the performance and attitude of employees towards work.

- In India, job security is more important due to widespread unemployment.
- However, job security often makes people complacent (self-satisfied) and they do not perform efficiently because of no risk of job. For example, many government employees do not perform efficiently as they have no fear of losing job. So, some terms and conditions must be attached with job security.

7. Employee Participation: It means physical and mental involvement of employees in decision-making process.

- Employees feel important when they are asked to make suggestions. It provides them psychological satisfaction.
- In many companies, these programmes are in practice in the form of joint management committees, work committees, canteen committees etc.
- Employees follow the decisions sincerely when they are made involved in the decision-making process.

8. Employee Empowerment: It involves giving more freedom and powers to subordinates. Empowerment makes people feel that their jobs are important and they contribute positively by using skills and talents in the job performance.
