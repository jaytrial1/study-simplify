# CHANNELS OF COMMUNICATION

A channel of communication is the path through which information flows from sender to receiver. Two main communication channels are:

1. Formal Communication
2. Informal Communication


## Formal Communication 

Formal Communication refers to official communication taking place in the organisation.

- Such communication may take place either between employees of different levels (SuperiorSubordinate) or of same levels.
- Formal communication is used to communicate official matters, such as orders, instructions and other organisational information.
- The communication may be oral or written, but it is generally recorded and filed in the office for future reference.
On the basis of direction, Formal Communication may be classified as:

1. Vertical Communication

- Downward Communication
- Upward Communication

2. Horizontal Communication
3. Diagonal Communication

## Downward Communication

Meaning: It refers to flow of information from higher level (superior) to lower level (subordinate) of the organisational hierarchy.
Purpose: To communicate policies, orders, instructions, etc. to subordinates. For example, meeting notice to employees, ordering subordinates to complete an assigned work, etc.

## Upward Communication

Meaning: It refers to flow of information from lower level (subordinate) to higher level (superior). It is reverse of downward communication.
Purpose: To communicate reports, suggestions, complaints, etc. to superiors. For example, application for grant of leave, submission of progress report, etc.

## Horizontal Communication or Lateral Communication

Meaning: It refers to flow of information between people of different departments working at the same level.
Purpose: To coordinate different activities of two or more departments. It also aims to resolve their interrelated problems.
Example: A production manager may contact marketing manager to discuss issues related to production and sale of goods.

## Diagonal Communication 

Meaning: It refers to flow of information between persons holding different levels of authority in different departments.
Purpose: To increase organisational efficiency by speeding up communication process and cutting across departmental barriers.

Diagonal Communication

Example: Discussion about the production schedule between marketing manager and production foreman.
Limitation: It violates the principle of unity of command. Moreover, superior may feel being ignored when his subordinate talks directly to managers of other departments.

## Features of Formal Communication

1. It takes place by following the formal lines of communication, i.e. it follows scalar chain.
2. It is used to communicate official matters, such as orders, instructions and other organisational information.
3. The communication may be oral or written, but it is generally recorded for future reference.
4. It helps in fixing responsibility as source of information can be located.

## Merits of Formal Communication

1. It ensures orderly flow of information.
2. It helps in fixing responsibility as source of information can be located.
3. It helps in maintaining authority relationship in the organisation.
4. It facilitates control over the work performance of different employees.

## Demerits of Formal Communication

1. It is time consuming as scalar chain is followed while passing information.
2. It obstructs free and uninterrupted flow of information.
3. It is usually conveyed in an impersonal manner.
4. Information may not be transmitted accurately in order to avoid unfavourable effect of communication.

## Informal Communication 

Informal Communication refers to the communication that takes place without following the formal lines of communication.

- Informal Communication arises from social interaction of people. It takes place because of the desire of people to exchange their views, which cannot be done through formal channels.
- Information system of communication is generally referred to as the 'Grapevine' as it spreads throughout the organisation with its branches going out in all directions in utter disregard to the levels of authority.
- Examples: (i) Workers chit-chatting in canteen about the behaviour of superior; (ii) Workers discussing about rumours that some employees are likely to be transferred.
Informal Communication often spread rumours, which may hamper work environment.

- This communication spreads rapidly and sometimes gets distorted.
- It is very difficult to detect the source of such communication. It may also generate rumours which are not authentic. Such rumours and informal discussions may even hamper work environment.
- Sometimes, informal channels are used by the managers to transmit information so as to know the reactions of subordinates.
An intelligent manager should make use of positive aspects of informal channels and minimise negative aspects of this channel of communication.


## Features of Informal Communication

1. It takes place without following the formal lines of communication.
2. It arises out of social needs of the employees.
3. This communication spreads rapidly.
4. It may lead to rumours as it is difficult to detect the source of such communication.

## Merits of Informal Communication

1. It carries information rapidly and therefore may be useful to the manager at times.
2. It is used by the manager to transmit information so as to know the reaction of subordinates.
3. It provides social satisfaction and helps in achieving better human relations.
4. It fills gaps of formal communication by discussing those matters, which cannot be discussed through official channels.
5. It links even those people who do not fall in official chain of command.

## Demerits of Informal Communication

1. It is not authentic and message may get distorted as information travels in a very unsystematic manner.

2. It is difficult to fix responsibility as source of information is not known.
3. It may lead to rumours.
4. It may also result in leakage of confidential information.

## Difference between Formal and Informal Communication 

| Basis | Formal Communication | Informal Communication |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 1. Meaning | It refers to official communication taking place in the organisation. | It refers to unofficial communication that arises from social interaction of people. |
| 2. Relations | It is based on the formal organisational relationship. | It is based on interpersonal contacts irrespective of their posts. |
| 3. Flow of direction | It flows in vertical, horizontal and diagonal directions. | It flows in every possible direction. |
| 4. Channel | It follows formal channels of communication. | It cuts across the formal channels and does not follow organisational hierarchy. |
| 5. Nature | It is rigid in nature. | It is flexible. |
| 6. Reliability | The communication is very reliable. | It is less reliable and may create rumours. |
| 7. Objectives | It aims at achieving organisational goals. | It aims at achieving personal needs and interests. |
| 8. Message | It involves official and well planned messages. | It involves unofficial messages like score of the cricket match. |
| 9. Speed | It is a slow means as communication has to pass through proper channel. | It flows very fast as there is no prescribed official channel. |
| 10. Fixing of responsibility | It is easy to fix responsibility as source of information is known. | It is very difficult to fix responsibility as source of information is not known. |
| 11. Record | It is generally recorded for future reference. | No record is maintained of informal communication. |
