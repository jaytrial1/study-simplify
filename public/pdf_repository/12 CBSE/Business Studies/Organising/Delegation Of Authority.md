# DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY 

When job or workload of a person grows beyond his capacity, then he must share it with subordinates for successful performance.

- The managers at various levels cannot manage and control everything in the organisation. The volume of work makes it impractical for him to handle all by himself.
- So, if he desires to meet the organisational goals, focus on objectives and ensure that all work is accomplished, he must share his workload with subordinates.
- But, just division of work does not guarantee success. In addition to sharing of work, the manager should also share some of his authority with the subordinate. Further, to ensure that subordinate performs the work in the expected manner,

The need for 'Delegation of Authority' arises when workload of a person becomes beyond his capacity.
the manager creates accountability.

- This whole process of sharing work, authority and creating accountability is done through "Delegation of Authority".
Delegation is the transfer of authority from superior to subordinate, entrustment of responsibility and creation of accountability for performance.

## Definitions of Delegation of Authority 

- Delegation is the entrustment of responsibility and authority to another and the creation of accountability for performance.
—Louis Allen
- Delegation of authority merely means the granting of authority to subordinates to operate within prescribed limits.
-Theo Haimann
- It is a downward transfer of authority from a superior to a subordinate.
- Delegation is a pre-requisite for the efficient functioning of an organisation as it enables a manager to concentrate on higher priority activities.
- It also satisfies the subordinate's need for recognition and provides him an opportunity to develop and exercise initiative.
- The person who delegates is known as Delegator and other person as Delegatee.
- The concept of delegation is based on principle of Division of Work.

Lack of Delegation increases the workload and leads to delay in decision-making

## No Delegation in one-man business

In one-man show, everything is done by the owner himself. However, when the owner hires an employee, then owner will share some of his work and authority with this employee. More delegation will take place when more people are employed. So, delegation is needed only when people work together in groups to achieve some common objective.

## Manager remains Accountable even after Delegation

The process of delegation does not relieve the superior of his accountability, i.e. delegation does not mean abdication. It means, delegation does not abdicate (give up or renounce or relinquish) manager of his responsibility, i.e. manager shall still be accountable for the performance of the assigned tasks. Moreover, the authority granted to a subordinate can be taken back and redelegated to another person. So, irrespective of the extent of delegated authority, the manager shall still be accountable to the same extent as before delegation.
For example, a chief accountant in an organisation may delegate his duty to prepare the Balance Sheet to one of his subordinates. However, if the Balance Sheet is not prepared as per format or within the prescribed time, then the ultimate responsibility will be of chief accountant.

Manager remains accountable even after delegation of work.

## Elements or Process of Delegation 

As stated before, according to Louis Allen, "Delegation is the entrustment of responsibility and authority to another and the creation of accountability for performance". It means, the process of delegation has three important elements:

## ELEMENTS OR PROCESS OF DELEGATION


1. Authority: Authority refers to the right of an individual to command his subordinates and to take action within the scope of his position.

- Authority originates by virtue of an individual's position.
- Extent of authority is highest at top level and reduces successively as we go down the organisational hierarchy. Thus, authority flows from top to bottom, i.e., the superior has authority over the subordinate.
- Authority relationship helps to maintain order in the organisation by giving the managers the right to exact obedience and give directions to the workforce under them.
- Authority determines the superior-subordinate relationship where subordinate has to execute the order of superior. The extent to which a superior can exact compliance also depends on the personality of the superior.
Authority is restricted by rules and regulation of the organisation, which limit its scope. However, as we move up in the management hierarchy, the scope of authority increases.

2. Responsibility: Responsibility is the obligation of a subordinate to carry out the assigned duties.

- It originates from the superior-subordinate relationship as subordinate is bound to perform the duty assigned to him by his superior.
- Responsibility flows upwards as every subordinate is responsible to his superior.
- When an employee is given responsibility for a job, then he must also be given necessary authority to carry it out.

Authority granted must be equal to Responsibility assigned
For effective delegation, the authority granted must commensurate with the assigned responsibility.

- If authority granted is more than responsibility, it may lead to misuse of authority.
- If responsibility assigned is more than authority, it may make a person ineffective.

3. Accountability: Accountability is the obligation to carry out responsibility, i.e. beings
answerable for the final outcome.

- Once authority has been delegated and responsibility accepted, one cannot deny accountability.
- It cannot be delegated and flows upwards, i.e., a subordinate is accountable to superior.
- Even after delegation of authority, superior is accountable if the subordinate fails to perform the delegated work. So, manager must ensure the proper discharge of duties by his subordinates.
- It is generally enforced through regular feedback on the extent of work accomplished. The subordinate is expected to explain the consequences of his actions or omissions.

Comparison between Authority, Responsibility and Accountability

Finally, it can be stated that while authority is delegated, responsibility is assumed and accountability is imposed. Responsibility is derived from authority and accountability is derived from responsibility.

## Importance of Delegation of Authority 

Delegation of Authority helps in the efficient functioning of the organisation. The importance of delegation may be identified in the following points:

1. Effective Management: Through delegation, managers are able to function more efficiently as they get more time to concentrate on important matters. Delegation gives them the freedom from doing routine work and provides them with opportunities to excel in new areas.
2. Employee Development: Due to delegation, employees get more opportunities to utilise their talent and skills and improves their career prospects.

- Delegation allows them to develop those skills which will enable them to perform complex tasks and assume those responsibilities which will improve their career prospects.
- It makes them better leaders and decision makers. Thus, delegation develops future managers.
- Delegation empowers the employees by giving them chance to use their skills, gain experience and develop themselves for higher positions.

3. Motivation of Employees: Delegation motivates the subordinates to work with greater responsibility and helps in developing their talents.

- Delegation also has psychological benefits. It shows the trust and confidence of superior on his subordinate.
- Responsibility for work builds the self-esteem of an employee and improves his confidence.
- He feels encouraged and tries to improve his performance further.

4. Facilitation of Growth: Delegation acts as a good training ground and helps in the expansion of an organisation by providing a ready workforce to take up leading positions in new ventures. Trained and experienced employees are able to play significant roles in the launch of new projects.
5. Basis of Management Hierarchy: Delegation establishes superior-subordinate relationship, which is the basis of hierarchy of management. It is the degree and flow of authority which determines who has to report to whom. The extent of delegated authority also decides the power that each job position enjoys in the organisation.
6. Better Coordination: The three elements of delegation (authority, responsibility and accountability) help to define powers, duties and answerability related to various job positions.

- It provides clarity in duties to be performed and avoids overlapping and duplication of work.
- Such clarity helps in developing and maintaining effective coordination amongst the departments, levels and functions of management.
