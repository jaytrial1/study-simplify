# STEPS IN THE PROCESS OF ORGANISING

Organising involves a series of steps that need to be taken in order to achieve the desired goal. It is the process of identifying and grouping the work to be performed, assigning duties to job positions and establishing relationships for the purpose of accomplishing objectives. The various steps involved in the process of organising are:

1. Identification and Division of Work: The first step in the organising process is to identify and divide the total work to be done in accordance with previously determined plans. The work is divided into manageable activities so that duplication of efforts can be avoided and the burden of work can be shared among the employees.
For example, in banks, the total work (like, cash deposit, withdrawal of cash, cheque collection, locker operation, etc.) is divided among different employees. Such division of work promotes specialisation and systematic working as each individual is assigned work as per his qualifications and capabilities.
2. Departmentation or Departmentalisation (Grouping of Work): After identification and division of work, the next step is to group related and similar jobs under one department. Such grouping is known as departmentation or departmentalisation. It facilitates specialisation. Some of the most popularly used basis of grouping are:

- Functions, like Purchase, Marketing, Finance, etc.
- Products, like Appliances, Clothes, Cosmetics, etc.
- Territory, like North, South, West, etc.

For example, sales and advertisement activities may be grouped under marketing department.

3. Assignment of Duties: After grouping various activities into departments, it is necessary to define the work of different job positions and to allocate work to various employees. Each department is placed under the charge of an individual.

- Duties should be assigned to the members of each department on the basis of their skills and competencies.
- A proper match between nature of job and ability of an individual is very important for the effective performance. The work must be assigned to those who are best fitted to perform it.
For example, purchase manager must be assigned duties relating to purchase of raw material.

4. Establishing Reporting Relationships: After assigning duties, the next step is to clearly define the authority and responsibility associated with each job.

- Each individual should also know who he has to take orders from and to whom he is accountable.
- The establishment of such clear relationships helps to create a hierarchal structure and principle of scalar chain follows this hierarchy (i.e. degree of authority goes down from top to bottom in hierarchy). It helps in coordination amongst various departments.
For example, it may be laid down that employees involved in production, purchases and stores will take orders from, and be accountable to production manager.
