This section explains the different objectives that management aims to achieve, categorized into organizational, social, and personnel objectives.

# Objectives of Management

Management seeks to achieve objectives derived from the basic purpose of the business. Objectives are the ends towards which all organizational activities are directed. Management must achieve these diverse objectives effectively and efficiently. Objectives are classified into three categories:

1.  **Organisational Objectives**
2.  **Social Objectives**
3.  **Personnel or Individual Objectives**

### Organisational Objectives

These are the main objectives needed to achieve the organization's economic goals. Management sets and achieves objectives considering the interests of all stakeholders (shareholders, employees, customers, government). The primary aim is to utilize resources efficiently and effectively for the organization's prosperity and growth. Every organization pursues three main organizational objectives:

1.  **Survival**: The basic objective is ensuring continued existence. Survival is possible only when the organization earns enough revenue to cover costs.
2.  **Profit**: Profit is crucial for business survival and growth, providing incentive and covering costs. Management must ensure the organization makes a profit.
3.  **Growth**: A business needs to expand for long-term survival. Growth indicates how well it exploits potential opportunities, measured by sales volume, employee count, market share, number of products, capital investment, etc. Growth is an important organizational objective.

### Social Objectives

These objectives benefit society. As part of society, every organization has social obligations. Management must consistently create economic value for various societal constituents. Major social objectives include:

*   Environmental protection through eco-friendly production methods.
*   Generating employment for underprivileged sections.
*   Providing basic amenities (schools, healthcare) for employees and the community.
*   Supplying good quality products at fair prices.
*   Conducting business lawfully.
*   Avoiding anti-social and unfair trade practices.

Reputed companies like TISCO, ITC, and Asian Paints integrate social responsibility into decision-making. TISCO was the first Indian company to conduct a social audit.

**Examples of Social Objectives by Indian Companies**

*   Ultratech Cement organizes medical camps, immunization, sanitation, school enrollment, plantation, water conservation, industrial training, and organic farming in villages.
*   Mahindra & Mahindra (M&M) runs programs like Nanhi Kali (girls' education), Mahindra Pride Schools (industrial training), and Lifeline Express (healthcare in remote areas).
*   Tata Group conducts social responsibility projects:
    *   Women empowerment, income generation, rural development through self-help groups.
    *   Providing scholarships for education.
    *   Healthcare projects like child education facilitation, immunization, and AIDS awareness.

### Personnel or Individual Objectives

Organizations are made of people with diverse backgrounds, experiences, personalities, and objectives. They join to satisfy various needs. Employees are valuable resources, so satisfying their objectives is important. Personnel objectives include:

*   Financial needs: Competitive salaries and perks.
*   Social needs: Peer recognition, self-respect, and respect for colleagues.
*   Higher-level needs: Policies for personal growth and development.
*   Good and healthy working conditions.

If personnel objectives are not met, employees may lose interest, hampering organizational performance. Management must reconcile personal goals with organizational objectives for harmony.
