This section describes the hierarchy of positions within an organization and defines the three main levels of management.

# Levels of Management

Management is a group activity involving many individuals at different positions with varying responsibilities and authority. This creates a chain of superior-subordinate relationships called the hierarchy or levels of management. The hierarchy of management positions from top to bottom defines the levels.

Usually, three levels of management are identified:

1.  **Top Level Management**
2.  **Middle Level Management**
3.  **Operational or Supervisory or Lower Level Management**

### Top Level Management

This level consists of the senior-most executives, a team from different functional areas (finance, marketing, etc.) heading their departments. Examples include Board of Directors, Chairman, CEO, COO, CFO, President, Vice-President, General Manager, and other senior executives. They are responsible for the organization's welfare and survival.

**Main Functions of Top Level Management**

*   Integrate diverse elements and coordinate activities of different departments according to overall organizational objectives.
*   Analyze the business environment (SWOT analysis) and its implications, staying in contact with external entities (government, competitors, suppliers).
*   Formulate overall organizational goals and strategies.
*   Responsible for all business activities and their impact on society.

The job is complex and stressful, demanding long hours and commitment.

### Middle Level Management

This level links top and lower-level management, subordinate to top management and superior to operational management. They are often called division heads, operations managers, or plant superintendents. This level includes departmental heads like Purchase Manager, Production Manager, Finance Manager, Marketing Manager, Personnel Manager, and other executives attached to departments.

**Main Functions of Middle Level Management**

Middle Level Management is responsible for implementing and controlling plans/strategies from top management and for activities of first-line managers. Their functions include:

*   Interpreting policies framed by top management.
*   Ensuring their department has necessary personnel.
*   Assigning duties and responsibilities.
*   Motivating employees to achieve objectives.
*   Cooperating with other departments for smooth functioning.

### Operational or Supervisory or Lower Level Management

This is the lowest level, also called First Line Managers. Their authority and responsibility are limited by top management plans. This level includes supervisors, foremen, superintendents, section officers, and others directly controlling operative employees.

**Main Functions of Operational Management**

*   Oversee the efforts of the workforce.
*   Interact with the actual workforce and pass instructions from middle management to workers.
*   Ensure quality output, minimize material wastage, and maintain safety standards. Workmanship quality and output quantity depend on workers managed by this level.
*   Represent workers' grievances to middle management as they are in direct contact.
*   Ensure a safe and proper working environment.

Additionally, Supervisory Management helps develop healthy superior-subordinate relations and motivates workers.
