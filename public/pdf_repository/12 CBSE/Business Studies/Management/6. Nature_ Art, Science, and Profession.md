This section explores the nature of management by comparing its characteristics with those of art, science, and a profession.

# Nature of Management

The study of management has evolved based on experience and practice. It is a dynamic subject with unique characteristics. The debate about whether management is an art, science, or profession is frequent. To determine its nature, we compare management features with those of art, science, and profession.

### Management as an Art

Art refers to the skillful and personal application of systematic knowledge to achieve desired results. It is acquired through study, observation, and experience.

**Features of Art Applied to Management**

1.  **Existence of Theoretical Knowledge**: Art is based on theoretical knowledge (e.g., literature on dancing, public speaking). This feature is present in management, which uses theoretical knowledge (principles, techniques). Successful managers apply the art of management based on study, observation, and experience. Extensive literature exists in management areas like marketing, finance, and human resources.
2.  **Personalised Application**: Art is personalized; individuals apply knowledge uniquely (e.g., two dancers differ). This feature is also in management; managers have unique styles despite learning the same theories.
3.  **Based on Practice and Creativity**: Art requires regular practice and creativity to improve skills. This is applicable to management. A good manager uses practice, creativity, imagination, initiative, and innovation. Managers apply knowledge personally and skillfully, formulating their own approaches, leading to different management styles. Students of management also apply principles differently based on creativity.

All management practices stem from the same principles, but the ability to apply them distinguishes successful managers.

**Conclusion: Management as an Art**

Management is an art as it satisfies all its characteristics. It is rightly said, 'Management is the art of getting work done through people'. However, management lacks the perfection found in arts; circumstances change, making exact repetition difficult.

### Management as a Science

Science is a systematized body of knowledge acquired through observation and experimentation, explaining general truths or laws.

**Features of Science Applied to Management**

1.  **Systematised body of knowledge**: Science has a systematic knowledge body with cause-and-effect principles. Management satisfies this; it has a systematized body of knowledge with theories, principles, and concepts developed over time.
2.  **Principles based on Observation and Experimentation**: Scientific principles are developed through observation and tested by repeated experimentation under controlled conditions. This feature is not fully present in management. Management principles evolved from observation and experimentation, but management deals with human behavior, which cannot be accurately predicted or replicated. Thus, management is an inexact science.
3.  **Universal validity**: Scientific principles have universal validity, applicable everywhere. This is also not fully present in management. Management principles are not as exact as science principles. Their application isn't universal and must be modified according to the situation.

**Conclusion: Management as a Science**

Management cannot be considered an exact science like Physics or Chemistry. It is better called an inexact science, social science, or soft science.

### Management: Both Science and Art

Management has elements of both science and art.
*   It is a **Science** because it has a systematized body of knowledge used for training managers.
*   It is an **Art** because it involves applying personal skills improved through practice.

Science provides knowledge, while art applies it. An efficient manager needs not just knowledge of principles but also practical knowledge and skills to achieve creative results. Thus, management as art and science are not mutually exclusive but complementary.

### Management as a Profession

A profession is an occupation backed by specialized knowledge and training, with restricted entry.

**Features of Profession Applied to Management**

1.  **Well-defined body of knowledge**: Every profession has a well-defined body of knowledge acquired through instruction. This is present in management, which has systematic knowledge (concepts, theories, principles) acquired through institutions (like IIMs) and literature.
2.  **Restricted Entry**: Professions restrict entry via examination or degree (e.g., CA exam, law degree). This is not applicable to management; entry is open, and anyone can be appointed manager regardless of educational qualifications. While professional knowledge and training are desirable qualifications today, a specific degree isn't mandatory. So, this criterion isn't strictly met.
3.  **Professional Association**: Professions are affiliated with associations regulating entry, granting practice certificates, and enforcing a code of conduct (e.g., Bar Council). This isn't strictly applicable to management; membership in management associations (like AIMA) isn't compulsory for practice and they lack legal status.
4.  **Ethical code of conduct**: Professions are bound by a code of conduct guiding members' behavior (e.g., doctors' oath). This feature isn't fully present in management; there's no uniform code of conduct. While some associations have codes, they lack legal support.
5.  **Service Motive**: The basic motive is serving clients with dedicated service (e.g., lawyer ensuring justice). This feature isn't fully applicable to management; the primary aim is organizational goal achievement (profit for business, service for hospital). However, profit maximization is evolving; management must consider social obligations alongside economic objectives, earning profits by providing quality products at reasonable prices.

**Conclusion: Management as a Profession**

Management doesn't possess all features of a profession but is moving towards becoming one, driven by the desire for social status and recognition enjoyed by professionals. There's a growing trend towards professionalization.
