This section focuses on coordination, explaining its role as a unifying force within management and its importance and characteristics.

# Coordination: The Essence of Management

Coordination is the force that binds all other management functions. Sometimes considered a separate function, it is fundamentally the essence of management, ensuring harmony among individual efforts towards group goals. Coordination is the process by which a manager synchronizes activities of different departments towards a common goal.

It is the common thread ensuring continuity in organizational work, implicit and inherent in all functions. Coordination is a continuous effort by every manager, who acts like an orchestra conductor directing musicians for a unified performance.

According to McFarland, Coordination is the process whereby an executive develops an orderly pattern of group efforts among his subordinates & secures unity of action in the pursuit of common purpose.

### Coordination: Essence of Management

Coordination is the managerial function of correlating and integrating diverse goals and activities. It's not a separate function but the essence of management because:

1.  **Coordination is needed in all management functions**: Like a thread binding a garland, coordination is part of every function:
    *   Begins at the **Planning** stage when top management plans for the whole organization.
    *   Organizational structure is developed based on these plans (**Organising**).
    *   Positions in the structure are **Staffed**.
    *   **Directing** ensures plans are correctly executed.
    *   **Controlling** addresses discrepancies between actual and planned activities.

2.  **Coordination is needed at all levels**:
    *   **Top level** needs coordination to integrate organizational activities for goal accomplishment.
    *   **Middle level** requires coordination to integrate efforts of different sections/sub-sections.
    *   **Lower level** needs coordination in workers' activities to ensure work proceeds according to plans.

Through coordination, managers ensure orderly arrangement of individual and group efforts for unity of action towards common objectives. It synchronizes efforts, providing the right amount, quality, timing, and sequence to achieve planned objectives with minimum conflict.

### Nature/Features/Characteristics of Coordination

The features of coordination are:

1.  **Coordination integrates group efforts**: It unifies diverse interests into purposeful work, giving a common focus to ensure performance aligns with plans.
2.  **Coordination ensures unity of action**: It aims for unity of action towards a common purpose, acting as a binding force between departments and focusing efforts on organizational goals.
3.  **Coordination is a continuous process**: It's a never-ending process needed at every step, from planning through controlling.
4.  **Coordination is an all pervasive function**: Needed in all departments and levels due to interdependence. It synchronizes activities (purchase, production, sales). Lack of coordination leads to overlapping and chaos. It's necessary for harmony and integration.
5.  **Coordination is the responsibility of all managers**: Every manager is responsible for coordination.
    *   Top managers coordinate with subordinates to ensure policies are carried out.
    *   Middle level coordinates with both top and first-line managers.
    *   Operational level coordinates workers' activities according to plans.
6.  **Coordination is a deliberate function**: Managers consciously and deliberately coordinate efforts. It doesn't happen spontaneously or by force. Even with cooperation, coordination provides direction.

'Coordination' and 'Cooperation' are interrelated. Cooperation without coordination can waste effort; coordination without cooperation can lead to dissatisfaction.

### Importance of Coordination

Coordination is important as it integrates efforts of individuals, departments, and specialists. It's needed because departments and individuals are interdependent, relying on each other for information and resources. Managers must reconcile differences in approach, timing, effort, or interest and harmonize individual and organizational goals.

Points highlighting the importance of coordination:

1.  **Growth in Size**: As organizations grow, employee numbers increase. Individuals differ in habits, goals, backgrounds, and approaches. Integrating their efforts becomes difficult. Coordination ensures individuals work towards common goals, harmonizing individual and organizational goals for efficiency.
2.  **Functional Differentiation**: Organizations divide functions into departments (finance, production, marketing), divisions, or sections, each with its objectives and style. This can lead to clashes (e.g., marketing wanting discounts, finance opposing). Departments aiming for isolated objectives can cause conflict. However, departments are interdependent. Coordination synchronizes departmental activities so they move together towards common goals, not as independent units.
3.  **Specialisation**: Due to complex technology and diverse tasks, specialists are hired. Specialists may believe only they can judge within their area, sometimes ignoring others' input. This can cause conflict among specialists and others. Coordination helps reconcile differences in approach, interest, or opinion among specialists.
