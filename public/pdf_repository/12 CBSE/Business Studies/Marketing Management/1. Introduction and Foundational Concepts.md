This section introduces the concept of marketing, defines key related terms like market and marketing management, and outlines the core activities involved in marketing.

# Introduction

Marketers engage in various activities aimed at increasing demand for products and generating profit by satisfying customer needs and wants. People buy products because they fulfill certain needs. The activities marketers perform to facilitate the exchange of goods and services between producers and users are known as *Marketing Activities*. Marketing is a vital functional area of management. Produced goods serve no purpose unless exchanged for value, involving movement from producers to ultimate consumers. Bringing producers and consumers together requires specific activities and functions, which form the subject matter of marketing.

To properly understand marketing, it is essential to first grasp some related concepts.

# Related Concepts in Marketing

The concept of marketing is evolving. Three key terms used are: *Market*, *Marketing*, and *Marketing Management*. Each has distinct meanings and implications.

## Meaning of Market

The concept of *market* is central to marketing and can be understood in both traditional and modern senses.

*   In the **traditional sense**, Market refers to a place where buyers and sellers physically come together to exchange goods and services.
*   In the **modern sense**, the term market has a broader meaning. It refers to a set of actual and potential buyers of a product or service. For example, the market for fans, bicycles, or shampoos refers to all the actual and potential buyers for these products, regardless of location.

## Classification of Markets

Markets can be classified based on:

*   **Product:** e.g., Cotton market, Gold market.
*   **Geographical location:** e.g., National Market and International Market.
*   **Type of Buyers:** e.g., Consumer Market and Industrial Market.
*   **Quantity of Goods Transacted:** e.g., Retail Market and Wholesale Market.

## Concept of Marketing

The term marketing is described in different ways.

*   Some view marketing as the same as 'Shopping'.
*   Others confuse marketing with 'Selling', believing it starts only after production.
*   Some describe it as 'Merchandising' or product design.

While these views contain partial truth, marketing is a much broader concept with two main approaches: Traditional and Modern.

*   In the **Traditional Sense**, Marketing refers to performing business activities that direct the flow of goods and services from producers to consumers. It is described in terms of its functions or activities.
*   In the **Modern Sense**, Marketing refers to a social process by which individuals and groups obtain what they need and want through creating, offering, and freely exchanging products and services of value with others.

Under the modern concept, marketing begins before production and continues even after the sale. It encompasses all activities that satisfy the needs and wants of present and potential customers *better than competitors*. It involves persuading, rather than forcing, people to purchase.

*Marketing Activities:* Firms produce goods for others' consumption. Moving goods and services from producers to consumers requires activities like product designing, packaging, warehousing, transportation, branding, selling, advertising, and pricing. These are referred to as marketing activities.

# Features of Marketing

The important features of marketing are:

1.  **Need and Want:** Marketing focuses on satisfying the needs and wants of individuals and organizations. Needs are basic and universal (e.g., food), while wants are culturally defined satisfiers of needs (e.g., dosa or chapatti for food). Marketing identifies unsatisfied needs and develops products to meet them.
    *   *Need Vs Want:* Needs are fundamental and product-neutral; wants are specific, culturally shaped objects satisfying needs.
2.  **Creating a Market Offering:** A *market offering* is a complete proposal for a product or service, detailing its features, quality, price, availability, etc. It provides full information about the product. A good market offer is based on analyzing potential buyers' needs and preferences.
3.  **Customer Value:** Consumers evaluate the cost and satisfaction derived from a product.
    *   A consumer buys only when the product provides the **greatest value for money**.
    *   The marketer's role is to **add value** to the product to make it preferable to competitors' offerings.
4.  **Exchange Mechanism:** Marketing operates through exchange, involving the exchange of products/services for money or value.
    *   Exchange occurs when a person obtains a desired product in return for money or value.
    *   It can happen directly between manufacturer and customer or through intermediaries.
    *   Exchange is considered the **'Essence of Marketing'** as it facilitates distribution across wide areas.

*Essential Conditions required for Exchange Mechanism:*
(i) At least two parties (buyer and seller, etc.).
(ii) Each party must offer something of value.
(iii) Each party must be able to communicate and deliver.
(iv) Each party must have the freedom to accept or reject.
(v) Both parties must be willing participants (voluntary exchange).

# Marketing activities also apply to Non-Profit Organisations

Marketing activities are relevant not only to businesses but also to *non-profit organizations (NPOs)* like hospitals, schools, sports clubs, and religious organizations. Marketing helps NPOs achieve goals such as spreading social messages, improving literacy, or providing healthcare.

## What can be Marketed?

In marketing, anything of value to the buyer is termed a *Product*. A product is a bundle of utilities or a source of satisfaction fulfilling human needs and wants. Anything of value can be marketed.

The meaning of *product* extends beyond physical objects (soap, TV) to include services, ideas, places, etc. It can be tangible (pencil, cycle) or intangible (doctor's service). "Anything that is of value to the other can be marketed."

## Some Examples - What all can be marketed?

*   Physical products: TV, iPad, Toothpaste.
*   Services: Insurance, Health Care, Banking.
*   Ideas: Polio vaccination, family planning, blood donation.
*   Persons: For election campaigns.
*   Place: Tourism (e.g., Visit Udaipur: The City of Lakes).
*   Experience: Lunch with a celebrity.
*   Events: Fashion show, music concert.

## Meaning of Marketer

A *Marketer* is any person or entity that takes a more active role in the exchange process. Generally, this is the seller who analyzes needs, develops offerings, and persuades buyers.

However, sometimes the buyer can be the marketer, especially when they need to convince a supplier to provide a product or service under specific conditions (e.g., a country convincing a nuclear fuel supplier of peaceful intent).

# Meaning of Marketing Management

*Marketing management* is the management of all activities related to the marketing function. It involves planning, organizing, directing, and controlling activities that facilitate the exchange of goods and services between producers and consumers/users. Philip Kotler defines it as "the art and science of choosing target markets and getting, keeping and growing customers through creating, delivering and communicating superior customer values of management."
