# MARKETING MANAGEMENT PHILOSOPHIES

Marketing management involves conscious efforts to facilitate exchange of goods or services from manufacturer to consumer. In order to achieve desired exchange outcomes with target markets, it is important to decide what philosophy or thinking should guide the marketing efforts of an organisation.
The marketers can get guidance from the following five distinct concepts of marketing, known as 'Marketing Management Philosophies'. Their understanding is important as it determines the emphasis or the weightage to be put on different factors (like designing the features or selling techniques or on customers' needs or the social concerns), in achieving the organisational objectives.

## 1. Production Concept

According to the production concept, profits could be maximised by producing at large scale, thereby reducing the per unit cost of production.

- Consumers favour those products, which are widely available at an affordable price. Availability and affordability of the product is considered as the key to success.
- Greater emphasis is placed on improving the production and distribution efficiency of the firms.
- Main Focus: Large-scale production to reduce the cost.
- Drawback: Availability and low price of the product do not ensure increased sale and survival and growth of the firm.


## 2. Product Concept

According to product concept, consumers favour those products, which offer superior quality, performance and features.

- Emphasis of the firms shifted from quantity of production to quality of products.
- Product improvement is the key to profit maximisation under the product concept.
- Main Focus: Quality, performance and features of product.
- Drawback: Customers may not buy a product just because it is of higher quality unless they need it.
Product Concept assumes that: (i) Consumers respond more on superior products with reasonable pricing; and (ii) Management can attract customers by making continuous improvement in quality

## 3. Selling Concept 

According to selling concept, firms believe that aggressive selling and promotional efforts will convince a customer to buy the product.

- Due to increased competition, only quality and availability of the product do not ensure survival and growth of the firm.
- Selling concept believes that customers will not buy the products unless they are adequately convinced and motivated to do so.
- Use of promotional techniques such as advertising, personal selling and sales promotion were considered essential for selling of products.
- Focus of business firms shifted to increasing the sale of products through aggressive selling techniques with a view to persuade (or lure or coax) the buyers to buy the products.
- Main Focus: Selling Existing Product (through aggressive selling and promotional efforts)
- Selling concept assumes that customers can be persuaded and manipulated to buy the products. According to them, goods are not bought, but they have to be sold, i.e. main aim is on 'selling what you have'.
- Drawback: Customers once wrongly persuaded can spoil the firm's image. So selling concept can succeed in the short run but not in the long run.
Selling Concept focus on selling by hook or crook. This concept believes that a firm can make a customer buy anything (even without his needs) through aggressive promotion. It involves selling what you have.


## 4. Marketing Concept

Marketing concept emphasise that firms can achieve the objective of profit maximisation by finding out needs and requirements of customers and satisfying them in an effective manner.

- All decisions are taken from the view point of customers, such as what product will be produced, with what features, at what price, etc. For example, if the customers want features of double door in a refrigerator, then organisation would produce a refrigerator with these features, would price it at a level which the customers are willing to pay and so on.
- The marketing efforts start from the identification of needs of the target market.
- This concept aims to make what the customer wants to buy and not what a marketer wants to sell. The basic role of firm under marketing concept is - 'Identify a need and satisfy it better than competitors'. It is a pre-requisite for the success of any organisation.
- Main Focus: Satisfaction of Customers' Needs.
- This concept implies that products are bought not because of their quality or other features but because they satisfy a specific need of a customer.
The marketing concept is based on the following pillars:

1. Identification of target market;
2. Understanding needs and wants of customers in the target market;
3. Developing products or services to satisfy needs of the target market;
4. Satisfying needs of target market better than the competitors;
5. Doing all this at a profit.

Thus, marketing concept recognises that profits can be maximised only after satisfying the needs of the customers.
Marketing Concept cannot be considered as adequate if we consider the challenges posed by social problems like environmental pollution, deforestation, shortage of resources, population explosion and inflation. Due to this reason, the concept of 'Societal Marketing Concept' came into existence.

## 5. Societal Marketing Concept 

Societal marketing concept emphasise that along with customer's satisfaction, due importance should be given to welfare of the customer and the society.

- This concept holds that organisation must identify the needs and wants of the target market and deliver the desired satisfaction in an effective and efficient manner so that long-term well-being of consumers and society is taken care of.
- It is an extension to marketing concept as apart from customer's satisfaction, it also pays attention to social, ethical and ecological aspects of marketing.
- This concept believes that company should not blindly follow the goal of customer satisfaction. For example, a company should not produce anything (say, drugs) just to satisfy a customer.
- Any activity which satisfies human needs but is detrimental to the interests of the society at large cannot be justified. Company should also consider issues of long-term social welfare.
- Main Focus: Satisfaction of customer needs and society's well being.
