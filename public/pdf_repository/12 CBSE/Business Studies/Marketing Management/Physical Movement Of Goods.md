# Physical Movement of Goods

Once goods are manufactured, packaged, branded, priced and promoted, these must be made available to customers at the right place, in right quantity and at the right time. Suppose a customer visits a market to buy a particular product and if that product is not available, he may buy the alternative available brand.
So, it is an important responsibility of the marketers to make the product physically available at a place where the customers would like them to buy. The physical handling and movement of goods from place of production to the place of distribution is referred to as physical distribution.

It covers all the activities required to physically move goods from manufacturers to the customers. Important activities involved in the physical distribution include order processing, transportation, warehousing and inventory control. These activities constitute major components of physical distribution.

## Components of Physical Distribution 

The process of physical movement of goods involves four activities:

## 1. Order Processing

Order Processing refers to time and steps involved between receipt of order from customer and delivery of goods.

- Customer satisfaction and speed of order processing are directly related, i.e. faster the order processing, higher is the rate of customer satisfaction.
- Orders flow in the reverse direction, from customers to the manufacturers.
- A good physical distribution system should provide for an accurate and speedy processing of orders, in the absence of which, goods would reach the customers late or in wrong quantity or specifications. This would result in customer dissatisfaction, with the danger of loss of business and goodwill.


## 2. Transportation

Transportation is the means of carrying goods and raw materials from the point of production to the point of sale.
It is one of the major elements of physical distribution because unless the goods are physically made available, the sale cannot be completed. For example, tea manufactured in Assam is transported to different places of consumption.
There are various means of transportation, like Rail, Road, Air, Water transport, etc. These modes are evaluated and compared on the basis of their advantages and disadvantages and the best mode suitable to the company is selected.

## 3. Warehousing

Storage or warehousing refers to holding and preserving goods till they are delivered to the buyers.

- Warehousing is the act of storing and assorting products in order to create time utility in them. Its basic purpose is to arrange placement of goods and provide facilities to store them.
- The need for warehousing arises because there is a time gap between production and consumption of goods. By bridging this gap, warehousing creates time utility.
Warehousing is necessary as some goods are produced in particular season like agricultural crops but are demanded throughout the year, while some goods are produced throughout the year but are demanded in particular season like woollen clothes.
- Warehousing directly affect the efficiency of firms in serving its customers. For example, more warehouses provides better customer service as less time is needed to serve customers at different locations.
- However, warehousing involves cost of storing. So, firm has to strike a balance between the cost of warehousing and the level of customer service.

- Warehouses are located near production sites for products requiring long storage (such as agricultural products). This helps in minimising the charges on transportation of the goods.
- On the other hand, warehouses are kept near the market for bulky products (like machinery or automobiles) and perishable products (like bakery or vegetables).


## 4. Inventory Control 

Inventory control requires deciding about the level of inventory. Inventory means maintenance of stock of goods held for distribution.

- Higher inventory level ensures better service to customers, but also raises the cost of carrying inventory as lot of capital would be tied up in the stock.
- Any shortage of inventory may lead to loss of customers to competitors' products.
- So, inventory control requires maintenance of inventory at an ideal level so that a balance is maintained between cost and customer satisfaction.
- With advancement in computers and information technology, the need for keeping higher inventory is reducing and the new concept of Just -in-Time-Inventory decision is becoming popular.
- Correct forecast about product demand also reduces the need to maintain higher inventory. It also helps the firm in terms of cash flows and its ability to maintain production at a consistent level.
- The major factors which determine Inventory Level are:
(i) Policy on level of Customer Service: If the firm's policy is to offer a higher level of customer service, then firm must keep large stock so that there is no delay in supply of goods.
(ii) Accuracy in Sales Forecasts: If accurate sales forecasts are available, then firm can manage with less stock. However, in absence of accurate sales forecasts, large stock is needed to meet unexpected demand.
(iii) Responsiveness of Distribution System: It means the ability of the system to transmit inventory needs back to the factory and get products in the market. If the firm can meet additional demand for the product in less time, then need for inventory will be low. However, if firm takes more time in responding to additional demand, then large stock will be needed.
(iv) Cost of Inventory: It includes cost of warehousing, investment in capital, etc. If the cost of inventory is high, then less stock will be maintained. However, in case of low cost of inventory, higher inventory can be maintained.
