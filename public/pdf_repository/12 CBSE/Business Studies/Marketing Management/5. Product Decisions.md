This section focuses on the 'Product' element of the marketing mix, detailing its components including branding, packaging, and labelling, and discussing their importance and functions.

# Product

*Product* means anything of value offered to the market to satisfy a want or need. It's not just the physical item but also the benefits it offers. A car purchase, for example, considers physical qualities alongside intangible factors like brand name, guarantee, status symbol, and company image.

A product is a mixture of tangible and intangible features exchanged for value to satisfy needs. *Product Mix* refers to decisions about quality, design, packaging, etc. A product can provide three types of benefits:

*   **Functional Benefits:** What the product does (e.g., transportation for a car).
*   **Psychological Benefits:** How it makes the user feel (e.g., prestige from a car brand).
*   **Social Benefits:** How it affects the user's standing with others (e.g., group acceptance from a car).

All these aspects are considered in product planning.

# Important Components of Product Mix

The key components of Product Mix are:

B. Packaging
C. Labelling

## A. Branding

*Branding* is a crucial product mix component.

*   Firms decide whether to sell under a *Generic Name* (Fan) or a *Brand Name* (Orient Fan).
*   *Generic name* refers to the class of product (e.g., 'Book'). Selling by generic names makes differentiation difficult.
*   Most marketers use a *brand name* to identify and distinguish their products.
*   Branding is the process of giving a name, sign, or symbol to a product for identification and differentiation. It builds a distinct image in the public mind.
*   An established brand name enhances reputation, goodwill, and generates repeat sales.

## Important Terms related to Branding

*   **Brand:** A name, sign, design, or combination used to identify and differentiate a product (e.g., Bata, Lifebuoy). It has two components:
    *   **Brand Name:** The spoken part of a brand (e.g., Mercedes, Nike).
    *   **Brand Mark:** The part that cannot be spoken but is recognized as a sign, symbol, or design (e.g., Star of Mercedes, Nike swoosh).
*   **Trade Mark:** A brand or part of a brand given legal protection by registration (under The Trade Marks Act, 1999). The registered firm has exclusive usage rights. All trade marks are brands, but not all brands are trade marks.

Though branding adds costs (packaging, labelling, legal, promotion), it offers advantages to marketers and customers.

# Advantages of Branding to Marketers

1.  **Helps in Product Differentiation:** Distinguishes a firm's product from competitors, aiding market control.
2.  **Helps in Advertising and Display Programmes:** Advertising popularizes the brand, not just the generic product. People ask for brands (Close Up, Colgate), not just "toothpaste".
3.  **Differential Pricing:** Established brands can charge higher prices as customers are willing to pay more for preferred brands.
4.  **Ease in Introduction of New Product:** New products introduced under a known brand benefit from its reputation (e.g., Maggie extending its name to ketchup, soups).

## Advantages of Branding to Customers

1.  **Helps in Product Identification:** Satisfied customers easily identify and repurchase desired brands.
2.  **Ensures Quality:** Branding assures a specific quality level. Customers can complain about deviations, building confidence and satisfaction.
3.  **Status Symbol:** Certain quality brands provide status and prestige, offering psychological satisfaction.

## Characteristics of Good Brand Name

Choosing a brand name is critical as changing it is difficult. Factors to consider:

1.  **Simple and easily pronounceable:** Short, easy to say and remember (Surf, Lux). Avoid difficult words.
2.  **Suggestive:** Should hint at benefits, quality, or purpose (Lijjat Papad suggests taste).
3.  **Distinctive:** Unique and easily differentiated (Liril, Sprite).
4.  **Adaptable:** Suitable for packaging, labelling, different media, and languages.
5.  **Versatile:** Can accommodate new products in the line (Maggie, Colgate).
6.  **Legal Protection:** Capable of being registered legally to prevent imitation (Haldiram).
7.  **Staying Power:** Should not become outdated (Bata, Philips).

# B. Packaging

The container or wrapper for a product is the *package*. *Packaging* is designing and producing this container/wrapper. It ensures the package is easy for consumers to use and protects goods from spoilage, leakage, breakage, etc., during handling.

## Levels of Packaging

There are three levels:

1.  **Primary Package:** The product's immediate container. It might be kept until use (plastic packet for socks) or for the product's life (toothpaste tube).
2.  **Secondary Packaging:** An additional protective layer, kept until the product is ready for use (cardboard box for toothpaste tube). Usually discarded after opening the primary package.
3.  **Transportation Packaging:** Outer packaging for protection during transport and storage. Contains multiple secondary packages (corrugated box with 20 units of toothpaste).

# Importance of Packaging

Packaging's importance stems from:

1.  **Rising Standards of Health and Sanitation:** Higher living standards mean preference for packed goods due to less chance of adulteration/contamination.
2.  **Self-Service Outlets:** Popularity of self-service stores transfers some promotional role from personal selling to packaging.
3.  **Innovational Opportunity:** Packaging innovations (e.g., soft drink packaging keeping products fresh, milk storage without refrigeration) expand market scope.
4.  **Product Differentiation:** Packaging helps create brand image and differentiates products. Color, size, material influence consumer perception of quality.

## Functions of Packaging

Packaging performs several important functions:

1.  **Product Identification:** Makes product recognition easy (e.g., Brooke Bond tea). Attractive packages help users remember and identify products.
2.  **Product Protection:** Protects from spoilage, breakage, leakage, damage, climate, etc., during storage, distribution, and transport (e.g., airtight container protects from moisture).
3.  **Facilitating Use of the Product:** Package size and shape should be convenient to open, handle, and use (e.g., Pet bottle of Pepsi/Coke).
4.  **Product Promotion:** Acts as a silent salesman, promoting the product and boosting sales.
    *   Eye-catching design attracts attention at purchase point, sometimes more effectively than advertising.
    *   Plays a key role in self-service stores.

# C. Labelling

A *Label* is part of the product or a tag attached to it, carrying information about the product or seller. It can be a slip or printed statement denoting nature, contents, ownership, price, instructions, etc. *Labelling* is the process of attaching labels.

Labels can be:

*   **Simple Tag:** For local products (sugar, wheat), providing basic info like quality or price.
*   **Descriptive Label:** For branded products, providing complete info on use, performance, features.

Labels are useful for providing detailed product information.

## Functions Performed by Labels

1.  **Describe the product and Specify its contents:** Labels describe the product, usage, cautions, and contents. Manufacturers provide clear descriptions (e.g., cooking procedure on canned food, ingredients/benefits on coconut oil).
2.  **Identify the product or brand:** Distinct labels make product identification easier (e.g., 'Tiger' on biscuit packet). Labels also provide manufacturer name/address, net weight, manufacturing date, MRP, and batch number.
3.  **To help in Grading of the Products:** Labels help categorize products by features or quality (e.g., Tata Tea uses different labels for Tata Agni, Tata Premium, Tata Gold).
