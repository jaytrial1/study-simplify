This section discusses the 'Price' element of the marketing mix, defining price and pricing and detailing the various factors that influence how a product's price is determined.

# Price

*Price* is the amount of money a buyer pays for a product or service. It represents the value consumers exchange for the benefits of having/using the product/service.

*   Price is known by different names (fare for transport, fees for education, premium for insurance).
*   *Pricing* involves determining the product's price. It's crucial in marketing; no product is launched without a price or pricing guidelines.
*   Price regulates demand; generally, demand is higher at lower prices.
*   Pricing is a key weapon in competition. It affects revenue and profits. Determining price is a critical decision for most firms.

## Factors Affecting Price Determination

Various factors influence product pricing:

## 1. Product Cost

Production cost is the primary factor. It includes total cost of producing, distributing, and selling.

*   Total Cost includes Fixed Costs (don't vary with output, like rent), Variable Costs (vary directly with output, like raw material), and Semi Variable Costs (vary but not directly, like salary + commission).
*   The sum of these costs sets the *minimum price* a product can be sold at. Firms usually price to cover costs and a profit margin.
*   Initially, a new product might be priced below cost, but long-term survival requires covering all costs.

# 2. Utility and Demand of the product

While cost sets the lower limit, the product's utility and demand intensity set the *upper limit* a buyer will pay.

*   Price must reflect both buyer and seller interests. Buyers pay up to the point where utility equals price sacrifice. Sellers aim to cover costs.
*   Demand is usually higher at lower prices.
*   Price is affected by *price elasticity of demand*. If demand is elastic (sensitive to price changes), small price changes cause large demand changes. If inelastic (insensitive), total revenue increases with price, allowing higher pricing.
*   Example: A product with many substitutes (LG TV) has highly elastic demand; a small price rise shifts customers to competitors (Samsung, Sony).

## 3. Extent of Competition in the Market

Competition level significantly impacts price. Higher competition tends to lower prices; lower competition allows prices closer to the upper limit.

*   Competitors' prices and reactions must be considered.
*   Competitive product quality and features also influence pricing decisions.
*   Example: Mobile pricing by Samsung, Motorola, Xiaomi is heavily influenced by their intense competition.

## 4. Government and Legal Regulations

Government intervention can regulate prices to protect public interest against unfair practices. Products can be declared essential, and their prices controlled.

*   Example: Government can regulate the price of a life-saving drug if a monopolist tries to fix a very high price.

## 5. Pricing Objectives

A firm's pricing objectives influence price. The general objective is profit maximization.

*   Short-run profit maximization might mean setting maximum price.
*   Long-run profit maximization might involve setting a low price to gain market share and increase profits through higher sales volume (e.g., Jio's low price policy for market share leadership).

*Other pricing objectives:*
(i) **Obtaining Market Share Leadership:** Low price to attract many customers.
(ii) **Surviving in a Competitive Market:** Offering discounts or promotions to clear stock when facing intense competition or substitutes.
(iii) **Attaining Product Quality Leadership:** Charging higher prices to cover higher quality and R&D costs (e.g., Rolls Royce).

# 6. Marketing Methods Used

Pricing is also affected by other marketing elements: distribution system, sales force quality, advertising quality/amount, sales promotion, packaging type, product differentiation, credit facilities, customer services.

*   If a product has uniqueness (due to intensive advertising or other reasons), the firm might charge a higher price.
