This section details the various activities that constitute the marketing function, from gathering information and planning to pricing, distribution, and promotion.

# Functions of Marketing

Marketing is composed of numerous activities known as *marketing functions*. These functions are involved in the exchange of goods from producer to consumer.

1.  **Gathering and Analysing Market Information:** A key function is collecting and analyzing market information.
    *   This is essential for identifying customer needs and making decisions for successful marketing.
    *   It involves analyzing opportunities, threats, and organizational strengths/weaknesses to determine viable opportunities.
    *   Increasingly, companies use interactive internet sites to gather customer views before making decisions.
2.  **Marketing Planning:** After analysis, the next function is developing marketing plans to achieve marketing objectives. For example, a TV marketer aiming to increase market share from 10% to 20% needs a plan covering production, promotion, and specific action programs.
3.  **Product Designing and Development:** This involves decisions about product features, design, shape, size, appearance, etc.
    *   Design makes a product attractive and can improve performance and provide a competitive advantage.
    *   For example, car buyers consider design alongside cost and mileage.
    *   Continuous product design and development are crucial for organizational success.
4.  **Standardisation and Grading:**
    *   **Standardisation:** Producing goods to predetermined specifications for uniformity and consistency (e.g., defining standards for size, quality, design, color). It assures quality, price, and packaging, reducing inspection needs.
    *   **Grading:** Classifying products into groups based on characteristics like quality or size. Necessary for products not made to predetermined specifications (e.g., agricultural products). Grading ensures specific quality levels and helps in obtaining higher prices for better quality. For example, classifying sugar as Normal, Refined, and Double Refined.
5.  **Packaging and Labelling:**
    *   **Packaging:** Designing and developing the container or wrapper.
    *   **Labelling:** Designing and developing the label for the package. Labels range from simple tags to complex graphics.
    *   Both are vital in marketing. Packaging protects the product (spoilage, leakage, etc.) and serves as a promotional tool. Packaging can influence perceived quality.
    *   Labelling is important as labels provide detailed information (contents, usage, manufacturing/expiry dates, price, batch number).
6.  **Branding:** Giving a special name to the product.
    *   Firms decide between selling under a *Generic Name* (e.g., Fan) or a *Brand Name* (e.g., Orient Fan).
    *   A brand name distinguishes the product from competitors, helps build customer loyalty, and promotes sales.
    *   Companies also choose branding strategies (separate names per product or extending one name).
    *   The brand name must be chosen carefully for product success.
7.  **Customer Support Services:** In competitive markets, customer support is crucial. This includes after-sale services, handling complaints, credit facilities, maintenance, and technical services.
    *   These services aim for maximum customer satisfaction, key to modern marketing success.
    *   Customer support encourages repeat sales and develops brand loyalty.
8.  **Pricing of Products:** Price is the money a customer pays for a product. Pricing involves determining this price.
    *   Pricing is crucial as consumers are price-sensitive, and price affects demand. Lower price often means higher demand.
    *   Marketers consider various factors for pricing, including objectives, strategies, determining the price, and changing prices.
9.  **Physical Distribution:** Deciding how to move goods and services from production to consumption.
    *   Involves two main decisions: (i) Choosing *channels of distribution* or intermediaries (wholesalers, retailers); (ii) Planning *physical movement* of goods.
    *   Key areas include inventory management, storage/warehousing, and transportation.
10. **Promotion:** Activities informing customers about products and persuading them to buy.
    *   The four main tools are advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, and public relation.
    *   Marketers make decisions about promotion budgets and the mix of tools used.
11. **Transportation:** Physical movement of goods from production to sale points.
    *   Consumers are geographically separated from production. Transportation bridges this gap (e.g., moving Assam tea).
    *   Firms analyze transportation needs based on product nature, cost, market location, and choose appropriate modes.
12. **Storage or Warehousing:** Holding and preserving goods between production and consumption.
    *   Needed due to time gaps (irregular demand like woolen garments, or irregular supply like seasonal crops).
    *   Warehousing maintains smooth flow, protects goods from damage, and allows for stocking against delivery delays or demand contingencies.
    *   Performed by manufacturers, wholesalers, and retailers.
