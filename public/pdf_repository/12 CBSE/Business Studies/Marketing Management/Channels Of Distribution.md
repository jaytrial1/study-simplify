# Channels of Distribution

In case of large number of consumer products, the potential buyers are scattered over a wide geographical area. In order to contact these people efficiently and effectively, it is important to take the help of number of intermediaries as contacting them directly may not be cost effective and may also be difficult. For example, a manufacturer of soap in Gujarat would find it very difficult to directly approach customers of Delhi and other far off places. In such a case, the manufacturer will take help of various middlemen or intermediaries so that soap can be supplied to all the consumers. ## Advantages of Channels of Distribution 

1. They bring economy of efforts by reducing the efforts of producer in distribution and of consumer in shopping.
2. They help to cover large geographical area and bring efficiency in distribution, including transportation, storage and negotiation.
3. They bring convenience to customers as they can buy all items at one store.
4. They provide authentic and useful market information as they are in direct contact with the customer.
5. They are very useful for big companies because direct selling is difficult for such companies.

## Types of Channels

The two most common channels used are:

1. Direct Channel
2. Indirect Channels

## TYPES OF CHANNELS


## 1. Direct Channel (Zero Level)

In this channel, manufacturer sells his product directly to consumers without using any intermediary. This channel is the shortest and simplest of all channels. Direct channel helps in establishing straight and direct relationship with the customer.

$$
\text { Manufacturer } \rightarrow \text { Consumer }
$$

## Methods of Direct Distribution

Under direct channel, goods can be sold to consumers in different ways:
(i) Company owned retail shops (like in case of Bata or McDonald's);
(ii) Door to door selling (like in case of Eureka Forbes);
(iii) Mail order selling;
(iv) Telemarketing;
(v) Internet Selling.

## 2. Indirect Channels 

When a manufacturer involves one or more intermediary to move goods and services to consumers, the distribution network is called indirect.
Indirect channels can be of following forms:
(i) One Level Channel: In this channel, manufacturer employs one intermediary (retailer) to sell goods to consumers. Manufacturer sells the goods to retailer, who sells them to the final users. It helps to cover wide area of market while retaining control over the channels. For example, cars, electronic appliances, etc. are sold through retailers.

$$
\text { Manufacturer } \rightarrow \text { Retailer } \rightarrow \text { Consumer }
$$

(ii) Two Level Channel: This channel consists of two types of intermediaries (wholesaler and retailer), through whom manufacturer sell goods to consumers.

- The manufacturer sells the goods in bulk to wholesaler, who sells in small lot to retailers and they finally sell to the consumers.
- This channel is commonly used for consumer goods like soaps, oils, rice, etc.
- Use of two middlemen enables the manufacturer to cover a much larger market area.

Manufacturer $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer
(iii) Three Level Channel: This channel consists of three intermediaries (agent, wholesaler and retailer), through whom manufacturer sell goods to consumers.

- In this channel, an agent in each major area is appointed and manufacturer uses his services for the initial distribution of goods.
- The agent in turn, distribute to wholesalers, who in turn sell to retailers.
- This channel is used when the manufacturer produces a limited product line and wants to cover a wide market.
For example, a company may have one sole selling agent acting as a national distributor.

Manufacturer $\rightarrow$ Agent $\rightarrow$ Wholesaler $\rightarrow$ Retailer $\rightarrow$ Consumer
