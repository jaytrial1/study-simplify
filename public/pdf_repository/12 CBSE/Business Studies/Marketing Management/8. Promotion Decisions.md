This section details the 'Promotion' element of the marketing mix, explaining its purpose and components: advertising, personal selling, sales promotion, and public relations, highlighting their features, merits, and differences.

# Promotion

After planning, pricing, and distribution, the marketing manager must formulate a strategy to communicate with prospective buyers. Without communication, customers won't know about the product, its utility, and benefits. *Promotion* refers to activities informing customers and persuading them to buy. It uses tools to encourage exchange.

# Elements of Promotion Mix

*Promotion mix* is the combination of promotional tools used to achieve communication objectives.
The tools are:
A. Advertising
B. Personal Selling
C. Sales Promotion
D. Public Relation

These tools can be combined differently based on factors like market nature, product nature, budget, and objectives. Consumer goods firms might use more advertising; industrial goods firms might use more personal selling.

## A. Advertising

## Concept of Advertising

*Advertising* is an impersonal, paid form of communication by an identified sponsor to promote goods or services.
David Ogilvy: "If you're trying to persuade people... you should use their language..."
Andy Travis: "We find that advertising works the way the grass grows. You can never see it, but every week you have to move the lawn."

*   Advertising is a common promotion tool, providing information on benefits, price, and availability.
*   Basic purpose is to increase demand and sales.
*   Common modes: Newspapers, Television, Magazines, Radio.

## Features of Advertising

*   **Paid Form:** Sponsor pays for the communication.
*   **Impersonality:** No direct face-to-face contact; it's a monologue.
*   **Identified Sponsor:** Undertaken by a known individual or company who pays the cost.

# Merits of Advertising

1.  **Mass Reach:** Reaches a large number of people over a vast area (newspaper, TV ads reach lakhs).
2.  **Enhancing Customer Satisfaction and Confidence:** Builds confidence in prospective buyers, making them feel more comfortable and assured about quality, leading to satisfaction.
3.  **Expressiveness:** Modern advertising uses techniques, graphics, and multimedia for forceful, appealing, and attractive messages.
4.  **Economy:** Economical due to mass reach; total cost spread over many people means low per-person cost.

## B. Personal Selling

## Concept of Personal Selling

*Personal Selling* involves oral presentation in a conversation with prospective customers to make sales. Salespersons contact buyers to build product awareness, preference, and ultimately make a sale.

## Features of Personal Selling

1.  **Personal Form:** Direct face-to-face communication between seller and buyer. Allows interactive discussion.
2.  **Development of Relationship:** Helps build personal, friendly relationships between salesperson and customer, important for sales.
3.  **Two-Way communication:** Both buyer and seller can talk, providing better response.
4.  **Oral Conversation:** Involves spoken interaction.
5.  **Flexibility:** Message can be adjusted based on the situation.

Advertising Vs Personal Selling

| Basis | Advertising | Personal Selling |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 1. Kind of Communication | Impersonal communication. | Personal form of communication. |
| 2. Uniformity of message | Transmits uniform messages to all customers. | Message is not uniform and has to be adjusted as per customer. |
| 3. Flexibility | Inflexible as message can't be adjusted. | Highly flexible as message can be adjusted. |
| 4. Reach | Reaches large number of people. | Reaches limited people because of time and cost consideration. |
| 5. Cost | Cost per person reached is very low. | Cost per person reached is quite high. |
| 6. Time | Covers market in short time. | Needs lot of time to cover the entire market. |
| 7. Feedback | Direct feedback is not possible. | Provides direct and immediate feedback. |
| 8. Role | More useful in creating & building interest of consumers. | Important at the awareness stage of decision making. |
| 9. Type of customers | More useful for ultimate consumers (large numbers). | More useful for industrial buyers or intermediaries (fewer numbers). |
| 10. Medium | T.V., radio, newspapers, magazines, etc. | Sales staff. |

# C. Sales Promotion

## Concept of Sales Promotion

*Sales Promotion* refers to short-term incentives offered to encourage immediate purchase. Activities include discounts, contests, free gifts, samples. It supplements advertising and personal selling. Tools are used for:

*   **Customers:** Discounts, free samples, contests.
*   **Traders/Middlemen:** Cooperative advertising, dealer discounts, incentives, contests.

Public Relation is a continuous activity for creating and managing successful relations with the public.

*Public Relations* refers to programs promoting and protecting a company's image or products in the public eye.

## Definitions of Public Relation

*   "Public Relations is the art and social science of analyzing trends, predicting their consequences, counseling organisational leaders and implementing planned programme of action which will serve both the organisation and the public interest." - The Public Association Relations
*   "Public Relations as a strategic management function that adds value to an organisation by helping it to manage its reputation." - The Chartered Institute of Public Relations

Public Relations involves programs to promote and protect a company's image/products, strengthening relations with stakeholders (customers, shareholders, employees, suppliers, investors) through news, speeches, events (sports, concerts, seminars).

## Important Points about Public Relation

*   Most organizations have a separate PR department or use outside agencies. Their task is to disseminate info, build goodwill, monitor public attitude, and generate positive publicity.
*   PR departments are vital during negative publicity, working to protect the company's image.
*   They advise top management on programs to enhance public image and prevent negative publicity.
*   PR uses various tactics, often providing info to independent media for favorable coverage.
*   It mixes promoting specific products/events with promoting the overall brand.
*   Good PR helps:
    (i) Smooth business functioning and objective achievement.
    (ii) Building corporate image that favorably affects products (e.g., maintaining parks, sponsoring sports).
    (iii) Building interest in established products and helping launch new ones.

*Maintaining good public relations helps achieve Marketing Objectives:*
(a) **Building Awareness:** PR department can place stories/dramatize products in media, creating excitement before launch and a favorable impression.
(b) **Building Credibility:** News about a product in media adds credibility, boosting sales.
(c) **Stimulates Sales Force:** Easier for sales force to deal with retailers if they've heard about the product in the news. Retailers find it easier to sell.
(d) **Lowers Promotion Costs:** More economical than advertising/direct mail, though it requires communication/interpersonal skills to get media coverage.

# Tools of Public Relation

PR departments use tools to build a better company image:

1.  **Press:** Newspapers, magazines, TV, radio provide information about the marketer/products. Good relations with the press help generate good PR.
2.  **Newsletters:** Regular publications distributed to target audiences to create product awareness.
3.  **Conferences and Seminars:** Conducted to inform people about the organization (e.g., travel companies offering packages).
4.  **Events:** Big image-building devices (opening ceremonies, star-studded events).
5.  **Written Material:** Published materials (annual reports, brochures, articles, magazines) used to improve/maintain public image.
6.  **Social Service Activities:** Engaging in public welfare (maintaining parks, charitable dispensaries) to create a good public image.
