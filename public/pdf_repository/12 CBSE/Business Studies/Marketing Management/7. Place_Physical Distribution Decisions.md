This section covers the 'Place' element of the marketing mix, focusing on making products available to customers. It explains channels of distribution and the physical movement of goods, including key activities like order processing, transportation, warehousing, and inventory control.

# Place/Physical Distribution

*Place* or *Physical Distribution* includes activities needed to make goods and services available at the right place for purchase.

It involves deciding how to distribute goods from production to consumption. Intermediaries (middlemen) are used to ensure goods are available to customers at the right place, quantity, and time.

This involves two main decisions:
A. Channels of Distribution
B. Physical Movement of Goods

## A. Channels of Distribution

For many consumer products, potential buyers are scattered widely. Using intermediaries is efficient and cost-effective compared to direct contact. *Channels of Distribution* are the set of firms and individuals that take title or assist in transferring title of goods/services from producers to consumers.

*   A channel is a team of merchants, agents, and institutions combining physical and title movement to reach destinations.
*   Intermediaries like agents, wholesalers, and retailers help make goods available (e.g., buying salt, tea, etc., from retailers).

# Advantages of Channels of Distribution

1.  **Economy of efforts:** Reduces distribution effort for producers and shopping effort for consumers.
2.  **Large geographical area coverage:** Brings efficiency in distribution, including transportation, storage, and negotiation.
3.  **Convenience to customers:** Allows buying various items at one store.
4.  **Authentic market information:** Provide useful market data due to direct customer contact.
5.  **Useful for big companies:** Direct selling is often difficult for large firms.

## Types of Channels

The two most common channels are:
1.  Direct Channel
2.  Indirect Channels

## 1. Direct Channel (Zero Level)

Manufacturer sells directly to consumers without intermediaries. This is the shortest, simplest channel, establishing a direct relationship with the customer.

## Methods of Direct Distribution

Goods can be sold directly via:
(i) Company owned retail shops (Bata, McDonald's).
(ii) Door-to-door selling (Eureka Forbes).
(iii) Mail order selling.
(iv) Telemarketing.
(v) Internet Selling.

# 2. Indirect Channels

Manufacturer uses one or more intermediaries to move goods.

(i) **One Level Channel:** Uses one intermediary (retailer). Manufacturer sells to retailer, who sells to consumers. Covers a wide area while retaining channel control (e.g., cars, electronic appliances).

| Manufacturer | $\rightarrow$ | Retailer | $\rightarrow$ | Consumer |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |

(ii) **Two Level Channel:** Uses two intermediaries (wholesaler and retailer). Manufacturer sells in bulk to wholesaler, who sells to retailers, who sell to consumers. Common for consumer goods (soaps, oils). Covers a much larger market area.

| Manufacturer | $\rightarrow$ | Wholesaler | $\rightarrow$ | Retailer | $\rightarrow$ | Consumer |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |

(iii) **Three Level Channel:** Uses three intermediaries (agent, wholesaler, retailer). Manufacturer appoints an agent in each major area for initial distribution. Agent distributes to wholesalers, who sell to retailers. Used when producing a limited product line for a wide market (e.g., sole selling agent as national distributor).

## B. Physical Movement of Goods

After manufacturing, packaging, branding, pricing, and promoting, goods must be physically available at the right place, quantity, and time. If a product isn't available, a customer might buy a competitor's alternative.

Making products physically available where customers want to buy is a key marketer responsibility. *Physical distribution* is the physical handling and movement of goods from production to distribution points.

It covers activities to physically move goods from manufacturers to customers. Important activities are order processing, transportation, warehousing, and inventory control.

# Components of Physical Distribution

Physical movement involves four activities:

## 1. Order Processing

Time and steps from receiving a customer order to delivery.

*   Customer satisfaction relates directly to order processing speed.
*   Orders flow from customers to manufacturers.
*   An efficient system needs accurate, speedy order processing to avoid late delivery, wrong quantity/specifications, and resulting dissatisfaction/loss of business.

## 2. Transportation

Carrying goods and raw materials from production to sale.

*   Major physical distribution element; sale isn't complete until goods are physically available.
*   Various modes (Rail, Road, Air, Water) are evaluated based on advantages/disadvantages, selecting the best fit.

## 3. Warehousing

Holding and preserving goods until delivery.

*   *Warehousing* is storing and assorting products to create *time utility*. It bridges the time gap between production and consumption.
*   Necessary for seasonal production/demand (agricultural crops, woollen clothes).
*   Affects customer service efficiency; more warehouses mean faster service.
*   Involves storage costs, requiring a balance between cost and service level.
*   Warehouses are located near production for long storage (agricultural) and near markets for bulky/perishable goods (machinery, vegetables).

# 4. Inventory Control

Deciding the level of inventory (stock of goods for distribution).

*   Higher inventory means better customer service but higher carrying costs (capital tied up).
*   Inventory shortage can lead to customer loss to competitors.
*   *Inventory control* aims for an ideal level balancing cost and satisfaction.
*   Advancements in computers/IT reduce need for high inventory; *Just-in-Time-Inventory* is becoming popular.
*   Accurate demand forecasts reduce need for high inventory, helping cash flow and consistent production.

*Major factors determining Inventory Level:*
(i) **Policy on level of Customer Service:** Higher service policy requires larger stock to avoid delays.
(ii) **Accuracy in Sales Forecasts:** Accurate forecasts allow less stock; inaccurate forecasts need large stock for unexpected demand.
(iii) **Responsiveness of Distribution System:** Ability to quickly transmit inventory needs back to factory and get products to market. Faster response needs less inventory; slower response needs more.
(iv) **Cost of Inventory:** Includes warehousing cost, capital investment. High cost means less stock; low cost allows higher inventory.
