This section defines decentralisation, contrasts it with centralisation, explains why both exist simultaneously, and highlights the advantages of decentralisation.

# Decentralisation

**Decentralisation** refers to systematic delegation of authority through all the levels of management and in all the departments except that which can be exercised only at central points.

## Definitions of Decentralisation

*   "Everything which goes to increase the importance of a subordinate's role is decentralisation, everything that goes to reduce it is centralisation."
*   "Decentralisation refers to systematic effort to delegate to the lowest level all authority except that which can be exercised at central points."
    - Louis Allen
*   Decentralisation explains the manner in which decision-making responsibilities are divided among hierarchical levels.
*   Decision-making authority is pushed down the chain of command, i.e. it is shared with lower levels and is placed nearest to the points of action.
*   An organisation can be regarded as greatly decentralised if the decisions taken by the lower levels are numerous as well as important.

## Decentralisation and Centralisation

**Centralisation** and Decentralisation refer to the extent to which the authority and responsibility is passed to lower levels.

*   If authority to take decisions is retained at top levels, then organisation is said to be centralised.
*   However, if decision-making authority is widely distributed, then organisation is said to be decentralised.
*   Centralisation of authority refers to concentration of the decision-making power at the top level of management. In a centralised organisation, managers at lower levels have limited role in decision-making. They have to just execute the decisions taken at the top level.

## Every Organisation is Centralised as well as Decentralised

Centralisation and decentralisation are relative terms, i.e. absolute or complete centralisation or decentralisation is not possible.

*   Complete centralisation means concentration of all decision-making powers at top level. It is not possible as it would obviate (remove) the need for a management hierarchy. A manager cannot retain all the authority and yet have subordinates in the organisation. It is possible in a one-man business. But, when the owner employs even one additional hand, some decentralisation takes place.
*   Complete decentralisation means delegation of all decision-making functions to the lower levels. This is also not possible as it would obviate the need for higher managerial positions. A manager cannot delegate all his authority without surrendering his position as a manager.
*   So, an organisation can never be completely centralised or decentralised. An organisation moves towards decentralised decision-making as it grows in size and complexity. It happens because in large organisations, employees involved with certain operations tend to have more knowledge about them than the top management who are indirectly associated with individual operations. So, in order to increase effectiveness and to improve overall functioning, the organisation must maintain a balance between centralisation and decentralisation.

## Difference between Centralisation and Decentralisation

| Basis                      | Centralisation                                                               | Decentralisation                                                                 |
| :------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Meaning                    | It refers to concentration of authority at the top level.                    | It refers to evenly and systematic distribution of authority at all levels.      |
| Delegation of authority    | There is no delegation as authority for decision-making is retained at the top level. | There is systematic delegation of authority at all levels of management.         |
| Suitability                | It is suitable for small organisations.                                      | It is suitable for large organisations.                                          |
| Freedom of decision-making | There is no freedom of decisions-making at middle and lower levels.          | Managers at all levels enjoy freedom of decision-making.                         |

## Decentralisation is an extension of Delegation

Delegation refers to transfer of authority by a superior to the subordinate, whereas, decentralisation involves distribution of authority throughout the organisation.

*   In delegation, authority is multiplied by two as authority is shared between superior and subordinate.
*   However, in decentralisation, authority is multiplied by many as authority is shared between different superiors and subordinates at all levels of management.
*   Example for Better Understanding: Suppose, Kapil (Managing Director of the Company) assigns the job of purchasing stationery for all the departments of the organisation to Shivam (Head of Production Department). Now, this assignment of work between two people, i.e. by Kapil to Shivam will be termed as **Delegation of Authority**. However, if this job of purchasing stationery is given by Kapil to each departmental head of the organisation, then this will be termed as **decentralisation**. So, it is rightly said that '*Decentralisation is an extension of delegation*' or '*If we delegate the authority, we multiply it by two; if we decentralise it, we multiply it by many*'.

## Importance of Decentralisation

Decentralisation is a philosophy that implies selective dispersal of authority as it promotes the belief that people are competent, capable and resourceful. However, management must carefully select the extent and type of decision-making power to be transferred to the lower levels.
Decentralisation offers following advantages to an enterprise:

1.  **Develops Initiative among Subordinates:** Decentralisation helps to promote self-reliance and confidence amongst the subordinates.
    *   It gives them greater freedom to take their own decisions and to depend on their own judgment.
    *   A decentralisation policy helps to identify executives with potential to become dynamic leaders.
2.  **Develops Managerial Talent for the future:** Decentralisation provides a better means to develop future managers as it gives them a chance to take decisions, exercise judgment and perform activities of top executives.
    *   Decentralisation creates a pool of qualified manpower, who can be considered to fill up more challenging positions through promotions. It gives them a chance to prove their abilities.
    *   It also helps to identify those who may not be successful in assuming greater responsibility.
3.  **Quick Decision-making:** Decentralisation facilitates prompt decision-making as decisions are taken by the subordinates at the point of action and there is no requirement for approval from higher authorities.
    *   They are not required to wait for consultation or instructions from the higher authorities. This speeds up the process, especially in dynamic operating conditions.
    *   On the other hand, decision-making is very slow in a centralised organisation as every decision is taken by the top management and information flow follows long channels.
4.  **Relief to Top Management:** Decentralisation reduces the need for direct supervision as subordinates are given freedom to act and decide within the limits.
    *   Personal supervision is generally replaced by other forms of control such as return on investment, etc.
    *   It also relieves the top management from routine and time-consuming operations.
    *   They can concentrate on important policy decisions, like strategic planning, major policy formulation, etc.
5.  **Facilitates Growth:** Decentralisation awards greater freedom or autonomy to the lower levels of management as well as divisional or departmental heads.
    *   This allows them to function in a manner best suited to their department and promotes competition amongst the departments.
    *   As each department put its best efforts, the overall productivity increases and organisation is able to generate more returns for expansion purposes.
6.  **Better Control:** Decentralisation makes it possible to exercise control by evaluating performance at each level and the departments can be held accountable for their results.
    *   The extent of achievement of organisational objectives as well as the contribution of each department in meeting the overall objectives can be ascertained.
    *   Feedback from all levels helps to analyse variances and improve operations.
    *   Control systems, such as balance score card and management information system (MIS) have been evolved for accountability of performance.

## Conclusion

Inspite of number of benefits of decentralisation, it should be applied with caution as it can lead to organisational disintegration if the departments start to operate on their own guidelines which may be contrary to the interest of the organisation. So, decentralisation must always be balanced with centralisation in areas of major policy decisions.
