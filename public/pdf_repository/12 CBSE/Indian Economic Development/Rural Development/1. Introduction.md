1_Introduction

In India, the majority of poor people live in rural areas, where they do not have access to the basic necessities of life. Around 22% of our total population still lives below the poverty line.

# Challenges in the Agricultural Sector

- Agriculture, with the maximum share of the rural economy, has grown at a meagre rate of 2.7% in the last fifty years. During 2007-12, agriculture output has grown at 3.2%.
- The share of the agriculture sector to GDP was on a decline and there has been an increase in the share of the industrial and service sector. However, the population dependent on the agricultural sector did not show any significant change. More than 2/3rds of India's population depends on agriculture, which has yet to become productive enough to meet all their needs.
- Moreover, after the economic reforms of 1991, the growth rate of the agriculture sector decelerated to 3% p.a. during 1991-2012, which was lower than the earlier years.
- During 2014-15, the Gross Value Added (GVA) growth rate of agriculture and its allied sectors was less than 1%.
- As per some scholars, a decline in public investment since 1991 was the major reason for this. They also argued that inadequate infrastructure, lack of alternate employment opportunities in the industry or service sector, increasing casualisation of employment etc., further obstructed the process of rural development.

# Conclusion

So, real progress of India is possible only when rural areas and rural people are developed, so that overall growth of India could be achieved.
