# Benefits of Organized Agricultural Credit System

Rapid expansion of the banking system had a positive effect on rural farm and non-farm output, income, and employment. After the green revolution, credit facilities helped farmers to obtain a variety of loans to meet their production needs. With buffer stocks of grains, famines became events of the past.

Rural Banking System has come up with much support for the farmers and has led to rural development, directly or indirectly. An organized or well-structured agricultural credit system is very essential for rural development as it ensures:

(i) Availability of adequate credit to the farmers.
(ii) Loans to the rural population for productive needs at cheaper rates of interest from formal credit sources.
(iii) Increase in the rural farm and non-farm output, income, and employment in rural areas. Even then, there are a number of problems faced by the agricultural credit structure of the country.

# Problems Faced in Rural Banking

Some of the problems faced in rural banking are:

## Insufficiency

The growth of the rural economy depends primarily on the infusion of capital. Farmers need funds due to their long gestation period. However, the volume of rural credit in the country is still insufficient compared to its demand.

## Dependence on Informal Credit System

Due to the absence of collateral, many small and marginal farmers and other rural poor do not have access to a formal banking system. As a result, they are forced to depend on an informal credit system, which pushes them into a debt-trap.

## Inadequate Coverage of Institutional Sources

The institutional credit arrangement continues to be inadequate, as it has failed to cover the entire rural farmers of the country.

## Inadequate Amount of Sanction

The amount of the loan sanctioned to the farmers is also inadequate. As a result, farmers often divert such loans for unproductive purposes, diluting the very purpose of such loans.

## Less Attention to Poor or Marginal Farmers

Less attention has been given to the credit requirements of needy (small and marginal) farmers. Due to a lack of collateral (i.e., something to be pledged as security for repayment of a loan), such needy farmers are generally rejected for credit from banks and other institutional sources. On the other hand, well-to-do farmers are getting more attention due to their better creditworthiness.

## Growing Overdues

The problem of overdues in agricultural credit continues to be an area of concern.

### Reasons for Overdues

- The basic reason for growing overdues is the poor repaying capacity of farmers. As a result, credit agencies are becoming cautious of granting loans to farmers.
- Agriculture loan default rates have been chronically high. It is alleged that farmers are deliberately refusing to pay back loans. It is a threat to the smooth functioning of the banking system and needs to be controlled.

Thus, the expansion and promotion of the rural banking sector have taken a backseat after reforms. Except for commercial banks, other formal institutions failed to develop a culture of deposit mobilization, lending to needy borrowers, and effective loan recovery.

### Improving the Situation

To improve the situation:

(i) Banks need to change their approach from just being lenders to building up relationship banking with the borrowers; and
(ii) Farmers should also be encouraged to inculcate the habit of thrift (saving) and efficient utilization of financial resources.

### Pradhan Mantri Jan-Dhan Yojana

The Government of India launched a scheme called 'Pradhan Mantri Jan-Dhan Yojana' on 28th August 2014. Under this scheme, people in India are encouraged to open bank accounts. The scheme was introduced to promote thrift habits and efficient allocation of financial resources. It aims to provide access to financial services to all households in India, especially the underprivileged sections of society and to encourage savings among low-income households in order to promote economic development.

#### Benefits of the Scheme

Under this scheme:

- Bank holders are entitled to get ₹1-2 lakh accidental insurance coverage and overdraft facilities up to ₹10,000 and get their wages, old-age pension, and other social security payments of the government transferred to bank accounts.
- There is no need to keep a minimum bank balance.
- More than 40 crore bank accounts have been opened till now and this has indirectly promoted thrift habits, particularly in rural areas.
- Banks have also mobilized funds of more than ₹1,40,000 crores through these accounts.

# Tamil Nadu Women in Agriculture (TANWA)

TANWA was a project initiated in the late 1980s in Tamil Nadu to train women in the latest agricultural techniques and in organic farming.

- It encouraged women to actively participate in raising agricultural productivity and family income.
- At a Farm Women's Group in Thiruchirapalli, run by Anthoniammal, trained women are successfully making and selling *vermicompost and earning money from this venture.
- Women are forming Farm Women's Groups, which function like SHGs.
- Many other Farm Women's Groups are creating savings in their group by functioning like mini banks through a micro-credit system.
- With the accumulated savings, they promote small-scale household activities like mushroom cultivation, soap manufacturing, doll making or other income-generating activities.

*Vermicompost is the manure that is made using earthworms to ensure higher-quality compost, which is added to soil to improve its quality.
