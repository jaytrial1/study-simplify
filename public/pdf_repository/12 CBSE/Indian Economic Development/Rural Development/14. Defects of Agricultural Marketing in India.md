14_Defects of Agricultural Marketing in India

The existing system of agricultural marketing has a number of defects. They are enumerated and explained below:

# Lack of Storage Facility

The facilities available for storing the food grains and commercial crops are far from satisfactory. Every year, 15 to 30% of the agricultural produce is damaged either by rats or rains or due to the absence of proper storage facilities.

# Distress Sale

Most Indian farmers are very poor and have no capacity to wait for a better price for their produce. As a result, they are forced to make distress sales (i.e., sale of output at a very poor price) to the village moneylenders-cum-traders.

# Lack of Transportation

Due to the absence of proper road transportation facilities, farmers cannot reach nearby mandis to sell their produce at a fair price. As a result, they have to sell their produce at the village markets themselves.

# Long Chain of Middlemen

There is a large number of intermediaries between the cultivator and the consumer. All these middlemen and dalals reduce the returns of the cultivators due to their high margins.

# Lack of Institutional Finance

In the absence of adequate institutional finance, Indian farmers have to come under the clutches of traders and moneylenders for taking loans. After harvest, they have to sell their produce to these moneylenders at unfavorable terms.

# Lack of Grading

Indian farmers do not give importance to grading their produce. They hesitate to separate the good quality crops from bad crops. As a result, they fail to fetch a good price for their quality product.

# Lack of Market Information

The agriculturist is not aware of the existing market price and the market trends. As a result, he is unable to take correct decisions about the timing of the sales. His ignorance is exploited by the money lenders and traders.
