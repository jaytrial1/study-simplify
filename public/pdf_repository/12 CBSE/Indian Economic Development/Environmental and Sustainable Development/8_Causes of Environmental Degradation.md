8_Causes of Environmental Degradation

The various factors responsible for the degradation of the natural environment of the country are:

# High Population Growth

## Impact of Population Growth

- Annual growth of about 2% has contributed to severe and accelerating degradation of the natural resources.
- It is consuming natural resources more than desirable and releasing more waste than the environment can absorb.

# Industrial Pollution

## Industrial Growth and Pollution

In India, unplanned and uncontrolled industrial growth is creating significant atmospheric pollution.
Moreover, industrial waste is constantly being discharged into rivers, lakes, and seas, creating substantial health hazards.

# Increasing Urbanisation

## Urbanisation Effects

Urbanisation has been rapid in recent decades, with larger towns growing at a much faster rate.

### Strain on Infrastructure

- Infrastructural services are under severe strain, depleting the precious environmental resource base of cities.

### Overcrowding

- Overcrowding in cities (population, vehicles, etc.) has led to the increase of slums and undesirable land-use changes.
- It has led to the deterioration of air and water quality and the generation of significant waste.

# Mass Poverty

## Poverty and Environmental Degradation

Widespread poverty is another reason for environmental degradation.

### Deforestation

- There is reckless felling of trees by the poor for fuel.

### Resource Exploitation

- Poor people earn their living through activities like gathering forest produce, collecting firewood, and intensively using small pieces of land or fishing in various ponds and rivers.
- All these activities lead to the loss of natural assets and environmental degradation.

# Pattern of Economic Growth

## Impact of Economic Growth

The pattern of growth has also caused damage to the natural environment. The emphasis on developing basic and capital goods industries and the technologies used for them have depleted natural resources and damaged the ecosystem, besides contaminating water, air, and land resources.

# Vehicular Pollution

## Transport and Pollution

The transport system in India relies heavily on petroleum products, significantly increasing air pollution. Increased road traffic and its noise have also resulted in noise pollution.

# Increase in Use of Chemical Fertilizers and Pesticides

## Impact of Agricultural Practices

Agricultural development due to environment-unfriendly farming methods (high use of chemical fertilizers and pesticides) adversely affects the environment, causing soil erosion, land salinization, and loss of fertility.
