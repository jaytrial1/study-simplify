3_Agreements Against Public Policy

This section expands on the concept of agreements deemed "opposed to public policy," outlining various specific categories that fall under this classification, despite the inherent difficulty in precisely defining public policy.

# Agreements Against Public Policy

It is challenging to exhaustively classify agreements opposed to public policy, as new grounds may be recognized by courts, albeit reluctantly. Judges often caution that "Public policy is an unruly horse" and can introduce uncertainty into law.

*   **Q:** Point out those agreements which are against public policy. "The contract opposed to public policy is illegal". Discuss this statement. Discuss the agreements opposed to public policies.
    > **Answer:** Agreements against public policy are those that are injurious to the public or against the interest of society. These agreements are considered illegal and void. Specific categories include trading with an enemy, agreements interfering with the administration of justice (like stifling prosecution or involving maintenance/champerty under certain conditions), trafficking in public offices, agreements creating conflicts of interest, those in restraint of parental rights, interfering with marital duties, marriage brokage agreements, restraining personal liberty, agreements in fraud of creditors, and agreements tending to create monopolies.

### Heads of Public Policy

Some examples of agreements opposed to public policy include:

#### 1. Trading with an Enemy

All trade with public enemies without government sanction is unlawful. An agreement between citizens of two countries at war is void and inoperative. Contracts entered into before a war are suspended or dissolved, or can be performed after hostilities cease.

#### 2. Agreements Interfering with the Administration of Justice

An agreement whose object is to stifle prosecution or interfere with the administration of justice is unlawful and void.

*   **Interference with the Course of Justice:** Agreements that obstruct the ordinary process of justice are legally unsound.
    *   **Example:** If multiple courts have jurisdiction over a case, an agreement between parties to try the dispute in one specific court is not contrary to public policy.
*   **Stifling Prosecution:** An agreement to prevent legal proceedings already instituted or to compromise a prosecution is illegal and void. This involves tampering with the administration of justice by a private individual.
    *   **Example:** An arbitration agreement entered into to withdraw a pending criminal case involving non-compoundable offenses was held invalid by the Supreme Court, as it was opposed to public policy due to stifling prosecution.
*   **Maintenance or Champerty:**
    *   **Q:** Short Note: Maintenance and Champerty.
        > **Answer:** *Maintenance* is an agreement to provide assistance, financial or otherwise, to another person to enable them to bring or defend legal proceedings when the helper has no legal interest in the matter. *Champerty* is an agreement where one party assists another in an action to recover money or property, with the understanding that they will share in the proceeds of that action. While both are void under English law, in India, they are valid if made with the *bona fide* object of asserting a claim believed to be just and if the agreed amount is reasonable.

#### 3. Trafficking in Public Offices and Titles

Agreements intended to induce a public servant to act corruptly, or to pay money to a public servant for corrupt action, are against public policy. Similarly, agreements for the sale or transfer of public offices and titles for monetary consideration are unlawful.

*   **Example:** An agreement to donate to charity in exchange for a promise to secure a knighthood was held void.

#### 4. Agreements Tending to Create Interest Opposed to Duty

An agreement with a public servant that requires them to follow a course of action inconsistent with their public or professional duty is void. If a person enters into an agreement knowing it creates conflicting duties for a public servant, the contract is void.

#### 5. Agreement in Restraint of Parental Rights

A parent (father, or in his absence, mother) has the sole right to the guardianship and custody of their minor child. This right cannot be given away through any agreement.

*   **Example:** An agreement by a father to give up complete custody and control of his child to the mother was held to be opposed to public policy.

#### 6. Agreements Interfering with Marital Duties

An agreement that interferes with the performance of marital duties is void.

*   **Example:** An agreement to lend money to a woman in consideration of her getting a divorce and marrying the lender is void.

#### 7. Marriage Brokage Agreement

A marriage brokage agreement is an agreement for a reward to procure a husband or wife. In England, such agreements are void as they are considered injurious to the moral welfare of citizens. However, in India, a marriage contract is not *per se* opposed to public policy, as social circumstances differ between the two countries.

#### 8. Agreement Restraining Personal Liberty

While law upholds individual contractual freedom, it does not tolerate undue restraint of personal liberty. Agreements that unduly restrain personal freedom are void.

*   **Examples:**
    *   An agreement where a borrower undertakes not to incur any obligation without the lender's consent, effectively surrendering all independence, was held opposed to public policy.
    *   An agreement by a debtor to perform manual labor for the creditor until a debt is fully repaid is void as being against public policy.

#### 9. Agreements in Fraud of Creditors

An agreement whose object is to defraud creditors or revenue authorities is not enforceable, being opposed to public policy.

*   **Example:** A contract where an employee receives an expense allowance grossly exceeding actual expenses is illegal and a fraud on revenue authorities.

#### 10. Agreements Tending to Create Monopoly

Agreements tending to create monopolies are void as they are opposed to public policy. Exclusive rights for selling common goods like clothes or vegetables are generally not permissible.
