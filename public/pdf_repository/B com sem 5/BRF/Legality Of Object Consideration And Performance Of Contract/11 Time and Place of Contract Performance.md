11_Time and Place of Contract Performance

This section outlines the rules governing the time and place for contract performance, especially in the absence of specific agreements, and discusses when "time is of the essence" in a contract.

# Time and Place of Contract Performance

The time and place of contract performance are typically determined by agreement between the parties. If no such agreement exists, Sections 46 to 50 of the Indian Contract Act provide the governing rules.

*   **Q:** State in brief the provision of the Contract Act relating to the time and place of performance of the contract.
    > **Answer:** The Contract Act provides rules for time and place of performance:
    > 1.  **No Application, No Time Specified (Sec. 46):** Performance must occur within a *reasonable time*.
    > 2.  **Time Specified, No Application (Sec. 47):** Performance must occur on the specified day during usual business hours at the proper place.
    > 3.  **Application Required (Sec. 48):** The promisee must apply for performance at a proper place and within usual business hours on the specified day.
    > 4.  **No Application, No Place Fixed (Sec. 49):** The promisor must apply to the promisee to appoint a reasonable place for performance and then perform there.
    > 5.  **Manner/Time Prescribed by Promisee (Sec. 50):** Performance can be made in any manner or at any time prescribed or sanctioned by the promisee.

### 1. Time for Performance Where No Application and No Time Specified

Section 46 states: "Where, by the contract, a promisor is to perform his promise without application by the promisee, and no time for performance is specified, the agreement must be performed within a reasonable time."
The determination of "reasonable time" is a question of fact in each specific case.

### 2. Time and Place for Performance Where Time is Specified and No Application to Be Made

Section 47 states: "Where a promise is to be performed on a certain day, and the promisor has undertaken to perform it without application by the promisee, the promisor may perform it at any time during the usual hours of business on such day and at the place at which the promise ought to be performed."

*   **Example:** A promises to deliver goods at B's warehouse on January 1st. If A brings the goods after usual closing hours, the promise is not performed.

### 3. Application for Performance on Certain Day to Be at Proper Time and Place

Section 48 states: "Where a promise is to be performed on a certain day, and promisor has not undertaken to perform it without application by the promisee, it is the duty of the promisee to apply for performance at a proper place and within the usual hours of business."
"What is a proper time and place" is a question of fact in each case.

### 4. Place for Performance Where No Application and No Place Fixed

Section 49 states: "When a promise is to be performed without application by the promisee and no place is fixed for the performance of it, it is the duty of the promisor to apply to the promisee to appoint a reasonable place for the performance of the promise and to perform it at such place."

### 5. Performance in Manner or at Time Prescribed or Sanctioned by the Promisee

Section 50 states: "The performance of any promise may be made in any manner or at any time which the promisee prescribes or sanctions."

*   **Example:** B owes A Rs. 2,000. A asks B to pay it to A's account with Banker C. B, who also banks with C, orders the amount transferred to A's credit, which C does. If C fails before A knows of the transfer, B has made a good payment.

### Time is the Essence of a Contract

Parties may stipulate that a contract must be performed within a specific period. The question of whether the other party can rescind for failure to meet this deadline depends on whether "time was the essence of the contract."

*   **Q:** "Time is the essence of contract" - Explain.
    > **Answer:** "Time is the essence of a contract" means that the timely performance of a contractual obligation is crucial to the agreement, and failure to perform by the specified time constitutes a breach allowing the non-defaulting party to rescind the contract. Whether time is of the essence depends on the contract terms, the parties' intention (gathered from surrounding circumstances, nature of property, contract construction), and the object of the contract. Generally, it's not essential in immovable property sales but is often essential in commercial transactions involving goods with fluctuating prices.

Whether time is of the essence depends upon:
*   The terms of the contract.
*   The intention of the parties, gathered from:
    *   Surrounding circumstances.
    *   Nature of the property.
    *   Construction of the contract.
*   The object the parties had in mind when entering the contract.

Generally, in contracts for the sale of immovable property, time is *not* deemed to be of the essence. In commercial transactions, the time of delivery of goods is often of the essence, but not necessarily the time of payment. Especially for goods subject to significant price fluctuations, delivery time is considered essential.

#### Effect of Failure: Rules

Section 55 of the Act lays down certain rules:

1.  **Effect of Failure to Perform at Fixed Time When Time is Essential (Sec. 55, Para I):**
    "Where a party to a contract promises to do a certain thing at or before a specified time, and fails to do any such thing at or before the specified time, the contract, or so much of it as has not been performed, becomes voidable at the option of the promisee, if the intention of the parties was that time should be of the essence of the contract."

2.  **Effect of Such Failure When Time is Not Essential (Sec. 55, Para II):**
    "If it was not the intention of the parties that time should be of the essence of the contract, the contract does not become voidable by the failure to do such thing at or before the specified time; but the promisee is entitled to compensation from the promisor for any loss occasioned to him by such failure."

3.  **Effect of Acceptance of Performance at Time Other Than Agreed Upon (Sec. 55, Para III):**
    "If in case of a contract voidable on account of the promisor's failure to perform his promise at the time agreed the promisee accepts performance of such promise at any time other than that agreed, the promisee cannot claim compensation for any loss occasioned by the non-performance of the promise at the time agreed, unless, at the time of such acceptance, the given notice to the promisor of his intention to do so."

#### Subsequent Notice

Time can be made the essence of a contract by subsequent notice. Such a notice should fix a reasonably long time, giving the other party an opportunity to perform the contract.
