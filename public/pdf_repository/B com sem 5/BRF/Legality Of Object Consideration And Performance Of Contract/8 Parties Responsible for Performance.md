8_Parties Responsible for Performance

This section details who is obligated to perform a contract, distinguishing between personal performance, performance by representatives, and performance by third parties, and also addresses the impact of the promisor's death.

# Parties Responsible for Performance

*   **Q:** Discuss: By whom a contract must be performed? State the persons who are entitled to perform the contract.
    > **Answer:** A contract must be performed by the promisor themselves if it involves personal skill or unique qualifications. Otherwise, it can be performed by their representatives or a competent person employed by them. Performance accepted from a third person also discharges the promisor's obligation. In cases not involving personal skill, the legal representatives are bound to perform the contract upon the promisor's death, though their liability is limited to the inherited assets.

### 1. Personal Performance

Section 40 (Para 1) states: "If it appears from the nature of the case that it was the intention of the parties to any contract that any promise contained in it should be performed by the promisor himself, such promise must be performed by the promisor."
Generally, contracts requiring personal skill must be performed by the promisor or their agent. This rule applies when the personality of the contracted person is material.

*   **Examples:**
    *   A agrees to paint a picture for B. A must perform this promise personally.
    *   A agrees to sing at Town Hall on Sunday. A must perform this promise personally.

### 2. Performance by Representatives

Section 40 (Para 2) states: "In other cases, the promisor or his representatives may employ a competent person to perform it." This aligns with the principle "Quid facit per alium facit per se" (He who does anything through another is deemed to have done it himself).

*   **Example:** A promises to pay B a sum of money. A can pay personally or arrange for someone else to pay. If A dies before payment, A's representatives must perform the promise or employ someone to do so.

### 3. Performance by Third Person

Section 41 states: "When a promisee accepts a performance of the promise from a third person he cannot afterwards enforce it against the promisor." In English law, prior authorization for third-party performance is usually required, but this section focuses on the actual acceptance of performance.

### 4. Death of the Promisor

In contracts involving personal skill, the death of either party implicitly dissolves the contract (Actio personalis moritur cum persona - a personal cause of action dies with the person). However, contracts not involving personal skill are *not* dissolved by death. In such cases, the legal representatives of the deceased party are bound to perform the contract, but their liability is limited to the assets inherited from the deceased.

*   **Examples:**
    *   A promises to marry B on a given date. If A dies before that date, the contract ends and cannot be enforced.
    *   A promises to deliver goods to B on a certain date for Rs. 1,000. If A dies before that day, A's representatives are bound to deliver the goods, and B is bound to pay Rs. 1,000 to A's representatives.

### 5. When Contracts Need Not Be Performed

Sections 62 to 67 of the Indian Contract Act list instances where a contract need not be performed:

1.  If the parties agree to substitute a new contract, rescind, or alter the original one (Sec. 62).
2.  If the promisee dispenses with, wholly or partially remits, extends time for, or accepts satisfaction for the promise (Sec. 63).
3.  When a voidable contract is rescinded, the other party is excused from performance (Sec. 64).
4.  If the promisee neglects or refuses to provide the promisor reasonable facilities for performing the promise, the promisor is excused from non-performance caused by such negligence or refusal (Sec. 67).
    *   **Example:** A contracts to repair B's house. If B neglects or refuses to show A the repair locations, A is excused for non-performance caused by B's actions.
