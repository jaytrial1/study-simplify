# Introduction to Contract Performance

A contract is an agreement enforceable by law that creates legal obligations. It remains in force until dissolved, either by actual performance or by other means prescribed by law. A contract is settled when parties fulfill their obligations within the specified time and manner, or when the promisee dispenses with, remits, or accepts an alternative performance.

Section 37, Part 1, of the Indian Contract Act states that "The parties to a contract must either perform or offer to perform, their respective promises, unless such performance is dispensed with or excused under the provisions of this Act, or of any other law." This chapter examines the general principles of contract performance, including who is bound to perform, who can demand performance, and the time, mode, and order of performance. It also emphasizes that performance or its offer must be made by the promisor.
