9_Joint Rights and Liabilities in Performance

This section clarifies the rules regarding how joint promises are performed and how joint rights and liabilities devolve among parties, as per the Indian Contract Act.

# Devolution of Joint Rights and Liabilities

When two or more persons enter into a joint agreement, Sections 42 to 45 of the Contract Act define who is liable to perform and who can demand performance.

*   **Q:** What are the rules laid down in the Indian Contract Act as to devolution of joint rights and liabilities? Discuss the devolution of joint rights and liabilities.
    > **Answer:** The Indian Contract Act lays down specific rules for joint rights and liabilities:
    > 1.  **Joint Liabilities:** All joint promisors must fulfill the promise during their joint lives. After the death of any promisor, their representative, jointly with the survivors, must fulfill it. After the death of the last survivor, the representatives of all jointly must fulfill it. Unlike English law, Indian law generally treats joint liability as joint and several.
    > 2.  **Compelling Performance:** The promisee can compel any one or more of the joint promisors to perform the entire promise, unless there's an express agreement to the contrary.
    > 3.  **Contribution:** Each joint promisor can compel every other joint promisor to contribute equally, unless the contract states otherwise. Losses from default in contribution are shared equally by the remaining promisors.
    > 4.  **Release of One Promisor:** Releasing one joint promisor by the promisee does not discharge the others, nor does it affect their inter-se responsibility.
    > 5.  **Joint Rights:** The right to claim performance rests with all joint promisees during their joint lives. After the death of any promisee, their representative, jointly with the survivors, holds the right. After the death of the last survivor, the representatives of all jointly hold the right.

### 1. Devolution of Joint Liabilities

Section 42 states: "When two or more persons have made a joint promise, then (1) unless a contrary intention appears by the contract, (2) all such persons, during their joint lives and (3) after the death of any of them, his representative jointly with the survivor or survivors and (4) after the death of the last survivor, the representatives of all jointly, must fulfil the promise."

Unlike English law, where liability devolves upon surviving promisors, Indian law generally considers all joint liability to be joint and several. This rule applies unless the contract specifies otherwise.

### 2. Any One of Joint Promisors May Be Compelled to Perform

Section 43 (Para 1) states: "When two or more persons make a joint promise, the promisee may, in the absence of an express agreement to the contrary, compel anyone or more of such joint promisors to perform the whole of the promise."

*   **Example:** A, B, and C jointly promise to pay D Rs. 3,000. D may compel A, B, or C to pay the entire Rs. 3,000.
This section significantly differs from English law, which requires all joint promisors to be sued jointly. In India, joint liability is joint and several.

#### Each Promisor May Compel Contribution

Section 43 (Para 2) states: "Each of two or more promisors may compel every other joint promisor to contribute equally with himself to the performance of the promise, unless a contrary intention appears, from the contract."

*   **Right of Contribution:** This right arises from the equity favoring persons jointly bound who have had to pay more than their fair share.
    *   **Example:** A, B, and C jointly promise to pay D Rs. 3,000. If C is compelled to pay the whole, and A is insolvent but can pay half his debts, C is entitled to receive Rs. 500 from A's estate and Rs. 1,250 from B (half of the remaining Rs. 2,500).

#### Sharing of Losses by Default in Contribution

Section 43 (Para 3) states: "If anyone of two or more joint promisors make default in such contribution, the remaining joint promisors must bear the loss arising from such default in equal sums."

*   **Example:** A, B, and C jointly promise to pay D Rs. 3,000. If C cannot pay anything and A is compelled to pay the whole, A is entitled to receive Rs. 1,500 from B.
The English law differs as it requires all joint promisors to be sued jointly. In India, the promisee can choose whom to proceed against.

*   **Explanation to Sec. 43:** "Nothing shall prevent a surety from recovering from his principal, payments made by the surety on behalf of the principal, or entitle the principal to recover anything from the surety on account of payments made by the principal."

### 3. Effect of Release of One Joint Promisor

Section 44 states: "Where two or more persons have made a joint promise, a release of one of such joint promisors by the promisee does not discharge the other joint promisor or joint promisors; neither does it free the joint promisor or joint promisors."

Unlike English law, where releasing one joint promisor releases all, in India, such a release does not affect the liability of the others or their inter-se responsibility.

### 4. Devolution of Joint Rights

Section 45 states: "When a person has made a promise to two or more persons jointly, then unless a contrary intention appears from the contract, the right to claim performance rests, as between him and them, with them during their joint lives, and after the death of any of them, with the representatives of such deceased person jointly with the survivor or survivors, and after the death of the last survivor, with representatives of all jointly."

*   **Example:** A promises B and C jointly to repay Rs. 5,000 with interest. If B dies, the right to claim performance rests with B's representatives jointly with C during C's lifetime, and after C's death, with the representatives of B and C jointly.

This section also differs from English law, where the right to demand performance devolves solely on the survivors upon the death of a joint promisee. In India, all promisees jointly hold the right during their joint lives, and upon one's death, their representative shares the right with the surviving promisees.
