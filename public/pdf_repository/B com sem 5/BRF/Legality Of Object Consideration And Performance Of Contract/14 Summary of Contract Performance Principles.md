14_Summary of Contract Performance Principles

This section provides a consolidated summary of the key principles and rules related to the performance of contracts, covering offer to perform, who must perform, joint obligations, reciprocal promises, time/place, appropriation of payments, and assignment.

# Summary of Contract Performance Principles

### How? Offer to Perform: (Tender - Sec. 38)

**Essentials of a Valid Tender:**
1.  Must be unconditional.
2.  Must be at a proper place and time.
3.  Must be by a person ready, able, and willing to perform it entirely.
4.  Must give reasonable opportunity for inspection.
5.  Tender to one is tender to all.

### By Whom? Performance of Contract:

1.  Must be performed personally (Sec. 40, Para 1).
2.  Or by the promisor's representatives (Sec. 40, Para 2).
3.  Promisee may accept performance from a third person (Sec. 41).
4.  Death of the promisor will dissolve contracts involving personal skill.
5.  Performance by joint promisees.

### Devolution of Joint Rights & Liabilities: (Secs. 42-45)

1.  **Devolution of Joint Liabilities (Sec. 42):** All joint promisors are liable jointly or along with their representatives.
2.  **Any One of Joint Promisors May Be Compelled to Perform (Sec. 43):** The promisee can compel any one or more of such joint promisors to perform the whole promise.
3.  **Effect of Release of One Joint Promisor (Sec. 44):** Does not discharge the other joint promisors.
4.  **Devolution of Joint Rights (Sec. 45):** All promisees are jointly entitled to performance or along with their legal representatives.

### Which Way? Reciprocal Promises: (Secs. 51, 54 & 57)

**Rules:**
1.  **Promisor Not Bound Unless Reciprocal Promisee Ready and Willing to Perform (Sec. 51):** Promises to be simultaneously performed.
2.  **Order of Performance of Reciprocal Promises (Sec. 52):** In the order fixed by the contract or that which the nature of the transaction requires.
3.  **Liability of Party Preventing Event (Sec. 53):** Contract becomes voidable and prevented party can claim compensation.
4.  **Effect of Default in First Performed Promise (Sec. 54):** The defaulting promisor cannot claim performance and must make compensation.
5.  **Promise to Do Things Legal & Also Other Things Illegal (Sec. 57):** The first is a contract, but the latter is a void agreement.

### Where? Time & Place of Performance: (Secs. 46 to 50)

1.  **Time:** Where no application is to be made and no time is specified (Sec. 46) - within reasonable time.
2.  **Time and Place:** Where time is specified and no application to be made (Sec. 47) - on the specified day during usual business hours at the proper place.
3.  **Application for Performance:** On a certain day to be at proper time and place (Sec. 48) - promisee's duty to apply.
4.  **Place:** Where no application is to be made and no place fixed for performance (Sec. 49) - promisor applies to promisee to appoint a reasonable place.
5.  **Performance in Manner or at Time Prescribed by Promisee (Sec. 50).**

### Manner? Appropriation of Payments: (Secs. 59-61)

1.  **Debtor's Right to Appropriate (Sec. 59):** Application of payment where debt to be discharged is indicated.
2.  **Creditor's Right to Appropriate (Sec. 60):** Application of payment where debt to be discharged is not indicated.
3.  **Order of Application (Sec. 61):** Application of payment where neither party appropriates (chronological order).

### Assignment of Contracts:

1.  **Assignment by the Act of Parties (Sec. 40):**
    *   Contracts involving personal skill cannot be assigned.
    *   Cannot assign liabilities or obligations under a contract.
    *   Rights and benefits, however, can be assigned.
2.  **Assignment by Operation of Law:**
    *   By death (excepting contracts of personal nature) pass on to the heirs.
    *   By insolvency - property of the insolvent vests in the official assignee.
