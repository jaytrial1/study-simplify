12_Appropriation of Payments

This section explains the rules for "appropriation of payments" when a debtor owes multiple distinct debts to the same creditor and makes a payment, determining which debt the payment applies to.

# Appropriation of Payments (Clayton's Case)

When a debtor owes several distinct debts to the same creditor and makes a payment, the question arises as to which specific debt the payment should be applied. The rules governing such payments are known as the rules for *Appropriation of Payments*, discussed in Sections 59 to 61 of the Contract Act. These sections largely derive from the English law rule established in *Clayton's case*, which applies particularly to current accounts between parties.

*   **Q:** State the rules regarding appropriation of payments. Rule in Clayton's case. Rules as regards appropriation of payment. Write short note on: Clayton's Law on Appropriation of Payment.
    > **Answer:** The rules for appropriation of payments determine how a payment made by a debtor owing multiple debts to a single creditor is allocated:
    > 1.  **Debtor's Right (Sec. 59):** If the debtor specifies how the payment should be applied (expressly or by implication), the creditor must apply it accordingly.
    > 2.  **Creditor's Right (Sec. 60):** If the debtor does not specify, the creditor may apply the payment to any lawful debt actually due and payable, even if it's time-barred.
    > 3.  **Order of Application (Sec. 61):** If neither party makes an appropriation, payments are applied to debts in chronological order, whether time-barred or not. If debts are of equal standing, the payment is applied proportionately.

### 1. Debtor's Right to Appropriate

Section 59 states: "Where a debtor, owing several distinct debts to one person, makes a payment to him, either with express intimation or under circumstance implying that the payment is to be applied to the discharge of some particular debt, the payment, if accepted, must be applied accordingly."

*   **Example:** A owes B Rs. 1,000 on a promissory note due June 1st, and no other debt of that amount. On June 1st, A pays B Rs. 1,000. This payment is applied to the promissory note.
If the debtor provides such intimation and the appropriation is made, the creditor cannot later change it. If the creditor disagrees with the proposed appropriation, they must refuse the payment; they cannot accept it under protest. This section does not apply to a single debt payable in installments.

### 2. Creditor's Right to Appropriate

Section 60 states: "Where the debtor has omitted to intimate and there are no other circumstances indicating to which debt the payment is to be applied, the creditor may apply it at his discretion to any lawful debt actually due and payable to him from the debtor, where its recovery is or is not barred by the law in force for the time being as to the limitation of suits."

The creditor can apply the payment to a debt barred by the Law of Limitation. They may also apply the payment first to interest, with the principal remaining due. This right can be exercised until the last moment, even up to the time of trial.

### 3. Order of Appropriation

Section 61 states: "Where neither party makes any appropriation, the payment shall be applied in discharge of the debts in order of time whether they are or are not barred by the Law in force for the time being as to the limitation of suits. If the debts are of equal standing, the payment shall be applied in discharge of each proportionately."

If a debt carries interest, and money is paid without definite appropriation, it should first be applied to the interest. Unless a contrary intention exists, credit items in an account are appropriated against debit items in chronological order. If debts are of the same date, the payment is applied proportionately.
