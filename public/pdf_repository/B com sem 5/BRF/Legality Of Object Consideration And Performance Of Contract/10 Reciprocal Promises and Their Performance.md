10_Reciprocal Promises and Their Performance

This section defines reciprocal promises and outlines the rules governing their performance, including conditions for simultaneous performance, order of performance, and consequences of default.

# Reciprocal Promises and Their Performance

### Definition

Section 2(f) defines "Reciprocal Promises" as "Promises which form the consideration or part of the consideration for each other."

### Rules for Performance of Reciprocal Promises

The rules for performing reciprocal promises are detailed in Sections 51 to 54 and 57 of the Contract Act:

*   **Q:** Discuss the provisions of the Contract Act relating to the performance of reciprocal promises.
    > **Answer:** The Indian Contract Act provides several rules for reciprocal promises:
    > 1.  **Simultaneous Performance (Sec. 51):** Neither promisor is bound to perform unless the promisee is ready and willing to perform their reciprocal promise simultaneously.
    > 2.  **Order of Performance (Sec. 52):** Promises must be performed in the order expressly fixed by the contract, or if not fixed, in the order required by the nature of the transaction.
    > 3.  **Preventing Performance (Sec. 53):** If one party prevents the other from performing their reciprocal promise, the contract becomes voidable at the option of the prevented party, who is also entitled to compensation for any loss.
    > 4.  **Default in First Performance (Sec. 54):** If a contract involves promises where one cannot be performed until the other has been, and the promisor of the first promise fails to perform it, they cannot claim performance of the reciprocal promise and must compensate the other party for any loss.
    > 5.  **Legal and Illegal Acts (Sec. 57):** If reciprocal promises involve both legal and illegal acts, the set of legal promises constitutes a valid contract, while the set of illegal promises forms a void agreement.

### 1. Promisor Not Bound Unless Reciprocal Promisee is Ready and Willing to Perform

Section 51 states: "When a contract consists of reciprocal promises to be simultaneously performed, no promisor need perform his promise unless the promisee is ready and willing to perform his reciprocal promise."

*   **Example:** A and B contract that A shall deliver goods to B, to be paid for by B on delivery. A need not deliver the goods unless B is ready and willing to pay on delivery.

### 2. Order of Performance of Reciprocal Promises

Section 52 states: "Where the order in which reciprocal promises are to be performed is expressly fixed by the contract, they shall be performed in that order; and, where the order is not expressly fixed by the contract, they shall be performed in that order which the nature of the transaction requires."

*   **Example:** A and B contract that A shall build a house for B at a fixed price. A's promise to build the house must be performed before B's promise to pay for it.

### 3. Liability of Party Preventing Event on Which the Contract is to Take Effect

Section 53 states: "When a contract contains reciprocal promises, and one party to the contract prevents the other from performing his promise, the contract becomes voidable at the option of the party so prevented: and he is entitled to compensation from the other party for any loss which he may sustain in consequence of the non-performance of the contract."

*   **Example:** A and B contract that B shall execute certain work for A for Rs. 1,000. B is ready and willing to execute the work, but A prevents him. The contract is voidable at B's option, and if B rescinds, he is entitled to compensation from A for any incurred loss.

### 4. Effect of Default as to That Promise Which Should Be First Performed

Section 54 states: "When a contract consists of reciprocal promises, such that one of them cannot be performed or that the performance cannot be claimed till the other has been performed, and the promisor of the promise last mentioned fails to perform it, such promisor cannot claim the performance of the reciprocal promise, and must make compensation to the other party to the contract for any loss which such other party may sustain by the non-performance of the contract."

*   **Example:** A hires B's ship to convey cargo from Calcutta to Mauritius, with B receiving freight. If A does not provide cargo, A cannot claim performance of B's promise and must compensate B for the loss due to non-performance.

### 5. Reciprocal Promise to Do Things Legal, and Also Other Things Illegal

Section 57 states: "Where persons reciprocally promised, firstly, to do certain things which are legal and secondly, under specified circumstances, to do certain things which are illegal, the first set of promises is a contract but the second is a void agreement."

*   **Example:** A and B agree that A shall sell B a house for Rs. 10,000, but if B uses it as a gambling house, B shall pay A Rs. 50,000. The first set of promises (selling the house for Rs. 10,000) is a contract. The second set (using the house as a gambling house for Rs. 50,000) is for an unlawful object and is a void agreement.
