1_Introduction to Legality of Object and Consideration

This section introduces the fundamental requirement for a valid contract: that its object and consideration must be lawful. It clarifies the distinct meanings of 'object' and 'consideration' within the context of contract law.

# Introduction to Legality of Object and Consideration

A contract must not only be based upon mutual assent of competent parties but must also have a *lawful object*. An agreement is not valid if its object or consideration is *unlawful*.

The words 'object' and 'consideration' are distinct in meaning. While *consideration* is used as defined in Section 2(d) of the Indian Contract Act, the word *object* refers to the broader purpose or design of the agreement. For instance, in a case where property was transferred for a sum of money (lawful consideration) with the purpose of defrauding creditors (unlawful object), the agreement was deemed void because the object offended insolvency law. Both the object and the consideration must be lawful, otherwise the agreement is *void*.
