7_Offer to Perform (Tender)

This section defines "tender" as an offer to perform a contract and outlines the essential conditions for a valid tender, along with the consequences of its refusal.

# Offer to Perform (Tender)

The "offer to perform" a contract is known as a *tender*. Sometimes, a party ready and willing to perform their promise encounters refusal from the other party. This is also termed 'attempted performance'. A valid tender functions as actual performance, excusing the promisor from further obligation and entitling them to sue the promisee for breach if the offer is wrongfully refused.

Section 38 (Para 1) states: "Where a promisor has made an offer of performance to the promisee, and the offer has not been accepted, the promisor is not responsible for non-performance, nor does he thereby lose his rights under a contract."

*   **Q:** Short note: Explain the term 'tender,' i.e., offer to perform. What do you mean by 'Tender'? State the essentials of a valid tender. State the effect of refusal to accept a tender. Discuss the essential factors of the tender.
    > **Answer:** 'Tender' is an offer to perform a contract. It signifies a party's readiness and willingness to fulfill their promise. The essentials of a valid tender are that it must be unconditional, made at a proper time and place, by a person who is ready, able, and willing to perform the whole promise, and, if delivering goods, must allow for reasonable inspection. The effect of refusal to accept a valid tender is that the promisor is excused from non-performance and retains their rights under the contract, potentially allowing them to sue the promisee for breach.

### Requisites of a Valid Tender

Section 38 (Para 2) specifies the conditions for a valid tender:

1.  **Unconditional:** The tender must align with the contract terms. Any condition beyond the contract's scope invalidates the tender, allowing the other party to reject it.
    *   **Example:** Offering less than the due amount is not a valid tender.
2.  **Proper Time and Place:** The tender must be made at a time and place appropriate to the contract, considering the parties' intention, circumstances, and trade customs. Sections 46 to 50 of the Contract Act provide further rules.
    *   **Example:** Delivering goods after usual working hours is not a valid tender.
3.  **By a Ready, Able, and Willing Person:** The promisee must have a reasonable opportunity to ascertain that the promisor is able and willing to perform the *entire* promise at that moment.
    *   **Example:** An offer to pay by post is not a valid tender if actual production of cash is required at that moment. For money payments, actual production of cash is necessary. For goods, the tenderer must go to the other party's place of business and inform them of readiness to deliver; mere intimation of goods being at the vendor's place is insufficient.
4.  **Reasonable Opportunity for Inspection:** If the tender involves delivering something, the promisee must have a reasonable opportunity to inspect it and confirm it is what was promised. A tender of goods at a time or place preventing inspection is invalid.
5.  **Tender to One is Tender to All:** An offer to one of several joint promisees has the same legal consequences as an offer to all of them. For instance, a tender to one joint lessor or co-mortgagee is valid.

### Effect of Refusal to Perform Wholly

Section 39 states: "When a party to a contract has refused to perform or disabled himself from performing his promise in its entirety, the promisee may put an end to that contract unless he has signified by words or conduct his acquiescence in its continuance."

*   **Example:** A singer (A) contracts to sing for a theatre manager (B) two nights a week for two months. If A willfully absents herself on the sixth night, B is at liberty to terminate the contract.
