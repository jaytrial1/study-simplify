13_Assignment of Contractual Rights

This section defines assignment in the context of contracts and explains how contractual rights and liabilities can be transferred, either by the actions of the parties or by operation of law.

# Assignment of Contractual Rights

Assignment refers to the transfer of contractual rights and liabilities under a contract to a third party by the act of the assignor. Assignment of a contract can occur either by the *act of parties* or by *operation of law*.

*   **Q:** Can a contract be assigned? If so, how?
    > **Answer:** Yes, a contract can be assigned.
    > **By Act of Parties:** Contracts involving personal skill, ability, or credit cannot be assigned. While liabilities cannot be assigned, rights and benefits (not involving personal skill) can be transferred. Actionable claims can always be assigned, provided the assignment is in writing and notice is given to the debtor.
    > **By Operation of Law:** Assignment occurs automatically upon the death of a party (rights and liabilities pass to heirs, except for personal service contracts) or upon insolvency (rights and liabilities pass to an official assignee or receiver).

### I. Assignment by the Act of Parties

The rules for assignment by the act of parties are:

1.  **Contracts Involving Personal Skill:** Contracts requiring personal skill, ability, credit, or other personal qualifications cannot be assigned (Sec. 40).
    *   **Example:** A contract to marry, paint a picture, or sing.
2.  **Liabilities Cannot Be Assigned:** A promisor cannot assign liabilities or obligations under a contract.
    *   **Example:** If A owes B Rs. 500, A cannot transfer this liability to C and force B to recover the money from C.
3.  **Performance Through Agent:** While the promisor usually performs personally, a party can have the contract performed through a competent agent unless the contract is personal in nature or the rights are incapable of assignment by law or agreement.
    *   **Example:** A undertakes work for B that does not require personal attention. A can get this work done through an equally competent person.
4.  **Rights and Benefits Can Be Assigned:** The rights and benefits under a contract (not involving personal skill, etc.) can be assigned.
    *   **Example:** A owes B Rs. 2,000. B may transfer their right to P to recover the amount from A. This is subject to existing equities; if A already paid part of the debt to B, A will pay proportionately less to P.
5.  **Actionable Claims:** An 'actionable claim' can always be assigned. The assignment must be evidenced by a written instrument, and notice of the assignment must be given to the debtor. An "actionable claim" or "chose in action" (defined in Section 3 of the Transfer of Property Act) is a claim to any debt (except a secured debt) or beneficial interest, whether existing, accruing, conditional, or contingent.
    *   **Examples:** A money debt, shares in a company, a right of action arising from a contract, book debts, and an option to repurchase property sold are all examples of 'actionable claims'.

### II. Assignment by Operation of Law

Assignment by operation of law occurs in two main situations:

1.  **Death:** Upon the death of a party, rights and liabilities under contracts (other than those for personal services) pass to their heirs and legal representatives. Some acts specify that certain contracts, like indemnity, gratuitous bailment, or agency, end with the promisor's death.
2.  **Insolvency:** In case of a person's insolvency, their rights and liabilities pass to an official assignee or official receiver, as applicable.
