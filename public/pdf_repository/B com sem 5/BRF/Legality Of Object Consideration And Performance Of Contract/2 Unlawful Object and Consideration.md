2_Unlawful Object and Consideration

This section details the specific circumstances under Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act where the consideration or object of an agreement is deemed unlawful, rendering the agreement void.

# Unlawful Object and Consideration

Under Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act, the consideration or object of an agreement is unlawful in the following cases:

*   **Q:** In what cases are the consideration and object of an agreement said to be unlawful? Which objects are unlawful? When can the consideration and objects of the contract be treated as illegal?
    > **Answer:** The consideration and object of an agreement are said to be unlawful if they fall into any of the categories outlined in Section 23 of the Indian Contract Act. These categories include when the act is forbidden by law, defeats the provisions of any law, is fraudulent, involves injury to person or property, or is regarded by the court as immoral or opposed to public policy.

### 1. Forbidden by Law

An act or undertaking is *forbidden by law* when it is punishable under the Penal Code, or when it is prohibited by specific legislation, regulations, or orders derived from the Legislature. The determination of whether a transaction is forbidden by an Act is a matter of interpreting the Act itself.

**Examples and Further Considerations:**

*   A promise for public service employment in exchange for a promise to violate the law is void as its consideration is unlawful.
*   The object of a contract might be prohibited only to facilitate the collection of state revenue. Such agreements are not considered immoral or illegal by the Legislature but are disallowed to prevent impairment of revenue collection.
*   An agreement may be forbidden because it contravenes a law intended to protect public interest and regulate particular trades. In such cases, the agreement is not enforceable between the parties.
*   Some agreements are explicitly declared illegal by law, making it impossible for people to enter into them. In these cases, the law not only refuses to enforce the agreement between immediate parties but also prohibits collateral transactions entered into with knowledge of the illegality.

### 2. Defeats the Provisions of Any Law

If the object or consideration of an agreement, though not directly forbidden by law, would *defeat the provisions of any statutory law*, the agreement is void. This applies to provisions of legislative enactments, rules of Hindu or Mohamedan Law, or other laws in force in India.

*   **Example:** An agreement between a debtor and creditor where the debtor agrees not to use the plea of limitations is void because it would defeat the provisions of the Limitation Act.

### 3. Fraudulent

An agreement whose object is to *defraud others* is void between parties. If an agreement has a fraudulent object or a purpose that will promote fraud, the resulting contract is void.

*   **Example:** When a debtor makes a composition deed with creditors, a secret arrangement to give one creditor an additional benefit is a fraud on the other creditors. This additional stipulation cannot be enforced by that creditor.

### 4. Involves or Implies Injury to the Person or Property of Another

If the object of an agreement is to cause *injury to the person or property of another*, it is unlawful. This includes agreements to indemnify a person against the consequences of an illegal act.

*   **Examples:**
    *   An agreement by a newspaper proprietor to indemnify printers against claims arising from libels published in newspapers is void.
    *   An agreement where a debtor promises to perform manual work for the creditor until a debt is fully repaid is void.

### 5. Immoral or Opposed to Public Policy

An agreement, the consideration or object of which is *immoral or opposed to public policy*, is unlawful.

#### Immoral Agreements

The term "immoral" is generally confined to *sexual immorality* and depends on the prevailing standards of morality approved by the courts at a particular time.

*   **Example:** An agreement where A agrees to let her daughter to B for concubinage is void.

#### Agreements Opposed to Public Policy

A contract *opposed to public policy* cannot be enforced by either party. While "public policy" is hard to define precisely, it refers to agreements injurious to the public or against the interest of society. Courts determine if an object or consideration is opposed to public policy based on the specific circumstances.

*   **Example:** A partnership formed to conduct a business in arrack or toddy on a license granted only to one partner is void *ab initio* if the contract was made before or after the license was granted to only one person. This is because it goes against the public policy that only approved, specifically licensed persons should sell liquor.

The principle of public policy should only be invoked in clear and incontestable cases of harm to the public. While courts can apply existing principles to new situations, evolving new heads of public policy should be done cautiously for societal stability. Contracts with unconscionable and unreasonable terms that affect a large number of people or groups, such as stereotyped employment contracts on a 'take it or leave it' basis, may be adjudged void under Section 23 as being opposed to public policy if they are detrimental to public interest.
