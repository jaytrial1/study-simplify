6_The Foundational Elements of a Contract

This section delves into the two core juristic elements of a contract: agreement and obligation, breaking down their constituent parts.

# Juristic Concept of Contract

The juristic concept of a contract comprises two constituent elements:
1.  **Agreement**
2.  **Obligations**

## Agreement

An agreement can be analyzed into four constituent elements:
1.  **Several Parties**: An obligation requires two or more persons.
2.  **Identity of Mind**: Both parties must agree on the subject matter at the same time and in the same sense (consensus ad idem).
3.  **Mutual Communication**: The agreeing minds must communicate with each other; without such communication, no agreement in law can exist.
    *Example:* F wrote to his nephew offering to buy his horse for 30-50 shillings, adding, "if I hear no more about this I will consider the horse mine at 30-50." The nephew decided to accept and instructed an auctioneer, B, to withdraw the horse. B mistakenly sold the horse, and F sued for damages, relying on the contract. Willes J held that "it is clear that the nephew in his own mind intended his uncle to have the horse at the price offered, but had not indicated such an intention to his uncle" (*Felthouse v. Bindley* (1862) 142 E.R. 1037). Hence, no contract resulted.
4.  **Legal Relations**: An intention to create a legal relationship between the parties is necessary.

## Obligations

An **obligation** is understood as a legal tie that imposes upon a determinate person or persons the necessity of doing, or abstaining from doing, a definite act or acts. It presupposes three things:
1.  **Several Parties**: There must be at least two persons.
    *Example:* A borrowed money from a joint account owned by A and B. The obligation was held not enforceable since A was both the creditor and the debtor.
2.  **Definite Act**: The obligation must relate to doing, or abstaining from doing, definite acts. There cannot be uncertainty or indefiniteness about obligations.
3.  **Legal Matter**: The obligations must relate to legal matters, not social or purely domestic matters.
    *Example:* D, on short leave in England, left his wife behind as her health prevented her from accompanying him to Ceylon, where he was employed. D promised to send her £30 a month as long as she remained separate. The wife sued to enforce this promise when D failed to remit the agreed amount. Lord Atkin observed, "It is necessary to remember that there are agreements between parties which do not result in contracts within the meaning of that term in our Law. The ordinary example is where two parties agree to take a walk together or where there is an offer and acceptance of hospitality. The most usual forms of agreement which do not constitute a contract are the agreements made between the husband and wife and they are not contracts because the parties do not intend that should be attended by legal consequence" (*Balfour v. Balfour* (1919) 2 K.B. 571).

It logically follows that a contract is an obligation, but not all obligations are contracts. In other words, "**All contracts are agreements, but all agreements are not contracts**."

**Q:** What is the juristic concept of contract?
> **Answer:** The juristic concept of a contract consists of two fundamental elements: agreement and obligation. An agreement involves several parties, identity of mind (meeting of the minds), mutual communication of intent, and an intention to create legal relations. An obligation is a legal tie requiring definite acts or abstentions from definite acts by at least two parties, concerning legal matters rather than social or domestic ones.

### Distinction Between Agreement and Contract

The differences between an Agreement and a Contract are summarized below:

| Aspect         | Agreement                                                               | Contract                                                               |
| :------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------- | :--------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Definition  | When one person proposes to another to do or not to do a certain act and the other party accepts it, it is an agreement. | An agreement which is enforceable at law is a contract.                |
| 2. Enforceability | It is not necessarily enforceable at law.                               | It is always enforceable at law.                                       |
| 3. Object      | An agreement may or may not create a legal obligation.                  | A contract always creates a legal obligation, which is to be performed. |
| 4. Consideration | In an agreement, consideration may not be present.                     | In a contract, consideration is a must.                                |
| 5. Damages     | In case of breach of agreement, no damages are awarded.                 | In case of breach of contract, damages are always awarded.             |
| 6. Comparison  | All agreements are not contracts.                                       | All contracts are agreements.                                          |
| 7. Binding Nature | Parties are not bound by agreement (if not legally enforceable).        | Obligations and rights created by contract are binding on parties to the contract. |
| 8. Formula     | Proposal + Acceptance = Agreement                                       | Agreement + Enforceability = Contract                                  |
