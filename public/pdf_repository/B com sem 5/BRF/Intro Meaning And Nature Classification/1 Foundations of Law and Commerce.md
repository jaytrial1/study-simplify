# Introduction to Legal Principles

In the current economic landscape, understanding the fundamental principles of law, particularly those governing daily transactions, is crucial for everyone. The legal maxim, '*Ignorance of law is no excuse*' (*ignorantia legis neminem excusat*), underscores the necessity for basic legal knowledge across all professions and aspects of life. Society and the commercial world rely heavily on established rules and principles to regulate conduct, protect property, and enforce contract rights. Without law, social interaction and business would devolve into chaos, prioritizing only the most ruthless. For business professionals, familiarity with mercantile law is essential to avoid common pitfalls stemming from ignorance.

**Laws** are defined as rules of conduct enforced by the State to maintain peace and order within society. They introduce security and uniformity in human behavior, which are vital for peaceful coexistence. The State is responsible for both creating and enforcing these rules.

Over time, the concept of "law" has evolved from the "Command of the King" to serving a "Welfare State" objective. Today, the primary goal of law is to establish a robust socio-economic structure. Its ultimate function is to preserve, guide, and advance community social values, thereby sustaining liberty, achieving justice, and maximizing benefit for the common person.

## Defining Mercantile Law

Law is broadly considered "the body of principles recognised and applied by the State in the administration of justice."

According to Holland, law is "a rule of external human action enforced by the sovereign political authority."

Anson observes: "The object of law is order, and the result of order is that men are enabled to look ahead with some sort of security as to the future. Although human action cannot be reduced to the uniformities of nature, men have yet endeavoured to reproduce by law something approaching to this uniformity."

Austin defines law as: "a rule of conduct imposed and enforced by the sovereign."

The term "**Mercantile Law**" is widely used to denote the branch of law dealing with rights and obligations arising from commercial transactions between mercantile persons. It encompasses legal rules related to industry, trade, and commerce. Also known as **Commercial Law**, it regulates transactions within the mercantile community.

Shri M.C. Shukla defines Mercantile Law as "that branch of Law which deals with the rights and obligations of mercantile persons arising out of mercantile transactions in respect of mercantile property. An individual, a partnership or a company, carrying on business is a mercantile person." The Calcutta High Court rules specify that "commercial suits include suits arising out of the ordinary transactions of merchants, bankers and traders; amongst others, those relating to the construction of mercantile documents, export and import of merchandise, infringement, carriage of goods by land, insurance, banking, mercantile agency and mercantile usage and debts arising out of such transactions."

The scope of mercantile law is broad, including laws related to:
*   Contract
*   Partnership
*   Sale of Goods
*   Negotiable Instruments
*   Companies

It is important to note that while these acts are part of Mercantile Law, they apply equally to non-traders.

**Q:** Define 'Law'. What is the need for the knowledge of law?
> **Answer:** Law refers to rules of conduct enforced by the State to maintain peace and order in society. The need for knowledge of law stems from the principle that 'ignorance of law is no excuse'. Basic legal understanding is essential for all persons to navigate daily transactions, ensure security, and conform to recognized rules of social conduct, particularly in the mercantile world where it regulates business operations, protects property, and enforces contractual rights.

**Q:** What does the term 'Mercantile Law' mean and include?
> **Answer:** Mercantile Law, also known as Commercial Law, refers to the branch of law that governs the rights and obligations arising from commercial transactions between individuals and entities involved in trade, industry, and commerce. It includes laws related to contracts, partnership, sale of goods, negotiable instruments, and companies, among others.
