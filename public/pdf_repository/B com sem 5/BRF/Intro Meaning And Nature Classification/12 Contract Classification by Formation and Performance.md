12_Contract Classification by Formation and Performance

This section further classifies contracts based on how they are formed (express, implied, quasi) and the extent to which their obligations have been fulfilled (executed, executory).

# Classification by Formation

A contract may be entered into in three different ways:
1.  It may be in writing.
2.  It may be made orally.
3.  It may be inferred from the circumstances of the case.

Contracts can be classified according to their mode of formation as follows:

## Express Contracts

When an offer enumerates express terms and is accepted subject to all these terms, the resulting contract is an **express contract**. A contract is said to be expressed when it is entered into by words spoken or written (Section 9).

## Implied Contract

An **implied contract** is one that is not expressly agreed upon between the parties but is inferred from their acts or conduct, their course of dealings, or from the surrounding circumstances.
*Example:* A person taking a public bus or entering a restaurant for a cup of tea implies a promise to pay for the service, resulting in an implied contract.

## Quasi Contract

A contract is intentionally entered into by the parties. A **quasi-contract**, on the other hand, is *created by law*, not by intentional agreement. While a true contract fulfills all requirements for formation, a quasi-contract does not and is not, in a strict sense, a contract at all. It rests on the principle of equity: "A person shall not be allowed to enrich himself unjustly at the expense of another."
*Example:* A, a tradesman, mistakenly leaves goods at B's house. If B treats the goods as his own, B is legally bound to pay for them, even though there was no express agreement.

# Classification by Performance

Contracts can also be classified based on the extent to which they have been performed:
1.  **Executed Contracts**
2.  **Executory Contracts**

## Executed Contract

An **executed contract** is one where both parties have performed their obligations under the contract.
*Example:* A agrees to sell his car to B for Rs. 20,000. When A delivers the car and B pays the price, the contract is said to be executed.

## Executory Contract

An **executory contract**, on the other hand, is one where both parties have yet to perform their obligations.
*Example:* In the above example, if A has not yet delivered the car and B has not paid the price, the contract is said to be executory.

A contract can also be partly executed and partly executory. For instance, if A has delivered the car but B has not yet paid the price, the contract is executed as to A and executory as to B.

**Q:** Explain (1) Executed and Executory contract
> **Answer:** An **executed contract** is one where both parties have fully performed their respective obligations under the agreement. An **executory contract** is one where both parties still have obligations yet to be performed. A contract can also be partly executed (one party has performed) and partly executory (the other party still needs to perform).

### Key Concepts & Examples

**Unilateral and Bilateral Contracts**

While not explicitly detailed in the original text's classification, the concept of contracts can also be understood based on the number of promises exchanged:

*   **Unilateral Contract**: A contract in which one party makes an express promise and the other party performs an act in response, without making a counter-promise. The contract is formed when the act is completed.
    *Example:* An offer of a reward for finding a lost pet. The offeror promises to pay if someone finds the pet. The finder does not promise to find the pet but performs the act of finding it. The contract becomes binding once the pet is found and returned.
*   **Bilateral Contract**: A contract in which both parties exchange promises to perform future acts. It is the most common type of contract.
    *Example:* A promises to sell his car to B, and B promises to buy the car from A. Both parties have made promises to perform in the future.

**Q:** Explain the meaning of Unilateral and Bilateral Contract.
> **Answer:** A **Unilateral Contract** involves a promise by one party in exchange for an act by the other party. The contract is formed when the act is completed, and only the promisor is bound at the outset. A **Bilateral Contract** involves an exchange of promises between two parties, where both parties are bound to perform future acts. It is formed as soon as the promises are exchanged.

### Overview of Contract Classification

Contracts can be broadly classified based on:

*   **Validity**:
    *   Valid Contract
    *   Void Agreement
    *   Void Contract
    *   Voidable Contract
    *   Illegal Contract
    *   Unenforceable Contract
*   **Formation**:
    *   Express Contract
    *   Implied Contract
    *   Quasi-contract
*   **Performance**:
    *   Executed Contract
    *   Executory Contract
