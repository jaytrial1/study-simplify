7_Core Requirements for a Valid Contract

This section outlines the various definitions of contractual terms and details the essential conditions that must be met for an agreement to be legally binding as a contract.

# Key Definitions

The word 'Contract' is derived from the Latin word *contractum*, meaning "draw together." It signifies the coming together of two or more minds to form a common intention that gives rise to an agreement.

According to Salmond, a contract is: "An agreement creating and defining obligations between parties."

Sir William Anson defines a contract as: "A legal binding agreement made between two or more persons by which rights are acquired by one or more to acts or forbearances on the part of the other or others."

## Indian Definition

The series of definitions provided in Section 2 of the Indian Contract Act can be arranged as follows:

*   **Contract**: The Indian Contract Act, Section 2(h), defines a Contract as: "**An agreement enforceable by law is a contract**."
*   **Agreement**: The term "Agreement" is defined in Section 2(e) as: "**Every promise or every set of promises forming the consideration for each other is an agreement**."
*   **Proposal**: A proposal is defined in Section 2(a) as: "When one person signifies to another his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything with a view to obtaining the assent of the other to such act or abstinence, he is said to make a proposal."
*   **Promise**: 'Promise' is defined in Section 2(b) as: "When the person to whom the proposal is made signifies his assent thereto, the proposal is said to be accepted. A proposal when accepted becomes a promise."

## Conditions for a Valid Contract

An agreement is enforceable at law when it fulfills certain conditions stipulated in Section 10 of the Contract Act. According to this Section:
"All agreements are contracts if they are made by the free consent of parties competent to contract, for a lawful consideration and with a lawful object, and are not hereby expressly declared to be void. Nothing herein contained shall affect any law in force in India and not hereby expressly repealed by which any contract is required to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses, or any law relating to the registration of documents."

Thus, all agreements are contracts if they are made:
1.  By the **free consent** of
2.  **Parties competent to contract**
3.  For a **lawful consideration** and **lawful object**
4.  And are **not expressly declared to be void**.
5.  They should also fulfill **legal formalities** prescribed by any other law, if any (e.g., writing, registration).

According to English Law, there is a further requirement: an "intention to create legal obligation." This principle is also followed in India and can be considered a sixth requisite element.

**Q:** A Contract is defined as 'an agreement enforceable at law' - Discuss.
> **Answer:** This definition highlights the two fundamental components of a contract: an agreement and its enforceability by law. An agreement is formed when one party makes a proposal and the other accepts it. However, for this agreement to become a contract, it must be legally binding, meaning it creates rights and obligations that can be enforced in a court of law. Social or domestic agreements, for example, are agreements but not contracts because they lack this legal enforceability, as the parties do not intend to create legal consequences. Thus, the enforceability element distinguishes a mere agreement from a legally binding contract.

**Q:** "All agreements are not contracts but all contracts are agreements" with this statement explain essential factors of valid contract.
> **Answer:** This statement is true because an agreement is the foundational step (proposal + acceptance), but it only becomes a contract if it meets specific legal requirements for enforceability. All legally binding contracts must first be agreements, but many agreements (e.g., social ones) lack the legal intent or other essentials to be enforceable contracts. The essential factors for a valid contract include: proposal and acceptance, intention to create legal relations, lawful consideration, capacity of parties, free consent, lawful object, certainty of terms, possibility of performance, not being expressly declared void, and fulfilling any required legal formalities.

**Q:** Define: Contract.
> **Answer:** A contract is defined as "an agreement enforceable by law."

### Essential Elements of a Valid Contract

An agreement that creates legal obligations must possess certain basic essentials to be valid and binding. These are the essential elements of a contract:

1.  **Proposal and Acceptance**: There must be at least two parties. One party must make an offer (proposal), and the other must accept it. This offer and acceptance must be legal, conforming to the rules laid down in the Contract Act for valid offers and acceptances.
2.  **Legal Relationship**: The parties to the agreement must intend to create legal relations between them. Simple social or domestic agreements are not contracts because they are not intended to be legally binding (e.g., an invitation for tea at a friend's house).
3.  **Lawful Consideration**: The agreement, to be enforceable by law, must be supported by consideration. A promise to do something for nothing is generally not enforceable. A bare promise without the intention to create legal relations has no legal consequence (*ex nudo pacto non obitur actio*). Thus, an agreement is legally enforceable when one receives something in return for something (*Quid Pro Quo*).
4.  **Capacity of Parties**: The parties must be competent to contract, meaning they are capable of entering into a valid contract. A person is competent if they:
    *   Are of the age of majority.
    *   Are of sound mind.
    *   Are not disqualified from contracting by any law to which they are subject.
    Want of capacity can arise from minority, lunacy, idiocy, drunkenness, insolvency, etc.
5.  **Free Consent**: Two or more parties are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense. For an agreement to be enforceable, it must be based on the free consent of all parties. Consent is considered free when it is not caused by:
    *   Coercion
    *   Undue influence
    *   Fraud
    *   Misrepresentation
    *   Mistake
6.  **Lawful Object**: The purpose (object) of the agreement must be lawful. It must not be:
    *   Illegal
    *   Immoral
    *   Against public policy
    Any agreement with an unlawful object is void.
7.  **Certainty of Terms**: The terms of the agreement must be definite and certain, not vague.
8.  **Possibility of Performance**: The agreement must be capable of being performed. An agreement to do an act impossible in itself is void (e.g., A promises to pay B Rs. 10,000 if B can prove that two parallel lines can meet).
9.  **Void Agreements**: The agreement must not have been expressly declared to be void by the Indian Contract Act. Examples of agreements expressly declared void include:
    *   Agreement in restraint of marriage (Sec. 26)
    *   Agreement in restraint of trade (Sec. 27)
    *   Agreement in restraint of legal proceedings (Sec. 28)
    *   Agreement having uncertain meaning (Sec. 29)
    *   Wagering agreement (Sec. 30)
10. **Legal Formalities**: Agreements can be oral or in writing. However, certain agreements (e.g., lease, gift) are legally required to be in writing, attested, and registered if mandated by law.

**Q:** What are the essentials of a valid contract?
> **Answer:** The essentials of a valid contract are: proposal and acceptance, intention to create legal relations, lawful consideration, capacity of parties, free consent, lawful object, certainty of terms, possibility of performance, and the agreement must not be expressly declared void. Additionally, any required legal formalities (like writing or registration) must be fulfilled.
