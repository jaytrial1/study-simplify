8_The Importance of Legal Intention in Contracts

This section elaborates on the necessity for parties to intend to create legal obligations for an agreement to be considered a contract, distinguishing between social and commercial contexts.

# Intention to Create Legal Relations

An agreement does not become a binding contract unless there is an intention to enter into legal relations. The parties must intend that their transaction should lead to legal consequences and create legal obligations. For a contract to be formed, there must be a common intention of the parties to undertake legal obligations.

The intention of the parties is determined from the terms of the agreement and the surrounding circumstances. It is up to the court in each case to ascertain whether the parties must have intended to enter into legal obligations.

## Social or Domestic Agreements

Agreements of a social or domestic nature typically do not contemplate legal relations and are therefore not considered contracts.
*Example:* A husband promised to pay his wife £30 a month for her support while he was working abroad. He later ceased payments, and his wife sued for damages. It was held that no binding contract existed, as no legal relationship was contemplated (*Balfour Vs. Balfour* (1919) 2 KB 571).

However, in some domestic cases, an intention to create a legal relationship can exist.
*Example:* A mother (A), her daughter (B), and a paying guest (C) jointly participated in a crossword puzzle using the mother's name, with expenses shared informally. The mother won the prize but refused to divide it. The court held she was bound to share it, as any reasonable person observing their conduct would conclude they intended to share the prize (*Simpkins Vs. Pays* (1955) 3 'All. E.R. 10).

## Commercial and Business Agreements

### Express Terms

In commercial and business agreements, there is a rebuttable presumption that the parties *intended* to create legal relations.
*Example:* In a commercial agreement, a document contained a condition stating "that it shall be attended by or give rise to any legal relationship rights, duties, consequences whatever or be legally enforceable or be the subject of litigation, but all such arrangements, agreements and transactions are binding in honour only." The court held that this condition was valid, and therefore, the agreement was not legally binding.
