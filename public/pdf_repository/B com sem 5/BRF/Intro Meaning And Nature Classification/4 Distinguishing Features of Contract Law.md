4_Distinguishing Features of Contract Law

This section highlights the key characteristics and principles governing the law of contracts, including its scope and relationship with other legal systems.

# Key Characteristics of Contract Law

From the preceding discussion, the following characteristics of the Indian Contract Act can be identified:

1.  **Not Comprehensive or Exhaustive**: The Law of Contract does not cover all aspects of mercantile transactions. For instance, transactions of sale are governed by the separate *Sale of Goods Act*, and relationships among partners are covered by the *Indian Partnership Act*. As Salmond stated, "the law of contract is not the whole law of agreements nor is it the whole law of obligations. It is the Law of these agreements which create obligations, and those obligations, which have their sources in agreements."
2.  **Resort to English Law**: Since the Act is not exhaustive, if there is no specific provision on a particular matter, courts often refer to principles of English Law, as decided in the case of *Kanhayalal V Dineshware Chaudra*.
3.  **Sanctions Usage and Customs**: The law recognizes and sanctions prevailing usages and customs. As Justice Cockburn observed in *Goodwin v. Reberts* (1875), "The law Merchant is neither more or less than the usages of merchants and traders in the different departments of trade, ratified by the decisions of Court of Law." The Indian Contract Act has incorporated Indian business customs and usages. However, where Indian customs and usages are not covered by the Act or a specific principle of law, Indian Courts generally apply English Law on the subject.
