10_Contract Classification Under English Law

This section details the primary classification of contracts according to English legal principles, distinguishing between formal and simple contracts.

# Classification by English Law

Under English law, contracts are broadly classified into two main types:
1.  **Formal Contracts**
2.  **Simple Contracts**

## Formal Contracts

Formal contracts include:
*   Contracts of Records
*   Contracts Under Seal

### Contracts of Records

Contracts of records derive their binding force from the authority of a Court. These are obligations whose terms are recorded by a court of record, such as the Supreme Court.

Contracts of records are of two types:

#### Judgements

A **judgement** is an obligation imposed by a Court upon one or more persons in favor of another, for example, to pay damages.

#### Recognisances

A **recognisance** is a written acknowledgment of a debt due to the Crown.

### Contract Under Seal

A **contract under seal** is made by a deed or speciality. It is in writing and is signed, sealed, and delivered by the parties. A key characteristic is that consideration is *not* necessary in the case of a contract under seal.

Usually, contracts that must be under seal include:
*   Contracts made without consideration.
*   Contracts made by corporations.
*   Conveyances of the legal estate in land or any interest in land, including leases of land for more than three years.
*   A transfer of a British ship or any share therein.

## Simple Contracts

All contracts not made under seal are **simple contracts**. Also known as "**parol**" contracts, these are the most important type of contracts under English law. They may be in writing, made orally, or implied from the conduct of the parties. Crucially, simple contracts must *always* be supported by **consideration**.
