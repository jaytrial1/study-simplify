11_Contract Classification by Validity in Indian Law

This section classifies contracts based on their legal enforceability under Indian law, defining void, voidable, and illegal agreements and contracts.

# Classification by Indian Law

Indian Law does not classify contracts based on their mode of formation in the same way English Law does (e.g., formal vs. simple). Indian Law primarily recognizes the "simple contracts" concept of English law. For convenience, contracts in India are classified according to their:
1.  **Validity**
2.  **Formation**
3.  **Performance**

Let us examine them in detail.

## Classification According to Validity

An agreement enforceable at law is a contract. An agreement is enforceable when it complies with certain essentials stipulated in Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act. If any of these elements are missing, the contract may be void, voidable, illegal, or unenforceable.

### Void Agreement

Section 2(g) states: "**An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void**." A void agreement has no legal effect whatsoever; it is a total nullity and creates no rights or obligations in the eyes of the law.
*Example:* An agreement made by a minor.
Such agreements fail to fulfill the requirements of Section 10 and are thus *void ab initio* (void from the very beginning). They never progress from the stage of agreement to that of a contract.

**Q:** Short note: Void Agreement
> **Answer:** A void agreement is an agreement that is not enforceable by law from its inception (void ab initio). It lacks legal effect and creates no rights or obligations for any party. Examples include agreements made by minors or those lacking essential elements of a valid contract as per Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act.

### Void Contract

Section 2(j) states: "**A contract which ceases to be enforceable by law becomes void when it ceases to be enforceable**."
An agreement that was legal and enforceable when initially entered into may subsequently become void due to reasons such as impossibility of performance, a change in law, etc.

**Q:** Short note: Void Contract
> **Answer:** A void contract is one that was initially valid and enforceable when made, but subsequently loses its legal enforceability due to unforeseen circumstances like impossibility of performance or a change in law. It becomes void from the point it ceases to be enforceable.

### "Void Contract" and "Void Agreement"

A **void contract** should be distinguished from a **void agreement**:
*   An agreement not enforceable at law is a **void agreement**. For instance, an agreement with a minor is void from the start, and no contract ever comes into existence.
*   In the case of a **void contract**, a contract *does* come into existence but later ceases to be enforceable by law.

**Q:** Short note: "Void Agreement" and "Void Contract"
> **Answer:** A **void agreement** is unenforceable by law from its very beginning (ab initio), meaning no contract ever forms (e.g., an agreement with a minor). In contrast, a **void contract** is an agreement that was initially valid and legally enforceable when made, but subsequently loses its enforceability due to events like impossibility of performance or a change in law. The key difference is the point at which enforceability is lost: at inception for a void agreement, and after formation for a void contract.

### Voidable Contract

A **voidable contract** is one that can be avoided by one of the parties to the agreement. Section 2(i) states: "**An agreement which is enforceable by law at the option of one or more parties thereto, but not at the option of the other or others, is a voidable contract**."

Contracts resulting from coercion, undue influence, fraud, and misrepresentation are voidable. In such cases, the consent obtained is not genuine. The party whose consent was so obtained has the option, upon discovering the true state of affairs, to either be bound by the contract or to disown/repudiate it.
*   If the party entitled to disaffirm chooses to do so, the contract becomes void.
*   However, the contract remains valid until it is repudiated.
*   If the party fails to exercise their option to avoid or if they affirm the contract, it continues to be valid.

*Example:* A coerces B into entering a contract for the sale of B's car to A. This contract can be avoided by B (but not by A). A cannot enforce the contract, but B can choose to enforce it against A.

If a voidable contract is avoided by the party with the legal right to do so, that injured party gains the right to recover compensation for any damage sustained due to the non-performance of the contract. Conversely, if they have received any benefit under the voidable contract, they must restore such benefit to the person from whom it was received (Section 64).

**Q:** Short Note: Voidable Contract.
> **Answer:** A voidable contract is an agreement that is enforceable by law at the option of one or more of the parties, but not at the option of the other(s). These contracts arise when consent is not freely given, such as due to coercion, undue influence, fraud, or misrepresentation. The aggrieved party has the choice to either affirm the contract, making it valid, or repudiate it, rendering it void. Until repudiated, the contract remains valid.

### Difference between Void & Voidable Contract

| Aspect             | Void Contract                                                | Voidable Contract                                            |
| :----------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Enforceability  | Not enforceable by law, i.e., a void agreement cannot be enforced at all. | Enforceable by law at the option of one of the parties, i.e., a voidable agreement can be enforced if the option is so exercised. |
| 2. Binding Nature  | It has no legally binding effect (from inception or upon becoming void). | It continues to be legal unless avoided by the party or parties entitled to do so. |
| 3. Defects         | In a void contract, the defects are incurable.              | In a voidable contract, the defect is curable and may be condoned. |
| 4. Effect on Third Party | A third party who purchases goods which had been the subject of a void contract will acquire no title to the goods. | A third party who purchases goods which have been the subject of a voidable contract, but not avoided, acquires a good title to the goods and cannot be compelled to surrender them to their former owner. |

### Illegal Contract

An **illegal agreement** is one that is against a law in force in India. This includes agreements that:
*   Transgress some rule of basic policy.
*   Are criminal in nature (e.g., an agreement to commit murder, dacoity).
*   Are immoral (e.g., to lend a car on hire to a prostitute for luring customers for immoral purposes).

Such an agreement is certainly **void** and has no legal effect. It is a nullity and cannot be enforced at law. However, not all void contracts are necessarily criminal in nature. For example, an agreement with a minor is void but not illegal. Therefore, "**all illegal agreements are void, but all void agreements are not necessarily illegal**."

#### Effect on Collateral Transaction

An illegal agreement is not only void between the immediate parties but also has the further effect that even the **collateral transactions** to it become tainted with illegality and hence void.
*Example:* R borrows Rs. 10,000 from Q and enters into a contract with an alien to smuggle goods into India. Q is aware of the purpose of the loan. The transaction between R & Q (the loan) is collateral to the main illegal agreement (smuggling). Since the principal contract is illegal, the collateral transaction is also illegal and void.

The underlying reason for this rule is that courts will not assist or enforce any agreement entered into with the object of assisting or abetting an illegal transaction.

However, if the principal agreement is simply void (e.g., an agreement with a minor) and not illegal, agreements that are incidental or collateral to it may still be valid.
*Example:* R borrows Rs. 500 from Q for the purpose of wagering. Even if Q is aware of the purpose of the loan, the transaction between R and Q is not affected. It is still a binding contract, as a wagering agreement, while void, is not illegal in the same sense as a criminal act.

**Q:** S. Note: Illegal Contract.
> **Answer:** An illegal contract (or agreement) is one that is forbidden by law, immoral, or against public policy. Such agreements are void from the beginning and have no legal effect. A key characteristic is that not only the illegal agreement itself but also any transactions collateral to it become tainted with illegality and are therefore also void. This is because courts will not lend assistance to agreements that facilitate unlawful acts.

### Difference between Void Contract & Illegal Contract

| Aspect                   | Void Contract                                                | Illegal Contract                                             |
| :----------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Comparison            | All void contracts are not necessarily illegal.              | All illegal contracts are void.                              |
| 2. Effect on Collateral Contracts | All collateral contracts to void contracts are not void.     | All collateral contracts to illegal contracts are also illegal and therefore void. |
| 3. Proof of Ground       | Ground for the voidness has to be proved by the parties.     | Court will, of its own motion, in case of an illegal contract, refuse to enforce it, even though the illegality has not been pleaded. |

### Unenforceable Contract

An **unenforceable contract** is one that, although perfectly valid in all respects, lacks some technical requirements needed to make it enforceable. Examples include:
*   Non-registration.
*   Want of proper stamp.
*   Lack of signature.

It can become enforceable in the future if the technical defect is cured. It may also include contracts that are incapable of proof due to the neglect of some formality required by special provisions of law.

**Q:** Short note: Unenforceable Contract.
> **Answer:** An unenforceable contract is an agreement that is valid in substance (i.e., it meets all the essentials of a contract) but cannot be enforced in a court of law due to a technical defect, such as lack of proper stamp, non-registration, or absence of a required signature. Such a contract can become enforceable if the technical defect is rectified.

### Valid Contract

An agreement enforceable at law is a **valid contract**. An agreement becomes a contract when all the essentials of a valid contract stipulated in Section 10 are complied with. It creates legal obligations enforceable at law.
