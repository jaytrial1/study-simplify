# Trademarks: Identifying Goods and Services

## Meaning of Trademark

A **trademark** is a marketable and commercial asset of the owner. It is a type of intellectual property having numerous rights. A Law Dictionary defines trademark as "A distinctive symbol that identifies particular products of a trader to the general public. The symbol may consist of device, words or combination of these."

A trademark can also be a combination of pictures, colors, drawings, words, letters, numbers etc. For example, the trademarks 'Rexona' and 'Lux' are distinguished by their names (trademarks) though both deal with similar products i.e., soap.

As per Section 2(1)(zb) of the Trademarks Act, 1999, trademark means a mark capable of being represented graphically and which is capable of distinguishing the goods or services of one person from those of others and may include shape of goods, their packaging and combination of colors.

## Elements/Characteristics of a Trademark

The elements or characteristics of a trademark are as follows:

1.  **Trademark must be a mark**: It must be a mark of any manner such as a label, signature, number, letter or a combination of any or all of them.
2.  **It is a mark capable of being represented graphically**: It should be capable of being graphically represented.
3.  **It must be used in relation to goods**: Trademark must be used (or proposed to be used) in relation (connection) to goods or services for which it is registered.
4.  **Commercial asset of the owner**: A trademark is a marketable and commercial asset of the owner. It is a type of intellectual property having numerous rights.
5.  **Valuable property**: The registered trademark is a valuable property, which can be transferred or sold or licensed to third parties. The brand name, label or logo of a Company can be registered as trademarks. Once it is registered, then it is protected against misuse by third parties.
6.  **Other features**:
    *   Trademark must have a distinguishable feature from the trademark of another.
    *   Trademark must preferably be an invented word.
    *   Trademark should be descriptive of goods or services or suggestive of the quality of goods. For example, a beauty shop which provides beauty products.
    *   Trademark may include the shape of goods and its packaging or combination thereof.
    *   It must show some nexus between the goods or services provided and the mark.
    *   It is available to the owner for perpetuity.

## Functions of Trademark [Roles of Trademark]

Trademark performs the following roles:

1.  **Trademark provides for the mark of origin of goods or services**: Trademark provides for the mark of origin of goods so as to enable the consumer to identify the source of goods and/or services provided by the proprietor of that particular region. It identifies the product and its origin.
2.  **It is a means of advertising the product**: It advertises the product. The trademark represents the product. In other words, trademark is a means of advertising the product.
3.  **It provides the feature of quality of the product**: It guarantees the quality of the goods or services. Customers prefer to select those trademarks which are known for their quality and thus trademarks help the customers to make decisions as to the products they purchase. It may be noted that trademark provides the feature of quality of the product. For example, Lakme may have a range of Kajal which would be similar to Revlon or Maybelline but the quality of these products are different.
4.  **Trademarks builds a brand image**: It builds a brand image of products or services on the mind of consumers. For instance, products of MNC companies create a good image of their brand products in India. Example: Zara creates a good image of a foreign clothing brand recently introduced in India.
5.  **Investment Function of Trademark**: In the case of *L'oreal Bellure*, the court recognized the investment function of trademarks. The owner of the trademark had made considerable investment in the trademarks.

## Trademark Act 1999

The Trademark Act is a special Act. It aims to protect trademarks from misuse, deception and confusion.

Some of the features of the Trademarks Act, 1999 are:

*   Registration of trademarks for services besides the goods.
*   Not to permit imitations of trademarks.
*   Factors to be considered for defining a well-known trademark.
*   Simplifying the procedure by producing only a single register and for simple application for registration in more than one class.
*   Enlarging the scope of permitted use.
*   Providing for enhanced punishments for offences.
*   Providing for Appellate Board.

## Trademark Registration Procedure

1.  The application is filed.
2.  Then the application is examined by the Trademarks office and objections, if any, are raised.
3.  After clearing the objections, if any, the mark is advertised in the Trademarks Journal and is open to third party opposition period of 4 months.
4.  The registration certificate is issued in 4 to 6 months after the completion of opposition period, if there is no opposition by the third party during this period.
5.  In case of opposition, the registration certificate is issued when the opposition is dismissed.
