7_Copyright: Protecting Original Works

This section defines copyright, outlines its key features, specifies the types of works protected in India, explains copyright ownership, details the duration of copyright protection, and introduces the concept of fair use.

# Copyright: Protecting Original Works

## Meaning of Copyright

1.  As per Oxford Dictionary, "**Copyright** means the exclusive right to publish or record a work."
2.  Section 14 of the Copyright Act defines copyright as "the exclusive right to do or authorize others to do certain acts in relation to (i) literary, dramatic or music work, (ii) artistic work (iii) cinematograph film, and (iv) sound recording."
3.  Copyright is the exclusive right to do or to do certain acts in relation to literary works, dramatic, musical and artistic works, cinematograph film, sound recording and computer databases.
4.  Copyright is an **intangible right** which is granted to the owner of a literary or artistic work to multiply copies for a specified period and publish the same.

> **Answer to "What is copyright?":** Copyright is an exclusive, intangible legal right granted to the owner of original literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, cinematograph films, sound recordings, and computer databases, allowing them to control the reproduction, distribution, adaptation, and public performance of their work for a specified period.

The features of Copyright are as follows:

1.  Copyright is an **intangible right**.
2.  It is an **exclusive right**, a type of monopoly right excluding others from using the same.
3.  Copyright can subsist only in an **original work**.
4.  It protects the owner of the original work from others.
5.  It consists of various rights in the same work.
6.  It gives eight exclusive rights to the copyright holder:
    *   Right of Reproduction.
    *   Right of Distribute.
    *   Right to Make Derivative Work.
    *   Right to Publicly Perform.
    *   Right to Follow.
    *   Right of Paternity.
    *   Sui Generis Rights.
    *   Private Copying.

## Classes of Works for which Copyright Protection is Available in India (What works are protected ?)

Section 13(1) of the Copyright Act provides that copyright subsists in:

1.  Original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works,
2.  Cinematograph films, and
3.  Sound recording.

The classes of works for which copyright protection is available in India are as follows:

1.  Original literary, dramatic or musical work.
2.  Original artistic work.
3.  Cinematograph film.
4.  Sound recording.
5.  Computer programmes.

These are explained in detail below:

### Original literary, dramatic or musical work

#### Literary Work

**Literary Work** means any work in written or printed manner so long as it is original and has been created by sufficient skill and labor. According to section 2(o) of the Copyright Act, "Literary Work includes computer programmes, tables and computer databases."

Some of the literary works in which copyright subsists are as follows:

*   Headlines of newspaper.
*   Head notes of law reports.
*   Letters.
*   Examination Question Paper.

For example: It was held in *University of London Press, v/s University of Tutorial Press (1916) 2 Ch 601)* that examination papers are original literary work since the person who sets the question paper invests labor and skill in preparing the same. Such person is the author of the question paper and the copyright would vest in him.

#### Dramatic Work

As per section 2(ff) of Copyright Act, "**Dramatic work** includes any piece of recitation, choreographic work or entertainment in dumb show, the scenic arrangement or acting form of which is fixed in writing or otherwise but does not include a cinematograph film."

A photograph which involves no movement or action cannot be treated as dramatic work. [*Creation Records V/s New Group Newspapers (1997) EMLR444*]

The consent or permission of the author/owner of the copyright is necessary in case of adaptation of dramatic work. **Adaptation of dramatic work** means modification of that work in some other form. In simple words, if a theatre play/drama is adapted and a cinematograph film is made thereof. A recent case is that of a film 'Oh My God' which is adapted from a Gujarati play/drama.

#### Musical Work

As per section 2(p) of Copyright Act, "**Musical Work** consisting of music and includes any graphical notation of such work but does not include any words or any action intended to be sung, spoken or performed with music."

It may be noted that musical work does not include any words or any action intended to be sung, spoken or performed with music. Such works will be either classified as literary works or dramatic works. Further, adaptation of original musical work also enjoys copyright protection.

**Adaptation** in relation to musical work means any arrangement or transcription of the work. Copyright will subsist in arranging the musical work by adding new compositions, new rhythms, new musical melodies, etc. For example: Remix songs are an example of adaptation in recent times. It does not constitute infringement of copyright.

Original work of music is determined by the amount of skill and labor put by the composer. Song lyrics (words) and musical composition work have copyright protection. For example: A very famous song of Kabir Singh movie namely BEKHAYALI which is written by Irshad Kamil and music composed by Sachet-Parampara, so the copyright over the lyrics will belong to Irshad Kamil and musical composition will be that of the composer, i.e., Sachet-Parampara.

### Original Artistic Work

According to section 2(c) of the Copyright Act, **Artistic work** means:

*   A painting, a sculpture, a drawing (including a diagram, map, chart or a plan) an engraving or a photograph, whether or not any such work possesses artistic quality.
*   A work of architecture, and
*   Any other work of artistic craftsmanship.

IPRs law provide protection to any kind of artistic work so long as it is original and sufficient amount of skill and labor is put on such work.

Following Artistic work has copyright protection:

1.  **Painting**: Painting is not defined under the Copyright Act. Original painting work has copyright protection.
2.  **Sculpture**: Sculpture means the art of making figures and shapes by carving or shaping stone, wood, metal etc. It also includes models and casts.
3.  **Drawing**: Drawing includes a diagram, a map, a chart or a plan.
4.  **Engraving**: Engraving includes lithographs, wood-cuts, etc. It means carving a design on a hard surface.
5.  **Architecture Work**: Architecture Work means any building or structure having an artistic character or design or structure.
6.  **Artistic Craftsmanship**: Artistic Craftsmanship is not defined under the Copyright Act. However, in common parlance, it can be said that artistic work by a worker skilled in craft. Hand painted tiles is an example of artistic craftsmanship.

### Cinematograph Film

**Cinematograph film** means any work of visual recording and includes a sound recording accompanying such visual recording. The concept of Cinematograph films is not limited to a movie which is played in theaters. [*Balwinder Singh v/s Delhi Administration, Delhi AIR 1984 Delhi 379*] It was clearly held that video as well as television both fall under the purview of cinematograph film.

The producer of the cinematograph film is the owner of copyright under the Copyright Act. Section 13(3)(a) of Copyright Act clarifies that no copyright would subsist in a cinematograph film if it is an infringement of copyright of any other work.

### Sound Recording

As per section 2 (xx) of Copyright Act, **Sound Recording** means - "a recording of sounds from which such sounds may be produced regardless of the medium on which such recording is made or the method by which such recording is produced."

Similar to a cinematograph film, in the case of sound recording, the owner of sound recording is the producer. However, the copyright in the music of sound recording vests with the composer.

### Computer Programme

As per section 2 (ffc) of Copyright Act, **Computer Programme** means, "a set of instructions expressed in words, codes, schemes or any other form, including a machine readable medium, capable of causing a computer to perform a particular task to achieve a particular result."

Computer Programme has copyright protection under this Act. Copyright can also exist in a source code of Computer Programme (Judgement of English Courts). In a Computer Programme, the elements such as codes, design and structure used by the programmers can be copyrighted.

## Ownership of Copyright

The ownership of Copyright vests with the **Author**.

As per section 2(d) of Copyright Act, "Author means:

*   In relation to literary or dramatic work, the author of the work.
*   In relation to musical work, the composer.
*   In relation to artistic work other than a photograph, the artist.
*   In relation to a photograph, the person taking the photograph.
*   In relation to a cinematograph or sound recording, the producer.
*   In relation to any literary dramatic, musical or artistic work which is computer generated, the person who causes the work to be created."

It may be noted that an author may create a work either (i) for himself (in which case he is the owner of the copyright), or (ii) on behalf of another person for a valuable consideration (in which case, in absence of contract to contrary, the person at whose instance is made is the owner of copyright), or (iii) in course of his employment in which case, the same would depend on the nature of employment.

Following Authors owe the rights of Copyright:

1.  **Author of the work**: In relation to literary or dramatic work, the author of the work owes the rights of copyright. It may be noted that if the author is under employment, they owe the rights, but no copyright can be claimed by the proprietor or manager of the company. It was held that in respect of a cartoon drawn by the cartoonist after termination of his employment with a magazine, no copyright can be claimed by the proprietor of that magazine.
2.  **The Composer**: In relation to musical work, the composer owes the right of Copyright. The composer of the musical work is the author of that work. However, if such work is carried out in the course of employment then the employer shall be the owner of copyright of such musical work.
3.  **The Artist**: In relation to artistic work other than photograph, the artist owes the copyrights.
4.  **The person taking the Photograph**: In relation to a photograph, the person taking the photograph owes the rights.
5.  **The Producer**: In relation to a cinematograph, the producer owes the rights. In relation to a sound recording, the producer owes the copyrights.
6.  **The person who has collected and edited the work**: In relation to a collective work, the person who has collected and edited the work (i.e. books, dictionary, newspaper, magazine, etc.) owes the copyrights. Collective work means when a variety of information is compiled in a book.
7.  **Computer Programmer (Creator)**: In relation to Computer Programme, the computer programmer (creator) owes the copyright.
8.  **The Paper Setter**: The Paper Setter is the owner of Copyright in such cases. However, if there is a contract to the contrary with the Board of Examination, such Board of Examination would be the owner of copyright.
9.  **First Owner**: In the case of Government work, the Government shall, in the absence of any agreement to the contrary, be the first owner of the copyright therein. In the case of a work of certain international organizations, the international organization concerned shall be the first owner of the copyright therein.

## Duration/Terms of Copyright

Each work under the Copyright Act subsists for a particular term which is mentioned under sections 22 to 29 of the Copyright Act.

The duration or term of copyright is as follows:

1.  **Term of Copyright in published literary, dramatic, musical and artistic work** (section 22): If published within the lifetime of the author, until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year, next following the year in which the author dies.
2.  **Term of Copyright in anonymous and pseudonymous works** (section 23): If published anonymously or pseudonymously, until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.
3.  **Term of Copyright in the posthumous work** (section 24): If posthumous works, until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.
4.  **Term of Copyright in photograph** (section 25): It shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.
    *Note: By virtue of the 2012 Amendment, Section 25, Term of Copyright in photograph has been deleted.*
5.  **Term of Copyright in cinematograph films** (section 26): In such case, copyright shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year, next following the year in which the film is published.
6.  **Term of Copyright in records** (section 27): In such case, copyright shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the sound recording is published.
7.  **Term of Copyright in Government Work** (section 28): In the case of Government work, where the Government is the first owner of the copyright therein, copyright shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.
8.  **Term of Copyright in Works of Public Undertakings** (section 28A): In the case of a work, where a public undertaking is the first owner of the copyright therein, copyright shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.
9.  **Term of Copyright in works of International Organisations** (section 29): In the case of a work of an international organization to which the provisions of section 41 apply, copyright shall subsist until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year next following the year in which the work is first published.

## Meaning of Fair Use (Fair Dealing)

**Fair use** means reasonable use. Section 52(1)(a) and (b) defines the term fair use or fair dealing of a copyrighted work under the Copyright Act.

Fair Use (dealing) means the use of a copyright of another by any person for specific purposes such as research, private use—including research, criticism, review, reporting of current events, current affairs, lectures in public, etc.

The factors which determine fair use are:

1.  The nature, extent, and quantum of use of the copyrighted work.
2.  The purpose of such use, including whether such use is for commercial or non-commercial nature.
3.  The likelihood of competition between two works.
4.  The effect of use upon the potential market value of the copyrighted work.
