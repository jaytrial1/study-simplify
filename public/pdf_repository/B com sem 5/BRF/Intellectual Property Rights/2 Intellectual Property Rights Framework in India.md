# Intellectual Property Rights Framework in India

India has established a constitutional, administrative, and jurisdictional framework to protect intellectual property rights within its territory, covering copyrights, patents, trademarks, industrial designs, and other aspects of IPRs.

Protecting and managing intellectual property rights is a crucial first step for any business aiming to establish a presence in India and should be an integral part of its asset growth strategy.

In India, different types of intellectual property are protected under separate laws:

## The Copyright Act, 1957

The Copyright Act, 1957, governs copyright law in India. It was the first post-independence copyright legislation and has been amended six times since 1957, with the most recent amendment being the Copyright (Amendment) Act, 2012, which incorporated a number of new provisions.

## The Trademark Act, 1999

In India, the Trademark Act is a specialized act designed to protect trademarks from misuse, deception, and confusion. It provides rules for the registration, regulation, and protection of trademarks in India, establishing strong trademark laws.

## The Patent Act, 1970

The Patent Act in India has a history spanning over 150 years, with the first legislation relating to patents being Act VI of 1856. The Patent Act, 1970, came into force on April 20, 1972, and has undergone further amendments in 1999, 2002, 2005, 2016, and 2017. The main objective of the Patent Act is to grant the patentee exclusive rights over their patent for a period of 20 years, enabling them to gain industrial and commercial advantage.

## The Designs Act, 2000

The current Designs Act of 2000 replaced the Designs Act 1911, which was prevalent during British India.

## Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999

This Act protects agricultural goods, natural goods, handicrafts, and other items in India, adequately safeguarding the interests of producers of such goods.

India is a member state of the World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO), an international organization responsible for promoting the protection of intellectual property worldwide. India is also a member of the following International Organizations and Treaties concerning Intellectual Property:

*   World Trade Organization (WTO).
*   The World Intellectual Property Organization (WIPO).
*   Paris Convention for Protection of Industrial Property.
*   Patent Co-operation Treaty (effective from December 7, 1998).
*   Budapest Treaty (effective from December 17, 2001).

India is also a member of the TRIPS Agreement (The Agreement on Trade-Related Aspects of Intellectual Property Rights). The TRIPS Agreement is comprehensive, covering all areas of technology, property, patents, trademarks, copyrights, and more. Its main features include standards, enforcement, and dispute settlement. TRIPS encourages member countries to frame their own legislation on intellectual property matters.
