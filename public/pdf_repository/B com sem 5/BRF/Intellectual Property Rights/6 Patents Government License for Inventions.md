This section delves into patents, defining them, outlining their key features, and detailing the remedies available to patent owners in cases of infringement.

# Patents: Government License for Inventions

## Meaning of Patents

1.  A **Patent system** is a contract between the inventor and authority (Government) whereby the inventor gets exclusive rights for a limited period (in India for a period of 20 years) in return for disclosing full details of the invention.
2.  "**Patent**" means a patent for any invention granted under the Act. [Section 2(m) of the Patents Act, 1970]

    A patent is granted for:
    *   Inventing a new product, or
    *   Inventing a new process; and
    *   The new invented product or process is capable of industrial application, and
    *   A new formula for making a product or article.
3.  The original word 'patent' comes from the Latin word 'patere', which means 'to lay open' or 'available for public usage'. It is in the interest of the inventor to get their invention or any improvement patented. The right of patent is available for a limited period of time.

> **Answer to "What is meant by Patent?":** A patent is a legal right granted by a government authority to an inventor for a limited period (20 years in India) in exchange for the public disclosure of the invention's details. It provides the inventor exclusive rights to prevent others from making, using, selling, or importing the patented product or process without permission.

## Salient Features of a Patent

The following are the salient features of a patent:

1.  **Patent must be in respect of Invention**: A patent must be in respect of an invention or creation, not merely a discovery. Invention means to create or design something new. The fundamental principle of Patent Law is that a patent is granted only for an invention which must be new. An application of an established technique to a new target is not an inventive step. It must involve an inventive step.
2.  **Patent for Respective (Particular) Invention**: Every single invention has its own single patent according to law. In respect of one single invention, there must be one single patent.
3.  **Patent may be for substance or process**: A patent may be in respect of a substance (i.e., article) or in respect of a process. It may be for a product or a process.
4.  **It grants exclusive right to the Inventor for his Invention**: A patent grants exclusive right to the inventor for their invention for a limited period of time. Generally, a 20-year term has been granted to the patent holder. However, in the case of inventions relating to the manufacturing of food, drugs, or medicine, it is for 7 years from the date of patent.
5.  **Patent registration can be filed Individually or Jointly**: In India, patent registration can be filed individually or jointly. If more than two persons have jointly applied for a patent license, both will own the patent separately.
6.  **Patent is Property Right**: A patent in law is a **property right** and hence, can be gifted, inherited, assigned, sold, or licensed.
7.  **Clear specifications and claims**: In order to have a complete patent, the specifications and the claims must be clearly and distinctly mentioned for obtaining a patent for a particular substance or process.
8.  **General principles applicable to working of patented inventions**: In exercising the powers for granting patents, the following considerations are kept in mind:
    *   Patents are granted to encourage inventions and to secure that the inventions are worked in India on a commercial scale.
    *   Patents are not granted merely to enable patentees (patent holders) to enjoy a monopoly for the importation of the patented article.
    *   The protection of patent rights contributes to the promotion of technological innovation and to the transfer of technology.
    *   Patents granted do not in any way prohibit the Central Government from taking measures to protect public health.
    *   Patents are granted to make the benefit of the patented invention available at reasonably affordable prices to the public.

> **Answer to "Explain the Characteristics of Patent ?":** The characteristics of a patent include: it must be an invention (not a discovery) and involve an inventive step; each invention requires a single patent; it can be for a product or a process; it grants exclusive rights to the inventor for a limited period (e.g., 20 years); it can be filed individually or jointly; it is a property right that can be transferred; and it requires clear specifications and claims. Additionally, patents aim to encourage innovation and ensure inventions are worked commercially in India, not just imported, while also considering public health and affordability.

## Remedies available to the patent owner/Patent holder/patentees

### Infringement of Patent

**Infringement** as such is not defined under the Patent Act. The patentee has certain rights over the patented invention. When these rights are violated or if any third party uses such rights over a patent without the permission of the patentee (patent holder), it constitutes infringement. However, in cases of infringement, the court has to consider what the infringed patent is, i.e., whether it is a product or a process.

Infringement can occur in the following manners:

1.  Copying the entire patented invention with minor modification/colorable variation.
2.  Using the essential ingredient of the process of the patented invention.
3.  Merely using a substitute in the invention/mechanical equivalent.
4.  Adopting essential features of an invention.

### Remedies are available against Patent Infringement

The Patent Act provides penal provisions to prevent infringement.

#### Administrative Remedy

If and when infringing goods are imported into Indian Territory, the patent owner can approach the Collector of Customs and prevent the entry of these goods into the Indian Market. The patent owner must provide the name of the exporter, consignee, port of entry, name of the ship, etc., to avail himself of this remedy.

#### Civil Remedy

To claim damages, the patent owner will have to pay a court fee on the damages claimed.

The courts in India grant two types of injunctions:

*   **Interim Injunctions**: Granted during the pendency of the case before a full-fledged trial.
*   **Permanent Injunctions**: Granted after a full-fledged trial.

An injunction is a normal remedy, though discretionary on the part of the court. It stops the infringement during the pendency of the proceedings. The court may also order that the goods which are found to be infringing materials and implements, the predominant use of which is in the creation of infringing goods, shall be seized, forfeited, or destroyed, as the court deems fit under the circumstances of the case without payment of any compensation.

#### Damages

**Damages** are claimed in the form of a monetary amount. In a suit for infringement, the plaintiff may seek damages. Damages account for the loss in money terms suffered by the patent owner due to infringement. In assessing damages, the sole question is what is the loss sustained by the patentee by reason of the unlawful sale of the defendant's goods. The loss must be a natural and direct consequence of the defendant's act.

#### Accounts of Profits

In a suit for infringement, the plaintiff may seek **accounts of profits**. Accounts relates to the account of net profits earned by the infringer (defendant). If there are no profits, "accounts" is not a remedy. Damages and accounts are alternative remedies; the patent owner can choose only one of them, not both. In a claim of profits, it is material to ascertain how much of the invention was actually appropriated to determine what proportion of the net profits realized by the infringer (defendant) was attributable to its use.

#### Otherwise (General Provision)

This is a general provision which authorizes the court to grant such other reliefs as it may deem necessary for complete redressal of the complaint. For example, the court may order that the infringing goods or materials and implements shall be seized, forfeited, or destroyed.

### Burden of Proof

Where there is an alleged infringement of a patented invention that is in the form of a product, the burden of establishing that an infringement has occurred lies on the patentee.

In the case of a process patent, the burden may shift to the infringer (defendant) provided the patentee is able to prove to the court that through reasonable efforts he has not been able to determine the process which has been used by the defendant or infringer.
