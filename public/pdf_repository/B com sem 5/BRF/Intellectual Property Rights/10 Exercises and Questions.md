# Exercises and Questions

1.  Define Intellectual Property. Explain the basic Intellectual Property Rights in India.
    > **Answer:** Intellectual property is the creation of human intellect (mind), referring to knowledge, ideas, invention, innovation, creativity, and research. It is an intangible right treated as movable property. The basic Intellectual Property Rights in India are protected under separate laws, including The Copyright Act, 1957; The Trademark Act, 1999; The Patent Act, 1970; The Designs Act, 2000; and Geographical Indications of Goods (Registration and Protection) Act, 1999.

2.  What is Intellectual Property? Explain the basic features of Intellectual Property.
    > **Answer:** Intellectual property is the creation of human intellect (mind), referring to knowledge, ideas, invention, innovation, creativity, research, etc., all being the product of human mind. It is an intangible right exercisable in respect of a tangible work.
    > The basic features are:
    > (1) Intellectual property is in the nature of 'intangible' property.
    > (2) It is a creation of human intellect (mind).
    > (3) It is treated as a movable property and can be assigned and transferred.
    > (4) The owner of Intellectual Property has absolute and exclusive right over it.
    > (5) It exists only under law. It is a legal right.

3.  Give the meaning of Intellectual Property Right. Discuss the types of IPRs.
    > **Answer:** According to World Trade Organisation (WTO), "Intellectual Property Rights are the rights given to persons over the creations of their minds. They usually give the creator an exclusive right over the use of his/her creation for a certain period of time."
    > The TRIPS Agreement covers seven categories or types of Intellectual Property Rights: (1) Patents, (2) Copyrights, (3) Trademarks, (4) Industrial Designs, (5) Trade Secrets (Know-how), (6) Geographical Indications, (7) Integrated Circuits.

4.  Write a note on: Patents.
    > **Answer:** A patent system is a contract between the inventor and the government, granting the inventor exclusive rights for a limited period (20 years in India) in return for disclosing full details of the invention. A patent is granted for inventing a new product or process that is capable of industrial application. It is a property right that can be gifted, inherited, assigned, sold, or licensed. Patents must be for an invention, involve an inventive step, and require clear specifications and claims.

5.  Write a note on: Trademarks.
    > **Answer:** A trademark is a marketable and commercial asset of the owner, identifying particular products or services of a trader to the general public. It can be a distinctive symbol, device, words, pictures, colors, drawings, letters, numbers, or combinations thereof, capable of graphical representation and distinguishing goods or services. Trademarks serve functions such as indicating origin, advertising products, guaranteeing quality, building brand image, and representing investment. The Trademark Act, 1999, governs their registration and protection in India.

6.  Write a note on: Copyrights.
    > **Answer:** Copyright is an exclusive, intangible right granted to the owner of original literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works, cinematograph films, sound recordings, and computer databases. It allows the owner to control specific acts related to their work, such as reproduction, distribution, and public performance. Copyright subsists only in original works and protects the owner from unauthorized use. The duration of copyright generally extends for 60 years after specific events, such as the author's death or publication.

7.  What do you mean by Government License (Patents)? And explain in detail, unless features of a Patent.
    > **Answer:** "Government License (Patents)" refers to the exclusive right granted by the government to an inventor for a limited period (20 years in India) in exchange for the public disclosure of their invention. This license enables the patentee to gain industrial and commercial advantage.
    > The salient features of a patent include:
    > (1) It must be in respect of an invention, not a discovery, and involve an inventive step.
    > (2) Each invention has a single patent.
    > (3) It can be for a substance (product) or a process.
    > (4) It grants exclusive rights to the inventor for a limited period (generally 20 years).
    > (5) Registration can be filed individually or jointly.
    > (6) It is a property right that can be transferred.
    > (7) It requires clear specifications and claims.
    > (8) General principles guide its working, aiming to encourage invention, ensure commercial working in India, promote technological innovation, and ensure affordability and public health.

8.  What works are protected under Copyright Act?
    > **Answer:** Under the Copyright Act, copyright subsists in:
    > (1) Original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works.
    > (2) Cinematograph films.
    > (3) Sound recording.
    > (4) Computer programmes.

9.  Discuss the general principles applicable to working of patented inventions.
    > **Answer:** The general principles applicable to the working of patented inventions, kept in mind when granting patents, are:
    > (i) Patents are granted to encourage inventions and to secure that the inventions are worked in India on a commercial scale.
    > (ii) Patents are not granted merely to enable patentees to enjoy a monopoly for the importation of the patented article.
    > (iii) The protection of patent rights contributes to the promotion of technological innovation and to the transfer of technology.
    > (iv) Patents granted do not in any way prohibit the Central Government in taking measures to protect public health.
    > (v) Patents are granted to make the benefit of the patented invention available at reasonably affordable prices to the public.

10. Write a note on: Remedies available to the patent owner.
    > **Answer:** Remedies available to the patent owner against infringement include:
    > (A) **Administrative Remedy**: The patent owner can approach the Collector of Customs to prevent infringing goods from entering the Indian market by providing details of the infringing consignment.
    > (B) **Civil Remedy**: This includes seeking injunctions (interim or permanent) to stop the infringement during or after legal proceedings. The court may also order seizure, forfeiture, or destruction of infringing goods and implements.
    > (C) **Damages**: The patent owner can claim monetary compensation for the loss suffered due to the infringement.
    > (D) **Accounts of Profits**: The patent owner can seek an account of the net profits earned by the infringer due to the use of the patented invention. Damages and accounts are alternative remedies.
    > (E) **Otherwise (General Provision)**: The court may grant any other relief deemed necessary for complete redressal.

11. What is infringement of patent? Discuss the remedies available against patent infringement.
    > **Answer:** Infringement of patent occurs when a third party uses the rights over a patented invention without the permission of the patentee. It can happen by copying the entire invention, using essential ingredients of a process, using substitutes or mechanical equivalents, or adopting essential features.
    > Remedies available against patent infringement include:
    > *   **Administrative Remedy**: Preventing infringing goods from entering India via customs.
    > *   **Civil Remedy**: Obtaining injunctions (interim or permanent) to stop infringement, and potential seizure or destruction of infringing materials.
    > *   **Damages**: Monetary compensation for losses incurred by the patentee due to infringement.
    > *   **Accounts of Profits**: Claiming the net profits earned by the infringer from the unauthorized use.
    > *   **General Provision**: Any other relief the court deems necessary for complete redressal.

12. What is copyright? State clauses of words for which copyrights protection is available in India.
    > **Answer:** Copyright is the exclusive right to do or authorize others to do certain acts in relation to literary, dramatic, musical, artistic works, cinematograph films, and sound recordings. It is an intangible right granted to the owner to multiply copies and publish their original work for a specified period.
    > Copyright protection is available in India for:
    > (1) Original literary, dramatic, musical and artistic works.
    > (2) Cinematograph films.
    > (3) Sound recording.
    > (4) Computer programmes.

13. What owes the copyright? Explain in detail.
    > **Answer:** The ownership of copyright vests with the Author.
    > The specific individuals or entities who owe copyright depending on the work are:
    > *   **Author of literary or dramatic work**: The author of the work.
    > *   **Composer of musical work**: The composer.
    > *   **Artist of artistic work (other than a photograph)**: The artist.
    > *   **Person taking the photograph**: The photographer.
    > *   **Producer of cinematograph or sound recording**: The producer.
    > *   **Person who causes computer-generated work to be created**: The person who causes the work to be created.
    > *   **Person who has collected and edited collective work**: The person who has collected and edited the work (e.g., books, dictionaries).
    > *   **Computer Programmer**: The computer programmer (creator).
    > *   **Paper Setter**: The paper setter, unless a contract with the Board of Examination states otherwise.
    > *   **Government work**: The Government, in the absence of a contrary agreement.
    > *   **Works of certain international organizations**: The international organization concerned.
    > Ownership can also depend on whether the work was created for valuable consideration or in the course of employment.

14. Write a note on: Ownership of Copyright.
    > **Answer:** The ownership of copyright primarily rests with the **Author** of the work. The Copyright Act, 1957, defines "Author" differently based on the type of work: the author for literary/dramatic works, the composer for musical works, the artist for artistic works (excluding photographs), the person taking the photograph, the producer for cinematograph films/sound recordings, and the person causing computer-generated works. Ownership can also transfer if the work is created for valuable consideration or during employment. For government works or works of international organizations, they can be the first owners.

15. Write a note on: Duration Term of Copyright.
    > **Answer:** The duration or term of copyright varies based on the type of work, as specified under sections 22 to 29 of the Copyright Act. Generally, for published literary, dramatic, musical, and artistic works published during the author's lifetime, copyright lasts until 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year following the author's death. For anonymous, pseudonymous, posthumous works, photographs, cinematograph films, sound recordings, government works, public undertaking works, and international organization works, the term is typically 60 years from the beginning of the calendar year following the year of first publication.

16. Write a note on: Fair Use (Fair Dealing).
    > **Answer:** Fair use, also known as fair dealing, refers to the reasonable use of a copyrighted work by another person for specific purposes without requiring permission from the copyright owner. Section 52(1)(a) and (b) of the Copyright Act define this concept. Permitted uses include research, private study, criticism, review, reporting of current events, current affairs, and public lectures. Factors determining fair use include the nature, extent, and quantum of use, the purpose (commercial or non-commercial), the likelihood of competition between the works, and the effect of the use on the copyrighted work's potential market value.

17. What is Trademark? Explain the elements or characteristics of a trade mark.
    > **Answer:** A trademark is a distinctive symbol (device, words, combination of these, pictures, colors, drawings, letters, numbers, shape of goods, packaging) that identifies particular products or services of a trader and distinguishes them from others. It is a marketable and commercial asset of the owner.
    > The elements or characteristics of a trademark are:
    > 1.  It must be a mark (label, signature, number, letter, or combination).
    > 2.  It must be capable of being represented graphically.
    > 3.  It must be used (or proposed to be used) in relation to goods or services for which it is registered.
    > 4.  It is a commercial asset of the owner.
    > 5.  A registered trademark is a valuable property that can be transferred, sold, or licensed.
    > 6.  It must have a distinguishable feature from other trademarks.
    > 7.  It is preferably an invented word.
    > 8.  It can be descriptive or suggestive of the quality of goods/services.
    > 9.  It may include the shape of goods and their packaging or combination.
    > 10. It must show a nexus between the goods/services and the mark.
    > 11. It is available to the owner for perpetuity.

18. What are the roles of Trademark? Explain in detail.
    > **Answer:** The roles or functions of a trademark are:
    > 1.  **Mark of origin**: It helps consumers identify the source of goods or services.
    > 2.  **Means of advertising**: It represents and advertises the product, building recognition.
    > 3.  **Quality assurance**: It provides a guarantee of the quality of goods or services, influencing consumer purchasing decisions.
    > 4.  **Brand image building**: It helps build a strong brand image of products or services in the minds of consumers.
    > 5.  **Investment function**: It represents the considerable investment made by the owner in developing and promoting the brand.

19. Discuss the functions of Trademark.
    > **Answer:** The functions of a trademark are:
    > 1.  **Indication of Origin**: Trademarks enable consumers to identify the source of goods and/or services.
    > 2.  **Advertising Tool**: Trademarks serve as a means of advertising and promoting products.
    > 3.  **Quality Guarantee**: Trademarks assure consumers of the quality of goods or services, guiding their purchase decisions.
    > 4.  **Brand Building**: Trademarks help establish and reinforce a brand image in the minds of consumers.
    > 5.  **Investment Protection**: Trademarks protect the substantial investments made by owners in their brands.
