4_Distinction Between Indemnity and Guarantee

This section provides a clear comparison between contracts of indemnity and contracts of guarantee, highlighting their fundamental differences in terms of parties, liability, and purpose.

# Distinction Between Indemnity and Guarantee

| Aspects               | Contract of Indemnity                                                                                              | Contract of Guarantee                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| :-------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Number of Contracts** | There is only one contract: between the indemnifier and the indemnified.                                           | There are three contracts: <br> (i) Between the principal debtor and the creditor, <br> (ii) Between the surety and the principal debtor (implied), and <br> (iii) Between the surety and the creditor.                                           |
| **2. Parties**        | There are only two parties: The indemnifier and the indemnified.                                                   | There are three parties: The creditor, the principal debtor, and the surety.                                                                                                                                                                 |
| **3. Liability**      | The liability of the indemnifier to the indemnified is primary and independent.                                    | The liability of the Principal Debtor is primary and that of the surety is only secondary or collateral.                                                                                                                                     |
| **4. Commencement**   | The liability of the indemnifier arises only on the happening of a contingency (i.e., actual loss).                  | There is an existing duty (i.e., the debtor's debt), the performance of which is guaranteed by the surety. Liability arises on default of the principal debtor.                                                                                 |
| **5. Purpose**        | Such a contract is for the reimbursement of the loss entered into with the object of protecting the promisee against the anticipated loss. | It is for the security of the creditor, ensuring that the debt or obligation is fulfilled.                                                                                                                                                   |
| **6. Privity of Contract** | An indemnifier cannot sue a third party for loss in his own name because there is no privity of contract. He can do so only if there is an assignment in his favor. | A surety, on discharging the debt due by the principal debtor, steps into the shoes of the creditor. He can proceed against the principal debtor in his own name (right of subrogation).                                                        |
| **7. Request**        | It is not necessary for the indemnifier to act at the request of the debtor (if a third party causes loss).         | It is necessary that the surety should give the guarantee at the request of the principal debtor (either express or implied).                                                                                                                  |
| **8. Capacity of Parties** | The parties must all be capable of contracting.                                                                     | The principal debtor may be a minor, in which case, the surety will be primarily liable to the creditor, as the minor's contract is void. However, the surety must be competent to contract.                                                    |
