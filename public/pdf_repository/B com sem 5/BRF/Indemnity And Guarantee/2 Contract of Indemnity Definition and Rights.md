2_Contract of Indemnity: Definition and Rights

This section delves into the definition of a contract of indemnity, identifies the parties involved, and outlines the specific rights of the indemnity-holder when faced with a legal claim.

# Contract of Indemnity: Definition and Rights

Contracts of Indemnity and Guarantee are special types of contracts discussed in Sections 124 to 147 of the Act. The principles of the general law of contracts are equally applicable to them.

## Contract of Indemnity

### Definition

"A contract by which one party promises to save the other from loss caused to him by the conduct of the promisor himself, or by the conduct of any other person, is called a 'contract of indemnity'." (Section 124)

**Example:** A contracts to indemnify B against the consequences of any proceedings which C may take against B in respect of a certain sum of 200 rupees. This is a contract of indemnity.

The person who promises to make good the loss is called the '**indemnifier**' and the person whose loss is to be made good is called '**indemnified**' or '**indemnity-holder**'. A contract of insurance is a kind of contract of indemnity. Really speaking, a contract of indemnity is a class of contingent contract.

### Ingredients and Scope of Definition

> **Answer:** A contract of indemnity, as per Section 124, involves one party promising to save another from loss caused by the promisor's own conduct or the conduct of any other person. The key ingredients are:
> 1.  **Promise to save from loss:** There must be a clear promise from one party (indemnifier) to protect another (indemnified) from suffering a loss.
> 2.  **Cause of loss:** The loss must be caused either by the conduct of the indemnifier themselves or by the conduct of any other person.
> 3.  **Two parties:** There are two parties involved – the indemnifier and the indemnity-holder.
>
> **Definition not exhaustive:** The definition of "Contract of Indemnity" as stated in Section 124 is not exhaustive since it only includes:
> *   Express promises to indemnify.
> *   Cases where the loss arises from the conduct of the promisor or of any other person.
>
> But it does not explicitly include:
> *   Implied promises to indemnify.
> *   Cases where loss arises from accidents and events not dependent on the conduct of any person (e.g., marine insurance, fire insurance, which are often considered contracts of indemnity in a broader sense, though not strictly covered by Section 124).

### Validity of Indemnity

As stated earlier, a contract of indemnity is a species of the general contract. Being but one species of contract, the contract of indemnity is subject to the general rules as to reality of consent, legality of object, etc. Thus, an agreement to indemnify sureties on a bail bond will be void as being against public policy. Again, where an indemnity is procured by fraud, it will not be enforceable.

**Example:** A asks B to slap C and promises to compensate him (B) against the consequences. B slaps C and is fined Rs. 50. B cannot recover the amount from A as the consideration and object of the contract is not legal.

## Rights of the Indemnity-Holder

The rights of the indemnity-holder are contained in Section 125 of the Contract Act. These rights are consequently the obligations of the indemnifier. Accordingly:

"The promisee in a contract of indemnity, acting within the scope of his authority, is entitled to recover from the promisor:

1.  All damages which he may be compelled to pay in any suit in respect of any matter to which the promise to indemnify applies.
2.  All costs which he may be compelled to pay in any such suit if, in bringing or defending it, he did not contravene the orders of the promisor, and acted as it would have been prudent for him to act in the absence of any contract of indemnity, or if the promisor authorized him to bring or defend the suit.
3.  All sums which he may have paid under the terms of any compromise of any such suit, if the compromise was not contrary to the orders of the promisor, and was one which it would have been prudent for the promisee to make in the absence of any contract of indemnity, or if the promisor authorized him to compromise the suit."

### Commencement of the Indemnifier's Liability

Different High Courts have expressed different views on this subject. Bombay and Nagpur High Courts have expressed the view that the indemnifier will be liable only after the actual loss has occurred. But according to High Courts of Calcutta, Madras, and Allahabad, the indemnity-holder can compel payment from the indemnifier even before he has met his liability. The latter view appears to be more correct.
