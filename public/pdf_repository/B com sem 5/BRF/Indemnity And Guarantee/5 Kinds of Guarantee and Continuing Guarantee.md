5_Kinds of Guarantee and Continuing Guarantee

This section categorizes different types of guarantees and provides a detailed explanation of a "continuing guarantee," including its definition and the circumstances under which it can be revoked.

# Kinds of Guarantee and Continuing Guarantee

Guarantee may be classified into several kinds:

*   **Retrospective Guarantee:** A guarantee given for an existing debt or obligation.
*   **Prospective Guarantee:** A guarantee given for a future debt or obligation.
*   **For Ascertained Debt:** The guarantee may be for a specific, already determined debt.
*   **Specific Guarantee:** When it extends to a single transaction or debt, it is called a specific or simple guarantee.
*   **Continuing Guarantee:** When it extends to a series of transactions, it is called a continuing guarantee.

## Continuing Guarantee

### Definition

"A guarantee which extends to a series of transactions is called a continuing guarantee." (Section 129)

**Example:** A, in consideration that B will employ C in collecting the rents of B's Jamindari, promises B to be responsible, to the amount of 5,000 rupees, for the due collection and payment by C of those rents. This is a continuing guarantee.

### What are not Continuing Guarantees

1.  A guarantee for the faithful discharge of his duties by a person appointed to a place of trust in a bank is not necessarily a continuing guarantee in all contexts; it is a guarantee of appointment. (Sen v. Bank of Bengal (1920) 47 I.A. 164.)
2.  A guarantee for payment of a certain sum by installments within a definite time is not a continuing guarantee; it is a guarantee of a specific loan. (Bhagvandas v. Secretary of State (1926) 28 Bom. L.R. 662.)

### Revocation of a Continuing Guarantee

A continuing guarantee can be revoked under the following circumstances:

1.  **By Notice:** A continuing guarantee may at any time be revoked by the surety, as to future transactions, by notice to the creditor. (Section 130)
    **Example:** A guarantees to B, to the extent of 10,000 rupees that C shall pay all the bills that B shall draw upon him. B draws upon C. C accepts the bill. A gives notice of revocation. C dishonors the bill at maturity. A is liable upon his guarantee for the accepted bill, but not for any future bills drawn after the notice of revocation.
2.  **By Death of Surety:** The death of the surety operates, in the absence of any contract to the contrary, as revocation of a continuing guarantee, so far as regards future transactions. (Section 131)
    The estate of the surety is liable for all transactions entered into prior to the death of the surety unless there was a contract to the contrary. It is not necessary that the creditor must have notice of the death for the revocation to be effective under Indian Law.
    *   **English Law:** The rule in English Law is that where a person dies without revoking a continuing guarantee, notice of his death to the creditor is necessary for the revocation of the guarantee.
