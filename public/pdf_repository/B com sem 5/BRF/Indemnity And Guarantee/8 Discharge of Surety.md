8_Discharge of Surety

This section explains the various ways a surety's liability under a contract of guarantee can be terminated, categorizing them into discharge by revocation, by the conduct of the creditor, and by the invalidation of the contract.

# Discharge of Surety

A surety is said to be discharged when his liability is at an end. Various modes of discharge of surety can be graphically shown as follows:

## Different Modes of Discharge

### I. Discharge by Revocation

1.  **Notice of Revocation:** A continuing guarantee may at any time be revoked by the surety as to future transactions, by notice to the creditor. (Section 130)
2.  **Death of Surety:** "The death of the surety operates in the absence of any contract to the contrary as revocation of a continuing guarantee, so far as regards to future transactions." (Section 131)
3.  **Novation:** When a new contract is substituted for an old one, the original guarantee may be discharged. (Section 62)

### II. Discharge by Conduct of the Creditor

1.  **Variance in Terms of Contract:** "Any variance, made without the surety's consent, in the terms of the contract, between the principal debtor and creditor, discharges the surety as to transactions subsequent to the variance." (Section 133)
    **Example:** A becomes surety to C for B's conduct, a Manager in C's Bank. Afterwards, B and C contract without A's consent, that B's salary shall be raised and that he shall become liable for one-fourth of the losses on over-draft. B allows a customer to overdraw, and the Bank loses a sum of money. A is discharged from his suretyship by the variance made without his consent and is not liable to make good this loss.
2.  **Release or Discharge of Principal Debtor:** "The surety is discharged by any contract between the creditor and the principal debtor, by which the principal debtor is released or by any act or omission, of the creditor, the legal consequences of which is the discharge of the principal debtor." (Section 134)
    **Example:** A contracts with B for a fixed price to build a house for B within a stipulated time, B supplying necessary timber. C guarantees A's performance of the contract. B omits to supply the timber. C is discharged from his suretyship.
    *   However, the surety is not discharged by operation of law (e.g., discharge of the principal debtor by insolvency) nor by the omission of the creditor to sue within the period of limitation. (Aypunnimani v. Kochouseph AIR 1966 Ker. 203 (FB))
3.  **Compounding by Creditor with Principal Debtor:** "A contract between the creditor and the principal debtor, by which the creditor makes a composition with, or promises to give time to, or not to sue, the principal debtor discharges the surety, unless the surety assents to such contracts." (Section 135.)
4.  **Creditor's Act or Omission Impairing Surety's Eventual Remedy:** "If the creditor does any act which is inconsistent with the rights of the surety or omits to do any act which his duty to the surety requires him to do, and the eventual remedy of the surety himself against the principal debtor is thereby impaired, the surety is discharged." (Section 139)
    **Example:** A puts M as apprentice to B, and gives a guarantee to B for M's fidelity. B promises on his part that he will, at least once a month, see M makes up the cash. B omits to see this done, as promised, and M embezzles, A is not liable to B on his guarantee.
    *   However, if the creditor does some act which by implication releases the debtor from his liability, the surety too is discharged.
    **Example:** When a person stood surety to a Bank for payment of a customer's current account, the act of the Bank in allowing such customer to open a separate account in his own name, was held to discharge the surety. (Ghuznavi v. National Bank of India, (1961) 20 C.W.N. 62)
5.  **Loss of Security:** "A surety is entitled to the benefit of every security which the creditor has against the principal debtor." (Section 141.)
    **Examples:**
    *   C, a creditor whose advance to B is secured by a decree, received also a guarantee for that advance from A. C afterwards takes B's goods in execution under the decree, and then, without the knowledge of A, withdraws the execution. A is discharged.
    *   Principal debtor secured cash credit facility from a bank on two securities - one by pledge of goods and the other on personal guarantee of a surety. Surety's liability for the cash credit was held to be discharged where the goods in the custody of the bank were lost through its negligence. (State Bank of Saurastra v. Chitranjan Rangnath Raja AIR 1980 SC 1528.)

### III. Discharge by Invalidation of the Contract

1.  **Guarantee Obtained by Misrepresentation or Concealment:**
    *   **Misrepresentation:** "Any guarantee which has been obtained by means of misrepresentation made by the creditor, or with his knowledge and assent, concerning a material part of the transaction, is invalid." (Section 142)
        **Example:** The guarantee was in respect of an agent's future liability to his employer. In fact, at the date of the guarantee, the agent was already indebted to the employer. The creditor had made misleading assertions in the matter of *suppressio veri* (suppression of truth) and *suggestio falsi* (suggestion of falsehood). It was held that the contract was vitiated by material misrepresentation. (Lee v. Jones (1864) 144 E.R. 194.)
    *   **Concealment:** "Any guarantee which the creditor has obtained by means of keeping silence as to material circumstances is invalid." (Section 143)
        **Example:** A engages B as clerk to collect money for him. B fails to account for some of his receipts and A, in consequence, calls upon him to furnish security for his duly accounting. C gives his guarantee for B's due accounting. A does not acquaint C with B's previous conduct. B afterwards makes default. The guarantee is invalid.
2.  **Failure of a Co-surety to Join as a Surety:** "Where a person gives guarantee upon a contract that a creditor shall not act upon it until another person has joined it as co-surety, the guarantee is not valid if that other person does not join." (Section 144)
3.  **Failure of Consideration:** A surety will be discharged on the failure of consideration as between the creditor and the principal debtor. Such a failure of consideration must be a substantial one which may operate so as to discharge the surety.

> **Answer to Q: State when a guarantee is invalid?**
> A guarantee is invalid under the following circumstances:
> 1.  **Obtained by Misrepresentation (Sec. 142):** If the guarantee was obtained by the creditor through misrepresentation, or with their knowledge and assent, concerning a material part of the transaction.
> 2.  **Obtained by Concealment (Sec. 143):** If the creditor obtained the guarantee by intentionally keeping silent about material circumstances relevant to the transaction.
> 3.  **Failure of Co-surety to Join (Sec. 144):** If a person gives a guarantee on the condition that another person will also join as a co-surety, and that other person does not join, the guarantee becomes invalid.
> 4.  **Failure of Consideration:** If there is a substantial failure of consideration between the creditor and the principal debtor, the surety may be discharged.
