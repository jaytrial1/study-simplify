# Discharge by Lapse of Time

This section explains how a contract can be discharged if it is not performed within the stipulated or reasonable time, leading to legal remedies being barred.

Every contract must be performed within the stipulated period of time or within a reasonable time. Contracts may be terminated by lapse of time. In civil litigations, the obligations arising out of contracts are barred by limitation. The Limitation Act provides that a contract should be performed within a specified period, called the period of limitation. If the contract is not performed within that period of limitation and, further, no action is taken by the promisee in a Law Court within that period of limitation, he is deprived of his remedy at Law. For all practical purposes, the contract is terminated. Thus, lapse of time may discharge the contract by barring the right to bring an action to enforce the contract under the Limitation Act.

**Example:** A sells goods to B without any stipulation as to credit. It has to be paid for within three years of the delivery of the goods. If the price is not paid and the creditor does not file a suit against the buyer for its recovery within three years, the debt becomes time-barred.
