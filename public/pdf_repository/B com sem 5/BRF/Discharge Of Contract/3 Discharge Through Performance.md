# Discharge by Performance

This section details discharge by performance, the most common method where parties fulfill their agreed-upon obligations, including actual and attempted performance (tender).

The obvious mode of discharge or dissolution of a contract, as parties originally contemplated at the time of entering into it, is by performance. Since the object of a contract is to see that the expectations of the parties are fulfilled, a contract will be properly discharged only if persons who have undertaken the obligation perform those obligations. This is known as 'discharge by performance'. Parties to a contract must perform or offer to perform their respective promises (Sec. 37).

However, when a party tenders their performance and the other party refuses to accept a valid tender, the party offering the performance is discharged and gains a right of action against the other party who is guilty of breach (Sec. 38).

*Tender* is also called *attempted performance*. It satisfies all the requirements of performance but falls short of complete performance solely because of wrongful refusal on the part of the other contractual party. Thus, a valid tender must possess all the features of a proper performance. Performance of a contract, which is the most usual mode of discharge, may either be:
1.  Actual performance
2.  Attempted performance (i.e., tender)
