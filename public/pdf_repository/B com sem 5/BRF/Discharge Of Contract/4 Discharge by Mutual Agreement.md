# Discharge by Mutual Consent

This section explores how contracts can be discharged through the mutual agreement of the parties, encompassing several specific methods.

The performance of a contract may be discharged by mutual consent or agreement. Sections 62 and 63 of the Indian Contract Act are relevant in this regard.

1.  "If the parties to a contract agree to substitute a new agreement for it, or to rescind or alter it, the original contract need not be performed." (Sec. 62)
    Section 62 provides three different modes:
    *   Novation
    *   Alteration
    *   Rescission

2.  "Every promisee may dispense with or remit, wholly or in part, the performance of the promise made to him, or may extend the time for such performance, or may accept instead of it any satisfaction which he thinks fit." (Sec. 63)
    Section 63 thus provides for another three ways:
    *   Remission
    *   Waiver
    *   Extension

The various cases of discharge of contract by mutual consent are discussed as under:

## Novation

Novation, also known as *novatio*, takes place when a new contract is substituted for an existing one, either between the same parties or between new parties. Thus, in novation, there must be an immediate or present substitution of another contract.

**Example:** A owes money to B under a contract. It is agreed between A, B, and C that B shall henceforth accept C as his debtor instead of A. The old debt of A to B is at an end, and a new debt from C to B has been contracted.

The essential feature of novation is that the right under the original contract is relinquished, and new rights referable to a new contract are created.

### Points to be noted for Novation:

*   It may be between the same parties with new terms, or with new parties and old terms.
*   The consideration for the new contract is the discharge of the old contract.
*   Novation cannot be compulsory; it must be the result of an agreement, i.e., mutual or tripartite (consent of all the parties concerned is necessary).
*   To effect novation, there must be a valid, enforceable substituted new contract.
*   Intention of substitution of a new contract can be gathered from the circumstances of each case.
*   Novation should take place before the breach, i.e., the new agreement must take effect before the expiry of the time of performance of the original contract.
*   If the new agreement is not enforceable for any reason, the old contract is revived and becomes binding.
    **Example:** A agrees to supply rice to B on a particular day. Before that date, A agrees to sell opium instead. Novation does not take place, and the old contract is valid and subsisting (agreement to sell opium is not enforceable).

## Alteration

Alteration means a change in one or more of the terms of a contract by the mutual consent of the parties to the contract.

**Example:** A enters into a contract with B for the supply of a stipulated quantity of timber at a stipulated price and at a given place. A and B mutually agree upon any change in the above terms.

### Points to be noted:

*   In an 'alteration', the original terms are deemed to be part and parcel of the contract with such changes as are mutually agreed upon.
*   In 'alteration', there is a change in terms of the contract but no change of the parties. In 'novation', there may be a change in the parties as well.
*   In 'alteration', the new agreement with the change in terms must be performed, and the original agreement comes to an end.

### Material Alteration:

If the alteration is what is called a *material alteration*, the document becomes void and unenforceable. However, if it is a mere *immaterial alteration*, it does not affect the validity of the instrument. A material alteration is one which changes the legal effect of the instrument or alters its legal character or identity. Whether any benefit accrues to the party making the alteration is an irrelevant consideration. An alteration will be immaterial if it is merely a correction of clerical errors or making explicit what was already expressed, though not completely, in the document.

## Rescission

Rescission means the cancellation of all or some of the terms of the contract. Total rescission is the discharge of the entire contract, while partial rescission is the variation in the original contract by deleting some terms. Rescission may be express or may be inferred from the circumstances or the conduct of the parties to the contract.

Rescission of a contract may occur in any one of the following manners:
1.  By mutual agreement between the parties to a contract (Sec. 62).
2.  Where one party fails in the performance of his obligations, the other party may rescind the contract without prejudice to his right to claim compensation for breach of contract.
    **Example:** A agrees to supply certain goods to B on 15th June and that B shall make the payment on 10th of July. A fails to supply the goods. B may rescind the contract and need not pay the price.
3.  Where a person at whose option a contract is voidable, rescinds it.
    **Example:** A induces B to enter into a contract under 'undue influence'. In this case, the contract is voidable at the option of B. He may rescind the contract.

## Remission

Remission means the acceptance of a lesser amount than what was contracted for in the discharge of the whole debt. This, properly speaking, refers to a state of things prior to the date of performance. Where the promisee, before the time of performance arrives, chooses to dispense with performance, such dispensation will be enough to discharge the promisor.

**Example:** A owes B Rs. 5,000. A pays B, and B accepts, in satisfaction of the whole debt, Rs. 2,000 paid at the time and place at which Rs. 5,000 were payable. The whole debt is discharged.

### Points to be noted:

*   It refers to a state of things prior to the date of performance.
*   Remission, i.e., such a waiver of performance, may be express or implied from conduct which is inconsistent with the continuance of the original right.
*   It may be conditional; in such a case, the promisor is effectively released only on fulfillment of the condition; if the condition is not fulfilled, the creditor may enforce all his rights.
*   A remission once made is irrevocable, and it is not open to the promisee to recover the amount remitted.

### Accord and Satisfaction

This is the English counterpart of the term "remission". Under English law, however, a promisee cannot remit a part of the amount unless the fresh promise is supported by consideration.

Under English law, "accord" means the promise to accept less than what is due under the original contract; "Satisfaction" means the actual payment or the smaller obligation. The accord is the agreement by which the obligation is discharged. The satisfaction is the consideration which makes the agreement operative. A mere accord is not binding or enforceable, but an accord followed by satisfaction is, and it shall discharge the pre-existing obligations.

**Example:** A owes B Rs. 5,000. B agrees to accept Rs. 2,000 in full and final satisfaction of his claim of Rs. 5,000/-. This promise (called accord) is unenforceable in England. But when Rs. 2,000 are actually paid and accepted, there is accord followed by satisfaction. Hence the debt of Rs. 5,000 is discharged.

This doctrine of 'accord & satisfaction' as applied in England has no place in India. Section 63 is very clear on this point. According to the section, the promisee may dispense with or remit wholly or in part, the performance of the promise made to him.

## Waiver

Waiver means the abandonment of a right. A contract may be discharged by agreement between parties to waive their rights arising under the contract. This amounts to mutual abandonment of rights by the parties to the contract. It, thereupon, releases the parties from their contractual obligations. Such a waiver of performance may be expressed or implied from conduct which is inconsistent with the continuance of the original right.

**Example:** A had 1/100th share in one property. This property was put to sale. In spite of notice, he did not press for his right. A is said to have waived his right.

## Extension of Time

When the parties to the contract agree to extend the time of performance, then the parties must perform their part within such extended period of time. In other words, they need not perform the contract as per the original terms of the contract.

**Example:** A promised B to return the money on 1.4.98. However, B extended the time to repay till 1.6.98. Now, A is discharged from payment on 1.4.98 but shall have to pay on 1.6.98.
