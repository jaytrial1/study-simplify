5_Termination of an Offer

This section details the various circumstances under which a proposal (offer) can lapse or be revoked, thereby ending its validity and preventing its acceptance.

# Lapse or Revocation of an Offer

Acceptance must occur before an offer lapses. Section 6 outlines the circumstances under which a proposal is revoked or terminated:

1.  **By communication of notice of revocation:**
    *   An offer is revoked when the proposer communicates the notice of revocation to the other party. This means an offer can be revoked at any time before it is accepted, but not after.
    *   *Example:* A makes the highest bid at an auction. If A withdraws the bid before the fall of the hammer (acceptance), there is no concluded contract.

2.  **By lapse of time:**
    *   **If time is stipulated:** If a specific time for acceptance is mentioned in the offer, the offer is revoked if not accepted within that period.
    *   **If time is not stipulated:** If no time is set, the offer lapses after a 'reasonable time'. What constitutes 'reasonable time' depends on the circumstances of each case.
    *   *Example:* On June 8th, M offered to take shares in R Co. He received a letter of allotment on November 23rd. M refused the shares. Held, M was entitled to refuse as the offer had lapsed due to the unreasonable delay of five months in acceptance. (Ramsgate Victoria Hotel Co. V. Montefiore 1866 L.R.I. Ex. 109.)

3.  **By failure to fulfill a condition precedent:**
    *   An offer is revoked if the acceptor fails to meet a condition that must be fulfilled before acceptance.
    *   *Example:* A agrees to sell his house to B on the condition that B pays the agreed price before a certain date. If B fails to pay by that date, the offer is revoked.

4.  **By death or insanity of the proposer:**
    *   The death or insanity of the offerer terminates the offer, provided the offeree becomes aware of this fact before accepting the offer.
    *   However, if the offeree accepts the offer *without knowledge* of the offerer's death or insanity, the acceptance is valid.
    *   *Note:* Under English law, an offer is revoked instantly upon the offerer's death, without the need for notice. Similarly, if the offeree dies before acceptance, the offer ceases to be operative. (Reynolds V. Atherton (1921) 125 L.T. 690)

In addition to the circumstances mentioned in Section 6, the following also lead to the termination of an offer:

5.  **By Counter-offer:**
    *   An offer is revoked if the offeree makes a *counter-offer*. A counter-offer is a new offer that rejects the original offer.
    *   *Example:* W offered to sell his house to H for £1,000. H offered £950. W refused. Subsequently, H offered to buy for £1,000. Held, no contract existed because H's offer of £950 rejected the original offer. (Hyde v. Wrench (1840) 3 Bcow 334.)

6.  **By rejection:**
    *   Once an offer is rejected, it is terminated and cannot be revived by a subsequent acceptance.
    *   *Example:* A offers to sell his house for Rs. 50,000. B declines. Later, B changes his mind and tenders Rs. 50,000. A is not bound, as B's original rejection terminated the offer. B's subsequent tender is a fresh offer which A can accept or reject.

> **Answer:** A proposal (offer) can be revoked or terminated under the following circumstances:
> 1.  **By notice of revocation:** The proposer communicates their withdrawal of the offer to the offeree before acceptance.
> 2.  **By lapse of time:** If a specific time for acceptance passes, or if no time is specified, a reasonable time passes without acceptance.
> 3.  **By failure to fulfill a condition precedent:** The offeree fails to meet a condition required before acceptance.
> 4.  **By death or insanity of the proposer:** If the offeree becomes aware of the proposer's death or insanity before accepting the offer.
> 5.  **By counter-offer:** The offeree makes a new offer in response, which implicitly rejects the original offer.
> 6.  **By rejection:** The offeree explicitly declines the offer.
