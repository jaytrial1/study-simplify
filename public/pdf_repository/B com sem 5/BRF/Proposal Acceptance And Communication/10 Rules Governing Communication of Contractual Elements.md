10_Rules Governing Communication of Contractual Elements

This section summarizes the legal principles regarding when the communication of an offer, acceptance, and revocation is considered complete, based on Sections 4 and 5 of the Act.

# Rules for Communication of Offer, Acceptance, and Revocation

The principles from Sections 4 and 5 regarding communication are summarized as follows:

## Rule 1: Communication of Offer, When Complete

The communication of an offer is complete when it comes to the knowledge of the person to whom it is made.
*   *Example:* A proposes by letter to sell his house to B. The letter is posted on June 1st at 10:00 a.m. and reaches B on June 3rd at 3:00 p.m. The communication of the offer is complete when B receives the letter, i.e., at 3:00 p.m. on June 3rd.

## Rule 2: Communication of Acceptance, When Complete

*   **a) As against the proposer:**
    The communication of acceptance is complete as against the proposer when it is put into a course of transmission to him, so as to be out of the power of the acceptor.
    *   *Example:* Continuing the above illustration, B accepts A's proposal by a letter sent by post on June 5th. He hands the letter to his peon at 2:00 p.m. for posting. The peon actually posts the letter at 2:30 p.m. The communication of acceptance is complete as against A when the letter is actually posted, i.e., at 2:30 p.m. on June 5th (not at 2:00 p.m., as the letter is out of the acceptor's power only when posted).

*   **b) As against the acceptor:**
    The communication of an acceptance is complete as against the acceptor when it comes to the knowledge of the proposer.
    *   *Example:* In the above illustration, the letter reaches A on June 8th at 11:00 a.m. The communication of acceptance is complete against B when the letter is received by A at 11:00 a.m. on June 8th.

## Rule 3: Communication of Revocation, When Complete

*   **a) As against the person who makes it:**
    Revocation means "taking back" or "cancellation." Offers or acceptances can be revoked. The communication of a revocation is complete as against the person who makes it when it is put into a course of transmission to the person to whom it is made, so as to be out of the power of the person who makes it.
    *   *Example:* Continuing the above illustration, A revokes his offer. A telegram to this effect is dispatched on June 4th at 1:00 p.m. The communication of revocation is complete as against A at 1:00 p.m.

*   **b) As against the person to whom it is made:**
    The communication of revocation is complete as against the person to whom it is made when it comes to his knowledge.
    *   *Example:* In the above instance, the telegram reaches B at 2:15 p.m. on June 5th. The communication of revocation is complete as against B at 2:15 p.m. on June 5th.

## Rule 4: Revocation of a Proposal

A proposal may be revoked at any time before the communication of its acceptance is complete as against the proposer, but not afterwards.
*   *Example:* In the above illustration, if the telegram revoking A's offer reaches B prior to 2:30 p.m. on June 5th (when B's acceptance is complete against A), the proposal can be validly revoked. If the telegram reaches B *after* 2:30 p.m., the revocation is not complete, and A is bound by the contract.

## Rule 5: Revocation of an Acceptance

An acceptance may be revoked at any time before the communication of the acceptance is complete as against the acceptor, but not afterwards.
*   *Example:* In the above illustration, if B wants to revoke his acceptance made to A, he can do so, provided his intimation to A reaches A prior to 11:00 a.m. on June 8th (when B's acceptance is complete against B).

### Unanswered Questions

*   **Q:** Short note: Rules as to Communication.
*   **Q:** Discuss briefly the law relating to communication of offer; acceptance and revocation.
*   **Q:** When can a proposal be revoked?
*   **Q:** When can an acceptance be revoked?

> **Answer:** The rules regarding communication define when an offer, acceptance, or revocation is considered legally complete.
> *   **Offer:** Complete when it comes to the knowledge of the offeree.
> *   **Acceptance (against proposer):** Complete when put into transmission, out of acceptor's power.
> *   **Acceptance (against acceptor):** Complete when it comes to the proposer's knowledge.
> *   **Revocation (against maker):** Complete when put into transmission, out of maker's power.
> *   **Revocation (against recipient):** Complete when it comes to the recipient's knowledge.
>
> A proposal can be revoked at any time before its acceptance is complete as against the proposer. An acceptance can be revoked at any time before its communication is complete as against the acceptor.
