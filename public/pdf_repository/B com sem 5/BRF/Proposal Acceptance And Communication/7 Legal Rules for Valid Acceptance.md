7_Legal Rules for Valid Acceptance

This section details the essential requirements that acceptance must meet to be legally effective, ensuring that it properly concludes the agreement process.

# Legal Rules for Valid Acceptance

For the acceptance of an offer to be legally effective, it must satisfy the following requirements:

1.  **It must be absolute and unqualified:**
    *   Acceptance must be absolute, unqualified, and agree to all terms of the offer. A conditional or qualified acceptance is not a true acceptance; it is a *counter-proposal* that the original offerer is free to accept or reject. (Kundamal Vs. Secretary of State for India, (1939) 14 Luck 710.)
    *   *Example:* M offered land to N at £280. N accepted and enclosed £80, promising to pay the balance in monthly instalments of £50. Held, there was no contract as this was not an unqualified acceptance. (Neale Vs. Merret (1930) W.N. 189)

2.  **It must be expressed in some usual or prescribed manner:**
    *   If the offerer specifies a mode of acceptance, it must be followed. For example, if the offerer says, "Wire Reply," and the reply is sent by post, it's not a valid acceptance.
    *   However, if the offerer fails to object to the non-prescribed mode within a reasonable time after communication, they are deemed to have accepted the acceptance. (Section 7(2))
    *   *Example:* A offers to buy cotton from B at a certain price and asks B to send a telegram if he accepts. B writes a letter accepting. A may insist on a telegram, but if A does not insist, the acceptance is valid.

3.  **It must be by the party named in the offer:**
    *   An offer made to a specific person must be accepted only by that person.
    *   *Example:* X sells his business to Y without informing customers. Z, a customer, sends an order to X by name. Y receives it and sends acceptance. Held, no contract between Y and Z because Z never made an offer to Y. (Boulton Vs. Jones (1857) E.R. 232)
    *   However, if an offer is made generally (e.g., a general offer), any member of the public can accept it.
    *   *Example:* A offers a reward of £5 by advertisement to anyone who returns his lost dog. Any member of the public can accept this offer.

4.  **An acceptance must be communicated to the offerer:**
    *   Just as an offer must be communicated, acceptance must also be communicated to the offerer. The acceptor must do something to signify their intention to accept. A mere mental acceptance, without words or conduct, is not acceptance in the eyes of law.
    *   A unilateral mental act of assent does not equate to communication. For instance, if a bank's resolution to sell land to A remained uncommunicated to A, it was held that no contract existed. (Central Bank of Yavatmal Vs. Vyankalesh (1949) A. Nag. 286).
    *   *Example 1:* A draft agreement for coal supply was sent to a Railway Company Manager. The Manager wrote "approved" and put it in his drawer, intending to send it to the solicitor. It remained in the drawer. Held, no contract, as there was no communication. (Brogden Vs. Metropolitan Rly. Co. (1877) 2. A.C. 666.)
    *   *Example 2:* A high school board resolved to appoint a headmaster. One trustee informed a headmaster personally, but there was no formal communication from the board. The board later rescinded the resolution. Held, no concluded contract due to lack of authorized communication. (Powell Vs. Lee (1908) 29 L.T. 284).
    *   A contract is binding only when the acceptor has *done something* to signify their intention to accept, not merely decided to do so.

    Section 8 states that: "Performance of the conditions of a proposal or the acceptance of any consideration for a reciprocal promise which may be offered with a proposal, is an acceptance of the proposal."
    Thus, acceptance can be inferred from:

    *   **a) Express Conduct:**
        *   **By performance of conditions:**
            *   *Example 1:* Mrs. Carlill used a smoke-ball as advertised, which offered £100 to anyone contracting influenza despite using it. She contracted influenza and claimed the reward. The Company argued she hadn't notified acceptance. Held, her use of the smoke-ball constituted acceptance by conduct, and no formal notice was necessary. (Carlill N. Carbolic Smoke Ball-Co. (1893) I. Q. B. 269)
            *   *Example 2:* A widow promised property to her niece if she stayed with her. The niece stayed until the widow's death. Held, the niece was entitled to the property as she accepted by conduct. (V. Rao v. A Rao (1916) 31 Mad 509 (P.C.))
        *   **By acceptance of consideration:**
            *   *Example:* Tata Bank informed clients of higher interest rates. Gaddermal, aware of this, accepted a loan. He argued he wasn't bound to higher rates as he hadn't formally intimated acceptance. Held, acceptance of money (consideration) after prior information implied acceptance of the higher rates. (Gaddermal v. Tata Industrial Bank AIR 1927 All 407.)
        *   **By accepting a benefit or service:**
            *   *Example:* A bus driver offers to carry passengers to Baroda for Rs. 20. A takes a seat. A accepts the offer by receiving the benefit of service and is bound to pay.

    *   **b) Implied Conduct:**
        *   Law may presume communication of acceptance even if the offerer is not explicitly aware.
        *   **Observance of the conditions of the offer:** This amounts to acceptance.
        *   **Acceptance communicated to an agent:** If an offer was made through an agent, or the agent had authority to receive acceptance, communication to the agent is valid and binding on the principal.
            *   *Example:* If A sends an offer to B via C, and B gives acceptance to C, acceptance is complete even if C forgets or loses it. (Grant v. The Norther Insurance Co. and Henthorm v. Faster (1892) 2 Ch. 27,33.)

    *   **c) Waiver of the communication of acceptance:**
        *   If the offerer waives the requirement of communication, acceptance need not be explicitly communicated, and the proposal becomes a contract without it.

    *   **d) Acceptance by post:**
        *   Communication of acceptance by post is complete *as against the proposer* as soon as the letter of acceptance is posted.

5.  **Acceptance must be within a reasonable time:**
    *   Acceptance must occur while the offer is still valid (before it lapses). If a time limit is prescribed, it must be accepted within that limit. If no time is prescribed, it must be accepted within "a reasonable time," which varies by circumstances.
    *   *Example:* M offered to take shares on June 8th and received acceptance on November 23rd. He refused, and it was held he was entitled to refuse as the offer had lapsed due to the unreasonable delay. (Ramsgate Victoria Hotel Co. v. Montefiore (1886) L.R. 1 Ex. 109.)

6.  **Acceptance cannot be in ignorance of the offer:**
    *   Acceptance cannot precede the offer, nor can acceptance made in complete ignorance of an offer result in a contract.
    *   *Example:* S offered a reward for his lost dog. F returned the dog without knowing of the offer. Held, F was not entitled to the reward, as he was unaware of the offer, so no acceptance occurred. (Faith v. Snedaker (1868) 38 N. Y. 248.)

### Unanswered Questions

*   **Q:** Explain the meaning of proposal and acceptance. Discuss the essential factors of the acceptance.
*   **Q:** What is acceptance? Discuss the essential factors of acceptance.
*   **Q:** What are the essentials of a valid acceptance?
*   **Q:** Explain the essential elements of valid acceptance.
*   **Q:** Explain clearly the meaning of 'acceptance' of an offer. When is communication of acceptance not necessary?

> **Answer:** Acceptance means signifying assent to a proposal. The essential factors of a valid acceptance are:
> 1.  **Absolute and unqualified:** It must agree to all terms without variation.
> 2.  **Expressed in a usual or prescribed manner:** It must follow any specified mode, or a reasonable one.
> 3.  **By the intended party:** Only the offeree or a specific class of persons can accept.
> 4.  **Communicated to the offerer:** It must be brought to the offerer's knowledge through words or conduct.
> 5.  **Within a reasonable time:** It must occur before the offer lapses.
> 6.  **Not in ignorance of the offer:** The acceptor must be aware of the offer.
>
> Communication of acceptance is not necessary when:
> *   The offer itself implies that performance of conditions or acceptance of consideration is sufficient (acceptance by conduct).
> *   The offerer has waived the requirement for explicit communication.
> *   Acceptance is communicated to an authorized agent of the offerer.
