8_Impact of Silence on Acceptance

This section explores whether silence can constitute acceptance, outlining specific instances where silence might imply assent to a proposal.

# Effect of Silence on Acceptance

Generally, an offer will not become an agreement merely because the offeree sends no reply, even if the offer states that silence will be taken as acceptance.

However, in certain instances, silence may indicate assent:

1.  **Benefit derived from goods or services:** Where the offeree has a reasonable opportunity to reject goods or services but derives a benefit from them, their silence may amount to acceptance.
    *   *Example:* A landlord demanded enhanced rent from a tenant. The tenant did not protest and continued to occupy the premises. This conduct tacitly amounted to acceptance of the higher rent. (Kashi Prasad Sajjadi Begam AIR. 1940 Oudh 287.)

2.  **Past dealings imply assent:** If, due to past dealings, the offeree has given the proposer reason to understand that silence was intended as a manifestation of assent, and the proposer genuinely understands it as such, silence may constitute acceptance.

### Unanswered Questions

*   **Q:** Short note: Effect of silence on acceptance.

> **Answer:** Generally, silence does not constitute acceptance, even if the offerer states that it will. However, silence can be indicative of assent in specific situations: (i) where the offeree, having a reasonable opportunity to reject goods or services, derives a benefit from them, and (ii) where past dealings between the parties have established a clear understanding that silence implies acceptance.
