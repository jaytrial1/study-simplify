11_Key Considerations in Contract Communication

This section highlights special points regarding the timing and implications of communication in contracts, particularly focusing on the differences between Indian and English law regarding acceptance revocation.

# Special Points to Note in Contract Communication

1.  **Acceptance binding proposer immediately upon posting:** An acceptance may be held communicated even if the offerer is not yet aware of it. The proposer becomes bound by the contract the moment the letter of acceptance is posted by the offeree.
2.  **Acceptor bound only upon receipt:** The acceptor, however, is bound only when their letter of acceptance has reached the proposer.
3.  **Acceptor's freedom to revoke vs. proposer's binding:** If a letter of acceptance posted by the acceptor is delayed or lost, the contract is complete for the proposer. However, it is not binding against the acceptor until the communication reaches the proposer. In such a scenario, the acceptor has the right to withdraw their acceptance before it reaches the proposer, while the proposer remains bound by it.
4.  **Revocation of acceptance: India vs. England:**
    *   In both India and England, an offer can be revoked.
    *   However, the position differs for the revocation of an acceptance.
    *   **In India:** Acceptance by letter can be revoked by a faster means (e.g., telegram or special messenger) if it reaches the offerer *before* the original letter of acceptance.
    *   **In England:** An acceptance, once posted, cannot be revoked, even by a faster means that reaches earlier. Once put into the course of communication to the offerer, acceptance is final. (Dunlop V. Higgins (1866) H.L. Cas. 381)
