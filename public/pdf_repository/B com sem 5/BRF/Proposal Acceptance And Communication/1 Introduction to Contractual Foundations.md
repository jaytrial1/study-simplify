This section introduces the fundamental concepts of proposal (offer) and acceptance as the building blocks of a contract, illustrating their significance with an example. It also provides a formal definition of proposal.

# Understanding Proposals and Contract Formation

A *proposal*, when accepted, becomes a *promise*. This core idea of *consensus* or *mutual assent* is central to contract formation and is expressed through the concepts of 'offer' and 'acceptance'. Every contract, whether explicit or implied, requires both a proposal and an acceptance.

**Example:** If person A wants to sell a scooter to B for Rs. 10,000 and states, "I want to sell my scooter for Rs. 10,000 to you," A has made an *offer* to B. If B replies, "I agree to buy your scooter at a price stated by you," an agreement is formed, creating legal obligations. This agreement then converts into a *contract*. Failure by either party to fulfill their obligation constitutes a *breach of contract*.

## Definition of Proposal or Offer

An *offer* or *proposal* is the initial step in forming a contract. It requires a lawful offer from one person to another and its unqualified acceptance by the recipient to form an agreement. An offer is a declaration by the *proposer* of their intention to be bound by an obligation if the *offeree* fulfills or undertakes to fulfill certain conditions.

Section 2(a) of the Act defines 'proposal' as:
"When one person signifies to another
1.  his willingness to do or to abstain from doing anything,
2.  with a view to obtaining the assent of that another to such act or abstinence,
3.  he is said to make a proposal."

The term 'proposal' is synonymous with the English word 'offer'. The person making the proposal is called the *proposer* or *offerer*, and the person to whom it is made is called the *offeree*. A proposal expresses willingness or intention, coupled with a request for the other party's acceptance.

## Conditions for a Valid Proposal

For a proposal or offer to be valid, it must meet the following conditions:
*   One person must demonstrate willingness to perform a specific act (e.g., selling a car).
*   Alternatively, they must show willingness to abstain from doing something.
*   This desire must be communicated to a definite person.
*   The intention behind the proposal must be to obtain the other person's consent.

A proposal can be made in these ways:
*   By express spoken words.
*   In writing.
*   By conduct.

**Examples:**
1.  When A asks B, "Will you buy this car for Rs. 20,000?", this is an express oral offer.
2.  When A writes a letter to B stating the same offer, it is an express written offer.
3.  A transport company operating a bus on a specific route makes an implied offer by conduct to passengers.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Proposer/Offerer:** The person who makes the proposal.
*   **Offeree:** The person to whom the proposal is made.
*   **Express Offer:** An offer clearly stated in words (oral or written).
*   **Implied Offer:** An offer conveyed through actions or conduct.
