2_Legal Requirements for a Valid Offer

This section outlines the essential characteristics an offer must possess to be legally binding upon acceptance, derived from established legal principles.

# Legal Rules Governing Offers

For an offer to become a contract upon acceptance, it must adhere to specific legal principles:

1.  **Offer must be capable of creating a legal relationship:**
    *   An offer must be designed to result in a valid contract when accepted. If the proposal does not intend to create legal relationship, it is not an offer in the eyes of law.
    *   *Example:* An invitation to a friend for dinner is not considered a legal offer because it typically lacks the intention to create legal consequences.

2.  **Offer must be definite and certain:**
    *   The terms of an offer must be clear, precise, and unambiguous. If the terms are vague or uncertain, no contract can arise from its acceptance.
    *   *Example:* An offer to sell "some" goods without specifying the quantity or type is not a valid offer.

3.  **Offer must be communicated:**
    *   An offer is effective only when it is communicated to the offeree. The offeree cannot accept an offer they are unaware of.
    *   *Example:* If A sends a letter offering to sell a car to B, the offer is complete only when B receives and reads the letter.

4.  **The offer may be conditional:**
    *   A proposer can make an offer subject to specific conditions, regardless of how stringent or unusual they may seem. If these conditions are properly communicated to the offeree, they become binding.
    *   *Example:* X agreed to buy an automatic machine from Y and signed an order form containing numerous clauses in small print without reading them. The clauses were held to be binding because they were part of the communicated terms.
    *   However, such terms must be presented in a way that a reasonable person could become aware of them *before* entering the contract.
    *   *Example:* A hotel's notice in a room exempting the proprietor from liability for loss of clients' goods is not effective if the client only becomes aware of it after the contract to take the room has been entered into. (Olley v. Malborough Court Ltd. (1949) I.K.B. 532)

5.  **The offer may be general or specific:**
    *   A *specific offer* is made to a definite person or a definite class of persons.
        *   *Example:* A offers to sell his motor car to B. This is a specific offer.
    *   A *general offer* is made to the world at large, not to any definite person or class of persons.
        *   *Example:* A person who loses their diamond ring offers a reward of Rs. 500 by advertisement to anyone who finds and returns it. This is a general offer.
    *   A specific offer can only be accepted by the person(s) it is addressed to, while a general offer can be accepted by any person.

6.  **Offer must be made with a view to obtaining assent:**
    *   An offer must be directed to another person with the intent of gaining their consent and thereby establishing a legal relationship.
    *   If a statement is merely intended to disclose an intention or solicit information, it is not considered a legal offer.

7.  **Offer must not thrust the burden of acceptance:**
    *   An offer cannot include a term stating that non-compliance will be assumed as acceptance.
    *   *Example:* A cannot state that if they do not hear from B within a week, the offer will be considered accepted.
