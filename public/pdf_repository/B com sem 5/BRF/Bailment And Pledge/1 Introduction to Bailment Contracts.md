# Introduction to Bailment Contracts

Contracts of bailment and pledge are specialized agreements governed by *Sections 148 to 181* of the Indian Contract Act. While the Act establishes general principles, specific types of bailments are also addressed by special legislation, such as the Carriers Act, 1865, and the Railways Act, 1890.

A contract of bailment occurs when one person delivers goods to another for a specific purpose, with the understanding that the goods will be returned or disposed of according to the deliverer's instructions once the purpose is fulfilled. Common examples include:
*   Giving gold to a goldsmith for making ornaments.
*   Handing cloth to a tailor for stitching.
*   Entrusting a motor car to a garage for repairs.

The provisions for bailment contracts within *Sections 148 to 181* of the Indian Contract Act can be broadly categorized into four groups:
1.  *Sections 148 to 167* and *Section 181* define bailment and outline the rights and duties of both the bailor and the bailee.
2.  *Sections 168 to 169* contain provisions related to the rights of a finder of goods.
3.  *Sections 170 and 171* address the rights of a bailee regarding special lien and general lien.
4.  *Sections 172 to 179* define pledge and specify the rights of the pawnor and pawnee.

**Q: What is bailment? State its definition.**
> **Answer:** Bailment is the delivery of goods by one person to another for a specific purpose, under a contract that the goods will be returned or disposed of as directed by the person delivering them once the purpose is achieved.

**Q: Define 'Bailment', 'Bailor' and 'Bailee'.**
> **Answer:**
> *   **Bailment:** The delivery of goods by one person to another for some purpose, upon a contract that they shall, when the purpose is accomplished, be returned or otherwise disposed of according to the directions of the person delivering them.
> *   **Bailor:** The person delivering the goods in a contract of bailment.
> *   **Bailee:** The person to whom the goods are delivered in a contract of bailment.
