3_Classification of Bailments

This section categorizes bailments into two main types: gratuitous and non-gratuitous, and provides a comparative analysis of their key differences.

# Classification of Bailments

Bailments can be primarily classified into:

### 1. Gratuitous Bailment
A *gratuitous bailment* is one where neither the *bailor* nor the *bailee* receives any remuneration or reward.
**Examples:**
*   Permitting a friend to use a car.
*   Loaning an article to a person.
*   Delivering goods to another for presentation as a gift (where the deliverer receives no reward).

### 2. Non-Gratuitous Bailment
A *non-gratuitous bailment* (or bailment for reward) is one where either the *bailor* or the *bailee* is entitled to remuneration.
**Examples:**
*   Articles given for repairs for a fee.
*   Clothes given to a laundry for washing on certain charges.
*   Goods given to a carrier for transportation at a price.

## Distinction between Gratuitous and Non-Gratuitous Bailment

| Aspect             | Gratuitous Bailment                                  | Non-Gratuitous Bailment                                     |
| :----------------- | :--------------------------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Consideration   | No consideration is necessary.                       | Consideration is essential.                                 |
| 2. Benefit         | Exclusive benefit either to the bailor or bailee.    | For the mutual benefit of both.                             |
| 3. Unknown Defects | Bailor is not liable for those defects in the goods, not known to him. | Liable for all defects—whether known to the bailor or not. |
| 4. Expenses        | Bailor must repay to bailee, all necessary expenses. | Bailor must repay only extraordinary expenses.              |
| 5. Time Limit      | It can be terminated before the specified time.      | It can be terminated only after the specified time.         |
| 6. Death of Party  | It is terminated by the death of either bailor or bailee. | It is not so terminated.                                    |

**Q: What do you mean by bailment? State the duties of bailmen.**
> **Answer:** Bailment refers to the temporary delivery of movable goods by one person (bailor) to another (bailee) for a specific purpose, with an agreement that the goods will be returned or disposed of as directed once the purpose is fulfilled. The duties of the bailee (assuming "bailmen" refers to bailee) include: taking reasonable care of the goods, not making unauthorized use of the goods, not mixing the goods with their own without consent, returning the goods as per directions, and delivering any increase or profit from the goods to the bailor.
