7_Understanding Lien

This section defines the legal concept of lien, outlines its essential characteristics, and differentiates between its two main types: general lien and particular lien.

# Understanding Lien

### 1. What is Lien?
In *Hammond Vs. Barclay (1801) 102 E.R. 356*, a lien was defined as: "The right of one to retain that which is in his possession, belonging to another till certain demands of him, the person in possession, are satisfied." The word '*retain*' implies prior possession, which must have been lawfully acquired. A lien subsists only as long as possession lasts.

### 2. Characteristics of a Lien
1.  There must be *possession*.
2.  The possession must be *rightful*.
3.  The possession must be *continuous*.

**Example:**
In *Hatton Vs. Car Maintenance Co. Ltd. (1915) 1 Ch. 621*, a company garaged a motor for H for three years, with H entitled to take the car out as and when she liked. The company later tried to detain the car claiming a lien for arrears of annual payment. It was held that H was entitled to take the car away as and when she pleased, and the company had no lien, as continuous possession was not maintained.

### 3. Types of Lien
A lien may be of two types: (a) General lien, and (b) Particular lien.

#### (A) General Lien
*Section 171* states: "Banker, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court, and policy-brokers may, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, retain as a security for a general balance of account, any goods bailed to them; but no other persons have a right to retain, as a security for such balance, goods bailed to them, unless there is an express contract to that effect."

A *general lien* entitles the bailee to retain any goods bailed to him for any amount due to him in respect of those goods or any other goods from the same bailor.

**Q: Who gets right of general lien?**
> **Answer:** The right of general lien is specifically granted by Section 171 of the Indian Contract Act to certain professional classes: bankers, factors, wharfingers, attorneys of a High Court, and policy-brokers. Other persons can only claim a general lien if there is an express contract to that effect.

#### (B) Particular Lien
*Section 170* states: "When the bailee has, in accordance with the purpose of the bailment, rendered any service involving the exercise of labour or skill in respect of the goods bailed, he has, in the absence of a contract to the contrary, a right to retain such goods until he receives due remuneration for the services he has rendered in respect of them."

A *particular lien* enables a bailee to detain the specific goods in respect of which charges are due for labor or skill expended on them.

**Example:**
A delivers a rough diamond to B, a jeweller, to be cut and polished. B is entitled to retain the stone until he is paid for the services he has rendered.

#### Limitations of Particular Lien:
The right of particular lien is subject to the following limitations:
*   The bailee must have acted in accordance with the purpose of the bailment.
    *   **Example:** A bailee who undertook repairs to a musical instrument for a fixed sum could not claim a lien for other repair work done to the instrument, as that work was not part of the original bailment purpose.
*   The service rendered by the bailee must involve the exercise of labor or skill in respect of the goods bailed.
*   It cannot be exercised until the services have been performed in full.
*   There must be no contract to the contrary.
*   If possession is lost for any reason, the lien is also lost.
*   The right is only to *retain* the particular goods; it does not extend to a right to sell the goods.

#### Other Particular Liens:
In India, various statutes provide for particular liens, including:
1.  Lien of a finder of goods (Section 168).
2.  Bailee's lien (Section 170).
3.  Pawnee's lien (Section 173).
4.  Agent's lien (Section 221).
5.  Unpaid seller's lien (Section 47, Sale of Goods Act).
6.  A partner's lien (Section 52 of the Partnership Act).

### 4. Loss of Lien
A lien is extinguished in the following ways:
1.  By abandonment or waiver.
2.  By payment or tender of the amount due.
3.  By loss or surrender of possession of the goods.
4.  By agreement of the parties.
5.  By misuse of the goods, such as selling or parting with them to a third party by the bailee.

**Q: How is a lien lost? (Short note).**
> **Answer:** A lien, which is the right to retain possession of goods until a debt is paid, can be lost in several ways. It is extinguished if the person holding the lien (lien holder) voluntarily gives up possession of the goods, if the debt or demand for which the lien was claimed is paid or tendered, if the lien holder waives their right to the lien, or if there is an agreement between the parties to terminate the lien. Additionally, misusing the goods by selling or transferring them to a third party can also result in the loss of lien.

**Q: Define lien. State the difference between general lien and particular lien.**
> **Answer:**
> **Lien:** A lien is a legal right to retain possession of another person's property until a debt or obligation owed by that person is satisfied. The possession must be lawful and continuous.
