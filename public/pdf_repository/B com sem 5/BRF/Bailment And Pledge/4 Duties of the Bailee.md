4_Duties of the Bailee

This section details the responsibilities and obligations that a bailee must fulfill regarding the goods entrusted to their care under a contract of bailment.

# Duties of the Bailee

### 1. Duty of Taking Care
As per *Section 151*, "In all cases of bailment the bailee is bound to take as much care of the goods bailed to him as a man of ordinary prudence would, under similar circumstances, take of his own goods of the same bulk, quality and value as the goods bailed."

*Section 152* further clarifies: "The bailee, in the absence of any special contract, is not responsible for the loss, destruction or deterioration of the thing bailed, if he has taken the amount of care of it described in Section 151."

**Example:**
In *Union of India v. Hafir Bashin Ahmed (1987) Supp. Sec. 174*, goods booked for delivery were kept at a port jetty instead of a railway godown after reaching the destination. When they were destroyed by fire, the railway authorities were held responsible for the loss because they failed to exercise the required standard of care.

### 2. Compensation for Unauthorized Use of Goods
*Section 154* states: "If the bailee makes any use of the goods bailed, which is not according to the conditions of the bailment, he is liable to make compensation to the bailor for any damage arising to the goods from or during such use of them."

**Example:**
A lends a horse to B for B's own riding only. B allows C, a member of his family, to ride the horse. C rides carefully, but the horse accidentally falls and is injured. B is liable to compensate A for the injury to the horse because the use was unauthorized.

### 3. Mixture of Bailor's Goods with Bailee's
The bailee must not mix the *bailor's* goods with their own. The rules regarding such mixtures are:

#### (i) Mixture with Bailor's Consent (Section 155)
"If the bailee with the consent of the bailor, mixes the goods of the bailor with his own goods, the bailor and the bailee, shall have an interest, in proportion to their respective shares, in the mixture thus produced."

#### (ii) Mixture Without Bailor's Consent, when Goods Can be Separated (Section 156)
"If the bailee, without the consent of his bailor, mixes the goods of the bailor with his own goods and the goods can be separated or divided, the property in the goods remains in the parties respectively; but the bailee is bound to bear the expenses of separation or division, and any damage arising from the mixture."

**Example:**
A bails 100 bales of cotton marked with a particular mark to B. B, without A's consent, mixes the 100 bales with other bales of his own bearing a different mark. A is entitled to have his 100 bales returned, and B is bound to bear all the expenses incurred in the separation of the bales, and any other incidental damage.

#### (iii) Mixture Without Bailor's Consent, when Goods Cannot be Separated (Section 157)
"If the bailee, without the consent of the bailor mixes the goods of the bailor with his own goods, in such a manner that it is impossible to separate the goods bailed from the other goods and deliver them back, the bailor is entitled to be compensated by the bailee for the loss of the goods."

**Example:**
A bails a barrel of Cape flour worth Rs. 45 to B. Without A's consent, B mixes the flour with country flour of his own, worth only Rs. 25 a barrel. B must compensate A for the loss of A's flour.

**Q: Effect of mixture of the goods by the bailee of the goods bailed with his own goods. (Short note).**
> **Answer:** When a bailee mixes the bailor's goods with their own, the legal consequences depend on whether the bailor consented and whether the goods can be separated. If mixed with consent, both parties have a proportional interest in the mixture. If mixed without consent but separable, the bailee must bear the expenses of separation and any damages. If mixed without consent and inseparable, the bailee must compensate the bailor for the loss of their goods.

### 4. Duty of Returning Goods
*Section 160* states: "It is duty of the bailee to return, or deliver according to the bailor's direction, the goods bailed, without demand as soon as the time for which they were bailed has expired or the purpose for which they were bailed has been accomplished."

#### (i) Effect when not Returned (Section 161)
"If, by the default of the bailee, the goods are not returned, delivered or tendered at the time, he is responsible to the bailor for any loss, destruction or deterioration of the goods from that time."

**Example:**
In *Bontex Knitting Works Ltd. Vs. St. John Garage (1944) 2 All, E.R. 690*, a carrier's driver left a van unattended, leading to the theft of goods. The carrier was held liable for damages because the driver's departure constituted a fundamental breach of the contract to deliver the goods, leading to their loss.

#### (ii) Restoration of Goods Lent Gratuitously (Section 159)
"A gratuitous bailment can be terminated at any time but if premature termination causes any loss to the bailee, the bailor must indemnify the bailee."

#### (iii) Rules Regarding 'Return of Goods'
*   **Duty of Return:** The obligation is to return "without demand" (Section 160).
*   **Failure to Return:** The bailee becomes responsible for loss, destruction, or deterioration of the article from the date fixed for return (Section 161).
*   **Increase or Profit:** The bailee is bound to deliver any increase or profit accrued from the goods (Section 163).
*   **Bailment by Joint Owners:** If several joint owners bail goods, the bailee may deliver them back to, or according to the direction of, one joint owner without the consent of all, unless there is an agreement to the contrary (Section 165).
*   **Bailee Not Responsible on Re-delivery to Bailor Without Title:** If the bailor has no title to the goods, and the bailee in good faith, delivers them back to, or according to the directions of the bailor, the bailee is not responsible to the true owner in respect of such delivery (Section 166).

**Example:**
In *Bank of Bombay v. Fazulbhoy (1923) 24 Bons, 4.0.5*, an executor pledged government bonds (bequeathed to the plaintiff) with a bank. When the bank delivered the bonds in good faith to a third person as per the executor's directions, the bank was held not liable to the plaintiff.

### 5. Accretion to the Goods Bailed
*Section 163* states: "In the absence of any contract to the contrary, the bailee is bound to deliver to the bailor, or according to his directions, any increase or profit which may have accrued from the goods bailed."

**Example:**
A leaves a cow in the custody of B to be taken care of. The cow has a calf. B is bound to deliver the calf as well as the cow to A.

### 6. Not to Set Up an Adverse Title
As a general rule, a bailee is *estopped* from denying the *bailor's* title or asserting their own title or the right of a third party against the *bailor*.
