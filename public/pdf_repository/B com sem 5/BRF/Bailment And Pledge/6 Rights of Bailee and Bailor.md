6_Rights of Bailee and Bailor

This section enumerates the various rights that accrue to both the bailee and the bailor within a contract of bailment, stemming from the duties and obligations of the other party.

# Rights of Bailee and Bailor

## Bailee's Rights

### 1. Enforcement of Bailor's Duties
The liabilities or duties of the *bailor* directly correspond to the *bailee's* rights, which the bailee can enforce through legal action.

### 2. Bailment by Several Joint Owners
*Section 165* states: "If several joint owners of goods bail them, the bailee may deliver them back to, or according to the directions of, one joint owner without the consent of all, in the absence of any agreement to the contrary."

### 3. Delivery of Goods to Bailor Without Title
*Section 166* states: "If the bailor has no title to the goods, and the bailee in good faith, delivers them back to, or according to the directions of, the bailor, the bailee is not responsible to the owner in respect of such delivery."

### 4. Bailee's Lien
The bailee has a right to retain the goods until they receive due remuneration for services rendered in respect of them. This is primarily a *particular lien* as per Section 170.

**Q: What are bailee's rights?**
> **Answer:** A bailee's rights include:
> *   The right to enforce the duties of the bailor.
> *   The right to deliver goods back to any one of several joint owners unless otherwise agreed.
> *   The right not to be held responsible to the true owner if they deliver goods back to a bailor without title, acting in good faith.
> *   The right of lien over the goods for services rendered and remuneration due (particular lien) or for a general balance of account (general lien, for specific categories of bailees).

**Q: Discuss the rights at Bailee.**
> **Answer:** The bailee possesses several key rights:
> *   **Right to remuneration and expenses:** In non-gratuitous bailments, the bailee has the right to receive agreed-upon remuneration. In gratuitous bailments, they have the right to be reimbursed for extraordinary expenses.
> *   **Right of lien:** The bailee can retain possession of the bailed goods until their lawful charges for labor or skill expended on the goods are paid (particular lien), or, for certain bailees like bankers, a general balance of account (general lien).
> *   **Right to compensation for undisclosed faults:** If the bailor fails to disclose known faults that cause damage, the bailee has a right to compensation.
> *   **Right to indemnity:** The bailee can seek indemnity from the bailor for any loss incurred due to the bailor's lack of title or authority.
> *   **Right to deliver to joint owners:** If goods are bailed by joint owners, the bailee can return them to any one of them unless there's a contrary agreement.
> *   **Protection against true owner:** If the bailee delivers goods back to the bailor in good faith, even if the bailor had no title, the bailee is not responsible to the true owner.

## Bailor's Rights

### 1. Enforcement of Bailee's Duties
The liabilities or duties of the *bailee* directly correspond to the *bailor's* rights, which the bailor can enforce through legal action.

### 2. Act Inconsistent with Conditions
*Section 153* states: "A contract of bailment is voidable at the option of the bailor, if the bailee does any act with regard to the goods bailed, inconsistent with the conditions of the bailment."

**Example:**
A lets to B for hire a horse for B's own riding only. B drives the horse in his carriage. This is, at the option of A, a termination of the bailment (Section 153).

### 3. Restoration of Goods Lent Gratuitously
*Section 159* states: "The lender of a thing for use may at any time require its return, if the loan was gratuitous, even though he lent it for a specified time or purpose. But if, on the faith of such loan made for a specified time or purpose, the borrower has acted in such a manner that the return of the thing lent before the time agreed upon would cause him loss exceeding the benefit actually derived by him from the loan, the lender must, if he compels the return, indemnify the borrower for the amount in which the loss so occasioned exceeds the benefit so derived."

**Q: What are bailor's rights?**
> **Answer:** A bailor's rights include:
> *   The right to enforce the duties of the bailee (e.g., duty of care, duty to return goods, duty not to make unauthorized use).
> *   The right to terminate the bailment if the bailee uses the goods inconsistently with the bailment conditions.
> *   The right to demand the return of goods in a gratuitous bailment at any time, subject to indemnifying the bailee for any loss caused by premature return.
> *   The right to receive any increase or profit that may have accrued from the bailed goods.
> *   The right to receive compensation for any damage to goods due to the bailee's negligence or unauthorized acts.

**Q: Discuss the rights and duties of bailor.**
> **Answer:**
> **Rights of the Bailor:**
> *   To demand return of goods: The bailor has the right to get the goods back once the purpose of bailment is achieved or the time period expires. In gratuitous bailment, this right can be exercised at any time, with an obligation to indemnify the bailee for premature return losses.
> *   To terminate bailment for inconsistent use: If the bailee uses the goods in a manner inconsistent with the terms of bailment, the bailor has the right to terminate the contract.
> *   To claim damages: The bailor can claim damages for any loss or deterioration of goods due to the bailee's negligence or unauthorized acts.
> *   To receive any accretion/profit: Any increase or profit accruing from the bailed goods belongs to the bailor.
> *   To enforce bailee's duties: All duties of the bailee are corresponding rights of the bailor.
>
> **Duties of the Bailor:**
> *   To disclose faults: The bailor must disclose any known faults in the goods that could interfere with their use or expose the bailee to extraordinary risks. In bailments for hire, this duty extends to unknown faults as well.
> *   To bear extraordinary expenses: For gratuitous bailments, the bailor must repay the bailee all necessary and extraordinary expenses incurred for the purpose of the bailment.
> *   To indemnify the bailee: The bailor must compensate the bailee for any loss incurred by the bailee if the bailor was not entitled to make the bailment, receive back the goods, or give directions regarding them.
