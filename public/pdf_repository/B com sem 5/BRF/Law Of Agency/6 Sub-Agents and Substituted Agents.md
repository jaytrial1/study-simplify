# Sub-Agents and Substituted Agents

The general rule in agency is that an agent cannot delegate their authority to another person. However, there are specific exceptions and distinctions regarding sub-agents and substituted agents.

*   **Q:** Explain 'sub-agent' and 'substituted-agent'. Discuss the position when sub-agent is properly appointed and not properly appointed?

> **Answer:** A *sub-agent* (Sec. 191) is a person employed by, and acting under the control of, the original agent in the business of the agency. The rule is that an agent cannot lawfully employ a sub-agent unless custom allows it or the nature of the agency requires it.
> *   **Properly Appointed Sub-agent:** If properly appointed, the principal is represented by the sub-agent to third persons and is bound by and responsible for their acts as if the sub-agent were originally appointed by the principal. The original agent is responsible to the principal for the sub-agent's acts. The sub-agent is responsible to the agent, but not to the principal, except in cases of fraud or willful wrong (Sec. 192).
> *   **Not Properly Appointed Sub-agent:** If an agent appoints a sub-agent without authority, the agent stands in the relation of principal and agent to the sub-agent and is responsible for their acts to both the principal and third persons. In this case, the principal is not represented by or responsible for the sub-agent's acts, nor is the sub-agent responsible to the principal (Sec. 193).
>
> A *substituted agent* (Sec. 194), or co-agent, is a person named by the original agent (who has express or implied authority to do so) to act for the principal in a part of the agency's business. Such a person is an agent of the principal, not a sub-agent. The original agent is not responsible for the acts or negligence of a properly selected substituted agent (Sec. 195).

## Rule: Delegation of Authority

The general rule, expressed by the maxim *Delegatus non potest delegare* ("a delegate cannot further delegate"), is that an agent who has a bare power from the principal must act personally and cannot delegate their authority without the principal's consent. (Sec. 190)

"An agent cannot lawfully employ another to perform acts which he has expressly or impliedly undertaken to perform personally, unless by the ordinary custom of trade a sub-agent may, or from the nature of the agency, a sub-agent must be employed". (Sec. 190)

## Exceptions

According to Section 190, an agent can delegate powers to a sub-agent if:
1.  By the ordinary custom of the trade, a sub-agent may be employed.
2.  From the nature of the agency, a sub-agent must be employed.

**Example:** A banker authorized to let out a house and collect rent may entrust the work to an estate agent.

Other exceptions include:
*   Where the principal is aware of the agent's intention to delegate.
*   Where there is an emergency or unforeseen circumstances.
*   Where the nature of the authority requires it.
*   Where the act is purely ministerial.
*   Where the agent's power to delegate can be inferred.
*   Where the principal permits the appointment of a sub-agent.

## Sub-Agent: Definition

"A 'sub-agent' is a person employed by, and acting under the control of, the original agent in the business of the agency." (Sec. 191)

### Where a Sub-Agent is Properly Appointed

"Where a sub-agent is properly appointed, the principal is, so far as regards third persons, represented by the sub-agent as is bound by and responsible for acts, as if he were an agent originally appointed by the principal." (Sec. 192)
*   The original agent is responsible to the principal for the acts of the sub-agent.
*   The sub-agent is responsible for their acts to the original agent, but not directly to the principal, except in cases of fraud or willful wrong.

### Where a Sub-Agent is Not Properly Appointed

"Where an agent, without having authority to do so, has appointed a person to act as a sub-agent stands towards such person in the relation of a principal and agent, and is responsible for his acts both to the principal and to third person; the principal is not represented by or responsible for the acts of the person so employed, nor is that person responsible to the principal." (Sec. 193)

## Substituted Agent or Co-Agent: Definition

"Where an agent, holding an express or implied authority to name another person to act for the principal in the business of the agency has named another person accordingly, such person is not a sub-agent, but an agent of the principal for such part of the business of the agency as is entrusted to him." (Sec. 194)

**Example:** A directs B, his solicitor, to sell his estate by auction and to employ an auctioneer for the purpose. B names C, an auctioneer, to conduct the sale. C is not a sub-agent but is A's agent for the conduct of the sale.

### Agent's Duty in Naming Such Person

"In selecting such agent for his principal, an agent is bound to exercise the same amount of discretion as a man of ordinary prudence would exercise in his own case; and if he does this he is not responsible to the principal for the acts or negligence of the agent so selected" (Sec. 195).

**Example:** A instructs B, a merchant, to buy a ship for him. B employs a ship surveyor of good reputation to choose a ship for A. The surveyor makes the choice negligently, and the ship turns out to be unseaworthy and is lost. B is not responsible, but the surveyor is responsible to A.

An agent is not concerned with the efficiency of the substitute.
