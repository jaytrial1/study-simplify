# Irrevocable Agency

In certain specific instances, an agency cannot be terminated or brought to an end; this is known as an *irrevocable agency*.

*   **Q:** S. Note: (1) Irrevocable Agency (2) Agency coupled with interest.

> **Answer:** An *irrevocable agency* is one that cannot be terminated by the principal. This typically occurs in three main instances:
> 1.  **Agency Coupled with Interest:** When the agent has a personal interest in the property that forms the subject matter of the agency. The agency cannot be terminated to the prejudice of this interest, unless there is an express contract allowing termination (Sec. 202). This type of agency is created to protect the agent's interest, not merely for remuneration.
> 2.  **Agent Incurred Personal Liability:** When the agent has personally incurred liability in the performance of the agency, the principal cannot revoke the authority to the prejudice of that liability (Sec. 203).
> 3.  **Authority Partly Exercised:** When the agent has already partly exercised the authority given, the principal cannot revoke the authority regarding those acts and obligations that have already arisen from the acts performed (Sec. 204).

The agency is irrevocable in the following instances:

## Where the Agency is Coupled with Interest

"Where the agent has himself an interest in the property which forms the subject-matter of the agency cannot, in the absence of an express contract, be terminated to the prejudice of such interest." (Sec. 202).

**Example:** A gives authority to B to sell A's land and to pay himself out of the proceeds the debts due to him from A. A cannot revoke this authority, nor can it be terminated by his insanity or death.

This applies only if the agency is created for the protection of the agent's interest, not merely for prospect of remuneration or for paying salary from managed property rents.

## When the Agent Has Incurred a Personal Liability

"The principal may, save as is otherwise provided by the last preceding section, revoke the authority given to his agent at any time before the authority has been exercised so as to bind the principal." (S.203). This implies that if the agent has incurred a personal liability, the principal cannot revoke the authority to the prejudice of that liability.

## Where the Agent Has Partly Exercised the Authority

On the same principle, if the authority has been partly exercised by the agent, it becomes irrevocable.
"The principal cannot revoke the authority given to his agent after the authority has been partly exercised, so far as regards such acts and obligation as arise from acts already done in the agency" (S. 204)

**Example:** A authorized B to buy 1000 bales of cotton on account of A, and to pay for it out of A's money remaining in B's hands. B buys 1000 bales of cotton in A's name, rendering himself personally liable for the price. A cannot revoke B's authority to pay for the cotton.
