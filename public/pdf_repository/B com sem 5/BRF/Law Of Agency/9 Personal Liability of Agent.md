# Personal Liability of Agent

Generally, an agent cannot personally enforce contracts made on behalf of their principal, nor are they personally bound by them. However, there are specific circumstances where an agent can incur personal liability.

*   **Q:** Point out those circumstances where an agent can be made personally liable in respect of contracts entered into by him on behalf of the principal.
*   **Q:** When 'Agent' is personally liable?
*   **Q:** In which circumstance Agent is responsible personally? Discuss.
*   **Q:** When agent is personally liable? Discuss.

> **Answer:** An agent can be made personally liable for contracts entered into on behalf of the principal in the following circumstances (Sec. 230):
> 1.  **Foreign Principal:** When the agent acts for a merchant residing abroad, as a foreign principal cannot typically sue or be sued directly.
> 2.  **Undisclosed Principal:** When the agent acts for a principal whose existence or name is not disclosed to the third party.
> 3.  **Principal cannot be sued:** If the principal is incompetent to contract (e.g., a minor) or cannot be sued (e.g., a foreign sovereign).
> 4.  **Express Contract:** When the contract expressly provides for the agent's personal liability.
> 5.  **Principal not in existence:** When the agent acts for a principal that does not yet exist (e.g., promoters for a company yet to be incorporated).
> 6.  **Agent signs in own name:** If the agent signs a contract in their own name without qualification (e.g., not stating "for and on behalf of"), implying personal responsibility.
> 7.  **Acts beyond authority:** If the agent exceeds their authority and the principal does not ratify the acts (Secs. 227, 228).
> 8.  **Misrepresentation or fraud:** If the agent makes misrepresentations or commits fraud in the course of the principal's business (Sec. 238).
> 9.  **Trade usage or custom:** If trade usage or custom dictates personal liability for the agent, unless a contract states otherwise.
> 10. **Authority coupled with interest:** If the agent has a personal interest in the subject matter of the agency, they may sue in their own name.

In the absence of any contract to that effect, an agent cannot personally enforce contracts entered into by him on behalf of his principal, nor is he personally bound by them. Such a contract shall be presumed to exist in the following cases (Sec. 230):
1.  Where the contract is made by an agent for sale or purchase of goods for a merchant resident abroad.
2.  Where the agent does not disclose the name of his principal.
3.  Where the principal, though disclosed, cannot be sued.

An agent is personally responsible in the following cases:
1.  **When the agent acts for a foreign principal:** A foreign principal typically cannot sue or be sued. This presumption can be rebutted if the foreign principal is made the actual contracting party directly in their name.
2.  **When the agent acts for an undisclosed principal:** The agent is personally liable, although the principal, once discovered, is also liable.
3.  **When principal cannot be sued:** If the principal is incompetent to contract (e.g., a minor) or cannot be sued (e.g., a foreign sovereign), the agent is personally liable as credit is presumed to be given to the agent.
4.  **When the contract expressly provides:** If the agent agrees to be personally responsible.
5.  **When the agent acts for a principal not in existence:** For example, promoters entering contracts for a company yet to be incorporated.
6.  **When the agent signs a contract in his own name:** If an agent signs a contract in their own name without qualifying words like "For and on behalf of," they are taken to contract personally, even if known to be an agent.
7.  **When the agent acts beyond his authority:** If the agent exceeds their authority and the principal does not ratify the acts, the agent is personally responsible to third parties. (Secs. 227 and 228)
8.  **Where there is a misrepresentation or fraud by agent:** An agent is personally responsible if they make misrepresentations or commit fraud while acting in the course of the principal's business. (Sec. 238)
9.  **Where the trade, usage or custom makes the agent personally liable:** If trade usage or custom dictates personal liability, the agent is liable unless there's a contrary contract.
10. **Where authority is coupled with interest:** An agent has the standing to sue in their own name if they have an interest in the subject matter of the agency.

### Right of Person Dealing with Agent Personally Liable (Creditor's Election)

In cases where the agent is personally liable, a person dealing with them may hold either the agent, or their principal, or both of them, liable. (Sec. 233)
