# Exercises and Key Concepts

This section provides a set of questions derived from the chapter content, along with their answers based solely on the provided text. It also highlights key concepts discussed throughout the chapter.

## Key Concepts

*   **Agent:** A person employed to do any act for another or to represent another in dealings with third persons; a connecting link. Any person can become an agent.
*   **Principal:** The person for whom an act is done by an agent or who is represented by an agent.
*   **Basis of Agency:**
    1.  What a person can do personally, they can do through an agent.
    2.  An act of the agent is the act of the principal.
*   **Ratification:** Affirmation of unauthorized acts of an agent by the principal, making them retroactively valid.
    *   **Ingredients for valid ratification:** Agent must act as agent; for an existing principal with contractual capacity; with full knowledge of facts; the act must be lawful and within reasonable time; the whole act must be ratified and communicated; act must be within principal's power; should not harm third parties.
*   **Agent's Authority:**
    *   **Actual Authority:** Express or implied delegation.
    *   **Ostensible (Apparent) Authority:** Authority as it appears to others due to principal's conduct.
    *   **Authority in Emergency:** Agent's power to act to protect principal from loss in urgent situations.
    *   **When Authority Exceeded:** If separable, authorized part binds; if not separable, principal is not bound.
*   **Sub-Agent:** Employed by and under the control of the original agent. If properly appointed, principal is bound by sub-agent's acts; original agent responsible to principal for sub-agent's acts.
*   **Substituted Agent:** Named by the original agent (with principal's authority) to act directly for the principal. Responsible to the principal, not the original agent.
*   **Duties of Agent:** Conduct business as directed, with skill/diligence, render accounts, communicate in difficulty, not deal on own account, ensure principal benefits, pay sums received, protect principal's interests on death/insolvency, not make secret profits.
*   **Rights of Agent:** Right to retainer, remuneration, lien, indemnification for lawful acts, indemnification for good faith acts causing third-party injury, compensation for principal's neglect.
*   **Duties of Principal:** Indemnify agent for lawful acts, indemnify agent for good faith acts causing third-party injury, not liable for agent's criminal acts, compensate agent for principal's neglect.
*   **Rights of Principal:** Compensation for agent's breach of duty, accounting for secret profits, resist agent's claims if agent acted as principal, revocation of agency.
*   **Agent's Personal Liability:** Arises in cases of foreign principal, undisclosed principal, principal cannot be sued, express contract, non-existent principal, agent signing in own name, acting beyond authority, misrepresentation/fraud, trade custom, or authority coupled with interest.
*   **Termination of Agency:**
    *   **By Act of Parties:** Agreement, revocation by principal (with limitations), renunciation by agent.
    *   **By Operation of Law:** Performance, efflux of time, death/insanity of principal or agent, principal's insolvency, destruction of subject-matter, principal becoming alien enemy, company dissolution, sub-agent's authority termination.
*   **Irrevocable Agency:** Cannot be terminated. Instances include agency coupled with interest, when agent has incurred personal liability, or when agent has partly exercised authority.

## Exercises

**Q1:** What is a contract of agency? Define and explain the terms 'principal' and 'agent.' What are the essentials of agency?
> **Answer:** A contract of agency is an agreement where one person (the agent) is employed to do any act for another (the principal) or to represent another in dealings with third persons. The 'principal' is the person for whom such act is done or who is so represented. The 'agent' is the person employed to do the act or represent the principal. The essentials of agency are an agreement between the principal and the agent, and the agent's intention to act on behalf of the principal.

**Q2:** (a) State who are, under the law, competent to employ as agent. Who are under the law, competent to become agents? (b) State types of agents. (c) Is consideration essential for the creation of a contract of agency?
> **Answer:**
> (a) Any person who is of the age of majority and of sound mind may employ an agent (Sec. 183). As between the principal and third persons, any person may become an agent; however, a person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind cannot become an agent so as to be responsible to their principal (Sec. 184).
> (b) Types of agents include General Agent, Particular Agent, Universal Agent, Mercantile Agent (including Factor and Broker), Commission Agent, Auctioneer, and Del Credere Agent.
> (c) No, consideration is not necessary to create an agency (Sec. 185).

**Q3:** (i) What are the different modes in which an agency may be created? (ii) Explain what is meant by "Agency by Ratification" Discuss fully.
> **Answer:**
> (i) An agency may be created by: (1) Express agreement, (2) Implied agreement, (3) Estoppel or holding out, (4) Necessity, and (5) Ratification.
> (ii) "Agency by Ratification" means that when an agent performs an act on behalf of a principal without prior authority, the principal may subsequently choose to confirm and approve that unauthorized act. If ratified, the act becomes as legally binding as if it had been authorized from the beginning. This principle is summarized as "ratification tantamounts to prior authority" or "ratification relates back" to the date of the original act. For a valid ratification, the agent must have purported to act as an agent for an existing principal with contractual capacity; the principal must have full knowledge of the material facts; the act must be lawful and valid; ratification must occur within a reasonable time and cover the whole transaction; it must be communicated; the act must be within the principal's power; and it should not subject a third party to damages or terminate their rights.

**Q4:** (a) What is ratification? State the essential requisites of a valid ratification. (b) Define Agency. Write explanatory note on agency by ratification.
> **Answer:**
> (a) Ratification is the affirmation by a principal of an unauthorized act done on their behalf by an agent, giving the act the same effect as if it had been previously authorized. The essential requisites for a valid ratification are: the agent must expressly contract as agent for an existing principal with contractual capacity; the principal must have full knowledge of facts; the act must be lawful, not void or illegal; ratification must be done within a reasonable time; the whole act must be ratified; ratification must be communicated; the act must be one the principal had the power to do; and it should not put a third party to damages.
> (b) Agency is the employment of one person (the agent) by another (the principal) to bring the latter into legal relations with a third person. Agency by ratification is a mode of creating agency where a principal retrospectively approves an act performed by an agent without initial authority, thereby making the act binding as if it had been authorized from the outset. This principle is crucial as it allows a principal to accept the benefits or consequences of an agent's unauthorized actions, provided all conditions for valid ratification are met.

**Q5:** State types of agent. When agency can be terminated?
> **Answer:** Types of agents include General Agent, Particular Agent, Universal Agent, Mercantile Agent (Factor, Broker), Commission Agent, Auctioneer, and Del Credere Agent. An agency can be terminated by: (1) Act of the parties (agreement, revocation by principal, renunciation by agent) or (2) Operation of law (performance, efflux of time, death/insanity of principal or agent, principal's insolvency, destruction of subject-matter, principal becoming alien enemy, dissolution of company, or termination of sub-agent's authority).

**Q6:** Discuss the nature and extent of the authority of an agent.
> **Answer:** The nature and extent of an agent's authority can be actual (express or implied), ostensible (apparent), or authority in an emergency. Actual authority is what is truly delegated. Ostensible authority is what the agent appears to have to third parties based on the principal's conduct. Authority in emergency allows an agent to act prudently to protect the principal from loss in urgent situations. The agent's acts within the scope of this authority bind the principal. If an agent exceeds authority, the principal is bound only by the authorized separable part; if inseparable, the principal is not bound. Notice to the agent in the course of business is considered notice to the principal.

**Q7:** (a) State the distinction between a sub-agent and a substituted agent. (b) Discuss the rights and liabilities flowing from such appointment.
> **Answer:**
> (a) A **sub-agent** is employed by and acts under the control of the original agent (Sec. 191). A **substituted agent** is named by the original agent (with authority to do so) to act directly for the principal, not under the original agent's control (Sec. 194). Key distinctions include who appoints them (agent vs. principal via agent), who controls them (agent vs. principal), privity of contract (with agent vs. with principal), and answerability (to agent vs. to principal).
> (b) **For a properly appointed sub-agent:** The principal is bound by and responsible for the sub-agent's acts to third persons as if they were originally appointed by the principal. The original agent is responsible to the principal for the sub-agent's acts. The sub-agent is responsible to the original agent, but not to the principal (except for fraud or willful wrong) (Sec. 192).
> **For a sub-agent not properly appointed:** The original agent acts as a principal to the sub-agent and is responsible for their acts to both the principal and third persons. The principal is not represented by or responsible for the sub-agent's acts, nor is the sub-agent responsible to the principal (Sec. 193).
> **For a substituted agent:** The original agent is bound to exercise ordinary prudence in selecting the substituted agent. If they do so, they are not responsible to the principal for the acts or negligence of the substituted agent (Sec. 195). The substituted agent is directly responsible to the principal.

**Q8:** Explain how an Agency can be terminated by the act of the parties and by operation of law.
> **Answer:** An agency can be terminated by the **act of the parties** through: (1) Mutual agreement between the principal and agent. (2) Revocation of authority by the principal, which can be done at any time before the agent's authority has been exercised to bind the principal (Sec. 203), though not for acts already done (Sec. 204), and requires reasonable notice (Sec. 206). (3) Renunciation of the business of the agency by the agent, also requiring reasonable notice.
> An agency can be terminated by **operation of law** due to: (1) Performance of the contract or completion of the agency's business. (2) Efflux of time, if the agency was for a fixed period. (3) Death or insanity of either the principal or the agent (Sec. 201). (4) Insolvency of the principal (Sec. 201). (5) Destruction of the subject-matter of the agency. (6) The principal becoming an alien enemy. (7) Dissolution of a company that is the principal. (8) Termination of the original agent's authority, which automatically terminates the sub-agent's authority (Sec. 210).

**Q8.A:** Define substituted Agent and distinguish between sub-agent and substituted agent.
> **Answer:** A **substituted agent** is a person named by an agent (who has express or implied authority to do so) to act for the principal in a part of the agency's business (Sec. 194). Such a person is an agent of the principal, not a sub-agent. For distinctions, refer to the table in Section 6, "Difference Between Sub-Agent and Substituted Agent."

**Q9:** Enumerate the rights and duties of an agent towards his principal.
> **Answer:** The duties of an agent towards their principal include: conducting business according to directions (Sec. 211), acting with skill and diligence (Sec. 212), rendering proper accounts (Sec. 213), communicating in difficulty (Sec. 214), not dealing on their own account without consent and full disclosure (Sec. 215), ensuring the principal gets the benefit of agent's dealings (Sec. 216), paying all sums received for the principal (Sec. 218), protecting the principal's interests upon death or insolvency (Sec. 209), and not making secret profits.
> The rights of an agent against their principal include: right to retainer for advances, expenses, and remuneration (Sec. 217), right to receive remuneration upon completion of the act (Sec. 219), right of lien on goods/property for dues (Sec. 221), right to be indemnified against consequences of lawful acts (Sec. 222), right to be indemnified against good faith acts causing third-party injury (Sec. 223), and right to compensation for injury caused by the principal's neglect (Sec. 225).

**Q10:** What are the duties of an agent to a principal and of a principal to an agent?
> **Answer:**
> **Duties of an Agent to a Principal:** Conduct business according to directions (Sec. 211), act with skill and diligence (Sec. 212), render accounts (Sec. 213), communicate in difficulty (Sec. 214), not deal on own account (Sec. 215), ensure principal benefits from dealings (Sec. 216), pay sums received (Sec. 218), protect principal's interests in specific circumstances (Sec. 209), and not make secret profits.
> **Duties of a Principal to an Agent:** Indemnify agent against consequences of lawful acts (Sec. 222), indemnify agent for good faith acts causing third-party injury (Sec. 223), not be liable to indemnify for criminal acts (Sec. 224), and compensate agent for injury caused by principal's neglect (Sec. 225).

**Q11:** What are the respective rights and liabilities of the agent, the principal and third parties if an agent acts for (a) a named principal, (b) an unnamed principal, and (c) an undisclosed principal?
> **Answer:**
> (a) **Named Principal:** The agent's acts are the principal's acts (Sec. 226). Principal is bound by acts within authority; if authority is exceeded, bound only if separable (Sec. 227), otherwise not (Sec. 228). Notice to agent is notice to principal (Sec. 229). Principal is bound if they induced belief in agent's unauthorized acts (Sec. 237). Misrepresentation/fraud by agent binds principal (Sec. 238). Agent is generally not personally liable.
> (b) **Unnamed Principal:** Principal is generally liable. Third party cannot sue agent if aware of agency. Agent becomes personally liable if they refuse to disclose principal's identity.
> (c) **Undisclosed Principal:** Principal may require performance, but third party has same rights against principal as against agent (Sec. 231, Part 1). Third party may refuse contract if principal discloses themselves before completion and their identity would have prevented the contract (Sec. 231, Para 2). Principal's performance is subject to existing rights/obligations between agent and third party (Sec. 232). If agent is personally liable, third party may hold agent, principal, or both liable (Sec. 233).

**Q12:** Explain fully the term 'Pretended Agent', and discuss his liability.
> **Answer:** A 'Pretended Agent' is a person who falsely claims to be the authorized agent of another, thereby inducing a third person to deal with them as if they were a legitimate agent. If the alleged principal does not ratify the pretended agent's acts, then the pretended agent is personally liable to compensate the third person for any loss or damage incurred as a result of dealing with them (Sec. 235). Furthermore, such an unauthorized agent cannot legally compel the third party to perform the contract if they were, in reality, acting on their own account and not as an agent (Sec. 236).

**Q13:** Point out those circumstances where an agent can be made personally liable in respect of contracts entered into by him on behalf of the principal.
> **Answer:** An agent can be made personally liable in the following circumstances: when acting for a foreign principal; for an undisclosed principal; when the principal cannot be sued (e.g., minor, foreign sovereign); when the contract expressly provides for personal liability; when acting for a principal not in existence; when signing a contract in their own name without qualification; when acting beyond their authority and the principal does not ratify; when making misrepresentations or committing fraud; where trade usage or custom dictates personal liability; or where the agent's authority is coupled with an interest.

**Q14:** Describe the various modes by which an agency may be terminated.
> **Answer:** An agency may be terminated by: (1) **Act of the parties**, including mutual agreement, revocation by the principal (subject to conditions regarding partial exercise of authority and reasonable notice), or renunciation by the agent (also requiring reasonable notice). (2) **Operation of law**, which includes performance of the contract, efflux of time, death or insanity of either the principal or the agent, insolvency of the principal, destruction of the subject-matter, the principal becoming an alien enemy, dissolution of a company (if the principal), and the automatic termination of a sub-agent's authority when the original agent's authority ends.

**Q15:** Discuss the responsibilities of agent towards third party.
> **Answer:** Generally, an agent is not personally responsible to third parties for contracts entered into on behalf of a disclosed principal within the scope of authority. The principal is bound by the agent's acts. However, an agent becomes responsible to third parties in specific cases: if they act for an undisclosed principal, a foreign principal, or a principal who cannot be sued; if the contract expressly makes them liable; if they act for a non-existent principal; if they sign a contract in their own name without qualification; if they exceed their authority and the principal does not ratify; if they commit misrepresentation or fraud (Sec. 238); if trade usage dictates personal liability; or if they act as a "pretended agent" (Sec. 235).

**Q16:** Explain the meaning 'Agency'. When 'Agent' is personally liable?

An agent is personally liable when acting for a foreign principal; for an undisclosed principal; when the principal cannot be sued; when the contract expressly provides for it; when acting for a principal not in existence; when signing in their own name; when acting beyond authority without ratification; for misrepresentation or fraud; due to trade usage; or when authority is coupled with interest.

**Q17:** In Which circumstance Agent is responsible Personally? Discuss.

**Q18:** Define Agency, Discuss the duties of Agent towards principal.
> **Answer:** Agency is a relationship where one person (agent) is employed to perform acts or represent another (principal) in dealings with third parties. The duties of an agent towards their principal include: conducting business according to the principal's directions or custom (Sec. 211), acting with reasonable skill and diligence (Sec. 212), rendering proper accounts on demand (Sec. 213), communicating with the principal in case of difficulty (Sec. 214), not dealing on their own account in the agency's business without the principal's consent and full disclosure (Sec. 215), ensuring the principal receives any benefit from the agent's dealings (Sec. 216), paying all sums received for the principal (Sec. 218), protecting and preserving the principal's interests upon death or insolvency (Sec. 209), and not making secret profits from the agency.

**Q19:** Discuss and explain:
(a) "The knowledge of the agent is the knowledge of the Principal."
> **Answer:** This maxim means that any notice given to an agent or information obtained by an agent, in the course of the business transacted for the principal, has the same legal consequences for the principal as if it had been directly given to or obtained by the principal themselves (Sec. 229). This principle ensures that the principal cannot escape liability or claim ignorance of facts that their agent, acting within their authority, was aware of.
(b) "Agency coupled with interest."
> **Answer:** Agency coupled with interest refers to a situation where the agent has a personal stake or interest in the property or subject-matter of the agency. In such cases, the agency cannot be terminated by the principal to the prejudice of that interest, unless there is an express contract allowing such termination (Sec. 202). This type of agency is considered irrevocable because it is created not merely for the principal's benefit but also to secure a right or interest of the agent.
(c) Termination of Agency.
> **Answer:** Termination of agency refers to the cessation of the principal-agent relationship. It can occur either by the act of the parties (mutual agreement, revocation by principal, renunciation by agent) or by operation of law (performance of the contract, efflux of time, death or insanity of either party, principal's insolvency, destruction of subject-matter, principal becoming an alien enemy, dissolution of a company, or termination of a sub-agent's authority due to the original agent's authority ending).
(d) Sub-agent and substituted agent.
> **Answer:** A **sub-agent** is employed by and acts under the control of the original agent (Sec. 191). A **substituted agent** is named by the original agent (with the principal's authority) to act directly for the principal, not under the original agent's control (Sec. 194). The key distinction lies in who controls them and who they are directly responsible to.
(e) Agent's authority in an emergency.
> **Answer:** An agent's authority in an emergency (agency of necessity) allows the agent to perform all such acts necessary to protect their principal from loss, acting with the prudence of an ordinary person in similar circumstances (Sec. 189). This authority arises when the agent cannot communicate with the principal, there is genuine necessity, and the agent acts in good faith.
(f) Agency by necessity.
> **Answer:** Agency by necessity is a type of implied agency that arises in urgent or emergency situations. It allows an agent to act on behalf of the principal without prior express authority, provided certain conditions are met: the agent was unable to communicate with the principal, there was a genuine and actual necessity to act, the agent took reasonable steps to protect the principal's interests, and the agent acted in good faith. A common example is a wife pledging her husband's credit for necessaries when not properly provided for.
(g) Del credere Agent.
> **Answer:** A Del Credere Agent is a mercantile agent who, for an extra commission, guarantees the solvency of the third parties with whom they bring the principal into contractual relations. They undertake to indemnify the principal against any loss resulting from the third party's failure to perform the contract, acting as a secondary guarantor similar to a surety.
(h) Substituted Agent.
> **Answer:** A substituted agent is a person named by the original agent (who has express or implied authority from the principal to do so) to act directly for the principal in a specific part of the agency's business. Unlike a sub-agent, a substituted agent is an agent of the principal, not of the original agent, and is directly responsible to the principal (Sec. 194).
(i) Agency by ratification.
> **Answer:** Agency by ratification occurs when a principal subsequently approves or confirms an unauthorized act performed by an agent on their behalf. This approval makes the act legally binding on the principal as if it had been authorized from the beginning, and the ratification "relates back" to the date the act was performed (Sec. 196).
(j) Personal liability of an Agent.
> **Answer:** Generally, an agent is not personally liable for contracts made on behalf of their principal. However, an agent can become personally liable in specific situations, such as when acting for a foreign or undisclosed principal, when the principal cannot be sued, when the contract expressly stipulates personal liability, when acting for a non-existent principal, when signing a contract in their own name without qualification, when exceeding their authority without ratification, for misrepresentation or fraud, or when trade usage or authority coupled with interest applies.
(k) Extent of Agent's authority.
> **Answer:** The extent of an agent's authority determines the scope within which their acts bind the principal. This authority can be actual (express or implied, including incidental authority), ostensible (apparent to third parties), or arise in an emergency. Acts done within this scope bind the principal (Sec. 226). If an agent exceeds authority, the principal is bound only if the authorized part is separable; otherwise, not at all (Secs. 227, 228).
(l) Agency.
> **Answer:** Agency is a legal relationship where one person (the agent) is authorized to act on behalf of another (the principal) to create legal relations between the principal and third parties. It is governed by the Indian Contract Act, 1872, and is based on the principle that "He who does through another does by himself."
(m) Agent, Servant and Contractor.
> **Answer:** An **Agent** binds their principal in contractual relations with third parties and acts under the principal's instructions but not necessarily their direct control over the mode of work. A **Servant** works under the direct control and supervision of their master, including the manner of work, and typically does not create contractual relations with third parties binding the master. An **Independent Contractor** undertakes to do work for another but exercises their own discretion as to the mode and time of doing the work, and generally does not represent the employer in dealings with other persons, being personally liable for their acts.
(n) Creation of Agency.
> **Answer:** Agency can be created through several modes: by express agreement (oral or written), by implied agreement (from conduct, situation, or relationship of parties), by estoppel or holding out (when conduct induces belief in agency), by necessity (in emergencies), and by ratification (subsequent approval of unauthorized acts).
(p) Requisites of valid Ratification of an Agency.
> **Answer:** The requisites for a valid ratification are: the agent must have purported to act as an agent for an identifiable principal; the principal must have been in existence and had contractual capacity at the time of the contract and ratification; the principal must ratify with full knowledge of all material facts; the act to be ratified must be lawful and not void or illegal; ratification must be done within a reasonable time; the principal must ratify the whole transaction; ratification must be communicated; the act must be one which the principal himself had the power to do; and ratification should not subject a third person to damages or terminate their rights.

**Q30:** Discuss the rights of an agent against his principal.
> **Answer:** The rights of an agent against their principal include: the right to retain sums received for advances, expenses, and remuneration (Sec. 217); the right to receive remuneration upon completion of the act (Sec. 219), unless guilty of misconduct (Sec. 220); the right of lien on the principal's goods or property for sums due (Sec. 221); the right to be indemnified by the principal against the consequences of all lawful acts done within authority (Sec. 222); the right to be indemnified for good faith acts that cause injury to third parties (Sec. 223); and the right to compensation for injuries caused by the principal's neglect or lack of skill (Sec. 225).

**Q31:** Answer in brief: (1) State the types of an Agent.
> **Answer:** The types of agents include General Agent, Particular Agent, Universal Agent, Mercantile Agent (Factor, Broker), Commission Agent, Auctioneer, and Del Credere Agent.

**Q32:** What is Agency? When Agency can be terminated?
> **Answer:** Agency is the employment of one person by another to bring the latter into legal relations with a third person. Agency can be terminated by acts of the parties (agreement, principal's revocation, agent's renunciation) or by operation of law (performance, efflux of time, death/insanity of principal or agent, principal's insolvency, destruction of subject-matter, alien enemy status, company dissolution, sub-agent authority termination).

**Q33:** Write a short note on: Mercantile Agent.
> **Answer:** A Mercantile Agent is defined as an agent who, in the customary course of business, has authority to sell goods, or to consign goods for the purpose of sale, or to buy goods, or to raise money on the security of goods. This category includes specific types like Factors (who have possession of goods for sale and can sell in their own name) and Brokers (who do not have possession but establish contractual privity).

**Q34:** Answer the following question in brief: (1) Explain - Agency coupled with interest.
> **Answer:** Agency coupled with interest refers to a situation where the agent has a personal, vested interest in the property or subject-matter of the agency itself. In such a case, the principal generally cannot terminate the agency to the prejudice of this interest, unless there is an express contract allowing it (Sec. 202). This type of agency is considered irrevocable because it is established not merely for the principal's benefit, but also to secure a right or interest of the agent.
