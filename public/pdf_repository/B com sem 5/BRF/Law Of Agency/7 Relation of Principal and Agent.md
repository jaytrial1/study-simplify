# Relation of Principal and Agent

The agency relationship imposes specific duties and grants certain rights to both the agent and the principal.

## Duties of Agent

*   **Q:** What are the duties of an agent towards his principal?

> **Answer:** The duties of an agent towards their principal include:
> 1.  **Conduct business according to directions:** An agent must conduct the principal's business according to the principal's instructions, or, if none, according to custom. Any loss from acting otherwise must be made good, and any profit accounted for (Sec. 211).
> 2.  **Act with skill and diligence:** An agent must conduct business with the skill generally possessed by those in similar business, unless the principal knows of their lack of skill. They must act with reasonable diligence and use their skill, compensating the principal for direct losses from neglect, lack of skill, or misconduct (Sec. 212).
> 3.  **Render accounts:** An agent is bound to provide proper accounts to their principal on demand (Sec. 213).
> 4.  **Communicate in difficulty:** In difficult situations, an agent must use reasonable diligence to communicate with the principal and seek instructions (Sec. 214).
> 5.  **Not to deal on own account:** An agent must not deal in the agency's business on their own account without the principal's consent and full disclosure of material circumstances. Failure to do so allows the principal to repudiate the transaction if concealment was dishonest or dealing was disadvantageous (Sec. 215).
> 6.  **Principal to get benefit of agent's dealings:** If an agent deals on their own account without the principal's knowledge, the principal is entitled to any benefit resulting from the transaction (Sec. 216).
> 7.  **Pay sums received for principal:** Subject to deductions, an agent must pay the principal all sums received on their account (Sec. 218).
> 8.  **Protect interests upon principal's death/insolvency:** If the agency terminates due to the principal's death or unsound mind, the agent must take reasonable steps to protect the entrusted interests on behalf of the principal's representatives (Sec. 209).
> 9.  **Not to make secret profits:** An agent should not make any secret profit from dealings without the principal's knowledge and assent (Sec. 215).

1.  **Conduct business according to directions:** "An agent is bound to conduct the business of his principal according to the directions given by the principal, or, in the absence of any such directions, according to the custom which prevails in doing business of the same kind at the place where the agent conducts such business. When the agent acts otherwise, if any loss be sustained, he must make it good to his principal, and if any profit accrues, he must account for it." (Sec. 211)

    **Examples:**
    *   B, a broker whose custom is not to sell on credit, sells A's goods on credit to C, who later becomes insolvent. B must compensate A for the loss.
    *   An agent instructed to insure goods neglects to do so. They are liable to the principal for the goods' value if lost.
2.  **Skill and diligence required of agent:** "An agent is bound to conduct the business of the agency with as much skill as is generally possessed by person engaged in similar business, unless the principal has notice of his want of skill. The agent is always bound to act with reasonable diligence, and to use such skill as he possesses and to make compensations to his principal in respect of the direct consequences of his own neglect, want of skill or misconduct, but not in respect of loss or damage which are indirectly or remotely caused by such neglect, want of skill or misconduct." (Sec. 212)

    **Example:** A, an agent for sale, sells goods on credit to B without proper inquiry into B's solvency. B is insolvent at the time of sale. A must compensate the principal for any sustained loss.
3.  **To render accounts to the principal:** "An agent is bound to render proper accounts to his principal on demand." (Sec. 213) This duty is an absolute obligation inherent to the agency contract.
4.  **To communicate with the principal in case of difficulty:** "It is the duty of an agent in cases of difficulty, to use all reasonable diligence in communicating with his principal, and in seeking to obtain his instructions." (Sec. 214)

    **Example:** An agent whose duty was to communicate a ship and cargo's state failed to do so despite telegraph availability. The principal insured the goods unaware of a material fact due to the agent's neglect, rendering the insurance void.
5.  **Agent not to deal on his own account:** "If an agent deals on his own account in the business of the agency, without first obtaining the consent of his principal and acquainting him with all material circumstances which have come to his own knowledge on the subject, the principal may repudiate the transaction, if the case shows either that any material fact has been dishonestly concealed from him by the agent, or that the dealing of the agent have been disadvantageous to him." (Sec. 215)

    **Example:** A directs B to sell A's estate. B buys it for himself in C's name. A, upon discovering B's purchase, may repudiate the sale if B dishonestly concealed material facts or the sale was disadvantageous to A.
6.  **Principal to get benefit of agent's dealings:** "If an agent, without the knowledge of his principal, deals in the business of the agency on his own account instead of, on account of his principal, the principal is entitled to claim from the agent any benefit which may have resulted to him from the transaction." (Sec. 216)

    **Example:** A directs B to buy a house for him. B says it cannot be bought, then buys it for himself. A, discovering this, can compel B to sell it to A at the price B paid.
7.  **Agent's duty to pay sums received for principal:** "Subject to such deductions, (vide Sec. 217) the agent is bound to pay to his principal all sums received on his account." (Sec. 218) An agent recovering money under an illegal or void contract must still account for it to the principal.
8.  **To protect and preserve the interests of the principal in case of his death or insolvency:** "When an agency is terminated by the principal dying or becoming of unsound mind, the agent is bound to take, on behalf of the representatives of his late principal, all reasonable steps for the protection and preservation of the interests entrusted to him." (Sec. 209). This is an exception to the general rule that agency terminates with death or insanity.
9.  **Not to make secret profits from agency:** "He is entitled to receive every profit or commission from his principal. Without the knowledge and assent of his principal, he should not make any secret profit in the dealings." (Sec. 215).

    **Example:** An auctioneer received a commission from the buyer in addition to what his principal paid. He was bound to hand over the commission to the principal.

## Rights of an Agent

*   **Q:** What are the rights of an agent against the principal?

> **Answer:** The rights of an agent against the principal include:
> 1.  **Right to Retainer:** An agent may retain from sums received on the principal's account all moneys due to them for advances, properly incurred expenses, and remuneration (Sec. 217).
> 2.  **Right to receive remuneration:** In the absence of a special contract, payment is due upon completion of the act, but an agent can retain moneys from goods sold even if not all goods are sold or the sale is not fully complete (Sec. 219). An agent guilty of misconduct is not entitled to remuneration for the misconducted part of the business (Sec. 220).
> 3.  **Right of lien:** An agent is entitled to retain goods, papers, and other property of the principal received by them until amounts due for commission, disbursements, and services are paid or accounted for (Sec. 221).
> 4.  **Right to be indemnified against lawful acts:** The principal must indemnify the agent against consequences of all lawful acts done in exercise of conferred authority (Sec. 222).
> 5.  **Right to be indemnified against consequences of acts causing injury to third parties:** If an agent, acting in good faith, causes injury to third parties' rights while doing an act for the principal, the employer is liable to indemnify the agent (Sec. 223).
> 6.  **Right to compensation for injury by principal's neglect:** The principal must compensate the agent for injuries caused by the principal's neglect or lack of skill (Sec. 225).

1.  **Right to Retainer:** "An agent may retain out of any sums received on account of the principal in the business of the agency, all moneys due to him in respect of advances made or expenses properly incurred by him in conducting such business, and also such remuneration as may be payable to him for acting as agent." (Sec. 217).
2.  **Right to receive remuneration:** "In the absence of any special contract, payment for the performance of any act is not due to the agent until the completion of such act; but an agent may detain moneys received by him on account of goods sold although the whole of the goods consigned to him for sale may not have been sold, or although the sale may not be actually complete." (Sec. 219).

    **Example:** An agent introduced a customer to purchase property. The sale was settled, earnest money paid, but the sale failed due to the customer's inability to find money. The agent was entitled to his agreed commission.
    *   **Agent not entitled to remuneration for business misconducted:** "An agent who is guilty of misconduct in the business of the agency is not entitled to any remuneration in respect of that part of the business which he has misconducted." (Sec. 220).
3.  **Right of lien:** "In the absence of any contract to the contrary, an agent is entitled to retain goods, papers and other property, whether movable or immovable, of the principal received by him, until the amount due to himself for commission, disbursements and services in respect of the same has been paid or accounted for to him." (Sec. 221).

## Duties of a Principal

*   **Q:** What are the duties of the principal towards the Agents?

> **Answer:** The duties of a principal towards their agent include:
> 1.  **Indemnify against consequences of lawful acts:** The employer must indemnify the agent against consequences of all lawful acts done within the conferred authority (Sec. 222).
> 2.  **Indemnify against good faith acts causing third-party injury:** If the agent performs an act in good faith that causes injury to a third person's rights, the employer is liable to indemnify the agent (Sec. 223).
> 3.  **Non-liability for criminal acts:** The employer is not liable to indemnify the agent for consequences of a criminal act, even if expressly promised (Sec. 224).
> 4.  **Compensate for injury by principal's neglect:** The principal must compensate the agent for injuries caused by the principal's neglect or lack of skill (Sec. 225).

1.  **Agent to be indemnified against consequences of lawful acts:** "The employer of agent is bound to indemnify him against the consequences of all lawful acts done by such agent in exercise of the authority conferred upon him." (Sec. 222).
2.  **Agent to be indemnified:** "Where one person employs another to do an act, and the agent does the act in good faith, the employer is liable to indemnify the agent against the consequences of the act, though it causes an injury to the rights of third persons." (Sec. 223).

    **Example:** A, a decree-holder, requires a court officer to seize goods, representing them as B's. The officer seizes them and is sued by C, the true owner. A is liable to indemnify the officer for the sum paid to C.
3.  **Non-liability of employer of agent to do a criminal act:** "Where one person employs another to do an act which is criminal, the employer is not liable to the agent, either upon an express or an implied promise, to indemnify him against the consequences of that act." (Sec. 224).

    **Example:** A employs B to beat C and agrees to indemnify B. B beats C and pays damages. A is not liable to indemnify B.
4.  **Compensation to agent for injury caused by principal's neglect:** "The principal must make compensation to his agent in respect of injury caused to such agent by the principal's neglect or want of skill." (Sec. 225).

    **Example:** A employs B as a bricklayer, puts up scaffolding unskilfully, and B is hurt. A must compensate B.

## Rights of a Principal

*   **Q:** What are the rights of a principal against the Agent?

> **Answer:** The rights of a principal against the agent include:
> 1.  **Compensation or damages:** The principal is entitled to compensation for any breach of duty by the agent.
> 2.  **Right of accounting of secret profits:** The principal has a right to obtain an account of any secret profits made by the agent and to recover them, and can also resist the agent's claim for remuneration if secret profits were made.
> 3.  **Resisting the claim of an agent:** The principal has a right to resist an agent's claim for indemnity against liability incurred if the agent acted as a principal himself and not as an agent.
> 4.  **Revocation:** The principal can revoke the agent's authority, subject to certain conditions.

1.  **Compensation or damages:** The principal is entitled to compensation for any breach of duty by the agent.
2.  **Right of accounting of secret profits:** The principal has a right to obtain an account of secret profits, recover them, and resist the agent's claim for remuneration.
3.  **Resisting the claim of an agent:** The principal has a right to resist an agent's claim for indemnity against incurred liability if the agent acted as a principal and not as an agent.
4.  **Revocation:** The principal can revoke the agent's authority, subject to certain conditions.
