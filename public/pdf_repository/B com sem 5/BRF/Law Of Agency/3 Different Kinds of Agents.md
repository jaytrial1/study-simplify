# Different Kinds of Agents

Agents can be categorized based on the scope and nature of their authority and functions. Understanding these distinctions is crucial for determining their responsibilities and liabilities.

*   **Q:** Enumerate and explain different kinds of Agents.
*   **Q:** Short note: Mercantile Agent.
*   **Q:** State types of agent.
*   **Q:** Define agency. Explain different types of agents.

> **Answer:** Different kinds of agents include:
> *   **General Agent:** Has authority to perform all acts connected with a particular trade, business, or employment (e.g., solicitors, brokers).
> *   **Particular Agent:** Has authority to perform only a specific or particular act or represent the principal in specific transactions (e.g., to sell a particular house).
> *   **Universal Agent:** Has unlimited authority to act for the principal, provided the act is legal and conforms to legal formalities.
> *   **Mercantile Agent:** Authorized to sell or buy goods for the principal in the customary course of business. This includes authority to sell goods, consign goods for sale, buy goods, or raise money on the security of goods.
> *   **Factor:** A mercantile agent entrusted with possession of goods for sale, with authority to sell in their own name and sue for the price, often having broad discretion regarding time and price. They have a general lien on the goods.
> *   **Broker:** A mercantile agent employed for buying or selling goods or other property without being entrusted with possession or control. Their main function is to establish contractual privity between two parties for a commission. They cannot act or sue in their own name and have no right of lien.
> *   **Commission Agent:** Secures buyers for sellers or sellers for buyers, earning a commission. They may buy goods on behalf of their employer in their own name and may or may not have possession of goods. Their position is similar to a broker.
> *   **Auctioneer:** Authorized to sell goods by auction for a commission. They can sell goods and sue for the price in their own name and receive the price. An auctioneer acts as an agent for the seller until the sale, and then as an agent for the buyer after the sale.
> *   **Del Credere Agent:** An agent who, for extra remuneration, guarantees the solvency of the parties with whom they bring the principal into contractual relations, indemnifying the principal against losses due to the party's failure to perform. Their liability is secondary, arising upon the insolvency of the other party.

## General Agent

A *general agent* is authorized to perform all acts connected with a particular trade, business, or employment. Examples include solicitors and brokers.

## Particular Agent

A *particular* or *special agent* is authorized only to perform a specific or particular act or represent their principal in specific transactions, such as selling a particular house or piece of land.

## Universal Agent

A *universal agent* has unlimited authority to act for the principal. Their authority is only limited by the rules that the act must be legal and conform to the legal formalities of the land.

## Mercantile Agent

A *mercantile agent* is authorized to sell or buy goods for the principal in the customary course of their business. This includes authority to sell goods, consign goods for sale, buy goods, or raise money on the security of goods.

## Factor

A *factor* is a mercantile agent entrusted with possession of goods for sale. They have the authority to sell in their own name, sue for the price, and generally have significant discretion regarding the time and price of sale. They can sell on reasonable credit and provide a warranty if customary. Factors cannot barter or pledge goods, nor can they delegate their authority. If they possess goods or documents of title with the owner's consent, any sale, pledge, or disposition made in the ordinary course of business is binding on the owner. A factor has a general lien on the goods.

## Broker

A *broker* is a mercantile agent employed for buying or selling goods or other property, but they are *not* entrusted with possession or control of the goods. Their primary role is to establish a contractual relationship between two parties, for which they receive a commission. A broker cannot act or sue in their own name and does not have a right of lien.

## Commission Agent

A *commission agent* facilitates transactions by securing buyers for sellers or sellers for buyers, in exchange for a commission on the transaction. They may buy goods on behalf of their employer in their own name, on the best available terms, and may or may not have possession of the goods. Their position is similar to that of a broker.

## Auctioneer

An *auctioneer* is authorized to sell a principal's goods by auction for a reward, usually a commission. They can sell the goods, sue for the price in their own name, and maintain an action for wrongful interference or damage to the goods. They have the authority to receive the price of sold goods. An auctioneer acts as the seller's agent until the moment of sale, and then as the buyer's agent after the sale. They must obey the principal's instructions, but the principal is liable to third parties for acts within the auctioneer's apparent authority. If an auctioneer sells below a reserved price despite instructions, the sale is binding on the owner unless the buyer knew of the limitation on authority.

## Del Credere Agent

*   **Q:** Short note: Del credere Agent.

> **Answer:** A *Del Credere Agent* (also known as Dubash in Madras or Banian in North India) is an agent who, in exchange for an extra remuneration, guarantees the solvency of the parties with whom they bring the principal into contractual relations. They undertake to indemnify the principal against any damage caused by the other party's failure to perform the contract. Their liability is secondary, similar to a surety, and arises only upon the insolvency of the other party or similar grounds.
