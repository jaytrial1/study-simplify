# Extent of Agent's Authority

The acts performed by an agent within the scope of their authority are binding on the principal (Sec. 226). An agent's authority can be categorized into actual, ostensible, or emergency authority.

*   **Q:** Discuss the nature and extent of the authority of an agent.
*   **Q:** Explain: (1) Incidental (2) Ostensible (Apparent) Authority. (3) Agent's authority in an emergency.

> **Answer:** The authority of an agent can be actual (express or implied), ostensible (apparent), or authority in an emergency.
> *   **Actual Authority:** This is the authority genuinely delegated to the agent, either explicitly stated (express) or inferred from circumstances, custom, or the nature of the business (implied). Implied authority includes *incidental authority*, which allows an agent to do every lawful thing necessary to perform an authorized act or carry on a business.
> *   **Ostensible or Apparent Authority:** This is the authority that an agent appears to have to third parties, based on the principal's words or conduct, even if actual authority was not given. Third parties can presume an agent employed for a particular business has authority for all necessary acts.
> *   **Authority in Emergency:** An agent has the authority, in an emergency, to perform all acts necessary to protect the principal from loss, acting with the prudence of an ordinary person in similar circumstances.

## Actual Authority

*Actual authority* refers to the authority truly delegated to the agent. "Authority of an agent may be expressed or implied." (Sec. 186)

**Example:** A owns a shop in Serampor, living in Calcutta and visiting occasionally. The shop is managed by B, who regularly orders goods from C in A's name and pays from A's funds with A's knowledge. B has an implied authority from A to order goods from C for the shop.

### Incidental Authority

An agent's authority extends to acts that are incidental to their main authorization:
*   An agent authorized to do an act has authority to do every lawful thing necessary to perform that act.
*   An agent authorized to carry on a business has authority to do every lawful thing necessary for that purpose, or usually done in the course of conducting such business. (Sec. 188)

**Example:** A constitutes B, his agent, to carry on his business as a shipbuilder. B may purchase timber and other materials and hire workmen for the purpose of carrying on the business.

## Ostensible or Apparent Authority

When an agent is employed for a specific business, persons dealing with them can assume they have authority to perform all acts necessary for that business. This is called *ostensible* or *apparent authority*. It is the authority of an agent as it appears to others and often aligns with actual authority.

**Example:** An estate agent appointed to find a purchaser accepted a deposit from a customer and misappropriated it. The principal was held liable because, even without explicit authority to accept deposits, an estate agent has an ostensible authority to do so.

## Authority in Emergency

"An agent has authority, in an emergency, to do all such acts for the purpose of protecting his principal from loss as would be done by a person of ordinary prudence, in his own case, under similar circumstances." (Sec. 189)

**Example:** A consigns provisions to B at Calcutta, with directions to send them immediately to Cuttack. B may sell the provisions at Calcutta if they will spoil on the journey to Cuttack.

## When the Agent Exceeds His Authority

### Principal How Far Bound

If an agent performs acts, and only a part of what they do is within their authority and can be separated from the unauthorized part, then only the part within their authority is binding on the principal (Sec. 227).

**Example:** A, owner of a ship and cargo, authorizes B to procure insurance for 4,000 rupees on the ship. B procures a policy for 4,000 rupees on the ship and another for the same sum on the cargo. A is bound to pay the premium for the ship's policy but not for the cargo's policy.

### Principal When Not Bound

"Where an agent does more than he is authorised to do, and what he does beyond the scope of his authority cannot be separated from what is within it, the principal is not bound to recognise the transaction." (Sec. 228)

**Example:** A authorizes B to buy 500 sheep for him. B buys 500 sheep and 200 lambs for a single sum of 6,000 rupees. A may repudiate the entire transaction.

## Effect of Notice to Agent (Imputed Notice)

"Any notice given to or information obtained by the agent, in the course of the business transacted by him for the principal, shall as between the principal and third parties have the same legal consequences as if it had been given to or obtained by the principal." (Sec. 229)

## Representation as to Liability

"When a person who has made a contract with an agent induces the agent to act upon the belief that the principal only will be held liable, or induces the principal to act upon the belief that the agent only will be held liable, he cannot afterwards hold liable the agent or principal respectively." (Sec. 234)

## Pretended Agent

*   **Q:** Explain the term 'Pretended Agent' and discuss his liability.

> **Answer:** A *pretended agent* is a person who falsely represents themselves as the authorized agent of another and, by doing so, induces a third person to deal with them as if they were a legitimate agent. If the alleged employer (principal) does not ratify the pretended agent's acts, the pretended agent is personally liable to compensate the third person for any loss or damage incurred as a result of dealing with them. (Sec. 235)

"A person untruly representing himself to be the authorised agent of another, and thereby inducing a third person to deal with him as such agent, is liable if his alleged employer does not ratify his act, to make compensation to the other in respect of any loss or damage which he has incurred by so dealing." (Sec. 235)

**Example:** A firm of solicitors was instructed by a client to defend a suit. The client subsequently became insane. The solicitors, unaware of the insanity, took steps to defend the suit. They were held personally liable for the other side's costs due to a breach of warranty of authority.

## Unauthorized Agent Cannot Enforce Performance

"Unauthorised agent is not entitled to require the performance of it if he was in reality acting, not as agent, but on his own account." (Sec. 236)

## Misrepresentation or Fraud by Agent

Misrepresentations made, or frauds committed by agents acting in the course of their business for their principals, are binding on the principals. (Sec. 238)

**Example:** A, being B's agent for the sale of goods, induces C to buy them by a misrepresentation that he was not authorized by B to make. The contract is voidable as between B and C at C's option.
