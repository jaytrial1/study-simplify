8_Position of Principal and Agent in Relation to Third Parties

# Position of Principal and Agent in Relation to Third Parties

The legal position of the principal and agent concerning third parties varies depending on whether the principal's existence and name are disclosed.

*   **Q:** Point out the legal position of the principal and the Agent in relation to third parties.
*   **Q:** Discuss the responsibilities at agent toaards third party.

> **Answer:** The legal position of the principal and agent in relation to third parties depends on the disclosure of the principal.
> *   **Named Principal:** When the principal's existence and name are disclosed, the agent's acts are treated as the principal's own (Sec. 226). If the agent exceeds authority, the principal is bound only by the authorized part if separable (Sec. 227), but not at all if inseparable (Sec. 228). Notice to the agent is considered notice to the principal (Sec. 229). The principal is bound by unauthorized acts if they induced a belief in the third party that such acts were authorized (Sec. 237). Misrepresentation or fraud by the agent in the course of business binds the principal (Sec. 238).
> *   **Unnamed Principal:** When the principal's existence is disclosed but not their name, the principal is still generally liable for the agent's contracts, unless trade custom or implied terms make the agent personally liable. The third party generally cannot sue the agent if they knew there was a principal. However, if the agent refuses to disclose the principal's identity, the agent becomes personally liable.
> *   **Undisclosed Principal:** When both the principal's existence and name are concealed, the agent appears to be contracting as a principal. The actual principal may demand performance of the contract, but the third party retains the same rights against the principal as they would have against the agent (Sec. 231). If the principal discloses themselves before contract completion, the third party can refuse to fulfill the contract if knowing the principal's identity or the agent's true role would have prevented them from entering the contract (Sec. 231, Para 2). Performance by the principal is subject to rights and obligations between the agent and the third party (Sec. 232). In cases where the agent is personally liable, the third party may hold either the agent, the principal, or both liable (Sec. 233).
> *   **Pretended Agent:** A person falsely representing themselves as an agent, inducing a third party to deal with them, is liable for losses incurred by the third party if the alleged principal does not ratify the act (Sec. 235). Such an unauthorized agent cannot enforce performance of the contract if they were acting on their own account (Sec. 236).

The relationship can be categorized as follows:
1.  Where the principal's existence and name are disclosed by the agent (Named Principal).
2.  Where the principal's existence is disclosed but not his name (Unnamed Principal).
3.  Where both the existence and the name of the principal are not disclosed (Undisclosed Principal).

## Named Principal

The position of a principal for the acts of agents when the name and existence of the principal are disclosed is as follows:
1.  **Acts of the agents are the acts of the principal:** "The legal consequence will be as if the contracts had been entered into and the acts done by the principal in person." (Sec. 226)
2.  **When agent exceeds his authority:**
    *   **When excess of agent's authority is separable:** "When an agent does more than he is authorised to do, and when the part of what he does, which is within his authority, can be separated, from the part which is beyond his authority, so much only of what he does as is within his authority is binding as between him and his principal." (Sec. 227)
    *   **When excess of agent's authority is not separable:** "Where an agent does more than he is authorised to do, and what he does beyond the scope of his authority cannot be separated from what is within it, the principal is not bound to recognise the transaction." (Sec. 228)
3.  **Consequences of notice given to agent:** "Any notice given to or information obtained by the agent, shall, have the same legal consequences as if it had been given to or obtained by the principal." (Sec. 229)
4.  **Liability of principal inducing belief that agent's unauthorised acts were authorised:** "The principal is bound by such acts or obligations if an agent has by his words or conduct induced such third persons to believe that such acts and obligations were within the scope of his authority." (Sec. 237)

    **Example:** A entrusts B with negotiable instruments endorsed in blank. B sells them to C in violation of private orders from A. The sale is good.
5.  **Effect, on agreement, of misrepresentation or fraud by agent:** "Misrepresentation made, or frauds committed, by agents acting in the course of their business for their principals, have the same effect on agreements made by such agents as if such misrepresentations or frauds had been made or committed by the principals; but misrepresentations made, or frauds committed, by agents, in matters which do not fall within their authority, do not affect their principals." (Sec. 238)

## Unnamed Principal

*   **Q:** S. Note: Unnamed principal.

> **Answer:** An *unnamed principal* refers to a situation where an agent enters into a contract with a third party, disclosing that they are acting as an agent, but without revealing the specific identity (name) of their principal. In such cases, the principal is generally still liable for the agent's contracts, unless a trade custom or an express or implied term makes the agent personally liable. A third party who contracts with such an agent, knowing there is a principal, typically cannot sue the agent directly. However, if the agent refuses to disclose the principal's identity, the agent may become personally liable on the contract.

The position of an agent contracting for and on behalf of an unnamed principal is as follows:
1.  The principal is still liable for the agent's contracts, unless there is a trade custom or an express or implied term making the agent personally liable.
2.  If the principal's name is not disclosed, the third party who contracts with an agent, knowing there is a principal, cannot sue the agent.
3.  However, if the agent declines to disclose the principal's identity, they will become personally liable on the contract.

## Undisclosed Principal

*   **Q:** S. Note: Undisclosed principal.

> **Answer:** The *doctrine of undisclosed principal* applies when an agent not only conceals the name of their principal but also the very fact that they are acting as an agent. The agent presents themselves as the principal, contracting in their own name. In these cases:
> 1.  The actual principal may require performance of the contract, but the third party retains the same rights against the principal as they would have had against the agent (Sec. 231, Part 1).
> 2.  If the principal reveals themselves before the contract is completed, the other contracting party may refuse to fulfill the contract if they can show that knowledge of the principal's identity or the agent's true role would have prevented them from entering the contract (Sec. 231, Para 2).
> 3.  The principal's right to performance is subject to any rights and obligations existing between the agent and the third party (Sec. 232).
> 4.  If the agent is personally liable, the third party may choose to hold either the agent, the principal, or both liable (Sec. 233).

In certain cases, an agent conceals not only the principal's name but also the fact that they are an agent. This is known as the 'doctrine of undisclosed principal', where the agent creates the impression of contracting as a principal.

1.  **Principal may require performance of the contract:** "If an agent makes a contract with a person who neither knows nor has reason to suspect that he is an agent, his principal may require the performance of the contract; but the other contracting party has, as against the principal, the same rights as he would have had as against the agent if the agent has been principal." (Sec. 231, Part 1)
2.  **Other party may refuse to fulfil the contract:** "If the principal discloses himself before the contract is completed, the other contracting party may refuse to fulfil the contract, if he can show that, if he had known who was the principal in the contract, or if he had known that the agent was not a principal, he would not have entered into the contract." (Sec. 231, Para 2)
3.  **Performance is subject to the rights and obligations between agent and the other party:** "Where one man makes a contract with another, neither knowing nor having reasonable ground to suspect that the other is an agent, the principal, if he requires the performance of the contract, can only obtain such performance subject to the rights and obligations subsisting between the agent and the other party to the contract." (Sec. 232).

    **Example:** A, who owes 500 rupees to B, sells 1,000 rupees worth of rice to B. A is acting as agent for C, but B has no knowledge or suspicion. C cannot compel B to take the rice without allowing B to set off A's debt.
4.  **Agent is personally responsible:** "In cases where the agent is personally liable, a person dealing with him may hold either him or his principal, or both of them, liable." (Sec. 233).

    **Example:** A enters into a contract with B to sell 100 bales of cotton, and later discovers B was acting as agent for C. A may sue either B or C, or both, for the price.

## Pretended Agent

*   **Q:** Short note: Pretended Agent.

> **Answer:** A *pretended agent* is an individual who falsely claims to be an authorized agent of another person, thereby inducing a third party to enter into a contract or deal with them. If the purported principal does not ratify the acts of this pretended agent, then the pretended agent becomes personally liable to compensate the third party for any loss or damage incurred as a result of relying on the false representation and dealing with them (Sec. 235). Furthermore, such an unauthorized agent cannot legally compel the third party to perform the contract if they were, in reality, acting on their own account and not as an agent (Sec. 236).

1.  **Liability of pretended agent:** "A person untruly representing himself to be the authorised agent of another and thereby inducing a third person to deal with him as such agent, is liable, if this alleged employer does not ratify his acts, to make compensation to the other in respect of any loss or damage which he has incurred by so dealing." (Sec. 235)
2.  **Agent not entitled to performance:** "A person with whom a contract has been entered into in the character of agent is not entitled to require the performance of it, if he was in reality acting, not as agent, but on his own account." (Sec. 236)
