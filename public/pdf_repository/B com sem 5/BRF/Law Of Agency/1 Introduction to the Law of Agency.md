# Introduction to the Law of Agency

The concept of agency allows one person to act on behalf of another, establishing legal relations with third parties. This fundamental principle is crucial for the efficient conduct of business and is governed by specific legal provisions.

In English law, "Agency is the employment of one person by another in order to bring the latter into legal relations with a third person." Since individuals cannot personally attend to all matters requiring legal interaction, they rely on others, known as *agents*, with the person granting authority being the *principal*. The relationship formed is called *agency*. Acts performed by an agent in the ordinary course of business are binding on the principal. In India, the Law of Agency is detailed in Chapter X (Sections 182 to 238) of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.

## Definition and Explanation

An *agent* is defined as "a person employed to do any act for another or to represent another in dealings with third person." The *principal* is "the person for whom such act is done, or who is so represented" (Sec. 182).

**Example:** P appoints A to buy a car on his behalf. P is the principal and A is the agent. The relationship between P and A is called 'agency'.

*   **Q:** Define a contract of Agency. State the basis and essentials of agency.
*   **Q:** Define an agent and principal.
*   **Q:** Define 'Agency'.

> **Answer:** A contract of agency is an agreement where one person (the agent) is employed to do any act for another (the principal) or to represent another in dealings with third persons. The basis of agency rests on two rules: (1) Whatever a person can do personally, he can do through an agent, and (2) He who does an act through another does it by himself, meaning all acts of the agent are the acts of the principal. The essentials of agency are an agreement between the principal and agent, and the agent's intention to act on behalf of the principal.

## Scope

A person with the capacity to contract can do so either personally or through an agent. Acting through an agent is encapsulated by the maxim *Qui facit per alium facit per se*, meaning "He who does through another does by himself." An agent's primary function is to establish a contractual relationship between their principal and third parties, effectively serving as a connecting link.

## Basis of Agency

The agency relationship is founded on two key rules:
1.  Whatever a person can do personally, they can do through an agent.
2.  He who does an act through another does it by himself. Consequently, all acts of the agent are considered acts of the principal.

## Essentials of Relationship of Agency

1.  **Agreement between the principal and the agent:** Any person may become an agent as between the principal and a third person (Sec. 184), meaning an agent does not need to be a competent person (even a minor or lunatic can act as an agent). No consideration is necessary to create an agency (Sec. 185). The principal's agreement to be represented is sufficient to support the contract.
2.  **Intention of the agent to act on behalf of the principal:** This is a question of fact, determining whether the person truly intends to act for another.

## Consequence

"Contracts entered into through an agent, and obligations arising from acts done by an agent, may be enforced in the same manner, and will have the same legal consequence, as if the contracts had been entered into and the act done by the principal in person." (Sec. 226)

## Who Can Employ & Who May Be Agent?

*   **Who can employ agent?** "Any person who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and who is of sound mind, may employ an agent." (Sec. 183)
*   **Who may be agent?** "As between the principal and third persons any person may become an agent, but no person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind can become an agent, so as to be responsible to his principal according to the provisions in that behalf herein contained." (Sec. 184)

*   **Q:** Who are competent to employ an agent? Who can become an agent?

> **Answer:** Any person who is of the age of majority and of sound mind may employ an agent. As between the principal and third persons, any person may become an agent. However, a person who is not of the age of majority and of sound mind cannot become an agent so as to be responsible to their principal.

## Test of Agency

To determine if a person is an agent, one asks: 'Has that person the capacity to bind the principal and make him answerable to a third person by bringing him into legal relations with that third person and thus establish a privity of contract between that party and the principal?' If the answer is yes, they are an agent; otherwise, not.
