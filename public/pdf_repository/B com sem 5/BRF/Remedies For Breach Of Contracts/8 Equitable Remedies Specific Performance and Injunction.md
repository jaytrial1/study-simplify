8_Equitable Remedies: Specific Performance and Injunction

This section describes two significant equitable remedies for breach of contract: specific performance, which compels the breaching party to fulfill their promise, and injunction, which restrains them from performing certain actions.

# Equitable Remedies: Specific Performance and Injunction

## Specific Performance

*Specific performance* means compelling a party to perform their promise *in specie* (exactly as agreed). Under very special circumstances, an injured person can obtain a decree for specific performance, which is a court order directing the breaching party to perform what they promised. Specific rules for this relief are found in the *Specific Relief Act*. This remedy is generally discretionary and granted when monetary compensation is inadequate.

## Injunction

An *injunction* is a court order restraining a person from doing some act that was the subject-matter of the contract.

*   A *mandatory injunction* compels a party to do an act; such injunctions are rare.
*   A *prohibitory* or *restrictive injunction* aims to restrain a party from doing an act and is usually negative in character. Generally, courts grant prohibitory injunctions more readily than mandatory ones. Injunctions are also discretionary and granted when damages would be an inadequate remedy.
