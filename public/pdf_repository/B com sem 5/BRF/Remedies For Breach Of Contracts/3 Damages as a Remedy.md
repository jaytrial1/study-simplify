3_Damages as a Remedy

This section elaborates on the concept of damages as a primary remedy for breach of contract, defining their nature and purpose in compensating the injured party.

# Damages as a Remedy

## Nature of Damages

The party injured by a breach of contract may initiate legal action to claim *damages*. As stated by Asquith J., the governing purpose of damages is to place the party whose rights have been violated in the same position, as far as money can, as if their rights had been observed.

The primary aim of the law of damages for a breach of contract is to put the plaintiff in the position they would have been in had the contract been fulfilled, or had the breach not occurred. Once this is achieved, the aim of the law of damages is satisfied.
*   **Example:** D agreed to grant a lease of property to P but later refused. The Court awarded P damages covering expenses incurred on preliminary legal work and the profits P would have earned if the lease had been granted.

Therefore, damages are granted as *compensation* for the loss suffered by the injured person, not as a punishment for the wrong-doer.
