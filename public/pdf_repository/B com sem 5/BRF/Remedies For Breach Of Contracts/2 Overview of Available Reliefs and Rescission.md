2_Overview of Available Reliefs and Rescission

This section outlines the various forms of relief available to an injured party upon a breach of contract, followed by a detailed explanation of rescission, its modes, and its consequences.

# Overview of Available Reliefs

When a breach of contract occurs, the injured or aggrieved party is entitled to the following reliefs:

1.  Rescission of the contract.
2.  Suit for damages.
3.  Suit upon *quantum meruit*.
4.  Suit for *specific performance* of the contract.
5.  Suit for *injunction*.
6.  Suit for rectification & cancellation.

Let us examine these in detail.

# Rescission of Contract

*Rescission* means the *termination* and *cancellation* of the terms of the contract.

## Modes of Rescission

Rescission can occur in the following ways:

1.  **Mutual Agreement (Sec. 62):** If both parties agree to rescind the contract, the original contract no longer needs to be performed.
2.  **Breach by One Party:** When one party breaks the contract, the other party may sue them, treat the contract as rescinded, and refuse further performance.
3.  **Voidable Contracts (Sec. 64):** When a person, at whose option a contract is voidable, rescinds it, the other party is relieved from performing any promise contained within it.

## Consequences of Rescission

1.  **Absolution from Obligations:** In case of rescission, without prejudice to the injured party's right to receive compensation for the breach, they are absolved from all their obligations under the contract and need not perform any promise.
2.  **Restoration of Advantage (Sec. 65):** When a contract is rescinded, any person who has received any advantage under such agreement or contract is bound to restore it or to make compensation for it to the party from whom it was received.
    *   **Example:** A pays B Rs. 1,000 in consideration of B's promising to marry C, A's daughter. C is dead at the time of the promise. The agreement is void, but B must repay A Rs. 1,000.
    *   This section applies mutually: if one party must disgorge an advantage, the other party may also be required to restore any advantage received. The restoration of advantage and payment of compensation must be mutual.
3.  **Restoration of Benefit in Voidable Contracts (Sec. 64):** If a party rightfully rescinding a voidable contract has received any benefit thereunder from another party, they must restore such benefit, as far as possible, to the person from whom it was received.
4.  **Right to Compensation (Sec. 75):** If a person rightfully rescinds their contract, they are entitled to compensation for any damage sustained due to the non-fulfillment of the contract by the other party.
    *   **Example:** A, a singer, contracts with B, a theatre manager, to sing for two nights a week for two months, with B paying Rs. 100 per night. On the sixth night, A willfully absents herself, and B rescinds the contract. B is entitled to claim compensation for damages sustained due to the non-fulfillment of the contract.

> **Answer to Q:**
> **What do you mean by damages? What are the different kinds of damages that may be awarded in case of a breach of contract?**
>
> *Damages* refer to the monetary compensation awarded to an injured party for the loss suffered due to a breach of contract. The primary purpose of damages is to place the aggrieved party in the same financial position they would have been in had the contract been fulfilled. The different kinds of damages that may be awarded in case of a breach of contract include ordinary or compensatory damages, special damages, exemplary or vindictive damages, and nominal or token damages.
