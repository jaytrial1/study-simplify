# Introduction to Capacity of Parties

As discussed earlier, there are certain essentials of a valid contract. Competence of parties to contract is one such condition.

**Capacity Defined:** According to Section 10, an agreement becomes a contract if it is entered into between the parties who are competent to contract. 'Capacity' referred to here means competence of the parties to enter into a valid contract. 'Capacity' here also means physical and mental capacity.

**As per Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act:**
> "Every person is competent to contract
> (1) who is of the age of majority according to the law to which he is subject, and
> (2) who is of sound mind, and
> (3) is not disqualified from contracting by any law to which he is subject."

It may be seen from this definition that not every person is capable of entering into a contract. It follows from the definition enunciated in Section 11 that a person is incapable of entering into a contract if he is:
(i) minor,
(ii) insane (of unsound mind), and
(iii) disqualified by law.

**Q: What do you understand by 'capacity to contract'?**
**Q: Which parties are not competent for contract?**
**Q: What is Competence of parties?**
> **Answer:** 'Capacity to contract' or 'Competence of parties' refers to the legal ability of individuals to enter into a binding agreement. According to Section 11 of the Indian Contract Act, a person is competent to contract if they are of the age of majority, of sound mind, and not disqualified from contracting by any law to which they are subject. Therefore, parties who are not competent for contract are minors, persons of unsound mind, and persons disqualified by law.
