16_Summary of Competent Parties and Their Capacities

This section provides a structured overview of who is considered competent to contract and who is not, summarizing the legal implications for minors, persons of unsound mind, and those disqualified by law.

# Summary of Competent Parties and Their Capacities

## Competent Parties: Section 11

### Who are Not Competent:

1.  Minor
2.  Insane (Unsound Mind)
3.  Disqualified by law

### Not a Minor: Minor's Contract

1.  Void *ab initio*.
2.  Minor can be a promisee or a beneficiary.
3.  No ratification possible.
4.  Claim for necessaries (property liable, not personal).
5.  No estoppel against a minor.
6.  Restitution or compensation (limited to identifiable property).
7.  No specific performance.
8.  No insolvency.
9.  Minor as a partner (only for benefits of partnership).
10. Minor can act as an agent.
11. Minor along with a major - major alone responsible.
12. Minor's guardian (can contract on minor's behalf within powers).
13. Liability for torts.

### Not of Unsound Mind:

1.  **The Test (Section 12, Para 1):**
    (a) Capacity to understand.
    (b) Ability to form a rational judgment.
    (c) As to the effect on person's interest.
2.  **Causes of Unsoundness:** Idiocy, Lunacy & Drunkenness.
3.  **Contract made during lucid interval (Section 12, Para 2) - valid.**

### Not Disqualified by Law:

1.  Aliens (especially alien enemies).
2.  Foreign Sovereigns.
3.  Corporations (limited by Memorandum/Statute).
4.  Married women (no disability in India).
5.  Professional incapacity (e.g., English Barristers).
6.  Insolvency.
7.  Convicts.
