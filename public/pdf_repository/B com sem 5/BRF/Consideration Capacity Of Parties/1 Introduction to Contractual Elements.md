1_Introduction to Contractual Elements

This section introduces the concept of consideration as a fundamental element of a valid contract, explaining its role as "something in return" and providing an illustrative example.

# Introduction to Consideration

Consideration is one of the essential requirements to support a contract. It is the sign and symbol of every bargain. Subject to certain exceptions, an agreement made without consideration is void. Consideration is the necessary evidence required by law of the intention of the parties to effect their legal relation. Consideration is (in other words) known as *quid pro quo* or "something in return of something." Broadly speaking, consideration is the price paid by the promisee for the obligation of the promisor.

**Example:** A sells his car to B for Rs. 2,00,000. In return B pays Rs. 2,00,000 to A. Here, A has given his car to B, in return for which A received Rs. 2,00,000. The motor car is the consideration for payment of Rs. 2,00,000, and Rs. 2,00,000 is the consideration for parting with the motor car. If, however, somebody agrees to give his car as a gift to B, there is no consideration. If B files a suit to get the motor car from A, he would not succeed, because there is no consideration and thus no contract.
