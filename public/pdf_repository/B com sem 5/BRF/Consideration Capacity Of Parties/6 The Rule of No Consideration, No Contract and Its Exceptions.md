6_The Rule of "No Consideration, No Contract" and Its Exceptions

This section explains the general principle that agreements without consideration are void and details several significant exceptions where contracts are enforceable even without consideration.

# The Rule of "No Consideration, No Contract" and Its Exceptions

According to Section 2(h), "An agreement enforceable by law is a contract"; and Section 2(g) says that "An agreement not enforceable by law is said to be void"; and under Section 25, an agreement is declared to be void unless supported by consideration. The general rule, therefore, is that every agreement must be supported by consideration, and an agreement without consideration is void. Thus, "A promise without consideration is a gift; one made for a consideration is a bargain" (Salmond and Winfield on Law of Contracts). Under Roman Law, an agreement without consideration was called a *nudum pactum* (a naked agreement) and was unenforceable.

To this general rule, there are certain exceptions which are mentioned in Section 25 of the Indian Contract Act, and apart from that, there are some other provisions in the Act where the requirements as to consideration are dispensed with.

## Exceptions

There are exceptional cases where a contract is enforceable even though it is not supported by consideration. They are as under:

### Natural Love and Affection

Where an agreement is:
> "expressed in writing and registered under the law for the time being in force for the registration of documents and is made on account of natural love and affection between the parties standing in a near relation to each other"
is enforceable even if there is no consideration. (Section 25 (1))

**Ingredients:** The following ingredients of Section 25 (1) may be noted:
(i) The parties to the agreement stand in a near relation to each other.
(ii) Agreement is made on account of *natural* love and affection.
(iii) The agreement should be in the form of a written document.
(iv) The document is registered.
Nearness of relationship, however, does not necessarily import love and affection.

*   **Example:** An agreement entered into by a husband with his wife during quarrels and disagreement, whereby the husband promised to give some property to the wife; the agreement is void, because under the circumstances, there is no natural love and affection between the parties. (*Rajlukhy Debee V. Bhootnatb* 1900 C. W. N. 488.)

### Compensation for Voluntary Services

Where:
> "It is a promise to compensate wholly or in part, a person who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor or something which the promisor was legally compellable to do"
is enforceable, even though without consideration [Section 25 (2)].

**Ingredients:** To apply this rule, the following essentials must exist:
(a) The act must have been done voluntarily for the promisor;
(b) Or it must be something which was the legal obligation of the promisor;
(c) The promisor must be in existence at the time when the act was done;
(d) The promisor must agree now to compensate the promisee.

This sub-section speaks of two contingencies:

(i) **Voluntary Services:** When there is a voluntary act by one party and there is a subsequent promise to pay compensation to the former.
    *   **Example:** A finds B's purse and gives it to him. B promises to give A Rs. 30/-. This is a contract.
    *   **It should be noted:**
        *   Something done must be for the promisor voluntarily. (*Ahmedabad Jubllee S. and M. Co v. Chhotalal Chhaganlal* 1908 10 Bom. L. R. 141)
        *   The promisor must have been in existence at the time when the act was done (e.g., no company promoter can ordinarily sue for work done before the incorporation of a company). (*See T. V. Krishna Iyer v. The Official Liquidator of Cape Comorin General Traffic Co.* A. I. R. 1952 T. C. 99.)
        *   He must have been competent to contract at the time when the service was rendered (e.g., the section will not apply to a contract by which a person ratifies on majority, a contract made during his minority).

(ii) **Legally Compellable Duty:** Another situation covered by the exception is where the promisee has done something for the promisor—"Which the promisor was legally compellable to do." A subsequent promise to pay for such an act is enforceable.
    *   **Example:** A supports B's infant son. B promises to pay A's expenses in so doing. This is a contract.

### Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt

Where:
> "it is a promise, made in writing and signed by the person to be charged therewith, or by his agent, generally or specially authorised in that behalf, to pay wholly or in part a debt of which the creditor might have enforced payment but for the 'limitation of suits',"
is enforceable, even though without consideration [Section 25 (3)].

**Ingredients:** Following points may be noted in this regard:
(i) The promise may be to pay the whole or any part of the debt.
(ii) The debt must be a liquidated or ascertained sum of money.
(iii) The debt must be such, of which the creditor might have enforced payment but for the law of limitation of the suit (time-barred debt).
(iv) There must be a definite promise to pay.
(v) A mere unconditional acknowledgment involving an implied promise to pay is not enough.
    *   **Example:** Where the debtor wrote, "the account having been checked the sum of Rs. 6,459 was found due from me. I admit the amount to be due from me." It was held that there was no promise to pay within the meaning of this section. (*Basheshar Nath v. Baji Nath* 1938 Lah. 264.) It was a mere acknowledgment.
(vi) This clause does not apply to a promise to pay time-barred debts of a third person. It is restricted to the promisor who is himself liable for the debt. So, where a Hindu son agrees to pay his deceased father's time-barred debt, there is no personal liability for the son, for it is only the joint-family property in his hands that will be answerable for the debt.

### Agency

According to Section 185, "No consideration is necessary to create an agency."

### Completed Gift

**Explanation (1) to Section 25** enunciates the rule that the principle "No consideration, no contract" does not apply to completed gifts. It states that:
> "Nothing in this section shall affect the validity as between the donor and the donee, of any gift actually made."

### Inadequacy of Consideration

> "An agreement to which the consent of the promisor is freely given is not void merely because the consideration is inadequate; but the inadequacy of the consideration may be taken into account by the Court in determining the question whether the consent to the promise was freely given." **Explanation (2) Section : 25**

Where the question is whether the consent was free or not, adequacy of consideration is an important piece of evidence to help the Court to come to a conclusion.

**Q: 'No consideration, no contract'-Discuss the maxim and point out the exceptions to this rule.**
**Q: "Contract without consideration is void "Explain the statement with its exceptions.**
> **Answer:** The maxim "No consideration, no contract" signifies that for an agreement to be legally enforceable as a contract, it must be supported by consideration. Consideration is the mutual exchange of something of value between the parties, acting as the "price" for the promise. Without it, an agreement is considered a bare promise (*nudum pactum*) and is generally void, meaning it cannot be enforced in a court of law. This rule ensures that promises are not made lightly and that there is a genuine intention to create legal relations.
>
> **Exceptions to this rule, where a contract is valid without consideration, are:**
> 1.  **Natural Love and Affection:** An agreement made in writing, registered, and based on natural love and affection between parties in a near relation.
> 2.  **Compensation for Voluntary Services:** A promise to compensate someone who has already voluntarily done something for the promisor or something the promisor was legally obliged to do.
> 3.  **Promise to Pay a Time-Barred Debt:** A written and signed promise to pay a debt that is legally time-barred by the law of limitation.
> 4.  **Agency:** No consideration is required to create an agency.
> 5.  **Completed Gifts:** The rule does not affect the validity of gifts already made between a donor and donee.
