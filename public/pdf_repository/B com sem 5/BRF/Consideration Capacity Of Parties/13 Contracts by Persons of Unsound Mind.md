13_Contracts by Persons of Unsound Mind

This section details the legal standing of agreements made by individuals with unsound minds, including the concept of lucid intervals and the limited liability for necessaries.

# Contracts by Persons of Unsound Mind

## Effect of Agreements Made by Persons of Unsound Mind

Contracts made by persons of unsound mind, like contracts by minors, are void. The reason is that a contract requires the assent of two minds, but a person of unsound mind has nothing which the law recognizes in him as a mind. The question of unsoundness of mind is a question of fact. There being a presumption in favor of sanity, the person who relies on unsoundness of mind must prove it sufficiently to satisfy the Court. The unsoundness must be referable to the time when the contract was formed. Mere weakness of mind or loss of memory is insufficient to prove unsoundness, and that while it is not necessary to prove utter mental darkness, it must at least be shown that the person was incapable of understanding business and of forming a rational judgment. (*Mahomed Yakub V. Abdul* (1923) AIR FAT. 187).

Unsoundness of mind may arise from:
1.  Idiocy
2.  Lunacy or insanity
3.  Drunkenness and similar other factors.

### Contract in Lucid Interval

The second and third paragraphs of Section 12 of the Indian Contract Act provide:
> "A person who is usually of unsound mind, but occasionally of sound mind may make a contract when he is of sound mind.
>
> A person who is usually of sound mind, but occasionally of unsound mind may not make a contract when he is of unsound mind."

*   **Example:** A patient in a lunatic asylum, who is at intervals of sound mind, may contract during these intervals.

### Effect of Agreements Made by Persons of Unsound Mind

Agreements by persons of unsound mind are void. However, an agreement entered into by a lunatic for the supply of necessaries for himself or for the persons whom he is bound to support is valid as a quasi-contract, under Section 68 of the Contract Act, but even in such cases, no personal liability attaches to them. It is only their estate which is liable.

Under English law, contracts made by persons of unsound mind are voidable, not void, if the other party knew of the unsoundness.
