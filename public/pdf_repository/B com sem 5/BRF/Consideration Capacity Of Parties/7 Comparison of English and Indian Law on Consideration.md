7_Comparison of English and Indian Law on Consideration

This section highlights the key differences in how English and Indian law approach the concept of consideration, particularly concerning contract classification, types of consideration recognized, its movement, and the validity of natural love and affection.

# Comparison of English and Indian Law on Consideration

The points of difference between English law and Indian law regarding consideration of a contract are as follows:

| Aspect         | Indian Law                                      | English Law                                  |
| :------------- | :---------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Classification | Only simple contracts                           | (a) Contracts under seal, and (b) Simple contracts. |
| 2. Types       | (i) Past (ii) Present (iii) Future.             | Only (i) Present & (ii) Future               |
| 3. Move from   | Promisee or any other person                    | Only promisee.                               |
| 4. Value       | Natural love and affection is good consideration. | Not So.                                      |

**Q: Point out the difference between the English Law and the Indian Law regarding consideration of a contract.**
> **Answer:** The key differences between English and Indian law regarding consideration are:
> 1.  **Contract Classification:** Indian law primarily recognizes simple contracts, while English law distinguishes between contracts under seal (deeds) and simple contracts.
> 2.  **Types of Consideration:** Indian law recognizes past, present, and future consideration. English law generally only recognizes present (executed) and future (executory) consideration, with past consideration usually being invalid.
> 3.  **Movement of Consideration:** In Indian law, consideration can move from the promisee or any other person. In English law, consideration must move directly from the promisee.
> 4.  **Natural Love and Affection:** In India, natural love and affection can be good consideration if the agreement is in writing, registered, and between parties in a near relation. In English law, natural love and affection alone are not considered valid consideration.
