15_Other Legal Formalities for a Valid Contract

This section discusses the additional legal formalities, such as writing and registration, that certain contracts must fulfill to be valid and enforceable, beyond the core essentials of consent, capacity, and consideration.

# Other Legal Formalities for a Valid Contract

We have seen in Section 2(h) that the Indian Contract Act makes a distinction between an agreement and a contract. The test is whether it is 'enforceable by law'. Section 10 prescribes qualifications for valid agreements. As already seen, the said essentials or qualifications are as under:

1.  Free consent of the contracting parties (Refer to Secs. 13 to 22).
2.  Competency to contract (Refer to Sec. 11).
3.  For a lawful consideration (Refer to Sec. 23).
4.  Not declared void (Refer to Sections 20, 26 to 30 and 56).
5.  Other legal formalities, if so required by any law in force in India.

The first four ingredients or qualifications have already been discussed in detail earlier. Let us now examine the fifth and the last one.

Part II of Section 10 provides:
> "Nothing herein contained shall affect any law in force in India and not hereby expressly repealed, by which any contract is required to be made in writing or in the presence of witnesses or any law relating to the registration of documents."

As seen earlier, a contract may be in writing, or made orally. Both carry the same legal effect. But in some specified cases, a particular law may require a contract to be reduced to writing. While in still other cases, the document may further be required to be stamped and registered. The effect of such provisions in those laws is an agreement which is otherwise enforceable as a contract as per Section 10 will create rights and liabilities only when they fulfill the additional requirements stipulated therein, viz., stamp, registration, etc.

## (A) Contracts Required to Be in Writing

Some of the contracts or documents containing the contract which require them to be in writing are as follows:
1.  Contract without consideration, i.e., gift [sub-section (1) of Sec. 25].
2.  Promise to pay time-barred debts [sub-sec. (3) of Sec. 25].
3.  Memorandum of Association and Articles of Association (Sections 15 and 30 of the Companies Act) etc.

## (B) Contracts Required to Be Registered

Some of the contracts which have to be registered are as follows:
1.  Document of which registration is compulsory under Sec. 17 of the Registration Act of 1908.
2.  Contracts made out of natural love and affection between the parties standing in near relation to each other, and made without any consideration. [Sec. 25 (1).]
3.  Transfer of any immovable property viz. Land, House (Transfer of Property Act). etc.

**Conclusion:** Thus, it may be seen that a contract may be made by words spoken or written. There is no enactment in India corresponding to the Statute of Frauds. Indian Sales of Goods Act also does not, unlike the corresponding English enactment, require that any contract of sale should be evidenced by a writing. The 'Simple' or 'Parol' Contract supported by consideration is the type of contract largely recognized under the Indian enactment.

**Q: State the 'importance of other legal formalities' of a valid contract.**
> **Answer:** While a contract can generally be oral or written, certain laws in India mandate specific "other legal formalities" for particular types of contracts to be valid and enforceable. These formalities include requiring the contract to be in writing, attested by witnesses, or registered. The importance of these formalities lies in:
> 1.  **Enforceability:** Without fulfilling these requirements, agreements that would otherwise be valid under Section 10 of the Contract Act may not create legally enforceable rights and liabilities.
> 2.  **Evidence and Proof:** Written and registered documents provide clear evidence of the terms of the agreement, reducing disputes and facilitating proof in court.
> 3.  **Public Record:** Registration creates a public record of certain transactions, especially those involving immovable property, offering transparency and preventing fraud.
> 4.  **Statutory Compliance:** They ensure compliance with specific statutes that deem such formalities necessary for the validity or effect of certain agreements (e.g., gifts without consideration, time-barred debt promises, property transfers).
