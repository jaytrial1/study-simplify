14_Persons Disqualified by Law to Contract

This section enumerates various categories of individuals who are disqualified from entering into contracts by law due to their status, explaining the specific limitations for each group.

# Persons Disqualified by Law to Contract

## Disqualified Persons

### (1) Aliens

An alien is a person who is not a subject of India; that is, he is a foreigner domiciled in India and is a competent person to contract. He may be either (i) an alien friend or (ii) an alien enemy. An alien living in India may be free to enter into contracts with citizens of India. But the State may impose restrictions. On the outbreak of war, contracts already entered into with an alien enemy are either suspended during the continuance of hostilities or are dissolved. If the period is fairly long, the contract will become void on the ground of impossibility of performance. An alien enemy can sue in an Indian Court only after receiving a license from the Central Government.

### (2) Foreign Sovereigns

Foreign sovereigns and accredited representatives of a foreign State or Ambassadors have a peculiar privilege in that they can enter into contracts and enforce them in Courts, but no contract can be enforced against them in any Court except under certain circumstances. (*United States of America V. Wagner* (1867) 2 Ch. A. 582.)

### (3) Corporations

A corporation is an artificial or fictitious person created by law having a legal existence apart from its members. A Corporation can both sue and be sued in the Courts of Law. A Company registered under the Companies Act, 1956, is regulated in case of its contractual capacity by terms of its Memorandum of Association; while the contractual capacity of a statutory Company is limited by the statute governing it; if it exceeds its powers, the contract is *ultra vires* the Company and void. However, the contractual capacity of a corporation is subject to certain limitations. The limitations are (i) inherent, i.e., formal or natural, or (ii) express, i.e., material.

### (4) Married Women

(a) **Position in India:** In India, there is no difference between man and woman, as regards contractual capacity. The rules and the limitations applicable to men are equally applicable to women also. Besides, she can bind her husband's properties for necessaries supplied to her. She becomes an agent for this purpose.
(b) **Position in England:** In England, the position was different prior to 1882. A married woman lost her contractual capacity after her marriage. Her legal personality was deemed to have been merged with that of her husband. However, this disability continued only so long as she was a married woman, so that if she became a *feme sole* whether by judicial separation, divorce or death of the husband, she once again regained her contractual capacity. This position has changed since 1883 after passing of several statutes. Now a married woman, just like in India, can enter into any contract and incur liabilities exactly in the same way as other persons.

### (5) Professional Incapacity

In England, law prohibits a member of any professional service from entering into a contract of fees with his clients. Thus, a Barrister or a member of Royal College of Physicians can only enter into a contract other than his professional fees. Thus, the services rendered by Barristers when acting in their professional capacity are deemed to be of a purely honorary character. (*Kennedy V. Brown* (1863) E. R. 268.) This is for the etiquette of their profession.
In India, these professional disqualifications do not exist. An Advocate under the Bar Council Act of India has both the liberty to act and plead and has got the right to contract, to sue and be sued.

### (6) Insolvency

Under the Insolvency Act, on adjudication, an insolvent's property vests in the Official Receiver or the Official Assignee, and he has no power to deal with that property. All the transactions, thereafter, can be entered into by the Official Receiver or Official Assignee pertaining to that property, and he can sue and be sued on behalf of the insolvent. These limitations are removed as soon as the court passes an order of discharge.

### (7) Felons or Convicts

A convict is a person undergoing imprisonment. He is incapable of entering into a contract. However, he can enter a contract while "on parole." As soon as the sentence period has expired, his incapacity also comes to an end.

**Q: Give a list of persons who are disqualified because of his status.**
**Q: Write a note on a person who is not competence for contract due to this status.**
> **Answer:** Persons who are disqualified from contracting due to their status by law include:
> 1.  **Aliens:** Specifically, alien enemies during wartime, who may have their existing contracts suspended or dissolved and require a license to sue.
> 2.  **Foreign Sovereigns/Ambassadors:** They generally cannot be sued in Indian courts without their consent or under specific circumstances.
> 3.  **Corporations:** Their contractual capacity is limited by their Memorandum of Association or governing statute; contracts *ultra vires* are void.
> 4.  **Married Women:** While generally competent in India, historically, in England, they had limited contractual capacity.
> 5.  **Professional Incapacity:** In England, certain professionals (like barristers) cannot contract for their professional fees, though this doesn't apply in India.
> 6.  **Insolvents:** Upon adjudication, their property vests in an Official Receiver/Assignee, and they lose the power to deal with that property until discharge.
> 7.  **Felons/Convicts:** Persons undergoing imprisonment are generally incapable of contracting, though this incapacity ceases upon release or parole.
