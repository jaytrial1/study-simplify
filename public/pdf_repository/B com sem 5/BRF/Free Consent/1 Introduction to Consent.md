1_Introduction to Consent

This section introduces the fundamental concept of consent within contract law, emphasizing its importance for a valid agreement, as stipulated by the Indian Contract Act.

# Introduction to Consent

Section 10 of the Indian Contract Act states that "All agreements are contracts if they are made by free consent of the parties..." This means that for an agreement to be valid, it must stem from the *free consent* of the parties involved. Section 13 defines "consent," and Section 14 outlines the conditions under which consent is considered "free."

A contract, even if it appears to have *consensus ad idem* (agreement on the same thing in the same sense), consideration, and parties with capacity, can still be defective due to a flaw in consent. These flaws may arise from:
*   Mistake
*   Misrepresentation
*   Coercion (Duress in English law)
*   Undue influence

## Definition of Consent

Section 13 defines "consent" as:
"Two or more persons are said to consent when they agree upon the same thing in the same sense."

Consent, therefore, requires a *unity of mind*. When parties agree upon the same thing in the same sense, they possess *consensus ad idem*. Without this, there is no contract. Sir John Salmond referred to this as "error in consensus."

**Example:** A tender was submitted by a company with the condition that it would not be able to deposit a bank guarantee. The Board placed a purchase order without referring to the company's terms. It was held that there was no concluded contract as there was no *consensus ad idem*. (Indian Meters Ltd V Punjab State Electricity Board (1993) I SCC 230).

For a valid contract, parties must be *ad idem*. Parties are *not ad idem* in the following cases:
*   When the misunderstanding relates to the identity of the other party to the agreement.
*   When it relates to the nature and terms of the contract.
*   When it relates to the subject matter of the agreement.

If parties are operating under a mistake regarding the identity of a party, the subject matter, or the nature of the transaction, the agreement will be void.

> **Answer:** Consent is defined as two or more persons agreeing upon the same thing in the same sense, signifying a "unity of mind" or "consensus ad idem." Without this shared understanding, a contract cannot be formed.
