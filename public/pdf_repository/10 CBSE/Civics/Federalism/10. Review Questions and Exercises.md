3.  Point out one feature in the practice of federalism in India that is similar to and one feature that is different from that of Belgium.
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Similar:** Both India and Belgium are 'holding together' federations, meaning a large country divided its power between the centre and states/regions. Both also have provisions for accommodating diversity (India through linguistic states and language policy, Belgium through community governments and regional governments).
    > *   **Different:** India has unequal powers for different states (some with special status, Union Territories), while constituent units in Belgium generally have more equal powers. India has a three-tier system (Union, State, Local), while Belgium primarily has two main levels (Federal and Regional/Community).
4.  What is the main difference between a federal form of government and a unitary one? Explain with an example.
    > **Answer:** The main difference is the division of power. In a **federal** system, power is divided between a central government and constituent units (like states or provinces), and both levels have their own independent jurisdiction. Example: India, where the Union government cannot order the state government on State List subjects. In a **unitary** system, either there is only one level of government, or sub-units are subordinate to the central government and must follow its orders. Example: Sri Lanka, where the national government holds all powers.
5.  State any two differences between the local government before and after the Constitutional amendment in 1992.
    > **Answer:**
    > *   Before 1992, elections to local government bodies were not held regularly; after 1992, it became constitutionally mandatory to hold regular elections.
    > *   Before 1992, local governments had very few powers or resources of their own and were directly under state control; after 1992, state governments were required to share some powers and revenue with local government bodies, and seats were reserved for SC, ST, OBC, and women.
6.  Fill in the blanks:
    Since the United States is a $\qquad$ type of federation, all the constituent States have equal powers and States are $\qquad$ vis-à-vis the federal government. But India is a $\qquad$ type of federation and some States have more power than others. In India, the $\qquad$ government has more powers.
    > **Answer:**
    > Since the United States is a **coming together** type of federation, all the constituent States have equal powers and States are **strong** vis-à-vis the federal government. But India is a **holding together** type of federation and some States have more power than others. In India, the **Central** government has more powers.

8.  The distinguishing feature of a federal government is:

    (b) Power is distributed among the legislature, executive and judiciary.
    (c) Elected officials exercise supreme power in the government.

12. Consider the following two statements.

    C. Sri Lanka is a federation because the country is divided into provinces.
    D. India is no longer a federation because some powers of the States have been devolved to the local government bodies.

    Which of the statements given above are correct?
    (a) A, B and C
    (b) A, C and D
    **(c) A and B only**
