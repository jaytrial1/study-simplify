This section contains questions and activities designed to test understanding and encourage reflection on the outcomes of democracy discussed in the chapter.

# End of Chapter Questions and Activities

1.  How does democracy produce an accountable, responsive and legitimate government?
    > **Answer:** Democracy produces an accountable government by establishing mechanisms like regular elections where citizens can choose or remove rulers, allowing for public debates, and ensuring transparency through norms and procedures like the Right to Information. It is responsive because elected representatives need to address the needs and demands of the people to get re-elected, though this is not always perfectly achieved. It is legitimate because it is the people's own government, chosen by them through elections, which generates broad support.

2.  What are the conditions under which democracies accommodate social diversities?
    > **Answer:** Democracies accommodate social diversities successfully when they understand that majority rule is not simply permanent rule by a majority community (based on religion, race, etc.). Instead, it means that in any given decision or election, different groups can form a majority, and the majority needs to work with the minority. Accommodation works when every citizen has a chance to be part of the majority at some point and is not excluded based on birth.

3.  Give arguments to support or oppose the following assertions:

    *   Industrialised countries can afford democracy but the poor need dictatorship to become rich.
        > **Answer:** Oppose. The chapter shows that while dictatorships had slightly higher growth rates overall (Table 1), the difference is negligible in poor countries. Economic development depends on many factors beyond the form of government. Also, democracy offers other valuable outcomes like dignity and freedom, which are not guaranteed under dictatorship, regardless of wealth.

    *   Democracy can't reduce inequality of incomes between different citizens.
        > **Answer:** Support (partially) and Oppose (partially). The chapter presents evidence (Table 2) showing high income inequality in many democracies and states that democracies "do not appear to be very successful in reducing economic inequalities." However, it doesn't say they *can't* reduce it at all, and some democracies show lower inequality than others (Denmark, Hungary vs. South Africa, Brazil in Table 2). The potential exists, but the actual record is weak.

    *   Government in poor countries should spend less on poverty reduction, health, education and spend more on industries and infrastructure.
        > **Answer:** Oppose. While industrial and infrastructure development is important for growth, the chapter highlights that economic growth doesn't automatically reduce inequality or poverty (Table 2, discussion on poverty). Spending on poverty reduction, health, and education directly addresses basic needs and improves human capital, which is crucial for long-term, inclusive development and reducing the very inequalities discussed in the chapter.

    *   In democracy all citizens have one vote, which means that there is absence of any domination and conflict.
        > **Answer:** Oppose. While "one person, one vote" establishes political equality, it does not eliminate domination and conflict in society. The chapter discusses how economic inequalities persist and can lead to the dominance of the rich. It also explains how democracies manage, rather than eliminate, social conflicts arising from differences. Having a vote provides the *means* to challenge domination and participate in conflict resolution, but doesn't automatically remove them.

4.  Identify the challenges to democracy in the following descriptions. Also suggest policy/institutional mechanism to deepen democracy in the given situations:

    *   Following a High Court directive, a temple in Orissa that had separate entry doors for dalits and non-dalits allowed entry for all from the same door.
        > **Answer:** Challenge: **Social inequality and discrimination** based on caste, which violates the principle of dignity and equal treatment. Mechanism: Judicial intervention (High Court directive) is a key institutional mechanism. Policy mechanisms could include strict enforcement of anti-discrimination laws, public awareness campaigns promoting social equality, and government schemes supporting the upliftment and equal access for disadvantaged castes.

    *   A large number of farmers are committing suicide in different states of India.
        > **Answer:** Challenge: **Economic distress and lack of responsiveness** of the government to the needs of a specific large section of the population (farmers), potentially linked to agricultural policies, debt, or market failures. Mechanism: Policies addressing agricultural debt relief, ensuring fair prices for produce (e.g., Minimum Support Price), improving irrigation and infrastructure, providing access to affordable credit and insurance, and establishing support systems for distressed farmers. Institutional mechanisms could include farmer cooperatives and accessible grievance redressal systems.

    *   Following an allegation of killing of three civilians in Gandwara in a fake encounter by Jammu and Kashmir police, an enquiry has been ordered.
        > **Answer:** Challenge: **Abuse of power by state institutions** (police) and potential violation of citizen's rights and dignity. Challenge to **accountability**. Mechanism: The ordering of an enquiry is an important institutional mechanism for ensuring accountability. Further mechanisms include independent judicial oversight, establishing civilian review boards for police conduct, ensuring transparency in investigations, providing legal aid to victims' families, and reforming police training and accountability structures.

5.  In the context of democracies, which of the following ideas is correct—democracies have successfully eliminated:
    A. conflicts among people
    B. economic inequalities among people
    C. differences of opinion about how marginalised sections are to be treated
    D. the idea of political inequality
    > **Answer:** D. the idea of political inequality. The chapter states democracy promotes political equality ("All individuals have equal weight in electing representatives"). It explicitly says democracies *haven't* eliminated conflicts (they manage them), economic inequalities (they persist), or differences of opinion.

6.  In the context of assessing democracy, which among the following is the odd one out. Democracies need to ensure:
    A. free and fair elections
    B. dignity of the individual
    C. majority rule
    D. equal treatment before law
    > **Answer:** C. majority rule. While majority rule is a characteristic of democracy, the chapter emphasizes that democracy is *not simply* majority rule, and successful democracy requires the majority to work with the minority and ensure everyone has a chance to be in the majority. Free and fair elections, dignity of the individual, and equal treatment before law are presented as fundamental requirements or outcomes democracy *must* ensure or strive for.

7.  Studies on political and social inequalities in democracy show that:
    A. democracy and development go together.
    B. inequalities exist in democracies.
    C. inequalities do not exist under dictatorship.
    D. dictatorship is better than democracy.
    > **Answer:** B. inequalities exist in democracies. The chapter explicitly discusses economic and social inequalities (caste, gender) that persist in democracies, even though the principle of equality is recognised. Option A is not strongly supported; the chapter shows a mixed record on economic growth. Option C is not supported; the chapter doesn't claim inequalities don't exist under dictatorships, and implies they might suppress differences. Option D is refuted by the chapter's overall argument for democracy's superiority in key areas like legitimacy, dignity, and managing social conflict.

8.  Read the passage below:

    Nannu is a daily wage earner. He lives in Welcome Mazdoor Colony, a slum habitation in East Delhi. He lost his ration card and applied for a duplicate one in January 2004. He made several rounds to the local Food and Civil Supplies office for the next three months. But the clerks and officials would not even look at him, let alone do his job or bother to tell him the status of his application. Ultimately, he filed an application under the Right to Information Act asking for the daily progress made on his application, names of the officials, who were supposed to act on his application and what action would be taken against these officials for their inaction. Within a week of filing application under the Right to Information Act, he was visited by an inspector from the Food Department, who informed him that the card had been made and he could collect it from the office. When Nannu went to collect his card next day, he was given a very warm treatment by the Food and Supply Officer (FSO), who is the head of a Circle. The FSO offered him tea and requested him to withdraw his application under the Right to Information, since his work had already been done.
    What does Nannu's example show? What impact did Nannu's action have on officials? Ask your parents their experiences when they approach government officials to attend to their problems.
    > **Answer:** Nannu's example shows the importance and effectiveness of the **Right to Information (RTI) Act** as a tool for citizens to ensure **accountability and responsiveness** from the government administration. It demonstrates that despite facing indifference and inefficiency from officials initially, a citizen can use legal mechanisms provided by democracy to demand transparency and action.
    > Nannu's action had a significant impact on the officials. It prompted immediate action on his pending application, which had been ignored for months. The officials, including the head of the office (FSO), became suddenly responsive and even tried to appease him (offering tea, requesting withdrawal of RTI application) to avoid scrutiny and potential consequences for their inaction. This shows that the RTI Act can make officials accountable and change their behaviour towards citizens.
    > *(Note: The prompt also includes an activity "Ask your parents their experiences...", which is for the student reader to perform and cannot be answered here).*
