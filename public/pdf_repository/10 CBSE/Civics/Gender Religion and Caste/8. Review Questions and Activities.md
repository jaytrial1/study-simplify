This section contains questions and activities to test understanding of the chapter's concepts on gender, religion, and caste in politics.

1. Mention different aspects of life in which women are discriminated or disadvantaged in India.

> **Answer:** Women face discrimination and disadvantage in India in various aspects of life, including lower literacy rates and educational attainment compared to men, underrepresentation in highly paid and valued jobs, lower wages than men for the same work, a skewed child sex ratio due to sex-selective abortions, and various forms of harassment, exploitation, and violence both inside and outside the home.

2. State different forms of communal politics with one example each.

> **Answer:** Different forms of communal politics mentioned are:
> *   Everyday beliefs involving religious prejudices and stereotypes (Example: Believing one's own religion is superior to others).
> *   Quest for political dominance of one's religious community (Example: Majority community seeking majoritarian dominance).
> *   Political mobilisation on religious lines using sacred symbols and emotional appeals (Example: A political party using religious leaders or symbols in campaigns to appeal specifically to voters of one religion).
> *   Communal violence, riots, and massacre (Example: Large scale riots between religious groups during Partition or in post-Independence India).

3. State how caste inequalities are still continuing in India.

> **Answer:** Caste inequalities are still continuing in India in several ways:
> *   Most people still marry within their own caste or tribe.
> *   Untouchability has not completely ended despite being constitutionally prohibited.
> *   Caste groups that had historical access to education continue to do well, while those denied access still lag behind.
> *   There is a disproportionately large presence of 'upper caste' among the urban middle classes.
> *   Caste remains strongly linked to economic status, with lower castes having higher proportions of poverty and lower representation among the rich compared to upper castes.

4. State two reasons to say that caste alone cannot determine election results in India.

> **Answer:** Two reasons why caste alone cannot determine election results in India are:
> *   No parliamentary constituency has a clear majority of a single caste, requiring candidates to appeal to multiple caste and community groups.
> *   Voters within the same caste or community often have different interests based on their economic condition, gender, etc., and do not vote as a single bloc; other factors like party attachment, government performance, and leader popularity also influence voting decisions.

5. What is the status of women's representation in India's legislative bodies?

> **Answer:** Women's representation in India's legislative bodies is very low. The percentage of women elected to the Lok Sabha reached 14.36% in 2019 for the first time, and their share in state assemblies is less than 5%. India is among the bottom group of nations globally in this regard, although the recent Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam (2023) aims to reserve 33% of seats for women in the Lok Sabha and state assemblies in the future.

6. Mention any two constitutional provisions that make India a secular state.

> **Answer:** Two constitutional provisions that make India a secular state are:
> *   There is no official religion for the Indian state; the Constitution does not give special status to any religion.
>   *   The Constitution provides freedom to all individuals and communities to profess, practice, and propagate any religion, or not to follow any. (Also acceptable: The Constitution prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion; The Constitution allows state intervention in religious matters to ensure equality within communities, e.g., banning untouchability).

7. When we speak of gender divisions, we usually refer to:
(a) Biological difference between men and women
(b) Unequal roles assigned by the society to men and women
(c) Unequal child sex ratio
(d) Absence of voting rights for women in democracies

> **Answer:** (b) Unequal roles assigned by the society to men and women

8. In India seats are reserved for women in
(a) Lok Sabha
(b) State legislative assemblies
(c) Cabinets
(d) Panchayati Raj bodies

> **Answer:** (d) Panchayati Raj bodies (Note: The text mentions the 2023 Act for Lok Sabha/Assemblies, but reservation has been implemented in Panchayati Raj bodies for a longer period, as stated earlier in the text).

9. Consider the following statements on the meaning of communal politics. Communal politics is based on the belief that:
A. One religion is superior to that of others.
B. People belonging to different religions can live together happily as equal citizens.
C. Followers of a particular religion constitute one community.
D. State power cannot be used to establish the domination of one religious group over others.
Which of the statements are correct?
(a) A, B, C, and D
(b) A, B, and D
(c) A and C
(d) B and D

> **Answer:** (c) A and C (Statement B contradicts the communal belief system described, and Statement D describes a secular principle, not a communal one).

10. Which among the following statements about India's Constitution is wrong? It
(a) prohibits discrimination on grounds of religion.
(b) gives official status to one religion.
(c) provides to all individuals freedom to profess any religion.
(d) ensures equality of citizens within religious communities.

> **Answer:** (b) gives official status to one religion. (The text explicitly states the Indian Constitution does NOT give special or official status to any religion).
