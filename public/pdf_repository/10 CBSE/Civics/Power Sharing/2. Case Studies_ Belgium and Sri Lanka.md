# Belgium and Sri Lanka: Contrasting Approaches to Diversity

Belgium is a small European country with a complex **ethnic** composition. 59% of the population lives in the Flemish region and speaks Dutch, 40% live in the Wallonia region and speak French, and the remaining 1% speak German. In the capital, Brussels, 80% speak French, and 20% are Dutch-speaking. The minority French-speaking community was historically richer and more powerful, causing resentment among the Dutch-speaking community, who benefited from economic development and education later. This led to tensions, particularly in Brussels, where the Dutch were a minority despite being a majority nationally.

Sri Lanka, an island nation near India, also has a diverse population. The major groups are Sinhala-speakers (74%) and Tamil-speakers (18%). Among Tamils, there are Sri Lankan Tamils (13%, natives) and Indian Tamils (whose ancestors came from India). Sri Lankan Tamils are concentrated in the north and east. Most Sinhala speakers are Buddhists, while most Tamils are Hindus or Muslims. About 7% Christians are both Tamil and Sinhala.
