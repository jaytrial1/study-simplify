# Accommodation in Belgium

Belgian leaders chose a different path, recognizing regional differences and cultural diversities. Between 1970 and 1993, they amended their constitution four times to create arrangements for coexistence. This Belgian model is innovative and unique:

*   **Equal Representation in Central Government:** The constitution mandates an equal number of Dutch and French-speaking ministers in the central government. Special laws require support from a majority of members from each linguistic group, preventing unilateral decisions by one community.
*   **State Governments:** Many central government powers were given to state governments for the two regions. These state governments are not subordinate to the central government.
*   **Brussels Government:** Brussels has a separate government with equal representation for both communities. French speakers accepted this equality in Brussels because Dutch speakers accepted equal representation in the central government.
*   **Community Government:** A third type of government, the 'community government', is elected by people belonging to one language community (Dutch, French, or German), regardless of where they live. This government handles cultural, educational, and language-related issues.

When European countries formed the European Union, Brussels was chosen as its headquarters, partly due to its successful model of accommodating diversity.
