# Applying Power Sharing Concepts

Here are some examples of power sharing. Which of the four types of power sharing do these represent? Who is sharing power with whom?

-   The Bombay High Court ordered the Maharashtra State Government to immediately take action and improve living conditions for the 2,000-odd children at seven children's homes in Mumbai.
    > **Answer:** This is **Horizontal Distribution of Power** (Checks and Balances). The Judiciary (High Court) is checking and influencing the actions of the Executive (State Government).
-   The government of Ontario state in Canada has agreed to a land claim settlement with the aboriginal community. The Minister responsible for Native Affairs announced that the government will work with aboriginal people in a spirit of mutual respect and cooperation.
    > **Answer:** This is **Power Sharing Among Different Social Groups**. The government is sharing power or accommodating the demands and interests of an aboriginal community (a social group).
-   Russia's two influential political parties, the Union of Right Forces and the Liberal Yabloko Movement, agreed to unite their organisations into a strong rightwing coalition. They propose to have a common list of candidates in the next parliamentary elections.
    > **Answer:** This is **Power Sharing Through Political Parties**. Two political parties are forming an alliance (potentially for a coalition government or to influence power).
-   The finance ministers of various states in Nigeria got together and demanded that the federal government declare its sources of income. They also wanted to know the formula by which the revenue is distributed to various State Governments.
    > **Answer:** This is **Vertical Division of Power** (Federalism). State governments are asserting their role and seeking transparency/influence regarding financial power held by the federal government.

1.  What are the different forms of power sharing in modern democracies? Give an example of each of these.
    > **Answer:** The four different forms of power sharing are:
    > 1.  **Horizontal Distribution of Power:** Power shared among legislature, executive, and judiciary (e.g., checks and balances system where courts can review laws made by the legislature).
    > 2.  **Vertical Division of Power:** Power shared among governments at different levels - central, state, local (e.g., the Union Government and State Governments in India, with powers defined by the Constitution).
    > 3.  **Power Sharing Among Different Social Groups:** Power shared among religious, linguistic, or ethnic groups (e.g., reserved constituencies in India for Scheduled Castes and Tribes).
    > 4.  **Power Sharing Through Political Parties, Pressure Groups, and Movements:** Power is shared through competition among parties, alliances forming coalition governments, or interest groups influencing policy (e.g., a coalition government formed by multiple parties after an election).

2.  State one prudential reason and one moral reason for power sharing with an example from the Indian context.
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Prudential Reason:** Power sharing helps reduce conflict between social groups. *Example:* In India, the system of reserved constituencies for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes ensures representation for these groups in legislatures, helping to integrate them into the political system and reduce potential social tensions.
    > *   **Moral Reason:** Power sharing is the spirit of democracy; citizens have a right to participate in their governance. *Example:* The Panchayati Raj system in India gives power to local self-governments at the village level, allowing ordinary citizens to directly participate in decision-making about local development and governance.

4.  The Mayor of Merchtem, a town near Brussels in Belgium, has defended a ban on speaking French in the town's schools. He said that the ban would help all non-Dutch speakers integrate in this Flemish town. Do you think that this measure is in keeping with the spirit of Belgium's power sharing arrangements? Give your reasons in about 50 words.
    > **Answer:** No, this measure is *not* in keeping with the spirit of Belgium's power-sharing arrangements. The Belgian model is based on *recognizing and accommodating* linguistic diversity through arrangements like community governments and equal representation. A ban on speaking a language spoken by a significant community goes against this spirit of mutual respect and accommodation, potentially causing alienation rather than integration.
