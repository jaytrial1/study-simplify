# Khalil's Dilemma

In Beirut, Lebanon, Khalil's parents were from different communities (Orthodox Christian father, Sunni Muslim mother). Lebanon experienced a bitter civil war. After the war, leaders agreed on power-sharing rules based on community: President must be Maronite Christian, Prime Minister Sunni Muslim, Deputy Prime Minister Orthodox Christian, and Speaker Shi'a Muslim. Christians agreed not to seek French protection, and Muslims not to seek unification with Syria. This pact was made when populations were roughly equal and has been respected even though Muslims are now a clear majority.

Khalil, a popular man with political ambition, dislikes this system because top positions are restricted by community, and he doesn't identify strongly with either parent's religion. He wants Lebanon to be a "normal" democracy where anyone can contest elections, and the winner becomes president based on votes, regardless of community. His elders, who lived through the civil war bloodshed, believe the current system guarantees peace.

Vetal asks Vikram: If you could rewrite Lebanon's rules, would you adopt Khalil's 'regular' rules, stick to the old rules, or do something else?
