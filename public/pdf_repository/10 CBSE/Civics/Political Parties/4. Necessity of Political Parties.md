# Necessity of Political Parties

The list of functions performed by parties demonstrates their importance. However, the question remains: why are modern democracies *necessary* for political parties?

We can understand this necessity by imagining a democracy without parties. Every election candidate would be independent. Without parties to group opinions and propose major policy directions, independent candidates could not make promises about large-scale policy changes. A government might form, but its stability and effectiveness would be uncertain. Elected representatives would be accountable only to their local constituency, with no single entity responsible for running the entire country.

Consider non-party based local elections, like panchayat elections in India. Even without formal parties, villages often divide into factions, each presenting a 'panel' of candidates. This informal grouping functions much like a political party. This is why political parties are found in almost all countries, regardless of size, age, or development level.

The rise of political parties is directly linked to the emergence of representative democracies. As societies became large and complex, they needed ways to:
*   Gather diverse views on issues and present them to the government.
*   Bring representatives together to form a responsible government.
*   Support or restrain the government, make and justify policies, or oppose them.

Political parties fulfill these essential needs of representative governments. Thus, parties are a necessary condition for democracy.
