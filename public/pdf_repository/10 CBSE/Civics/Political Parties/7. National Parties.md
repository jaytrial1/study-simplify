# National Parties

In federal systems like India, there are typically two types of parties: those limited to one federal unit (states) and those present across several or all units. In India, the latter are called **national parties**. These parties have state-level units that generally follow policies, programs, and strategies decided at the national level.

Every party must register with the Election Commission. The Commission gives special facilities to large, established parties, recognizing them. **Recognised political parties** receive a unique election symbol that only their official candidates can use.

The Election Commission sets criteria for recognition based on votes and seats secured:
*   A party is recognized as a **State party** if it secures at least six per cent of the total votes in an election to the Legislative Assembly of a State and wins at least two seats.
*   A party is recognized as a **national party** if it secures at least six per cent of the total votes in Lok Sabha elections or Assembly elections in four States and wins at least four seats in the Lok Sabha.

As of the Election Commission notification in 2023, there are six recognized national parties in India.

Here are brief profiles of some national parties mentioned:

*   **Aam Aadmi Party (AAP):** Formed in 2012 following the anti-corruption movement. Focuses on accountability, clean administration, transparency, and good governance. Formed governments in Delhi and Punjab. Secured one seat in the 2019 Lok Sabha election. Recognized as a national party.

*   **Bahujan Samaj Party (BSP):** Formed in 1984 under Kanshi Ram. Aims to represent and secure power for the *bahujan samaj* (Dalits, Adivasis, OBCs, minorities). Inspired by Sahu Maharaj, Mahatma Phule, Periyar, and Ambedkar. Works for the interests and welfare of oppressed people. Strong base in Uttar Pradesh and neighbouring states. Formed UP government multiple times. Polled 3.63% votes and won 10 seats in the 2019 Lok Sabha elections. Recognized as a national party.

*   **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP):** Founded in 1980, reviving the Bharatiya Jana Sangh (1951). Aims for a strong, modern India based on ancient culture and values, and Deendayal Upadhyaya's integral humanism and Antyodaya. **Cultural nationalism** ('Hindutva') is important to its identity. Supports full integration of Jammu & Kashmir, a uniform civil code, and a ban on religious conversions. Grew significantly in the 1990s, expanding beyond north/west and urban areas. Led the National Democratic Alliance (NDA) government from 1998. Emerged as the largest party with 303 seats in the 2019 Lok Sabha elections and currently leads the NDA government. Recognized as a national party.

*   **Communist Party of India - Marxist (CPI-M):** Founded in 1964. Believes in Marxism-Leninism, supporting socialism, secularism, and democracy while opposing imperialism and communalism. Accepts democratic elections as a means for socio-economic justice. Strong support in West Bengal, Kerala, and Tripura, especially among the poor, workers, farmers, and intelligentsia. Critical of new economic policies allowing foreign capital/goods. Ruled West Bengal for 34 years. Won 1.75% votes and 3 seats in the 2019 Lok Sabha elections. Recognized as a national party.

*   **Indian National Congress (INC):** Known as the Congress Party. Founded in 1885, one of the world's oldest parties, has experienced splits. Dominant in Indian politics nationally and in states for decades after Independence. Under Jawaharlal Nehru, aimed to build a modern secular democratic republic. Ruled federally until 1977, and from 1980-1989. Support declined after 1989 but remains present nationwide, cutting across social divisions. A **centrist** party ideologically, advocating secularism and welfare for weaker sections/minorities. Supports new economic reforms with a 'human face'. Led the United Progressive Alliance (UPA) government from 2004 to 2014. Won 19.5% votes and 52 seats in the 2019 Lok Sabha election. Recognized as a national party.

*   **National People's Party (NPP):** Formed in 2013 under P.A Sangma. The first national party from North East India. Believes in India's diversity and regional development challenges. Core philosophy: education, employment, and empowerment for all sections. Formed government in Meghalaya and present in many North Eastern States. Secured one seat in the 2019 Lok Sabha election. Recognized as a national party.
