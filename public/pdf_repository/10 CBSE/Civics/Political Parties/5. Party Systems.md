# Party Systems

Any group of citizens in a democracy is free to form a political party. In India, over 750 parties are registered with the Election Commission, but only a few are serious contenders. The question is: how many major or effective parties are beneficial for a democracy?

Some countries have **one-party systems**, where only one party is allowed to control and run the government. China, where only the Communist Party rules, is an example. Although forming other parties might be legally possible, the electoral system prevents free competition. This is not considered a democratic option, as a democratic system requires at least two parties to compete fairly for power.

In **two-party systems**, power typically alternates between two main parties. Other parties may exist and win some legislative seats, but only the two major parties have a realistic chance of winning a majority to form a government. Examples include the United States and the United Kingdom.

A **multiparty system** exists when several parties compete for power, and more than two parties have a reasonable chance of coming to power, either alone or in alliance. India has a multiparty system. In this system, governments are often formed by **coalitions** of multiple parties. When several parties in a multiparty system join hands to contest elections and win power, it is called an **alliance** or a **front**. Examples in India include the National Democratic Alliance (NDA), the United Progressive Alliance (UPA), and the Left Front.

A multiparty system can appear messy and potentially lead to political instability. However, it also allows a wider variety of interests and opinions to be politically represented.

Which system is better is not a simple question. A party system is not chosen but evolves over time based on a country's society, social and regional divisions, political history, and electoral system. These factors cannot be changed quickly. India's multiparty system, for instance, evolved because its vast social and geographical diversity cannot be easily represented by just two or three parties. No single system is ideal for all countries or situations.
