This section discusses parties recognized at the state level in India, often called regional parties, and their increasing influence in national politics, particularly in forming coalitions.

# State Parties

Parties not recognized as national parties are classified by the Election Commission as **State parties**. These are commonly referred to as **regional parties**. However, not all state parties are regional in their ideology; some are all-India parties that have succeeded only in certain states. Parties like the Samajwadi Party and Rashtriya Janata Dal have national-level organizations but are primarily recognized at the state level. Some parties, such as Biju Janata Dal, Sikkim Democratic Front, Mizo National Front, and Telangana Rashtra Samithi, strongly identify with their specific state.

Over the last three decades, the number and strength of these state parties have grown, leading to a more diverse Parliament. Until 2014, no single national party could secure a majority in the Lok Sabha on its own. This compelled national parties to form alliances with state parties. Since 1996, most state parties have had the opportunity to join national-level coalition governments. This trend has contributed to strengthening both **federalism** and **democracy** in India.
