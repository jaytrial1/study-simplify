This section details the various roles and activities that political parties perform in a democratic system.

# Functions of Political Parties

Political parties serve to fill political offices and exercise political power by performing a series of key functions:

1.  **Contest Elections:** In most democracies, elections are primarily contested by candidates nominated by political parties. Parties have different methods for selecting candidates; in some countries like the USA, members and supporters choose candidates, while in countries like India, top party leaders make these selections.
2.  **Put Forward Policies and Programmes:** Parties present different policies and programs for voters to choose from. They consolidate diverse opinions into a few basic positions, providing a direction for government policy. The **ruling party** is expected to base its policies on its stated positions.
3.  **Make Laws:** Parties play a decisive role in law-making. Although laws are debated and passed in the legislature, members generally vote according to the party leadership's direction, often regardless of personal opinion.
4.  **Form and Run Governments:** Parties recruit and train leaders, then appoint them as ministers to run the government. Major policy decisions are made by the political executive, which is drawn from the political parties.
5.  **Play the Role of Opposition:** Parties that lose elections act as the opposition. They voice different views, criticize the government's failures or wrong policies, and mobilize opposition.
6.  **Shape Public Opinion:** Parties raise and highlight issues, using their widespread network of members and activists. Pressure groups are often linked to political parties. Parties may also launch movements to address public problems. Opinions in society often form along party lines.
7.  **Provide Access to Government and Welfare Schemes:** Parties offer ordinary citizens easier access to government machinery and welfare programs, often through local party leaders. This connection leads people to feel closer to parties, even if they don't fully trust them. Parties must be responsive to public needs to avoid being rejected in elections.

**Glossary**

*   **Ruling Party:** Political party that runs the government.
