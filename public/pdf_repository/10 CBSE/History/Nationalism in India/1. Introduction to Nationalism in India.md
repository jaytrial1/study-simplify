# The Rise of Nationalism in India

As observed in Europe, modern nationalism became intrinsically linked with the formation of nation-states, profoundly altering people's self-perception, identity, and sense of belonging. New symbols, icons, songs, and ideas forged fresh connections and redefined community boundaries. The development of this national identity was a protracted process in most countries. In India, similar to other colonies, the evolution of modern nationalism was deeply intertwined with the anti-colonial struggle.

People began to discover their collective unity through their shared struggle against colonialism. The pervasive feeling of being oppressed under colonial rule created a common bond that brought together many disparate groups. However, each class and social group experienced the effects of colonialism differently, leading to varied experiences and often divergent ideas about what 'freedom' truly meant. The Indian National Congress, under the leadership of Mahatma Gandhi, endeavored to unite these diverse groups within a single, cohesive movement. Yet, this unity was not achieved without significant internal conflicts and challenges.

This chapter will delve into the narrative of nationalism in India from the 1920s onwards, examining the pivotal Non-Cooperation and Civil Disobedience Movements. We will explore the strategies employed by the Congress to expand the national movement, the ways in which different social groups participated, and how the concept of nationalism resonated with the Indian populace.
