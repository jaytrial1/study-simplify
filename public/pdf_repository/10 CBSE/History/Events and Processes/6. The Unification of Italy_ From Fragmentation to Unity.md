This section details the complex process of Italian unification, highlighting the roles of key figures like Mazzini, Cavour, and Garibaldi, and the challenges faced in transforming a fragmented peninsula into a unified nation-state.

## Italy Unified

Like Germany, Italy too had a long history of political fragmentation. Italians were scattered over several dynastic states as well as the multi-national Habsburg Empire. During the middle of the nineteenth century, Italy was divided into seven states, of which only one, Sardinia-Piedmont, was ruled by an Italian princely house. The north was under Austrian Habsburgs, the centre was ruled by the Pope and the southern regions were under the domination of the Bourbon kings of Spain. Even the Italian language had not acquired one common form and still had many regional and local variations.

During the 1830s, Giuseppe Mazzini had sought to put together a coherent programme for a unitary Italian Republic. He had also formed a secret society called Young Italy for the dissemination of his goals. The failure of revolutionary uprisings both in 1831 and 1848 meant that the mantle now fell on Sardinia-Piedmont under its ruler King Victor Emmanuel II to unify the Italian states through war. In the eyes of the ruling elites of this region, a unified Italy offered them the possibility of economic development and political dominance.

### Activity

Describe the caricature. How does it represent the relationship between Bismarck and the elected deputies of Parliament? What interpretation of democratic processes is the artist trying to convey?
> **Answer:**
> *   **Description of the Caricature:** The caricature depicts Otto von Bismarck, holding a whip menacingly, standing over a group of cowering, fearful, and submissive elected deputies in what appears to be a parliament. The deputies are shown in various states of distress, some hiding their faces, others shrinking away, clearly intimidated by Bismarck's presence.
> *   **Relationship between Bismarck and Deputies:** It represents Bismarck as an authoritarian and dominant figure who wields immense power over the elected parliament. The whip symbolizes his ability to coerce, intimidate, and control the deputies, forcing them into submission rather than allowing for independent debate or decision-making. The deputies are portrayed as powerless and fearful, lacking agency in the face of his will.
> *   **Interpretation of Democratic Processes:** The artist is trying to convey that democratic processes, such as parliamentary debate and representation, are being undermined or are merely a facade under Bismarck's rule. Instead of a system where elected representatives genuinely deliberate and make decisions, it suggests a system where one powerful individual dictates terms, effectively rendering the parliament a rubber stamp. It highlights a lack of true democratic participation and the suppression of dissent.

Chief Minister Cavour who led the movement to unify the regions of Italy was neither a revolutionary nor a democrat. Like many other wealthy and educated members of the Italian elite, he spoke French much better than he did Italian. Through a tactful diplomatic alliance with France engineered by Cavour, Sardinia-Piedmont succeeded in defeating the Austrian forces in 1859. Apart from regular troops, a large number of armed volunteers under the leadership of Giuseppe Garibaldi joined the fray. In 1860, they marched into South Italy and the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies and succeeded in winning the support of the local peasants in order to drive out the Spanish rulers. In 1861 Victor Emmanuel II was proclaimed king of united Italy. However, much of the Italian population, among whom rates of illiteracy were very high, remained blissfully unaware of liberal-nationalist ideology. The peasant masses who had supported Garibaldi in southern Italy had never heard of Italia, and believed that 'La Talia' was Victor Emmanuel's wife!

### Activity

Look at Fig. 14(a). Do you think that the people living in any of these regions thought of themselves as Italians?
> **Answer:** Given the political fragmentation, diverse rulers (Austrian Habsburgs, Pope, Bourbon kings of Spain), and numerous regional and local variations in language, it is highly unlikely that most people living in these regions in 1858 thought of themselves primarily as "Italians." Their primary identities would have been regional (e.g., Lombard, Venetian, Tuscan, Neapolitan, Sicilian) or tied to their local ruler. The concept of a unified "Italia" was still largely an aspiration among a smaller, educated elite.

### Activity

Examine Fig. 14(b). Which was the first region to become a part of unified Italy? Which was the last region to join? In which year did the largest number of states join?
> **Answer:**
> *   **First region to become part of unified Italy:** Sardinia-Piedmont (already unified by 1858, and spearheaded the unification).
> *   **Last region to join:** Rome and the remaining Papal States (joined in 1870).
> *   **Year with the largest number of states joining:** 1860 (when the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies and central Italian states like Tuscany and parts of the Papal States joined).
