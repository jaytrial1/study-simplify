### Write in brief

1.  Write a note on:
    a) **Giuseppe Mazzini:** An Italian revolutionary born in Genoa in 1805. He was a member of the Carbonari secret society and was exiled in 1831 for attempting a revolution. He subsequently founded "Young Italy" in Marseilles and "Young Europe" in Berne. Mazzini believed that nations were natural units intended by God and advocated for a unified Italian Republic within a wider alliance of nations. His vision of democratic republics and opposition to monarchy made him a "most dangerous enemy of our social order" to conservatives like Metternich.
    b) **Count Camillo de Cavour:** The Chief Minister of Sardinia-Piedmont who led the movement to unify Italy. He was neither a revolutionary nor a democrat but a shrewd politician who spoke French better than Italian. Through a tactful diplomatic alliance with France, Cavour engineered the defeat of Austrian forces in 1859, paving the way for Italian unification under King Victor Emmanuel II.
    c) **The Greek War of Independence:** A struggle for independence by Greeks against the Ottoman Empire, beginning in 1821. It mobilized nationalist feelings across Europe, drawing support from exiled Greeks and West Europeans who admired ancient Greek culture. Poets and artists, like Lord Byron, championed the Greek cause. The war culminated in the Treaty of Constantinople of 1832, which recognized Greece as an independent nation.
    d) **Frankfurt Parliament:** An all-German National Assembly formed in 1848 by middle-class professionals, businessmen, and prosperous artisans in the city of Frankfurt. Convened in the Church of St Paul, its 831 elected representatives drafted a constitution for a unified German nation to be headed by a monarchy subject to a parliament. However, their offer of the crown was rejected by the King of Prussia, and the parliament, losing support from workers and artisans due to its middle-class dominance, was eventually disbanded by troops.
    e) **The role of women in nationalist struggles:** Women played a significant and active role in nationalist struggles across Europe, particularly during the 1848 revolutions. They formed their own political associations, founded newspapers, and participated in political meetings and demonstrations. Despite their active involvement and contributions, they were largely denied political rights, including suffrage, in assemblies like the Frankfurt Parliament, where they were only admitted as observers.

2.  **What steps did the French revolutionaries take to create a sense of collective identity among the French people?**
    > **Answer:** The French revolutionaries took several steps to create a sense of collective identity:
    > *   Introduced the ideas of *la patrie* (the fatherland) and *le citoyen* (the citizen), emphasizing a united community with equal rights.
    > *   Adopted a new French tricolour flag, replacing the old royal standard.
    > *   Renamed the Estates General as the National Assembly, elected by active citizens.
    > *   Composed new hymns, took oaths, and commemorated martyrs in the name of the nation.
    > *   Established a centralized administrative system with uniform laws for all citizens.
    > *   Abolished internal customs duties and adopted a uniform system of weights and measures.
    > *   Discouraged regional dialects and promoted French as the common language.

3.  **Who were Marianne and Germania? What was the importance of the way in which they were portrayed?**
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Marianne** was the female allegory for the French nation. She was a popular Christian name chosen to represent the idea of a people's nation. Her characteristics were drawn from Liberty and the Republic, featuring the red cap, the tricolour, and the cockade.
    > *   **Germania** was the female allegory for the German nation. She was typically portrayed wearing a crown of oak leaves, as the German oak symbolised heroism.
    > *   **Importance of their portrayal:** The portrayal of nations as female figures was important because it provided an abstract idea (the nation) with a concrete, recognisable form. These allegories served as powerful national symbols, reminding the public of national unity and encouraging identification with the nation. They were used on statues, coins, stamps, and paintings to popularise and solidify nationalist sentiments among the populace.

4.  **Briefly trace the process of German unification.**
    > **Answer:** German unification was a process led by Prussia, moving away from liberal-democratic initiatives. After the failed Frankfurt Parliament of 1848, Prussia's Chief Minister, Otto von Bismarck, took charge. He was the architect of unification, using the Prussian army and bureaucracy. The process involved three wars over seven years:
    > *   Against Denmark (1864)
    > *   Against Austria (1866)
    > *   Against France (1870-1871)
    > These wars resulted in Prussian victories, consolidating German states under Prussian dominance. Finally, in January 1871, Prussian King William I was proclaimed German Emperor in the Hall of Mirrors at Versailles, marking the establishment of the new German Empire.

5.  **What changes did Napoleon introduce to make the administrative system more efficient in the territories ruled by him?**
    > **Answer:** Napoleon introduced several reforms to make the administrative system more efficient in the territories he controlled:
    > *   He introduced the **Civil Code of 1804 (Napoleonic Code)**, which abolished privileges based on birth, established equality before the law, and secured the right to property.
    > *   He simplified administrative divisions.
    > *   He abolished the feudal system and freed peasants from serfdom and manorial dues.
    > *   In towns, guild restrictions were removed, allowing for greater economic freedom.
    > *   He improved transport and communication systems.
    > *   He introduced uniform laws, standardised weights and measures, and a common national currency, which facilitated the movement of goods and capital.

### Discuss

1.  **Explain what is meant by the 1848 revolution of the liberals. What were the political, social and economic ideas supported by the liberals?**
    > **Answer:** The 1848 revolution of the liberals refers to a series of widespread uprisings across Europe (e.g., in German states, Italy, Austro-Hungarian Empire) that occurred parallel to revolts by the poor and unemployed. These revolutions were primarily led by the educated middle classes who demanded constitutionalism and national unification.
    >
    > **Ideas supported by liberals in 1848:**
    > *   **Political Ideas:**
    >     *   Establishment of nation-states based on parliamentary principles.
    >     *   Demands for a constitution.
    >     *   Freedom of the press.
    >     *   Freedom of association (to form political groups).
    >     *   Government by consent.
    >     *   End of autocracy and clerical privileges.
    >     *   However, they often advocated for limited suffrage, typically for property-owning men, excluding women and non-propertied men.
    > *   **Social Ideas:**
    >     *   Abolition of aristocratic privileges.
    >     *   Equality before the law for all (though this often had limitations in practice, particularly regarding gender).
    > *   **Economic Ideas:**
    >     *   Freedom of markets.
    >     *   Abolition of state-imposed restrictions on the movement of goods and capital (e.g., customs duties).
    >     *   Creation of unified economic territories for unhindered trade.

2.  **Choose three examples to show the contribution of culture to the growth of nationalism in Europe.**
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Romanticism and Folk Culture:** Romantic artists and philosophers, like Johann Gottfried Herder, emphasized emotions, intuition, and mystical feelings over reason. They sought to discover "true German culture" among the common people (*das volk*) through folk songs, folk poetry, and folk dances. Collecting and popularizing these forms of folk culture helped to build a sense of shared collective heritage and the "true spirit of the nation" (*volksgeist*), which became a basis for national identity.
    > *   **Language as a Tool of Resistance:** In partitioned Poland, where it no longer existed as an independent territory, national feelings were kept alive through language. After Russian occupation, the Polish language was forced out of schools. However, Polish clergy used their language for Church gatherings and religious instruction, defying Russian authorities. This act of linguistic resistance became a powerful symbol of the struggle against Russian dominance and contributed significantly to maintaining Polish national identity.
    > *   **Music and National Symbols:** Karol Kurpinski, a Polish composer, celebrated the national struggle through his operas and music. He transformed folk dances like the polonaise and mazurka into nationalist symbols. This use of traditional music and dance forms helped to keep national feelings alive and mobilise people, even in the absence of a sovereign state, by associating cultural expressions with the national cause.

3.  **Through a focus on any two countries, explain how nations developed over the nineteenth century.**
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Germany:** In the early 19th century, Germany was a fragmented collection of numerous states and principalities. Nationalist sentiments initially emerged among the educated middle classes, who, in 1848, attempted to unify Germany under a liberal, constitutional parliament (Frankfurt Parliament). This effort failed due to opposition from the monarchy and military. Subsequently, the unification process was spearheaded by Prussia under Otto von Bismarck, who adopted a policy of "blood and iron." Through three decisive wars against Austria, Denmark, and France, Prussia achieved military victories that led to the consolidation of German states. The new German Empire was proclaimed in 1871 under Prussian dominance, shifting the focus of nationalism from liberal ideals to state power and military strength, with Prussian institutions becoming a model for the new nation.
    > *   **Italy:** Italy was also politically fragmented in the mid-19th century, divided into seven states under various rulers, including Austrian Habsburgs, the Pope, and Spanish Bourbons, with only Sardinia-Piedmont ruled by an Italian princely house. Early attempts at unification by revolutionaries like Giuseppe Mazzini (who advocated for a unitary Italian Republic through "Young Italy") failed in 1831 and 1848. The mantle then passed to Sardinia-Piedmont, led by Chief Minister Cavour. Cavour, a shrewd diplomat, forged an alliance with France to defeat Austria in 1859. Simultaneously, Giuseppe Garibaldi led his "Red Shirts" in 1860, conquering the Kingdom of the Two Sicilies in the south and handing it over to King Victor Emmanuel II. This combined effort led to the proclamation of a united Italy in 1861 under Victor Emmanuel II, with Rome finally joining in 1870. The development of the Italian nation was thus a blend of diplomatic maneuvering, military campaigns, and popular revolutionary movements.

4.  **How was the history of nationalism in Britain unlike the rest of Europe?**
    > **Answer:** The history of nationalism in Britain was unique compared to the rest of Europe because it was not the result of a sudden revolution or a struggle for independence against foreign rule. Instead, it was a long, gradual process driven by the steady growth of the English nation's wealth, importance, and power.
    > *   **Gradual Evolution vs. Revolution:** While much of Europe saw nationalism emerge through revolutionary upheavals (like France) or wars of unification (like Germany and Italy), Britain's nation-state formation was evolutionary.
    > *   **English Dominance:** The English Parliament, after seizing power from the monarchy in 1688, became the instrument for forging a nation-state with England at its center.
    > *   **Acts of Union:** Key acts, like the Act of Union with Scotland (1707) and the forcible incorporation of Ireland (1801), effectively meant that England imposed its influence on these regions.
    > *   **Suppression of Other Identities:** The growth of a "British identity" involved the systematic suppression of the distinct cultures and political traditions of the Welsh, Scots, and Irish. Their languages, national dresses, and attempts at independence were often repressed, making them subordinate partners in the new union.
    > In essence, British nationalism was less about a shared, pre-existing common identity and more about the imposition of a dominant English culture and political system over other ethnic groups within the British Isles.

5.  **Why did nationalist tensions emerge in the Balkans?**
    > **Answer:** Nationalist tensions emerged in the Balkans for several interconnected reasons:
    > *   **Geographical and Ethnic Variation:** The Balkans was a diverse region with many different ethnic and linguistic groups (Slavs, Romanians, Bulgarians, Albanians, Greeks, etc.), each with their own historical claims and aspirations.
    > *   **Disintegration of the Ottoman Empire:** A large part of the Balkans was under the weakening Ottoman Empire. As the empire declined, its European subject nationalities saw an opportunity to break away and declare independence, fueled by the ideas of romantic nationalism.
    > *   **Historical Claims:** Balkan peoples based their claims for independence on historical arguments, asserting that they had once been independent but were subsequently subjugated by foreign powers. They viewed their struggles as efforts to reclaim lost independence.
    > *   **Mutual Jealousy and Territorial Ambition:** As different Slavic nationalities struggled to define their identities and independence, they became fiercely jealous of each other. Each emerging Balkan state hoped to gain more territory at the expense of its neighbors, leading to intense regional conflicts.
    > *   **Big Power Rivalry:** The Balkans became a scene of intense rivalry among major European powers (Russia, Germany, England, Austro-Hungary). Each power sought to extend its own control over the area and counter the influence of others, manipulating the nationalist aspirations of the Balkan peoples for their own imperialist aims. This complex interplay of internal nationalist ambitions and external imperialist rivalries made the Balkans a highly explosive region, ultimately contributing to the outbreak of the First World War.
