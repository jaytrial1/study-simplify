This section introduces the concept of industrialisation as a period of rapid technological change and progress, as perceived in the early 20th century. It sets the stage for exploring the historical realities of this era.

# The Dawn of the Century: A Triumphant Vision

In 1900, a popular music publisher, E.T. Paull, produced a music book featuring a cover announcing the 'Dawn of the Century'. This illustration depicts a goddess-like figure, the *angel of progress*, bearing the flag of the new century. She is gently perched on a wheel with wings, symbolising time, and her flight carries her into the future. Behind her float symbols of progress: railways, cameras, machines, printing presses, and factories.

This glorification of machines and technology is even more pronounced in a picture from a trade magazine published in 1901. It contrasts two magicians:
*   **Aladdin from the Orient:** Represents the East and the past, who built a beautiful palace with his magic lamp.
*   **The Modern Mechanic:** Stands for the West and modernity, who, with his modern tools, weaves a new magic by building bridges, ships, towers, and high-rise buildings.
The term *Orient* refers to the countries to the east of the Mediterranean, usually Asia, viewed from a Western perspective as premodern, traditional, and mysterious.

These images present a triumphant account of the modern world, associating it with rapid technological change, innovations, machines, factories, railways, and steamships. This narrative often simplifies the history of industrialisation into a story of simple development and progress.

However, this raises several questions:
*   Is industrialisation always based on rapid technological development?
*   Can we still glorify continuous mechanisation of all work today?
*   What has industrialisation truly meant for people's lives?

To answer these questions, we need to delve into the history of industrialisation, focusing first on Britain, the pioneer industrial nation, and then on India, where industrial change was profoundly shaped by colonial rule.

### Activity: Modern Development and its Problems

Give two examples where modern development associated with progress has led to problems. You may think of areas related to environmental issues, nuclear weapons, or disease.

> **Answer:**
> 1.  **Environmental Degradation:** Rapid industrialisation, while bringing economic growth, has led to widespread pollution of air, water, and soil. Factories release harmful gases and effluents, contributing to climate change, respiratory diseases, and loss of biodiversity. For example, the burning of fossil fuels for energy has significantly increased greenhouse gas emissions, leading to global warming.
> 2.  **Nuclear Weapons Proliferation:** The development of nuclear technology, initially seen as a scientific marvel and a source of immense power, led to the creation of nuclear weapons. These weapons pose an existential threat to humanity, with the potential for widespread destruction and long-term environmental catastrophe. The arms race during the Cold War is a prime example of how advanced technology can lead to global instability and fear.
