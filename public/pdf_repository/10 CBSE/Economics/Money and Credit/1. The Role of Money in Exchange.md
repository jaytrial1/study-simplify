This section introduces the concept of money and its fundamental function as a medium of exchange, contrasting it with the barter system.

# Money as a Medium of Exchange

The use of money is widespread in our daily lives. We constantly see transactions involving money, whether buying goods, exchanging services, or making promises to pay later.

Transactions are made with money because it can be easily exchanged for any commodity or service a person desires. This is why everyone prefers to receive payments in money, which they can then use to acquire the things they want.

Consider a shoe manufacturer who wants to sell shoes and buy wheat. In a system using money, the manufacturer first sells shoes for money and then uses that money to buy wheat.

Imagine the difficulty without money. The shoe manufacturer would need to find a farmer who not only wants to sell wheat but also specifically wants to buy shoes in exchange. This requirement, where both parties must agree to sell and buy each other's commodities simultaneously, is known as **double coincidence of wants**. It is an essential feature of a **barter system**, where goods are exchanged directly without money.

Money eliminates the need for double coincidence of wants by acting as an intermediate step in the exchange process. The shoe manufacturer only needs to find a buyer for shoes. Once shoes are exchanged for money, that money can be used to buy wheat or anything else. Because money facilitates this exchange, it is called a **medium of exchange**.
