# Chapter Summary

This chapter explored modern forms of money and their connection to the banking system. Banks serve as intermediaries between depositors (who save money) and borrowers (who need money).

Credit is essential for economic activities and can have either a positive outcome, helping borrowers increase earnings, or a negative outcome, potentially leading to a debt trap.

Credit is available from formal sources (banks, cooperatives) and informal sources (moneylenders, traders, employers, etc.). The terms of credit, including interest rates, vary significantly between these sectors.

Currently, richer households primarily access formal credit, while poorer households rely more on informal sources. It is crucial to expand the total formal sector credit to reduce dependence on expensive informal credit. Furthermore, formal loans need to be distributed more equally to benefit the poor. Both increasing formal credit availability and ensuring equitable access are vital for development.
