# Terms of Credit and Examples

Every loan agreement includes specific conditions. The primary term is the **interest rate**, which the borrower must pay to the lender along with the repayment of the **principal** amount.

Lenders often require **collateral** (or security) against loans. Collateral is an asset owned by the borrower (like land, building, vehicle, livestock, or bank deposits) that is used as a guarantee to the lender until the loan is fully repaid. If the borrower fails to repay the loan, the lender has the right to sell the collateral to recover the money. Common examples of collateral include land titles, bank deposits, and livestock.

## Variety of Credit Arrangements: Example of a Village

To understand different credit arrangements, Rohit and Ranjan visited Sonpur, an irrigated village. They spoke to farmers and labourers.

**Shyamal (Small Farmer):** Needs loans each season for his 1.5 acres. Previously borrowed from a village moneylender at 5% interest per month (60% per annum). For the last few years, he borrows from an **agricultural trader** at 3% per month. The trader provides inputs on credit and requires Shyamal to sell his crop to him after harvest. This ensures repayment but also allows the trader to profit by buying at low post-harvest prices and selling later when prices rise.

**Arun (Medium Farmer):** Has seven acres and is one of the few in Sonpur to get a **bank loan** for cultivation. The interest rate is 8.5% per annum, repayable within three years. Arun plans to repay after harvest using part of his crop sales. He intends to store the rest in cold storage and take a fresh bank loan against the storage receipt, a facility offered by the bank to crop loan borrowers.

**Rama (Agricultural Labourer):** Has no land and needs credit for daily expenses during months without work, or for emergencies like illness or family functions. She depends on her **employer**, a medium landowner, for credit. The landowner charges 5% interest per month. Rama repays by working for the landowner. Often, she has to take a new loan before the previous one is repaid, leading to increasing debt. She currently owes Rs 5,000. Despite poor treatment, she continues working for him because he is her only source of credit. For landless people in Sonpur, landowner-employers are often the only credit source.

## Loans from Cooperatives

Besides banks, **cooperative societies** (or cooperatives) are another major source of cheap credit in rural areas. Members of a cooperative pool their resources. Examples include farmers' cooperatives, weavers' cooperatives, etc.

The Krishak Cooperative in a nearby village has 2300 farmer members. It accepts deposits from members. Using these deposits as collateral, the Cooperative gets large loans from banks, which are then used to provide loans to its members. As members repay, more lending can occur.

Krishak Cooperative provides loans for various purposes: buying agricultural implements, cultivation, agricultural trade, fishery, house construction, and other expenses.
