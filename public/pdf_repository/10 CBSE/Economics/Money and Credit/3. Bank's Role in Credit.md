This section explains how banks use the deposits they receive to provide loans and how this activity generates income for the bank.

# Bank's Role in Credit

Banks use the deposits they accept from the public in an interesting way. They keep only a small portion of the deposits as **cash reserve** (around 5% in India currently). This reserve is held to meet the potential daily withdrawals by depositors. Since only a fraction of depositors withdraw money on any given day, the bank can manage with this small cash balance.

The **major portion** of the deposits is used by banks to **extend loans**. There is significant demand for loans for various economic activities. Banks use the funds from depositors (who have surplus funds) to meet the needs of borrowers (who need funds).

Banks charge a **higher interest rate** on the loans they provide than the interest rate they pay on deposits. The difference between the interest charged to borrowers and the interest paid to depositors is the bank's **main source of income**. This process allows banks to mediate between savers and borrowers.
