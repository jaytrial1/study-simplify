This section traces the historical development of money and describes the forms used in the modern economy, specifically currency and bank deposits.

# Evolution and Modern Forms of Money

Historically, various objects served as money before the introduction of coins. Early Indians used **grains and cattle** as money. Later, **metallic coins** made of gold, silver, and copper became common and were used for centuries.

## Currency

Modern money includes **currency**, which consists of paper notes and coins. Unlike older forms like precious metals or cattle, modern currency has no intrinsic use or value of its own.

Its acceptance as a medium of exchange comes from the fact that it is **authorised by the government** of the country. In India, the **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** issues currency notes on behalf of the central government. Indian law prohibits any other individual or organisation from issuing currency. The law also **legalises the use of the rupee** as a medium of payment that cannot be refused in India. This legal backing ensures the rupee is widely accepted.

## Deposits with Banks

Another way people hold money is through **deposits with banks**. People typically only need a small amount of currency for daily needs. Any extra cash, such as a monthly salary received at the start of the month, is often deposited in bank accounts.

Banks accept these deposits and pay **interest** on them, providing safety for the money and earning for the depositor. Depositors can **withdraw** their money whenever needed. Because these deposits can be withdrawn on demand, they are called **demand deposits**.

Demand deposits offer a facility that gives them the essential characteristics of money: they can be used as a medium of exchange, particularly through **cheques**.

### Cheque Payments

A **cheque** is a paper instruction from a person with a bank account to their bank, ordering the bank to pay a specific amount from their account to the person named on the cheque.

For example, if a shoe manufacturer, M. Salim, needs to pay a leather supplier, he can write a cheque for the required amount. The cheque instructs Salim's bank to transfer the money to the leather supplier. The supplier deposits this cheque into their own bank account, and the money is transferred electronically between the banks, completing the transaction without cash.

Demand deposits, facilitated by cheques, share the essential features of money as they are widely accepted for payments. Modern money, comprising currency and deposits, is closely linked to the banking system. Without banks, demand deposits and cheque payments would not exist.
