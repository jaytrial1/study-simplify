This section introduces Self-Help Groups as an innovative approach to providing credit to the poor who lack collateral.

# Self-Help Groups (SHGs) for the Poor

Poor households often rely on informal credit because banks are not widely available in rural areas, and bank loans require documents and **collateral**, which the poor often lack. Informal lenders know borrowers personally and may lend without collateral, but they charge very high interest, keep poor records, and can harass borrowers.

In recent years, **Self-Help Groups (SHGs)** have emerged as a way to address this. SHGs organise rural poor, particularly women, into small groups (typically 15-20 members from one neighbourhood) who meet and save regularly. Members save small amounts (e.g., Rs 25 to Rs 100 or more).

Members can take **small loans from the group itself** for their needs, at an interest rate lower than moneylenders'. After a year or two of regular saving, the SHG becomes eligible for a **bank loan**. The loan is sanctioned to the group and used to create **self-employment opportunities** for members (e.g., for inputs like seeds or raw materials, housing materials, or assets like sewing machines or livestock).

Decisions about savings and loans (purpose, amount, interest, repayment) are made by the group members. The group is responsible for loan repayment. If one member defaults, others in the group follow up seriously. This collective responsibility makes banks willing to lend to SHGs even without individual collateral.

SHGs help borrowers overcome the **lack of collateral**, providing timely loans at reasonable rates for various purposes. They also serve as building blocks for organising the rural poor, helping women become financially self-reliant and providing a platform to discuss social issues.

Without being part of such a group or having significant personal assets/income proof, getting a traditional bank loan would be difficult.
