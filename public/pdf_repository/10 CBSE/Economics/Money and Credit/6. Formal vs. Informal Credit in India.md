# Formal vs. Informal Credit in India

Loans can be grouped into **formal sector loans** and **informal sector loans**.
*   **Formal sources** include banks and cooperatives.
*   **Informal lenders** include moneylenders, traders, employers, relatives, and friends.

> **Answer:** Based on Graph 1, more credit for rural households comes from the **formal sector** (Commercial Banks + Cooperative Banks/Societies + Other Formal Agencies = 51% + 10% + 5% = 66%) compared to the informal sector (Moneylenders + Relatives & Friends + Other Informal Agencies = 23% + 7% + 4% = 34%).

The **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** supervises the functioning of **formal sources**. The RBI monitors banks to ensure they maintain minimum cash reserves and that they lend not just to profitable businesses but also to small cultivators, small industries, and other small borrowers. Banks must periodically report to the RBI on their lending activities, including amounts, recipients, and interest rates.

There is **no organisation** that supervises the credit activities of **informal lenders**. They can charge any interest rate they choose and may use unfair means to recover money.

Informal lenders typically charge **much higher interest rates** than formal lenders. This higher cost of borrowing means borrowers have to use a larger portion of their earnings to repay the loan, leaving them with less income (like Shyamal). High interest can even lead to the repayment amount exceeding the borrower's income, causing increasing debt and potentially a debt trap (like Rama). The high cost can also discourage people from borrowing to start new enterprises.
