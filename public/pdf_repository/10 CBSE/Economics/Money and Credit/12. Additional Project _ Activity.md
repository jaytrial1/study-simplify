# Additional Project / Activity

The following table shows people in a variety of occupations in urban areas. What are the purposes for which the following people might need loans? Fill in the column.

| Occupations                       | Reason for needing a Loan                                                                 |
| :-------------------------------- | :---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Construction worker               | Daily expenses during no work, medical emergencies, house repair, tools, children's education |
| Graduate student who is computer literate | Further studies, training course fees, buying a laptop, starting a small business         |
| A person employed in government service | Buying a house, car, consumer goods, children's marriage/education, medical expenses      |
| Migrant labourer in Delhi         | Sending money home, daily expenses, medical needs, travel back home                       |
| Household maid                    | Medical emergencies, family functions, children's education, housing needs                |
| Small trader                      | Buying inventory/stock, expanding business, shop rent/deposit, unexpected expenses        |
| Autorickshaw driver               | Buying/repairing autorickshaw, vehicle permit/license, daily expenses, family needs       |
| A worker whose factory has closed down | Daily living expenses, starting a new small business, training for new skills             |

Next, classify the people into two groups based on whom you think might get a bank loan and those who might not. What is the criterion that you have used for classification?

> **Answer:**
> **Likely to get a bank loan (Formal Credit):**
> *   Graduate student who is computer literate (if pursuing a formal course or starting a registered business, may qualify for education or business loans with co-signer or collateral)
> *   A person employed in government service (stable income, likely has necessary documents and potentially assets for collateral, good repayment capacity)
> *   Small trader (if the business is registered and they can show income/business plan, may get business loans, especially if they have some assets)
> *   Autorickshaw driver (may get vehicle loans if they have necessary permits and can show regular income from fares, potentially with a guarantor)
>
> **Less likely to get a bank loan (More dependent on Informal Credit or SHGs):**
> *   Construction worker (income often irregular, job security low, may lack documents/collateral)
> *   Migrant labourer in Delhi (often informal employment, no permanent address or documents, income highly variable)
> *   Household maid (income often low and informal, may lack documents/collateral)
> *   A worker whose factory has closed down (no current income, potentially no assets, high risk for lender)
>
> **Criterion Used for Classification:** The primary criterion is the **perceived ability to repay the loan and the availability of collateral and formal documentation**. People with stable, verifiable incomes (like government employees) or those undertaking activities that banks have specific loan products for (like registered businesses or vehicle purchases) are more likely to meet bank requirements. Those in informal sectors with irregular income, lack of assets for collateral, and insufficient formal documentation face significant barriers to accessing formal bank credit and may have to rely on informal lenders or alternative models like SHGs.
