# Foreign Trade and Integration of Markets
Historically, foreign trade was the primary link between distant countries, facilitating the exchange of raw materials, food, and finished goods.

The basic function of foreign trade is to:
*   Create opportunities for producers to sell beyond domestic markets and compete in global markets.
*   Expand the choice of goods available to buyers beyond what is produced domestically through imports.

## Chinese Toys in India
*   Chinese manufacturers saw an opportunity to export toys to India where prices were high.
*   They began exporting plastic toys to India.
*   Indian buyers gained a wider choice between Indian and Chinese toys.
*   Due to cheaper prices and new designs, Chinese toys became very popular, replacing a large percentage of Indian toys in shops.
*   Toy prices in India became cheaper overall.

This example shows that trade:
*   Brings goods from one market to another.
*   Increases the choice of goods available to consumers.
*   Tends to equalise prices of similar goods in different markets.
*   Creates close competition between producers in different countries, despite geographical distance.

Foreign trade thus leads to the **connecting of markets** or the **integration of markets** in different countries.

## LET'S WORK THESE OUT

1. What was the main channel connecting countries in the past? How is it different now?
> **Answer:** In the past, the main channel connecting countries was foreign trade (exchange of raw materials, food, and finished products). Now, in addition to trade, countries are connected through foreign investment by MNCs, which involves controlling production across borders, and also through the movement of technology, services, and to some extent, people.
2. Distinguish between foreign trade and foreign investment.
> **Answer:** **Foreign trade** is the exchange of goods and services between countries (buying/selling across borders). **Foreign investment** is the money spent by a multinational corporation (MNC) to buy assets (like land, buildings, machines) in a foreign country to set up or control production.
3. In recent years China has been importing steel from India. Explain how the import of steel by China will affect.
(a) steel companies in China.
(b) steel companies in India.
(c) industries buying steel for production of other industrial goods in China.
> **Answer:**
> (a) **Steel companies in China:** They will face increased competition from imported Indian steel. This might force them to lower prices, improve quality, or reduce production if they cannot compete effectively.
> (b) **Steel companies in India:** They will benefit from increased demand for their steel from China. This can lead to increased production, potentially higher profits, and expansion.
> (c) **Industries buying steel for production of other industrial goods in China:** These industries will benefit from having a greater choice of steel suppliers (domestic and Indian) and potentially lower prices due to competition, which can reduce their production costs.
4. How will the import of steel from India into the Chinese markets lead to integration of markets for steel in the two countries? Explain.
> **Answer:** The import of steel from India into China links the steel market in India with the steel market in China. Indian producers can now sell directly to Chinese buyers, and Chinese buyers have access to Indian steel. This increases competition between producers in both countries and causes the price of steel in China to be influenced by the price of steel in India (and vice versa), making the two geographically separate markets function more like a single, integrated market for steel.
