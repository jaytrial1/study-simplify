# Exercises

1 What do you understand by globalisation? Explain in your own words.
> **Answer:** Globalisation is the process by which countries become increasingly interconnected and integrated, mainly through the movement of goods, services, technology, and investment facilitated by multinational corporations (MNCs). It means that markets and production are linked across the world, and events in one country can quickly affect others.
2. What were the reasons for putting barriers to foreign trade and foreign investment by the Indian government? Why did it wish to remove these barriers?
> **Answer:** The Indian government put barriers to foreign trade and investment after Independence to protect domestic industries from foreign competition. Industries were just starting in the 1950s and 60s, and it was feared they would not survive if exposed to competition from more established foreign companies. The government wished to remove these barriers starting around 1991 because it felt Indian producers were ready to compete globally, and competition would push them to improve quality and efficiency. This was also supported by international organisations promoting free trade.
3. How would flexibility in labour laws help companies?
> **Answer:** Flexibility in labour laws would help companies by allowing them to hire workers on a temporary basis for short periods when needed, rather than permanently. This reduces the cost of labour as companies don't have to pay for workers during lean periods or provide benefits associated with permanent employment, making it easier and cheaper to adjust their workforce size according to production demands.
4. What are the various ways in which MNCs set up, control or produce in other countries?
> **Answer:** MNCs set up, control, or produce in other countries in various ways:
> *   Setting up new factories and offices (foreign investment).
> *   Forming joint ventures with existing local companies.
> *   Buying up existing local companies.
> *   Placing orders for production with small local producers.
5. Why do developed countries want developing countries to liberalise their trade and investment? What do you think should the developing countries demand in return?
> **Answer:** Developed countries want developing countries to liberalise trade and investment because it opens up new markets for their goods and services and provides opportunities for their MNCs to invest and set up production, often taking advantage of lower costs. Developing countries should demand in return:
> *   That developed countries also reduce or eliminate their own trade barriers and subsidies, especially in sectors where developing countries have a comparative advantage (like agriculture or textiles).
> *   Special and differential treatment, allowing them flexibility to protect certain nascent domestic industries or implement policies supporting their development goals.
> *   Access to technology and financial assistance.
> *   A stronger voice and fairer rules within international trade organisations like the WTO.
6. "The impact of globalisation has not been uniform." Explain this statement.
> **Answer:** This statement means that globalisation has affected different groups of people and different sectors of the economy in different ways, leading to both positive and negative outcomes. For example, well-off consumers and some large Indian companies have benefited from increased choice, quality, and opportunities. However, many small producers have faced ruin due to competition, and workers in many industries face job insecurity and poor working conditions as companies try to cut costs to compete globally. So, while some have prospered, others have suffered.
7. How has liberalisation of trade and investment policies helped the globalisation process?
> **Answer:** Liberalisation of trade and investment policies helps globalisation by removing government restrictions (like tariffs and quotas) that hinder the movement of goods, services, and capital across borders. This makes it easier and cheaper for MNCs to trade globally and invest in different countries, directly facilitating the integration of national economies into the global economy.
8. How does foreign trade lead to integration of markets across countries? Explain with an example other than those given here.
> **Answer:** Foreign trade integrates markets by connecting buyers and sellers in different countries. When trade barriers are low, goods from one country can easily enter the market of another. This increases competition among producers in both countries and makes the prices of similar goods tend towards equality across markets.
> **Example:** Consider the market for smartphones. If India allows free import of smartphones from South Korea, Indian consumers gain access to a wider variety of Samsung phones. Indian smartphone companies like Micromax (if it were still making phones) would face direct competition from Samsung. This competition would likely lead to lower prices for smartphones in India and push Micromax to improve its quality or features to compete. The price of Samsung phones in India would also be influenced by their price in South Korea (considering transport costs), effectively linking the Indian and South Korean smartphone markets.
9. Globalisation will continue in the future. Can you imagine what the world would be like twenty years from now? Give reasons for your answer.
> **Answer:** (This is a speculative question based on the chapter's themes)
> Twenty years from now, the world might be even more interconnected due to further advancements in technology (faster internet, AI, automation). MNCs might be even more dominant, with production processes potentially becoming more automated, potentially reducing the need for cheap manual labour in some sectors. The movement of services (like remote work) might increase significantly.
> However, the challenges highlighted in the chapter might also intensify. The gap between skilled/wealthy individuals and unskilled workers might widen. Competition could become even fiercer, potentially leading to more job insecurity in certain sectors. There might also be increased focus on addressing the negative impacts, such as calls for fairer labour practices, environmental sustainability in global supply chains, and policies to support those left behind by globalisation. Geopolitical factors and potential backlashes against globalisation (like protectionist policies) could also influence the extent and nature of future integration.
10. Supposing you find two people arguing: One is saying globalisation has hurt our country's development. The other is telling, globalisation is helping India develop. How would you respond to these arguments?
> **Answer:** I would respond by saying that both arguments have some truth, reflecting the uneven impact of globalisation as discussed in the chapter.
> *   The person saying globalisation has hurt development might be focusing on the negative impacts: how small domestic industries have suffered due to competition, leading to job losses; how workers face precarious employment and poor conditions; how it might increase inequality between different sections of society; or how it can lead to exploitation of resources.
> *   The person saying globalisation is helping might be focusing on the positive impacts: how it has increased consumer choice and quality; brought in foreign investment and technology; created new jobs in certain sectors (especially services and industries attracting MNCs); helped some Indian companies become globally competitive; and contributed to overall economic growth.
> My response would be that globalisation has brought both benefits and challenges to India's development. It has certainly opened up opportunities and led to growth in some areas, but it has also created difficulties for vulnerable sectors and widened inequalities. Therefore, the focus should be on how to manage globalisation effectively and ensure 'fair globalisation' where the benefits are more widely shared and the negative impacts are mitigated through appropriate government policies and collective action.
