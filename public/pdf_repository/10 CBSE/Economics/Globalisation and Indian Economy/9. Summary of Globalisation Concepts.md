# Summing Up
This chapter explored the current phase of globalisation, defined as the **rapid integration of countries** primarily through increased **foreign trade and foreign investment**. **MNCs** are key drivers, seeking cheap production locations globally and organising production in complex, interlinked ways across borders.

Factors enabling globalisation include rapid improvements in **technology**, particularly **IT**, which facilitates global communication and coordination, and the **liberalisation** of trade and investment policies through the removal of barriers. The **WTO** has pressured developing countries to liberalise, although practices are often seen as uneven, with developed countries retaining protections.

While globalisation has benefited well-off consumers and skilled/wealthy producers, it has posed significant challenges for many **small producers and workers** due to rising competition, leading to job losses and precarious employment conditions.

The goal of a **fair globalisation** is to ensure opportunities and benefits are shared more equitably among all people. Governments and people's movements play vital roles in advocating for policies and rules that protect vulnerable groups and promote fairness in the global economy.
