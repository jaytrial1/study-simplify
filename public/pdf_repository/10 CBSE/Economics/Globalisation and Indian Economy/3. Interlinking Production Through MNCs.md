This section details the various strategies MNCs use to connect and control production in different countries, including foreign investment, joint ventures, and buying local companies.

# Interlinking Production Across Countries
MNCs establish production facilities based on factors like:
*   Proximity to markets.
*   Availability of skilled and unskilled labour at low costs.
*   Availability of other factors of production.
*   Favourable government policies.

The money spent by MNCs to acquire assets like land, buildings, machines, and equipment is called **investment**. Investment made by MNCs is specifically termed **foreign investment**. The goal of any investment is to earn profits.

MNCs can interlink production in several ways:

*   **Joint Ventures:** Partnering with local companies.
    *   **Benefits for local company:**
        *   MNCs provide money for additional investments (e.g., new machines).
        *   MNCs bring the latest technology.

*   **Buying Local Companies:** This is a common method for MNCs with significant wealth. They can easily acquire smaller local companies to expand production and market reach.
    *   **Example:** Cargill Foods, a large American MNC, bought Parakh Foods in India, gaining control of its marketing network and oil refineries. Cargill became the largest edible oil producer in India.
    *   Many top MNCs have wealth exceeding the budgets of developing country governments, giving them immense power and influence.
*   **Placing Orders with Small Producers:** Large MNCs order goods (like garments, footwear, sports items) from small producers globally.

Read the passage on the left (Ford Motors example) and answer the questions.

1. Would you say Ford Motors is a MNC? Why?
> **Answer:** Yes, Ford Motors is an MNC because it owns or controls production (setting up a large plant) and sells its products (cars) in more than one nation (India, South Africa, Mexico, Brazil, USA).
2. What is foreign investment? How much did Ford Motors invest in India?
> **Answer:** Foreign investment is the money spent by MNCs to buy assets like land, buildings, machines, and equipment in a foreign country. Ford Motors invested Rs. 1700 crore in India.
3. By setting up their production plants in India, MNCs such as Ford Motors tap the advantage not only of the large markets that countries such as India provide, but also the lower costs of production. Explain the statement.
> **Answer:** India offers a large consumer base (large market) for cars. Additionally, the costs of labour and other resources in India are generally lower compared to developed countries. By producing in India, Ford Motors can sell to the large Indian market and also benefit from lower production costs, making their operations more profitable.
4. Why do you think the company wants to develop India as a base for manufacturing car components for its global operations? Discuss the following factors:
(a) cost of labour and other resources in India
(b) the presence of several local manufacturers who supply autoparts to Ford Motors
(c) closeness to a large number of buyers in India and China
> **Answer:**
> (a) **Cost of labour and other resources in India:** India offers cheaper skilled and unskilled labour compared to developed countries, significantly reducing manufacturing costs for components. Other resources like land and utilities might also be cheaper.
> (b) **Presence of several local manufacturers who supply autoparts to Ford Motors:** A network of existing local suppliers means Ford doesn't have to build everything from scratch. They can source components locally, reducing complexity and potentially costs, while ensuring a reliable supply chain.
> (c) **Closeness to a large number of buyers in India and China:** While India is a large market itself, exporting components from India to China (another huge market) or using components produced in India for assembly plants closer to other major Asian markets can be logistically efficient and cost-effective compared to manufacturing components further away.
5. In what ways will the production of cars by Ford Motors in India lead to interlinking of production?
> **Answer:** Ford's production in India interlinks production globally by:
> *   Producing cars in India for sale *globally* (South Africa, Mexico, Brazil, USA), connecting Indian production to international markets.
> *   Exporting car *components* from India for its factories worldwide, making India a part of Ford's global supply chain for parts.
> *   Potentially sourcing raw materials or components from other countries for its Indian plant.
6. In what ways is a MNC different from other companies?
> **Answer:** An MNC is different from other companies because it operates or controls production facilities in *more than one country*, whereas a typical company primarily operates within a single country. MNCs leverage global locations for resources, production, and markets, giving them a wider reach and greater economic power.
7. Nearly all major multinationals are American, Japanese or European, such as Nike, Coca-Cola, Pepsi, Honda, Nokia. Can you guess why?
> **Answer:** These are countries that were among the first to industrialize and develop strong economies. Companies originating from these developed nations had early access to technology, capital, large domestic markets, and government support, allowing them to grow large enough to expand internationally and establish operations in other countries.
