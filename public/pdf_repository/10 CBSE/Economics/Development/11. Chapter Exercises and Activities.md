1.  Development of a country can generally be determined by
    (i) its per capita income
    (ii) its average literacy level
    (iii) health status of its people
    (iv) all the above
    > **Answer:** (iv) all the above
2.  Which of the following neighbouring countries has better performance in terms of human development than India?
    (i) Bangladesh
    (ii) Sri Lanka
    (iii) Nepal
    (iv) Pakistan
    > **Answer:** (ii) Sri Lanka
3.  Assume there are four families in a country. The average per capita income of these families is Rs 5000. If the income of three families is Rs 4000, Rs 7000 and Rs 3000 respectively, what is the income of the fourth family?
    (i) Rs 7500
    (ii) Rs 3000
    (iii) Rs 2000
    (iv) Rs 6000
    > **Answer:** (iv) Rs 6000
    > Calculation: Total income of 4 families = Average income * Number of families = 5000 * 4 = Rs 20000.
    > Sum of income of 3 families = 4000 + 7000 + 3000 = Rs 14000.
    > Income of the fourth family = Total income - Sum of income of 3 families = 20000 - 14000 = Rs 6000.
4.  What is the main criterion used by the World Bank in classifying different countries? What are the limitations of this criterion, if any?
    > **Answer:** The main criterion used by the World Bank is **per capita income**. The limitations are that it only considers income and does not account for the distribution of income (hides disparities) or other crucial aspects of development like health, education, environmental sustainability, freedom, or security.
5.  In what respects is the criterion used by the UNDP for measuring development different from the one used by the World Bank?
    > **Answer:** The UNDP uses the Human Development Index (HDI) which is a broader measure than the World Bank's per capita income criterion. HDI considers **per capita income** along with **health status** (measured by life expectancy at birth) and **educational levels** (measured by literacy rate and mean years of schooling). This makes the HDI a more comprehensive measure of human well-being and overall development compared to relying solely on income.
6.  Why do we use averages? Are there any limitations to their use? Illustrate with your own examples related to development.
    > **Answer:** We use averages to get a quick overall picture or to compare different groups or entities on a single characteristic. Averages simplify complex data and make comparisons easier. However, the main limitation of averages is that they **hide disparities**. They don't show the distribution of the characteristic among the population. For example, if we compare the average height of two classes, they might be the same, but one class could have students all of similar height, while the other has a mix of very tall and very short students. In development, comparing countries by average income (per capita income) hides the income distribution – a country with high average income could still have widespread poverty alongside extreme wealth, which the average doesn't reveal (as shown in the example of Country A and Country B in Table 1.2).
7.  Kerala, with lower per capita income has a better human development ranking than Haryana. Hence, per capita income is not a useful criterion at all and should not be used to compare states. Do you agree? Discuss.
    > **Answer:** I disagree that per capita income is not a useful criterion *at all*. Per capita income is a valuable indicator of the economic resources available on average to the people in a state or country. It reflects the potential for development and ability to provide goods and services. However, the example of Kerala and Haryana clearly shows that per capita income is **not a sufficient** criterion on its own. Kerala's higher performance in health and education indicators despite lower income demonstrates that factors like effective public facilities and social sector investments are crucial for human development. So, per capita income is useful as one measure, but it must be used along with other indicators like health, education, and equity for a complete picture of development.
8.  Find out the present sources of energy that are used by the people in India. What could be the other possibilities fifty years from now?

    > *   Renewable sources (solar, wind, biomass, small hydro).

9.  Why is the issue of sustainability important for development?
    > **Answer:** Sustainability is important for development because it ensures that the progress made today does not come at the expense of future generations. Development that is not sustainable depletes essential resources (like groundwater or fossil fuels) or causes irreversible environmental damage (like pollution or climate change), making it difficult or impossible for people in the future to meet their own needs and maintain a good quality of life. Sustainable development aims for a balance between economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection, ensuring long-term well-being.
10. "The Earth has enough resources to meet the needs of all but not enough to satisfy the greed of even one person". How is this statement relevant to the discussion of development? Discuss.
    > **Answer:** This statement is highly relevant to the discussion of development, particularly concerning sustainability. It highlights that resource scarcity is often caused not by the Earth's inability to provide for basic human needs, but by excessive consumption and accumulation driven by greed. Unsustainable development patterns, often fueled by the pursuit of profit and material wealth without considering limits, lead to the overuse and depletion of resources like water, forests, and fossil fuels, as well as environmental degradation. This ultimately threatens the ability of the planet to support everyone's needs, especially those of future generations and marginalized communities. Sustainable development, in contrast, requires managing resources responsibly and promoting equitable distribution, focusing on meeting needs rather than fueling excessive wants.
11. List a few examples of environmental degradation that you may have observed around you.

    > *   Water pollution in rivers, lakes, or ponds (garbage, sewage, industrial waste).
    > *   Accumulation of plastic waste and garbage in public spaces.

    > *   Deforestation or cutting down trees in parks or local areas.
    > *   Soil erosion in open areas.
    > *   Depletion of groundwater levels (e.g., wells drying up).

13. The following table shows the proportion of adults (aged 15-49 years) whose BMI is below normal (BMI < 18.5 kg/m²) in India. It is based on a survey of various states for the year 2019-21. Look at the table and answer the following questions.

    | State        | Male <br> (%) | Female <br> (%) |

    | Kerala       |      8.5      |       10        |

    Source: National Family Health Survey-5, 2019-21, http://rchiips.org
    (i) Compare the nutritional level of people in Kerala and Madhya Pradesh.
    > **Answer:** Based on the data, the nutritional level of adults in Kerala appears significantly better than in Madhya Pradesh. A much smaller percentage of adults in Kerala are underweight (BMI below normal) (8.5% male, 10% female) compared to Madhya Pradesh (28% male, 28% female). This indicates a higher prevalence of undernutrition in Madhya Pradesh.
    (ii) Can you guess why around one-fifth of people in the country are undernourished even though it is argued that there is enough food in the country? Describe in your own words.
    > **Answer:** Even if there is enough food produced in the country overall, undernutrition can still exist for several reasons:
    > *   **Poverty:** Many people may not have enough income to afford sufficient or nutritious food, even if it's available in the market.
    > *   **Unequal Distribution:** Food might be available in certain areas but not accessible or affordable in others. Issues with the public distribution system or market access can contribute.
    > *   **Lack of Awareness:** People may not have knowledge about proper nutrition or balanced diets.
    > *   **Health Issues:** Poor sanitation, lack of clean water, and inadequate healthcare can lead to illnesses that prevent the body from absorbing nutrients properly, even if food is consumed.
    > *   **Focus on Cereals:** Dietary habits might be heavily reliant on staple cereals without sufficient intake of proteins, fruits, and vegetables needed for a balanced diet.
