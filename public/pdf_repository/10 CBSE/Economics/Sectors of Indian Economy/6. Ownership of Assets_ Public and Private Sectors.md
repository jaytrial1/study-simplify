# Sectors in Terms of Ownership: Public and Private Sectors

Another classification is based on who owns assets and delivers services.

In the **public sector**, the government owns most assets and provides services (e.g., railways, post office).

In the **private sector**, ownership and service delivery are by private individuals or companies (e.g., TISCO, RIL).

Private sector activities are driven by profit motive. Services require payment to individuals/companies. The public sector's purpose is not solely profit. Governments use taxes and other means to fund public services. Modern governments spend on many activities.

Why do governments spend on certain activities?
*   **Public Goods/Services:** Society needs things the private sector won't provide at a reasonable cost because they require huge investment beyond private capacity (e.g., roads, bridges, railways, harbours, electricity generation, irrigation dams). Collecting money from all users is difficult, and private providers might charge high rates. Governments undertake this spending to ensure availability for everyone.
*   **Supporting Industry:** Governments support activities to prevent private sector shutdown. Selling electricity at cost might raise industry production costs, closing small units. Government supplies electricity at affordable rates, bearing part of the cost.
*   **Supporting Farmers and Consumers:** The Indian government buys wheat/rice from farmers at a 'fair price', stores it, and sells it cheaper through ration shops (as in Food Security). Government bears some cost, supporting both farmers and consumers.
*   **Primary Responsibilities:** Governments must spend on essential services like health and education for all. Providing proper schools and quality elementary education is a government duty. India has a large illiterate population.
*   **Human Development:** Government must address malnutrition, infant mortality, safe drinking water, housing for the poor, and food/nutrition. It must also focus spending on the poorest and most neglected regions.

# Summarising Sector Classifications

We classified economic activities by:
1.  **Nature of activity:** Primary, Secondary, Tertiary. In India, tertiary contributes most to GVA, but primary employs most people. We discussed increasing employment.
2.  **Employment conditions:** Organised, Unorganised. Most people are in the unorganised sector and need protection.
3.  **Ownership:** Public, Private. Public sector focuses on essential services and areas where private sector is inadequate.
