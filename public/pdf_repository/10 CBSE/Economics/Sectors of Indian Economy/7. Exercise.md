10. What do you understand by disguised unemployment? Explain with an example each from the urban and rural areas.
> **Answer:** Disguised unemployment is a situation where people are apparently working, but they are working less than their full potential, and their removal from work would not affect total production.
> *   **Rural example:** Laxmi's family on a small farm, where all five members work on a plot that could be managed by two, without affecting output.
> *   **Urban example:** A small shop or street stall run by multiple family members when one or two could easily manage it, or many casual labourers doing odd jobs but not finding work every day.

11. Distinguish between open unemployment and disguised unemployment.
> **Answer:** Open unemployment is when a person is willing and able to work but cannot find any job and is visibly unemployed. Disguised unemployment is when a person is working but is underemployed, meaning they are working below their capacity, and their contribution to production is negligible; their unemployment is hidden.

17. Explain the objective of implementing the MGNREGA 2005.
> **Answer:** The objective of MGNREGA 2005 is to implement the Right to Work by guaranteeing 100 days of wage employment in a financial year to adult members of rural households who volunteer for unskilled manual work. It aims to increase employment opportunities in rural areas, reduce underemployment, and create productive assets (like infrastructure) that improve land productivity. It also provides unemployment allowance if work is not provided.

20. Give a few examples of public sector activities and explain why the government has taken them up.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Railways:** Taken up by government because it requires massive investment, connects the entire country, and is a basic necessity for transport that the private sector might not provide extensively or affordably.
> *   **Providing healthcare facilities (government hospitals/clinics):** Taken up because health is a fundamental right, and the government ensures access to basic healthcare for all citizens, especially the poor, which private sector might not serve adequately or affordably.
> *   **Providing education (government schools):** Taken up because education is crucial for development and a basic right. The government ensures access to education, particularly elementary education, for all children, which the private sector might make inaccessible due to cost.
> *   **Construction of large dams:** Requires huge capital, benefits large areas (irrigation, electricity), and involves complex planning and land acquisition, making it difficult for private entities. It's a public good.

22. The workers in the unorganised sector need protection on the following issues: wages, safety and health. Explain with examples.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Wages:** Workers often receive very low daily wages, sometimes below the minimum wage, and payment can be irregular. Example: A construction labourer might be paid less than the official minimum wage and only on days they find work. Protection is needed to ensure fair wages and timely payment.
> *   **Safety:** Workplaces are often unsafe with no proper equipment or training. Example: Headload workers carry heavy loads without proper support, or small workshop employees work with dangerous machinery without safety guards. Protection is needed to enforce safety standards.
> *   **Health:** Workers lack medical benefits, paid sick leave, or health insurance. They are exposed to risks without support. Example: A street vendor might get sick but lose their daily income if they don't work and have no access to affordable healthcare. Protection is needed for medical support and paid sick leave.

24. The following table gives the GVA in Rupees (Crores) by the three sectors:

| Year | Primary | Secondary | Tertiary |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 2000 | $12,56,000$ | $10,12,000$ | $18,17,000$ |
| 2013 | $17,87,000$ | $24,59,000$ | $46,30,000$ |

(i) Calculate the share of the three sectors in GDP for 2000 and 2013.
> **Answer (Calculation of Share in GVA/GDP):**
> *   **Year 2000:**
>     *   Total GVA = 12,56,000 + 10,12,000 + 18,17,000 = 40,85,000 Crores
>     *   Primary Share = (12,56,000 / 40,85,000) * 100 = **30.75%**
>     *   Secondary Share = (10,12,000 / 40,85,000) * 100 = **24.77%**
>     *   Tertiary Share = (18,17,000 / 40,85,000) * 100 = **44.48%**
> *   **Year 2013:**
>     *   Total GVA = 17,87,000 + 24,59,000 + 46,30,000 = 88,76,000 Crores
>     *   Primary Share = (17,87,000 / 88,76,000) * 100 = **20.13%**
>     *   Secondary Share = (24,59,000 / 88,76,000) * 100 = **27.70%**
>     *   Tertiary Share = (46,30,000 / 88,76,000) * 100 = **52.16%**

> **Answer:** (This requires drawing a bar diagram. It should be a stacked bar chart with two bars, one for 2000 and one for 2013. (iii) What conclusions can we draw from the bar graph?
> **Answer:** Conclusions from the bar graph (and calculations):
