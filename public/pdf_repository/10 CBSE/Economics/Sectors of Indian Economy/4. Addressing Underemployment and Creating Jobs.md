# How to Create More Employment?

Significant underemployment persists in agriculture, alongside visible unemployment. How can employment be increased?

For Laxmi's two-hectare plot, government spending or bank loans could fund a well for irrigation. With irrigation, she could grow a second crop, like wheat, employing two more family members for 50 days during the season. Constructing a new dam and canals could irrigate many farms, generating significant employment within agriculture itself and reducing underemployment.

Increased production means farmers need to sell more. Government investment in transportation and storage, or building better rural roads, allows farmers with irrigation access to sell their crops in nearby towns. This creates productive employment not just for farmers but also for those in transport or trade services.

Farmers also need inputs like seeds, fertilisers, equipment, and pumpsets. Poor farmers like Laxmi cannot afford these. Providing cheap agricultural credit through banks helps them buy inputs and improve farming.

Another approach is to identify and promote industries and services in semi-rural areas to employ more people. Examples:
*   Setting up a dal mill to process pulse crops grown by farmers.
*   Opening a cold storage for farmers to store produce like potatoes and onions and sell when prices are favourable.
*   Starting honey collection centres near forest areas.
*   Establishing industries to process vegetables and agricultural produce (potato, sweet potato, rice, wheat, tomato, fruits) for outside markets.

These initiatives create employment in industries located in semi-rural areas, reducing the need to migrate to large urban centres.

In India, about 60% of the population is aged 5-29 years, but only about 51% attend educational institutions. Many others, especially those under 18, may be at home or working as child labourers. If these children attended school, more buildings, teachers, and staff would be needed. A study estimated nearly 20 lakh jobs could be created in the education sector alone. Improving health requires more doctors, nurses, and health workers, especially in rural areas. These investments create jobs and address development goals.

Every region has potential for increasing income and employment, such as tourism, regional crafts, or new services like IT. This requires government planning and support. A study suggested improving tourism could create over 35 lakh additional jobs annually.

Some suggestions take time. For the short term, quick measures are needed. Recognizing this, the central government enacted the **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act 2005 (MGNREGA 2005)**, guaranteeing 100 days of employment per year to rural residents who need work and are able to do it, in about 625 districts. If the government cannot provide employment, it must give unemployment allowances. Work under the Act prioritizes projects that increase land productivity.

## Let's Work These Out

1. Why do you think MGNREGA 2005 is referred to as 'Right to work' ?
> **Answer:** MGNREGA 2005 is called the 'Right to Work' because it legally guarantees 100 days of employment in a year to eligible rural households. This provides a legal entitlement to work, backed by the government's obligation to provide employment or unemployment allowance.

2. Imagine that you are the village head. In that capacity suggest some activities that you think should be taken up under this Act that would also increase the income of people? Discuss.
> **Answer:** (This is a discussion prompt. Possible activities under MGNREGA that increase income/assets include: construction of wells/irrigation channels, building rural roads, land development, afforestation, building check dams, creating ponds. These activities directly employ people and improve land productivity or connectivity, indirectly boosting income.)

3. How would income and employment increase if farmers were provided with irrigation and marketing facilities?
> **Answer:** Irrigation allows farmers to grow multiple crops per year or switch to higher-value crops, increasing production and income. Marketing facilities (transport, storage, access to markets) help farmers sell their increased produce at better prices, reducing wastage and dependence on local traders. Both lead to more farming activity and related services (transport, storage, processing), thus increasing overall employment and income in the rural area.

4. In what ways can employment be increased in urban areas?
> **Answer:** Employment can be increased in urban areas through:
> *   Promoting small-scale industries.
> *   Investing in infrastructure (construction creates jobs).
> *   Developing new services, especially those based on technology.
> *   Improving education and healthcare services.
> *   Supporting urban informal sector workers through better facilities and access to credit.
