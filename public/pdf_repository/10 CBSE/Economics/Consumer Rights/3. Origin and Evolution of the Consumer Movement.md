This section describes how the consumer movement began as a response to unfair market practices and evolved over time, leading to significant legal protections.

# The Rise of Consumer Advocacy

The *consumer movement* originated from the dissatisfaction of consumers facing unfair practices by sellers. Initially, there was no legal system to protect them from exploitation. Consumers often just avoided specific products or shops if they were unhappy. It was assumed that consumers were responsible for being careful when buying. It took many years for organisations in India and globally to raise consumer awareness and shift the responsibility for product quality onto sellers.

In India, the consumer movement started as a 'social force' to protect consumer interests against unethical practices. Issues like food shortages, hoarding, black marketing, and adulteration of food and oil in the 1960s spurred the organised consumer movement. In the 1970s, consumer organisations focused on writing articles, holding exhibitions, and forming groups to address malpractices in ration shops and public transport overcrowding. More recently, the number of consumer groups in India has significantly increased.

# Consumers International

In 1985, the United Nations adopted the *UN Guidelines for Consumer Protection*. These guidelines provided a framework for nations to protect consumers and empowered consumer advocacy groups to pressure their governments for action. Globally, these guidelines became the foundation for the consumer movement. Today, Consumers International is an umbrella body representing over 200 member organisations from more than 100 countries.

1. What could have been the steps taken by consumer groups?
> **Answer:** Consumer groups could have taken steps like:
> *   Writing articles and holding exhibitions to raise awareness.
> *   Forming groups to monitor market practices (e.g., in ration shops).
> *   Educating consumers about their rights.
> *   Advocating for legal changes and better enforcement.
> *   Helping consumers file complaints and seek redressal.

2. There may be rules and regulations but they are often not followed. Why? Discuss.
> **Answer:** Rules and regulations may not be followed due to weak enforcement mechanisms, lack of consumer awareness to report violations, corruption, insufficient penalties for offenders, or the scattered nature of consumers making it difficult to collectively demand adherence to rules.
