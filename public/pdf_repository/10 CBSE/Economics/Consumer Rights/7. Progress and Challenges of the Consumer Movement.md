# Moving Forward: Achievements and Hurdles

India celebrates 24 December as the *National Consumers' Day*, commemorating the enactment of the *Consumer Protection Act in 1986*. India is unique in having exclusive authorities for consumer redressal.

The consumer movement has grown, with over 2000 consumer groups, although only about 50-60 are well-organised and active.

However, the consumer redressal process still faces challenges:
*   It can be *cumbersome, expensive, and time-consuming*.
*   Consumers often feel the need to *engage lawyers*.
*   Cases require time for filing and attending commission proceedings.
*   Lack of cash memos for many small purchases makes *gathering evidence difficult*.
*   Most purchases are small retail sales, making formal complaints seem disproportionate.

*COPRA* was amended in 2019 to strengthen consumer protection. This amendment:
*   Includes purchases made through the internet.
*   Holds service providers and manufacturers responsible for service deficiency or defective products, with penalties including imprisonment.
*   Encourages *mediation* (settlement with a neutral intermediary) at all three tiers of Consumer Commissions to resolve disputes outside formal proceedings.

Despite over 30 years since COPRA's enactment, consumer awareness in India is spreading slowly. Enforcement of laws protecting vulnerable groups, like workers in the unorganised sector, and market regulations often remains weak.

Nevertheless, there is potential for consumers to realise their importance and role. The effectiveness of consumer movements relies heavily on active consumer involvement. It requires voluntary effort and collective participation.
