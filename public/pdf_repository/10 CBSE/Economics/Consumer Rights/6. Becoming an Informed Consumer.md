# The Informed Consumer

Becoming conscious of our consumer rights helps us make informed choices when buying goods and services. This requires knowledge and skill to be a *well-informed consumer*. Awareness campaigns, like posters and advertisements, play a key role in educating people.

The enactment of *COPRA* led to the creation of separate Departments of Consumer Affairs at the central and state levels. The government uses various means, including advertisements, to spread information about legal processes available to consumers.

## Quality Certification Marks: ISI and Agmark

When buying many products, you might see logos like *ISI*, *Agmark*, *Hallmark*, or *+F*. These marks certify quality and help consumers feel assured. Organizations that monitor quality issue these certificates, allowing producers to use the logos if they meet certain standards.

3. What is the difference between consumer protection council and Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission?
> **Answer:** A *consumer protection council* (or forum) is typically a voluntary organisation that guides consumers, provides advice, creates awareness, and sometimes represents consumers. A *Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission* is a quasi-judicial body established under COPRA at the district, state, and national levels. Its role is to hear consumer complaints and pass judgments or orders to resolve disputes and provide redressal (like compensation or replacement).

5. If the standardisation ensures the quality of a commodity, why are many goods available in the market without ISI or Agmark certification?
> **Answer:** Standardisation marks like ISI or Agmark are only mandatory for certain products that directly impact health, safety, or are of mass consumption (like LPG cylinders, packaged water, etc.). For many other goods, obtaining these certifications is voluntary for producers. Therefore, many goods are available without these marks because the law does not require them to have it, or the producers choose not to get certified.

6. Find out the details of who provides Hallmark and ISO certification.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Hallmark:** In India, the *Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS)* is the authority that provides Hallmark certification for gold and silver jewellery, guaranteeing purity.
> *   **ISO certification:** *ISO (International Organization for Standardization)* develops international standards. Certification is issued by various independent *certification bodies* accredited to audit and verify that a company or product meets a specific ISO standard (e.g., ISO 9001 for quality management).
