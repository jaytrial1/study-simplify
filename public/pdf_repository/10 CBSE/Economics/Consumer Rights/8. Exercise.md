# EXERCISES

1. Why are rules and regulations required in the marketplace? Illustrate with a few examples.
> **Answer:** Rules and regulations are needed in the marketplace to protect consumers from exploitation and unfair practices by powerful producers or sellers. Examples include rules against selling underweight goods, charging more than the maximum retail price (MRP), selling expired or defective products, and spreading false information about products or services. These rules ensure fair trade and consumer safety.

2. What factors gave birth to the consumer movement in India? Trace its evolution.
> **Answer:** The consumer movement in India was born out of consumer dissatisfaction due to unfair trade practices like hoarding, black marketing, food shortages, and adulteration in the 1960s. Initially, organisations focused on awareness through articles and exhibitions. In the 1970s, they started forming groups to monitor malpractices. The movement gained momentum, leading to the enactment of the Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) in 1986, establishing a legal framework for consumer redressal. Over time, the number of consumer groups increased, and COPRA was amended in 2019 to address new issues like online purchases and strengthen enforcement.

3. Explain the need for consumer consciousness by giving two examples.
> **Answer:** Consumer consciousness is needed so consumers can protect themselves and demand fair treatment.
> Example 1: An aware consumer checks the expiry date on medicines or food products, preventing the purchase of potentially harmful expired goods.
> Example 2: A conscious consumer insists on a bill for purchases, which serves as crucial evidence if they need to file a complaint about a defective product or overcharging.

4. Mention a few factors which cause exploitation of consumers.
> **Answer:** Factors causing consumer exploitation include:
> *   Lack of consumer awareness about their rights.
> *   Limited availability of goods or services, giving sellers more power (hoarding).
> *   Few producers or large powerful companies dominating the market.
> *   Scattered and unorganised consumers.
> *   Weak enforcement of consumer protection laws and regulations.
> *   Unfair trade practices like adulteration, underweight selling, and false advertising.

5. What is the rationale behind the enactment of Consumer Protection Act 1986?
> **Answer:** The rationale behind enacting COPRA 1986 was to provide a legal framework in India specifically for the protection of consumers against unfair trade practices and exploitation. It aimed to establish a system for consumers to seek redressal easily and effectively, recognizing that individual consumers are often in a weak position against powerful businesses.

6. Describe some of your duties as consumers if you visit a shopping complex in your locality.
> **Answer:** As a consumer visiting a shopping complex, my duties include:
> *   Being aware of my rights (safety, information, choice, etc.).
> *   Checking product details like MRP, manufacturing/expiry dates, and quality marks (ISI, Agmark).
> *   Insisting on and keeping a bill or cash memo for purchases.
> *   Making informed choices and not being misled by false advertisements.
> *   Reporting any unfair practices or defective products to the seller and, if necessary, to consumer forums.
> *   Using products safely according to instructions.

7. Suppose you buy a bottle of honey and a biscuit packet. Which logo or mark you will have to look for and why?
> **Answer:** For a bottle of honey and a biscuit packet, you should look for the *Agmark* certification. Agmark is a certification mark used in India for agricultural products, assuring that they conform to standards specified by the Directorate of Marketing and Inspection of the Government of India. This helps ensure the quality and purity of the honey and the ingredients in the biscuit packet.

8. What legal measures were taken by the government to empower the consumers in India?
> **Answer:** The primary legal measure taken by the government to empower consumers in India is the enactment of the *Consumer Protection Act (COPRA) in 1986*. This act defined consumer rights and established a three-tier quasi-judicial system (District, State, and National Commissions) for consumer redressal. More recently, COPRA was amended in 2019 to include online transactions, define unfair contracts, and introduce mediation, further strengthening consumer protection. The *Right to Information (RTI) Act 2005* also indirectly empowers consumers by allowing them to access information about government services.

9. Mention some of the rights of consumers and write a few sentences on each.
> **Answer:** Some key consumer rights are:
> *   **Right to Safety:** Consumers have the right to be protected against products and services that are hazardous to life and property. Producers must ensure their goods meet safety standards.
> *   **Right to Information:** Consumers have the right to be informed about the quality, quantity, purity, standard, and price of goods or services before buying, usually through packaging details and labels.
>   **Right to Choice:** Consumers have the right to choose from a variety of goods and services at competitive prices, and should not be forced to buy products or services they do not want.
>   **Right to be Heard (or Representation):** Consumers have the right to have their interests considered and represented in appropriate forums. This includes the right to file complaints and be heard in consumer commissions.
>   **Right to Seek Redressal:** Consumers have the right to seek compensation or resolution against unfair trade practices or exploitation, and can approach consumer forums for justice.
>   **Right to Consumer Education:** Consumers have the right to acquire knowledge and skills to be well-informed consumers throughout life, which helps prevent exploitation.

10. By what means can the consumers express their solidarity?
> **Answer:** Consumers can express their solidarity and strengthen the consumer movement through various means:
> *   Forming or joining consumer groups and organisations.
>   Participating in consumer awareness campaigns and activities.
>   Collectively boycotting products or services from businesses engaging in unfair practices.
>   Sharing experiences and information about market exploitation.
>   Using social media and other platforms to voice concerns and mobilise support.
>   Supporting voluntary consumer organisations financially or through volunteering.

11. Critically examine the progress of consumer movement in India.
> **Answer:** The consumer movement in India has made significant progress, notably by pushing for and achieving the enactment of COPRA in 1986, establishing a dedicated legal framework and redressal system. Awareness among consumers has increased over the decades due to the efforts of consumer groups and government campaigns. The 2019 amendment to COPRA shows the movement's influence in updating the law to address new market realities like e-commerce. However, challenges remain. The redressal process can still be slow, expensive, and complex. Many consumers, especially in rural areas, lack awareness or face difficulties in gathering evidence. The enforcement of rules is often weak. Despite having many consumer groups, only a few are well-organised and effective, limiting the movement's overall reach and impact across the vast population.

13. Say True or False.
(i) COPRA applies only to goods.
(ii) India is one of the many countries in the world which has exclusive authorities established for consumer disputes redressal.
(iii) When a consumer feels that he has been exploited, he must file a case in the District Consumer Commission.
(iv) It is worthwhile to move to consumer commissions only if the damages incurred are of high value.
(v) Hallmark is the certification maintained for standardisation of jewellry.
(vi) The consumer redressal process is very simple and quick.
(vii) A consumer has the right to get compensation depending on the degree of the damage.

> **Answer:**
> (i) COPRA applies only to goods. (**False**) - It applies to both goods and services.
> (ii) India is one of the many countries in the world which has exclusive authorities established for consumer disputes redressal. (**True**) - The text states India is one of the countries that have exclusive authority.
> (iii) When a consumer feels that he has been exploited, he must file a case in the District Consumer Commission. (**False**) - They can also approach State or National Commissions depending on the value of the claim, or seek advice from consumer forums first.
> (iv) It is worthwhile to move to consumer commissions only if the damages incurred are of high value. (**False**) - While high value cases are often pursued, the commissions are designed for all levels of complaints, and the principle is to seek justice for any valid grievance.
> (v) Hallmark is the certification maintained for standardisation of jewellry. (**True**)
> (vi) The consumer redressal process is very simple and quick. (**False**) - The text mentions it can be cumbersome, expensive, and time-consuming.
> (vii) A consumer has the right to get compensation depending on the degree of the damage. (**True**)
