# Core Rights of Consumers

## Right to Safety

### Reji's Suffering

Reji Mathew, a healthy Class IX student, became crippled for life due to improper anaesthesia during a tonsillectomy at a private clinic in Kerala. His father filed a complaint with the State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, claiming compensation for medical negligence. The State Commission dismissed the case due to insufficient evidence. Reji's father then appealed to the National Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission in New Delhi. The National Commission found the hospital responsible for medical negligence and ordered them to pay compensation.

Reji's case highlights the *right to be protected against the marketing of goods and delivery of services that are hazardous to life and property*. Producers must strictly follow safety rules and regulations. Many goods and services require special attention to safety, such as pressure cookers with safety valves or medicines. Manufacturers must ensure high quality, and government action is needed to maintain this. However, poor quality products exist because supervision is weak and the consumer movement is not strong enough.

## Right to Information

When you buy a product, its packaging usually provides details like ingredients, price, batch number, manufacturing and expiry dates, and manufacturer's address. Medicine packets include directions for use, side effects, and risks. Garments have washing instructions.

Rules mandate manufacturers to display this information because consumers have the *right to be informed* about the particulars of goods and services. This allows consumers to complain and seek compensation or replacement if a product is defective. For example, a defective product within its expiry period can be replaced. If the expiry date wasn't printed, the manufacturer might evade responsibility. Selling expired medicines can lead to severe action. Consumers can also protest if charged more than the Maximum Retail Price (*MRP*) and can even bargain for a price lower than MRP.

The *right to information* has expanded to government services. In October 2005, the *Right to Information (RTI) Act* was enacted, allowing citizens to access information about government department functions.

### Waiting...

Amritha, an engineering graduate, applied for a government job but received no news after submitting certificates and attending the interview. Officials refused to provide information. She used the RTI Act, asserting her right to know the result within a reasonable time to plan her future. She not only learned the reasons for the delay but also received her appointment letter because she had performed well in the interview.

## Right to Choose

### A Refund

Abirami joined a two-year coaching course, paying the full fee upfront. She decided to leave after one year due to poor teaching quality and asked for a refund for the second year, which was denied. She filed a case with the District Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission, which ordered the institute to refund Rs 28,000, stating she had the *right to choose*. The institute appealed to the State Consumer Commission, which upheld the decision, fined the institute Rs 25,000 for a frivolous appeal, and ordered payment of Rs 7000 as compensation and litigation cost.

The State Commission also prohibited educational and professional institutions in the state from charging full course fees in advance, with violations potentially leading to penalties and imprisonment.

This case demonstrates that any consumer receiving a service has the right to choose whether to continue receiving that service, regardless of the type of service or the consumer's background.

The *right to choice* means a consumer cannot be forced to buy something they do not want. For example, a shopkeeper cannot force you to buy a toothbrush if you only want toothpaste. Similarly, gas dealers cannot insist you buy a stove from them when getting a new connection. Consumers should not be forced into unwanted purchases.

# LET'S WORK THIS OUT

The following are some of the catchy advertisements of products that we purchase from the market. Which of the following offers would really benefit consumers? Discuss.

- 15 gm more in every 500 gm pack.
- Subscribe for a newspaper with a gift at the end of a year.
- Scratch and win gifts worth Rs 10 lakhs.
- A milk chocolate inside a 500 gram glucose box.
- Win a gold coin inside a pack.
- Buy shoes worth Rs 2000 and get one pair of shoes worth Rs 500 free.

> **Answer:** Offers that provide a tangible and guaranteed benefit directly related to the product purchased are most likely to benefit consumers.
> *   **15 gm more in every 500 gm pack:** This is a direct, guaranteed benefit (more product for the same price).
> *   **Subscribe for a newspaper with a gift at the end of a year:** This is a benefit, but conditional on a year's subscription and the gift's value is uncertain.
> *   **Scratch and win gifts worth Rs 10 lakhs:** This is a lottery-based offer with a very low probability of winning a significant prize. It benefits very few.
> *   **A milk chocolate inside a 500 gram glucose box:** This is a small, tangible bonus, but might not be desired by all consumers.
> *   **Win a gold coin inside a pack:** Another lottery-based offer with low probability.
> *   **Buy shoes worth Rs 2000 and get one pair of shoes worth Rs 500 free:** This is a clear discount/bonus, offering a tangible product.

> The offers that *really* benefit consumers consistently and tangibly are "15 gm more in every 500 gm pack" and "Buy shoes worth Rs 2000 and get one pair of shoes worth Rs 500 free". The others involve chance or conditional benefits.
