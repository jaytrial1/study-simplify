# Accessing Justice for Consumers

Cases like Reji Mathew's and Abirami's show how consumer rights can be denied. Such incidents are common. Where can consumers go for justice?

Consumers have the *right to seek redressal* against unfair trade practices and exploitation. If a consumer suffers damage, they have the right to compensation based on the extent of the damage. An easy and effective public system is needed for this.

A consumer can file a complaint with the appropriate *consumer forum* on their own, without a lawyer. The process for seeking compensation is illustrated by Prakash's case. He sent a money order that never reached his daughter for her marriage. He filed a case in a district-level Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission.

These days, consumers can file complaints physically or online and participate in proceedings via video conferencing.

The consumer movement in India has led to the formation of *consumer forums* or *consumer protection councils*. These organisations guide consumers on filing cases and sometimes represent them. Voluntary consumer organisations receive government financial support to raise awareness.

*Residents' Welfare Associations* in residential colonies also take up cases on behalf of their members if they face unfair trade practices.

Under *COPRA*, a three-tier quasi-judicial machinery exists for consumer redressal:
*   District-level commissions
*   State-level commissions
*   National-level commission
