This section defines an ecosystem, elaborates on its biotic and abiotic components, and classifies organisms based on how they obtain sustenance.

# Ecosystem - What Are Its Components?

All organisms, including plants, animals, microorganisms, and human beings, interact with each other and their physical surroundings to maintain a natural balance. An **ecosystem** is formed by all the interacting organisms in an area together with the non-living constituents of the environment.

An ecosystem consists of:
*   **Biotic components**: Comprising all living organisms (plants, animals, microorganisms).
*   **Abiotic components**: Comprising physical factors like temperature, rainfall, wind, soil, and minerals.

For instance, a garden is an ecosystem where different plants (grasses, trees, flowers like rose, jasmine, sunflower) and animals (frogs, insects, birds) interact. Their growth, reproduction, and other activities are influenced by abiotic components like sunlight, water, and soil. Forests, ponds, and lakes are examples of **natural ecosystems**, while gardens and crop-fields are **human-made (artificial) ecosystems**.

## Activity 13.1: Designing an Aquarium

An aquarium is a human-made ecosystem. To design one, we need to consider:
*   **Space:** A large jar or tank for fish to swim freely.
*   **Water:** The aquatic medium.
*   **Oxygen:** Provided by an oxygen pump (aerator).
*   **Food:** Fish food available in the market.

**Question:** If we add a few aquatic plants and animals, it can become a self-sustaining system. Can you think how this happens?
> **Answer:** Adding aquatic plants makes the system self-sustaining because plants act as **producers**. They perform photosynthesis, producing oxygen (which fish and other aquatic animals need for respiration) and food. The fish and other animals (consumers) produce waste, which can be broken down by bacteria (decomposers) into nutrients that the plants can then reuse, thus completing a cycle.

**Question:** Can we leave the aquarium as such after we set it up? Why does it have to be cleaned once in a while? Do we have to clean ponds or lakes in the same manner? Why or why not?
> **Answer:** No, we cannot leave the aquarium as such; it needs to be cleaned once in a while. This is because an aquarium is a small, *artificial* ecosystem with limited capacity for natural waste breakdown and nutrient cycling. The accumulation of fish waste, uneaten food, and dead organisms can quickly lead to toxic levels of ammonia and nitrates, making the water unhealthy. Ponds and lakes, being large, *natural* ecosystems, have a much greater volume of water and a more diverse community of decomposers and other organisms. This allows for a more efficient and robust natural cycling of nutrients and breakdown of waste, so they generally do not need to be cleaned by humans in the same manner as an aquarium.

Organisms can be grouped based on how they obtain sustenance:
*   **Producers:** Organisms that can make organic compounds (like sugar and starch) from inorganic substances using radiant energy (e.g., sunlight) in the presence of chlorophyll. All green plants and certain bacteria (photosynthetic bacteria) are producers.
*   **Consumers:** Organisms that depend on producers either directly or indirectly for their sustenance. They consume food produced by producers or by feeding on other consumers. Consumers can be classified as:
    *   **Herbivores:** Consume plants directly (e.g., deer, cows).
    *   **Carnivores:** Consume other animals (e.g., tigers, snakes).
    *   **Omnivores:** Consume both plants and animals (e.g., humans, bears).
    *   **Parasites:** Live on or in another organism (host) and obtain nutrients from it (e.g., tapeworms, lice).
*   **Decomposers:** Microorganisms (comprising bacteria and fungi) that break down the dead remains and waste products of organisms. They convert complex organic substances into simple inorganic substances that return to the soil and are reused by plants. Without decomposers, garbage, dead animals, and plants would accumulate, and the natural replenishment of soil nutrients would not occur.

## Activity 13.2: Food Chains in an Aquarium

**Question:** While creating an aquarium did you take care not to put an aquatic animal which would eat others? What would have happened otherwise?
> **Answer:** If we had put an aquatic animal that eats others (a predator) without considering the balance, it would likely consume the smaller fish or other organisms, disrupting the ecosystem's balance. This could lead to a rapid decline in the prey population and potentially the eventual starvation of the predator if its food source runs out, causing the ecosystem to collapse.

**Question:** Make groups and discuss how each of the above groups of organisms are dependent on each other.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Producers (e.g., aquatic plants):** They are the base, converting sunlight into food. All other organisms depend on them directly or indirectly.
> *   **Primary Consumers (e.g., small fish, snails feeding on algae):** They depend on producers for food.
> *   **Secondary Consumers (e.g., larger fish eating smaller fish):** They depend on primary consumers for food.
> *   **Decomposers (e.g., bacteria, fungi):** They break down dead producers and consumers, returning nutrients to the environment for producers to reuse. This cycle shows the interdependence; each group plays a vital role in the flow of energy and nutrients.

**Question:** Write the aquatic organisms in order of who eats whom and form a chain of at least three steps.
> **Answer:**
> Algae/Aquatic plants $\rightarrow$ Small fish $\rightarrow$ Large fish
> (Producer) $\rightarrow$ (Primary Consumer) $\rightarrow$ (Secondary Consumer)

**Question:** Would you consider any one group of organisms to be of primary importance? Why or why not?
> **Answer:** Yes, **producers** are of primary importance. They are the only organisms that can convert solar energy into chemical energy, forming the base of almost all ecosystems. Without producers, there would be no initial energy source for consumers, and the entire food web would collapse. Decomposers are also critically important for nutrient recycling, but producers initiate the energy flow.
