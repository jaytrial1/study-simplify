This section delves into the modes of reproduction primarily used by single-celled organisms, focusing on how they divide to create new individuals through various patterns of fission.

# Modes of Reproduction Used by Single Organisms

The ways in which different organisms reproduce depend significantly on their body design. For unicellular organisms, cell division, or **fission**, directly leads to the creation of new individuals.

## Fission

Many different patterns of fission have been observed in unicellular organisms:

*   **Binary Fission:** Many bacteria and protozoa simply split into two equal halves during cell division. In organisms such as *Amoeba*, the splitting of the two cells during division can take place in any plane.

    However, some unicellular organisms exhibit a more organized body structure, such as *Leishmania* (the parasite causing kala-azar), which has a whip-like structure at one end of the cell. In such organisms, binary fission occurs in a definite orientation relative to these structures.

*   **Multiple Fission:** Other single-celled organisms, such as the malarial parasite, *Plasmodium*, divide into many daughter cells simultaneously through **multiple fission**.

    *Yeast*, on the other hand, reproduces by putting out small buds that separate and grow further.

## Activity 7.1

Dissolve about 10 gm of sugar in 100 mL of water.
Take 20 mL of this solution in a test tube and add a pinch of yeast granules to it.
Put a cotton plug on the mouth of the test tube and keep it in a warm place.
After 1 or 2 hours, put a small drop of yeast culture from the test tube on a slide and cover it with a coverslip.
Observe the slide under a microscope.
