This section explores advanced asexual reproductive methods in plants, including vegetative propagation and tissue culture, as well as spore formation in fungi.

# Vegetative Propagation

There are many plants in which parts like the root, stem, and leaves can develop into new plants under appropriate conditions. Unlike in most animals, plants can indeed use such a mode for reproduction. This property of **vegetative propagation** is used in methods such as layering or grafting to grow many plants like sugarcane, roses, or grapes for agricultural purposes.

Plants raised by vegetative propagation can bear flowers and fruits earlier than those produced from seeds. Such methods also make possible the propagation of plants such as banana, orange, rose, and jasmine that have lost the capacity to produce seeds. Another advantage of vegetative propagation is that all plants produced are genetically similar enough to the parent plant to retain all its characteristics.

### Tissue Culture

In **tissue culture**, new plants are grown by removing tissue or separating cells from the growing tip of a plant. The cells are then placed in an artificial medium where they divide rapidly to form a small group of cells or **callus**. The callus is transferred to another medium containing hormones for growth and differentiation. The plantlets are then placed in the soil so that they can grow into mature plants. Using tissue culture, many plants can be grown from one parent in disease-free conditions. This technique is commonly used for ornamental plants.

## Spore Formation

Even in many simple multi-cellular organisms, specific reproductive parts can be identified. The thread-like structures that developed on the bread in Activity 7.2 are the **hyphae** of the bread mould (*Rhizopus*). They are not reproductive parts. However, the tiny blob-on-a-stick structures are involved in reproduction. These blobs are **sporangia**, which contain cells, or **spores**, that can eventually develop into new *Rhizopus* individuals.

## Q U E S T I O N S

1.  How does binary fission differ from multiple fission?
    > **Answer:** In **binary fission**, a single parent cell divides into two approximately equal daughter cells. This is common in organisms like *Amoeba* and *Leishmania*. In **multiple fission**, the parent cell divides into many daughter cells simultaneously. This occurs in organisms like *Plasmodium*.

2.  How will an organism be benefited if it reproduces through spores?
    > **Answer:** Organisms that reproduce through spores benefit in several ways:
    > *   **Wide Dispersal:** Spores are generally light and can be carried by wind or water, allowing the organism to colonize new areas far from the parent.
    > *   **Survival in Unfavorable Conditions:** Spores often have thick, protective walls that enable them to survive harsh environmental conditions (e.g., extreme temperatures, lack of moisture) until favorable conditions return.
    > *   **Mass Production:** A single organism can produce a large number of spores, increasing the chances of survival and reproduction.

3.  Can you think of reasons why more complex organisms cannot give rise to new individuals through regeneration?
    > **Answer:** More complex organisms cannot give rise to new individuals through regeneration because:
    > *   **High Degree of Specialization:** Their bodies are highly specialized with complex organization of cells into tissues, organs, and organ systems, each performing specific functions.
    > *   **Limited Pluripotency:** Unlike simpler organisms (e.g., *Planaria*) which have pluripotent cells distributed throughout their body, complex organisms have very few such cells, typically restricted to specific areas or for repair purposes, not whole-body regeneration.
    > *   **Complex Development:** The development of a complete organism from a single cell type or fragment would require a highly complex and coordinated sequence of developmental steps that are not possible in their differentiated body plans.

4.  Why is vegetative propagation practised for growing some types of plants?
    > **Answer:** Vegetative propagation is practiced for growing some types of plants for several reasons:
    > *   **Faster Growth and Earlier Fruiting/Flowering:** Plants grown through vegetative propagation (e.g., layering, grafting) often bear flowers and fruits earlier than those grown from seeds.
    > *   **Propagation of Seedless Plants:** It allows the propagation of plants that have lost the capacity to produce seeds (e.g., banana, orange, rose, jasmine).
    > *   **Genetic Uniformity:** All plants produced are genetically identical to the parent plant, ensuring that desirable characteristics (e.g., high yield, disease resistance, specific flower color) are consistently maintained in the offspring.
    > *   **Disease-Free Propagation:** Techniques like tissue culture allow for the growth of disease-free plants from a single parent.

5.  Why is DNA copying an essential part of the process of reproduction?
    > **Answer:** DNA copying is an essential part of reproduction because DNA carries the genetic information (the blueprint) for the organism's body design and functions. For a new individual to be created, this genetic information must be accurately duplicated and passed on to the daughter cells. Without DNA copying, new cells or organisms would not have the necessary instructions to grow, develop, and maintain life processes, ensuring the continuity of the species.
