# Anatomy and Function of the Human Eye

The *retina* is a delicate membrane located at the back of the eye, containing an enormous number of light-sensitive cells. These cells become activated when illuminated by light, generating electrical signals. These electrical signals are then transmitted to the brain via the *optic nerves*. The brain interprets these signals, processing the information so that we perceive objects as they are.

Here's a breakdown of the key parts of the human eye and their functions:

*   **Cornea:** The transparent, bulging front part of the eye. It is the primary surface where light refraction occurs as light enters the eye.
*   **Iris:** A dark, muscular diaphragm located behind the cornea. It controls the size of the pupil.
*   **Pupil:** The opening in the center of the iris. It regulates the amount of light entering the eye by changing its size (dilating in dim light, constricting in bright light).
*   **Crystalline Lens:** A transparent, biconvex lens behind the pupil. It provides the fine adjustment of focal length to focus light onto the retina.
*   **Ciliary Muscles:** Muscles attached to the crystalline lens. They change the curvature (and thus the focal length) of the eye lens.
*   **Retina:** The light-sensitive screen at the back of the eye. It contains light-sensitive cells (rods and cones) that convert light into electrical signals.
*   **Optic Nerve:** A bundle of nerves that transmits the electrical signals generated by the retina to the brain for interpretation.
*   **Aqueous Humour:** A clear fluid filling the space between the cornea and the crystalline lens.
*   **Vitreous Humour:** A clear, gel-like substance that fills the large cavity behind the crystalline lens, helping to maintain the eye's spherical shape.
