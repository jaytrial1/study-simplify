# Hypermetropia (Farsightedness) and its Correction

**Hypermetropia**, also known as **far-sightedness**, is a refractive defect where a person can see distant objects clearly but cannot see nearby objects distinctly. For a person with hypermetropia, the *near point* is farther away from the normal near point (25 cm). Such a person often needs to hold reading material much beyond 25 cm from the eye for comfortable reading.

### Characteristics of Hypermetropia

In a hypermetropic eye, light rays from a close-by object are focused at a point *behind the retina*. This leads to blurred vision for near objects.

### Causes of Hypermetropia

This defect arises either because:
*   **The focal length of the eye lens is too long:** The lens has insufficient converging power.
*   **The eyeball has become too small:** The retina is too close to the lens.

### Correction for Hypermetropia

Hypermetropia can be corrected by using a **convex lens** (converging lens) of appropriate power. Eye-glasses with converging lenses provide the additional focusing power required to ensure the image is formed correctly on the retina, allowing clear near vision.
