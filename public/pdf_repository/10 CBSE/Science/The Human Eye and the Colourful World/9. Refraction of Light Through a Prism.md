This section introduces the phenomenon of light refraction through a transparent prism, contrasting it with refraction through a rectangular glass slab, and details an activity to observe this effect.

# Refraction of Light Through a Prism

In the previous chapter, we learned about the refraction of light through a rectangular glass slab. For parallel refracting surfaces, like those in a glass slab, the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray, although it is slightly displaced laterally. However, the refraction of light through a transparent prism behaves differently.

A triangular glass prism has two triangular bases and three rectangular lateral surfaces. These surfaces are inclined to each other. The angle between its two lateral faces is called the **angle of the prism**.

### Activity: Studying Refraction Through a Triangular Glass Prism

Let's perform an activity to study the refraction of light through a triangular glass prism:

1.  Fix a sheet of white paper on a drawing board using drawing pins.
2.  Place a glass prism on the paper such that it rests on its triangular base.
3.  Trace the outline of the prism using a pencil.
4.  Draw a straight line, PE, inclined to one of the refracting surfaces of the prism (e.g., surface AB).
5.  Fix two pins, say at points P and Q, on the line PE.
6.  Look for the images of the pins (fixed at P and Q) through the other face of the prism (e.g., face AC).
7.  Fix two more pins, at points R and S, such that the pins at R and S, and the images of the pins at P and Q, all lie on the same straight line.
8.  Remove the pins and the glass prism.
9.  The line PE meets the boundary of the prism at point E. Similarly, join and extend the line through points R and S. Let this line meet the boundary of the prism at point F.
10. Join points E and F. This line EF represents the refracted ray inside the prism.
11. Draw perpendiculars (normals) to the refracting surfaces AB and AC of the prism at points E and F, respectively.
12. Mark the **angle of incidence** (∠i), the **angle of refraction** (∠r), and the **angle of emergence** (∠e) as shown in the diagram.

In this setup:
*   PE is the **incident ray**.
*   EF is the **refracted ray**.
*   FS is the **emergent ray**.
*   ∠A is the **angle of the prism**.
*   ∠i is the **angle of incidence**.
*   ∠r is the **angle of refraction**.
*   ∠e is the **angle of emergence**.
*   ∠D is the **angle of deviation**.

When the light ray enters from air to glass at the first surface (AB), it bends *towards the normal*. At the second surface (AC), the light ray enters from glass to air, and thus it bends *away from the normal*. Unlike a glass slab, the unique shape of the prism causes the emergent ray to bend at an angle to the direction of the incident ray. This angle is called the **angle of deviation** (∠D).
