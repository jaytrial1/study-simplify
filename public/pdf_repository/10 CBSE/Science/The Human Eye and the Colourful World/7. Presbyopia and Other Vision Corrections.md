This section discusses presbyopia, an age-related vision defect, and explains how it is corrected using bifocal lenses, along with other modern corrective methods.

# Presbyopia and Other Vision Corrections

### Presbyopia

**Presbyopia** is a common vision defect that usually develops with aging. For most people, the *near point* gradually recedes away, making it difficult to see nearby objects comfortably and distinctly without corrective eye-glasses. This defect arises due to:

*   The gradual **weakening of the ciliary muscles**.
*   The **diminishing flexibility of the eye lens**.

### Correction for Presbyopia

Sometimes, a person may suffer from both myopia and hypermetropia. Such individuals often require **bi-focal lenses**. A common type of bifocal lens consists of both concave and convex lenses:
*   The **upper portion** contains a **concave lens**, which facilitates distant vision (corrects myopia).
*   The **lower part** contains a **convex lens**, which facilitates near vision (corrects hypermetropia/presbyopia).

### Modern Vision Correction Methods

Beyond traditional spectacles, it is now possible to correct refractive defects through:
*   **Contact lenses:** Thin lenses placed directly on the surface of the eye.
*   **Surgical interventions:** Procedures like LASIK that permanently reshape the cornea.
