# Introduction to the Human Eye

The human eye is one of the most valuable and sensitive sense organs, enabling us to perceive the wonderful world and its vibrant colors. While other senses like smell, taste, sound, or touch allow us to identify objects to some extent, only vision provides the full experience of colors.

The human eye functions much like a camera. It has a lens system that forms an image on a light-sensitive screen called the *retina*. Light first enters the eye through a thin, transparent membrane known as the *cornea*, which forms a bulge on the front surface of the eyeball. The eyeball is approximately spherical, with a diameter of about 2.3 cm. Most of the bending (refraction) of light rays entering the eye occurs at the outer surface of the cornea. The *crystalline lens* within the eye provides the finer adjustment of focal length needed to focus objects at various distances onto the retina.

Behind the cornea, there is a dark, muscular diaphragm called the *iris*. The iris controls the size of the *pupil*, which is the opening in its center. The pupil, in turn, regulates and controls the amount of light entering the eye. The eye lens forms an inverted, real image of the object on the retina.
