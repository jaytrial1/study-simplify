# Myopia (Nearsightedness) and its Correction

**Myopia**, also known as **nearsightedness**, is a refractive defect where a person can see nearby objects clearly but cannot see distant objects distinctly. For a person with myopia, the *far point* is nearer than infinity, meaning they can only see clearly up to a distance of a few meters.

### Characteristics of Myopia

In a myopic eye, the image of a distant object is formed *in front of the retina*, instead of directly on it. This results in blurred vision for distant objects.

### Causes of Myopia

This defect can arise due due to two main reasons:
*   **Excessive curvature of the eye lens:** The lens is too curved, leading to too much converging power.
*   **Elongation of the eyeball:** The eyeball is too long, causing the retina to be too far from the lens.

### Correction for Myopia

Myopia can be corrected by using a **concave lens** (diverging lens) of suitable power. A concave lens diverges the incoming light rays slightly before they enter the eye, ensuring that the image is formed precisely on the retina, thus correcting the defect and allowing clear distant vision.
