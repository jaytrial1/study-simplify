## Exercises

1.  Which of the statements about the reaction below are incorrect?
    $2\mathrm{PbO}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{s}) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$
    (a) Lead is getting reduced.
    (b) Carbon dioxide is getting oxidised.
    (c) Carbon is getting oxidised.
    (d) Lead oxide is getting reduced.
    (i) (a) and (b)
    (ii) (a) and (c)
    (iii) (a), (b) and (c)
    (iv) all

    > **Answer:**
    > In the reaction $2\mathrm{PbO}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{s}) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{s})+\mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$:
    > *   $\mathrm{PbO}$ loses oxygen to become $\mathrm{Pb}$, so $\mathrm{PbO}$ is reduced. (Statement d is correct)
    > *   Carbon (C) gains oxygen to become $\mathrm{CO}_{2}$, so Carbon is oxidised. (Statement c is correct)
    > *   Lead ($\mathrm{Pb}$) is the product of reduction, not getting reduced. (Statement a is incorrect)
    > *   Carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO}_{2}$) is the product of oxidation, not getting oxidised. (Statement b is incorrect)
    >
    > Therefore, the incorrect statements are (a) and (b).
    > **Correct option: (i) (a) and (b)**

4.  What is a balanced chemical equation? Why should chemical equations be balanced?

    > **Answer:**
    > A **balanced chemical equation** is a symbolic representation of a chemical reaction where the number of atoms of each element on the reactant side (left-hand side, LHS) is equal to the number of atoms of the same element on the product side (right-hand side, RHS).
    >
    > Chemical equations should be balanced to satisfy the **Law of Conservation of Mass**. This law states that mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction. Therefore, the total mass of the reactants must be equal to the total mass of the products. Balancing an equation ensures that the number of atoms of each element remains the same before and after the reaction, thus conserving mass.

9.  What does one mean by exothermic and endothermic reactions? Give examples.

    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Exothermic reactions:** These are chemical reactions that release energy, usually in the form of heat, light, or sound, into the surroundings. This causes the temperature of the surroundings to increase.
    >     *   **Example:** Burning of natural gas: $\mathrm{CH}_{4}(\mathrm{~g}) + 2\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow \mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + 2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{g}) + \text{Heat}$
    > *   **Endothermic reactions:** These are chemical reactions that absorb energy from the surroundings, usually in the form of heat, light, or electricity. This causes the temperature of the surroundings to decrease.
    >     *   **Example:** Decomposition of calcium carbonate: $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3}(\mathrm{~s}) + \text{Heat} \rightarrow \mathrm{CaO}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$

10. Why is respiration considered an exothermic reaction? Explain.

    > **Answer:** Respiration is considered an exothermic reaction because it is a process that releases energy. During respiration, glucose ($\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_{6}$), obtained from the food we eat, combines with oxygen in the cells of our body to produce carbon dioxide, water, and a significant amount of energy. This energy is essential for carrying out various life processes. Since energy is released, respiration is classified as an exothermic reaction.
    > $\mathrm{C}_{6}\mathrm{H}_{12}\mathrm{O}_{6}(\mathrm{aq}) + 6\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow 6\mathrm{CO}_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) + 6\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l}) + \text{Energy}$

11. Why are decomposition reactions called the opposite of combination reactions? Write equations for these reactions.

    > **Answer:** Decomposition reactions are called the opposite of combination reactions because of the fundamental change in the number of reactants and products involved.
    > *   In a **combination reaction**, two or more simple substances (elements or compounds) combine to form a single, more complex product.
    > *   In a **decomposition reaction**, a single, complex reactant breaks down into two or more simpler products (elements or compounds).
    >
    > **Equations:**
    > *   **Combination Reaction Example:** Formation of water
    >     $2\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})$
    > *   **Decomposition Reaction Example:** Electrolysis of water
    >     $2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l}) \xrightarrow{\text {Electricity}} 2\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$

12. Write one equation each for decomposition reactions where energy is supplied in the form of heat, light or electricity.

    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Decomposition by Heat (Thermal Decomposition):**
    >     Heating of lead nitrate: $2\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{NO}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{~s}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} 2\mathrm{PbO}(\mathrm{s}) + 4\mathrm{NO}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$
    > *   **Decomposition by Light (Photochemical Decomposition):**
    >     Decomposition of silver chloride: $2\mathrm{AgCl}(\mathrm{~s}) \xrightarrow{\text {Sunlight}} 2\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{~s}) + \mathrm{Cl}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$
    > *   **Decomposition by Electricity (Electrolytic Decomposition):**
    >     Electrolysis of water: $2\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l}) \xrightarrow{\text {Electricity}} 2\mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g})$

13. What is the difference between displacement and double displacement reactions? Write equations for these reactions.

    > **Answer:**
    > The main difference between displacement and double displacement reactions lies in the number of elements or ions that are exchanged or replaced.
    >
    > *   **Displacement Reaction:** In a displacement reaction, a more reactive element displaces a less reactive element from its compound. It involves the replacement of one element by another.
    >     *   **Equation Example:** Zinc displaces copper from copper sulphate solution.
    >         $\mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{CuSO}_{4}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow \mathrm{ZnSO}_{4}(\mathrm{aq}) + \mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s})$
    >
    > *   **Double Displacement Reaction:** In a double displacement reaction, there is an exchange of ions (or groups of atoms) between two reactant compounds to form two new compounds. Both reactants are typically ionic compounds in solution.
    >     *   **Equation Example:** Reaction between sodium sulphate and barium chloride.
    >         $\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}(\mathrm{aq}) + \mathrm{BaCl}_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow \mathrm{BaSO}_{4}(\mathrm{~s}) + 2\mathrm{NaCl}(\mathrm{aq})$

14. In the refining of silver, the recovery of silver from silver nitrate solution involved displacement by copper metal. Write down the reaction involved.

    > **Answer:** The reaction involved in the recovery of silver from silver nitrate solution by copper metal is a displacement reaction. Copper is more reactive than silver, so it displaces silver from its salt solution.
    > $\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s}) + 2\mathrm{AgNO}_{3}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{NO}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) + 2\mathrm{Ag}(\mathrm{s})$

15. What do you mean by a precipitation reaction? Explain by giving examples.

    > **Answer:** A **precipitation reaction** is a type of chemical reaction that occurs in an aqueous solution where two soluble ionic compounds react to form at least one insoluble product, called a **precipitate**. The precipitate is a solid that separates from the solution.
    >
    > **Example 1:** When solutions of barium chloride and sodium sulphate are mixed, a white precipitate of barium sulphate is formed.
    > $\mathrm{BaCl}_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) + \mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow \mathrm{BaSO}_{4}(\mathrm{~s}) \downarrow + 2\mathrm{NaCl}(\mathrm{aq})$
    > (Barium sulphate is the white precipitate)
    >
    > **Example 2:** When solutions of lead(II) nitrate and potassium iodide are mixed, a yellow precipitate of lead(II) iodide is formed.
    > $\mathrm{Pb}(\mathrm{NO}_{3})_{2}(\mathrm{aq}) + 2\mathrm{KI}(\mathrm{aq}) \rightarrow \mathrm{PbI}_{2}(\mathrm{~s}) \downarrow + 2\mathrm{KNO}_{3}(\mathrm{aq})$
    > (Lead(II) iodide is the yellow precipitate)

16. Explain the following in terms of gain or loss of oxygen with two examples each.
    (a) Oxidation
    (b) Reduction

    > **Answer:**
    > (a) **Oxidation:** Oxidation is a chemical process that involves the **gain of oxygen** by a substance.
    >     *   **Example 1:** Burning of magnesium in air. Magnesium gains oxygen to form magnesium oxide.
    >         $2\mathrm{Mg}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{MgO}(\mathrm{s})$
    >     *   **Example 2:** Formation of copper(II) oxide from copper. Copper gains oxygen.
    >         $2\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} 2\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s})$
    >
    > (b) **Reduction:** Reduction is a chemical process that involves the **loss of oxygen** by a substance.
    >     *   **Example 1:** Reduction of copper(II) oxide by hydrogen. Copper(II) oxide loses oxygen.
    >         $\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{H}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} \mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l})$
    >     *   **Example 2:** Reduction of zinc oxide by carbon. Zinc oxide loses oxygen.
    >         $\mathrm{ZnO}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{C}(\mathrm{s}) \rightarrow \mathrm{Zn}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{CO}(\mathrm{g})$

17. A shiny brown coloured element 'X' on heating in air becomes black in colour. Name the element 'X' and the black coloured compound formed.

    > **Answer:**
    > *   The shiny brown coloured element 'X' is **Copper** (Cu).
    > *   When copper is heated in air, it reacts with oxygen to form a black coloured compound. This black coloured compound is **Copper(II) oxide** (CuO).
    > The reaction is: $2\mathrm{Cu}(\mathrm{s}) + \mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) \xrightarrow{\text {Heat}} 2\mathrm{CuO}(\mathrm{s})$

18. Why do we apply paint on iron articles?

    > **Answer:** We apply paint on iron articles primarily to prevent **corrosion**, specifically rusting. Iron articles rust when they are exposed to moisture and oxygen in the air. Paint acts as a protective barrier, preventing direct contact between the iron surface and the atmospheric oxygen and water, thereby slowing down or completely stopping the rusting process.

19. Oil and fat containing food items are flushed with nitrogen. Why?

    > **Answer:** Oil and fat containing food items are flushed with nitrogen because nitrogen is an inert gas (it does not readily react with other substances). Fats and oils in food are susceptible to oxidation when exposed to oxygen in the air, which leads to **rancidity** (changes in smell, taste, and spoilage). By flushing the food packaging with nitrogen, the oxygen is displaced, creating an oxygen-free environment that significantly slows down the oxidation process, thus preserving the freshness and quality of the food items for a longer period.

20. Explain the following terms with one example each.
    (a) Corrosion
    (b) Rancidity

    > **Answer:**
    > (a) **Corrosion:** Corrosion is the process by which metals are gradually eaten away or degraded due to the chemical reaction with substances present in their environment, such as moisture, acids, gases, etc. It is a type of oxidation reaction.
    >     *   **Example:** Rusting of iron. When iron articles are exposed to moist air for a long time, they react with oxygen and water to form a reddish-brown flaky substance called rust (hydrated iron(III) oxide).
    >         $4\mathrm{Fe}(\mathrm{s}) + 3\mathrm{O}_{2}(\mathrm{~g}) + x\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{l}) \rightarrow 2\mathrm{Fe}_{2}\mathrm{O}_{3} \cdot x\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{s})$ (Rust)
    >
    > (b) **Rancidity:** Rancidity is the process of oxidative degradation of fats and oils present in food items, leading to an unpleasant smell, taste, and overall spoilage of the food. This occurs when fats and oils react with oxygen from the air.
    >     *   **Example:** When potato chips or fried snacks are left exposed to air for a long time, the oils and fats in them get oxidised, causing them to develop a stale, unpleasant smell and taste. This is why chips packets are often flushed with nitrogen gas.

## Group Activity

Perform the following activity.

*   Take four beakers and label them as A, B, C and D.
*   Put 25 mL of water in A, B and C beakers and copper sulphate solution in beaker D.
*   Measure and record the temperature of each liquid contained in the beakers above.
*   Add two spatulas of potassium sulphate, ammonium nitrate, anhydrous copper sulphate and fine iron fillings to beakers A, B, C and D respectively and stir.
*   Finally measure and record the temperature of each of the mixture above.

Find out which reactions are exothermic and which ones are endothermic in nature.

> **Expected Observations and Answers for Group Activity:**
>
> *   **Beaker A (Water + Potassium Sulphate):**
>     *   Observation: Temperature might slightly increase or remain nearly constant. (Potassium sulphate dissolves with a small heat change, often slightly exothermic or thermoneutral)
>     *   Type of Reaction: Likely **exothermic** (slight) or **thermoneutral** dissolution.
> *   **Beaker B (Water + Ammonium Nitrate):**
>     *   Observation: Temperature will significantly decrease, making the beaker feel cold.
>     *   Type of Reaction: **Endothermic** (dissolution of ammonium nitrate is highly endothermic).
> *   **Beaker C (Water + Anhydrous Copper Sulphate):**
>     *   Observation: Temperature will increase, making the beaker feel warm.
>     *   Type of Reaction: **Exothermic** (hydration of anhydrous copper sulphate is exothermic).
> *   **Beaker D (Copper Sulphate Solution + Fine Iron Fillings):**
>     *   Observation: Temperature might slightly increase. The blue colour of the solution will fade and turn pale green, and the iron fillings will get a reddish-brown coating.
>     *   Type of Reaction: **Exothermic** (displacement reaction of iron with copper sulphate is exothermic).
