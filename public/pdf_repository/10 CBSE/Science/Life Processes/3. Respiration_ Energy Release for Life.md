# Respiration

The food material taken in during nutrition is used by cells to provide energy for various life processes. Different organisms achieve this in different ways. Some use oxygen to completely break down glucose into carbon dioxide and water (aerobic respiration), while others use pathways that do not involve oxygen (anaerobic respiration).

*   **Conclusion:** This tells us that **carbon dioxide is a product of fermentation** (a type of anaerobic respiration) carried out by yeast.

### Breakdown of Glucose

In all cases of respiration, the first step is the breakdown of **glucose** (a six-carbon molecule) into a three-carbon molecule called **pyruvate**. This initial process, called **glycolysis**, takes place in the **cytoplasm** of the cell and releases some energy.

Further breakdown of pyruvate depends on the presence or absence of oxygen:

1.  **Anaerobic Respiration (Absence of Oxygen):**
    *   **In Yeast (Fermentation):** Pyruvate is converted into **ethanol** (a two-carbon molecule) and **carbon dioxide**. This process occurs without air (oxygen).
    *   **In Our Muscle Cells (Lack of Oxygen):** During sudden intense activity, when there is insufficient oxygen, pyruvate is converted into **lactic acid** (a three-carbon molecule). The buildup of lactic acid in muscles causes cramps.

2.  **Aerobic Respiration (Presence of Oxygen):**
    *   This process takes place in the **mitochondria**.
    *   Pyruvate is completely broken down into **three molecules of carbon dioxide** and **water**.
    *   Aerobic respiration releases a significantly *greater amount of energy* compared to anaerobic respiration.

### ATP: The Energy Currency

The energy released during cellular respiration is immediately used to synthesize a molecule called **ATP (Adenosine Triphosphate)**. ATP is the *energy currency* for most cellular processes. When the terminal phosphate linkage in ATP is broken using water, a fixed amount of energy (equivalent to 30.5 kJ/mol) is released, which drives endothermic reactions in the cell.

**ADP + (P) + Energy → ADP-(P) = ATP**
(P): Inorganic Phosphate

Just as a battery provides energy for various uses (mechanical, light, electrical), ATP can be used in cells for diverse activities like muscle contraction, protein synthesis, and conduction of nervous impulses.

### Gas Exchange in Organisms

Since aerobic respiration requires oxygen, aerobic organisms must ensure sufficient oxygen intake.

*   **Plants:** Exchange gases through **stomata** on leaves, and also across stems and roots. Large inter-cellular spaces ensure all cells are in contact with air. Carbon dioxide and oxygen are exchanged by diffusion.
    *   **At night:** No photosynthesis occurs, so CO₂ elimination (from respiration) is the major exchange activity.
    *   **During the day:** CO₂ produced during respiration is used for photosynthesis, so there is no CO₂ release. Instead, oxygen release (from photosynthesis) is the major event.

*   **Animals:** Have evolved different organs for oxygen uptake and carbon dioxide removal.
    *   **Aquatic Organisms:** Live in water and use dissolved oxygen.
        *   **Fishes:** Take in water through their mouths and force it past their **gills**. Gills have a large surface area and are richly supplied with blood vessels to absorb dissolved oxygen.
    *   **Terrestrial Organisms:** Breathe oxygen from the atmosphere. Their respiratory organs have structures that increase surface area for gas contact, are delicate, and are usually protected inside the body with passages for air movement.

### Human Respiratory System

In human beings, air is taken into the body through the **nostrils**.

*   The air is filtered by fine hairs and mucus lining the passage in the nostrils, trapping dust and germs.
*   From here, air passes through the throat (pharynx and larynx) and into the **lungs**.
*   **Rings of cartilage** are present in the throat (trachea) to ensure that the air-passage does not collapse.

Within the lungs, the air passage divides into smaller and smaller tubes (bronchioles) which finally end in balloon-like structures called **alveoli** (singular: alveolus). The alveoli provide a vast surface area for the exchange of gases. Their walls contain an extensive network of blood vessels.

When we breathe in, our ribs lift, and the diaphragm flattens, increasing the chest cavity size. This draws air into the lungs, filling the expanded alveoli. Blood brings carbon dioxide from the body to the alveoli for release, and oxygen from the alveolar air is absorbed by the blood in the alveolar blood vessels to be transported to all body cells. During the breathing cycle, the lungs always retain a **residual volume of air** to allow sufficient time for oxygen absorption and carbon dioxide release.

### Respiratory Pigments

In large animals, diffusion alone cannot efficiently deliver oxygen to all body parts. Instead, **respiratory pigments** take up oxygen from the air in the lungs and carry it to oxygen-deficient tissues.

*   In human beings, the respiratory pigment is **haemoglobin**, found in red blood corpuscles. Haemoglobin has a very high affinity for oxygen.
*   Carbon dioxide is more soluble in water than oxygen and is mostly transported in the dissolved form in our blood.

### Did You Know?

*   The total surface area of all alveoli in the lungs, if spread out, would cover about 80 square meters. This vast surface area significantly increases the efficiency of gas exchange.
*   If oxygen were to rely solely on diffusion to move from the lungs to our toes, it is estimated that it would take approximately three years for a single oxygen molecule to reach its destination. This highlights the critical role of haemoglobin and the circulatory system in rapid oxygen transport.

### Important Note on Tobacco

Using tobacco directly or in any product (cigars, cigarettes, bidis, hookah, gutkha, etc.) is harmful. Tobacco use commonly affects the tongue, lungs, heart, and liver. Smokeless tobacco is also a major risk factor for heart attacks, strokes, pulmonary diseases, and various cancers. India has a high incidence of oral cancer due to chewing gutkha. Say NO to tobacco for a healthy life!

The upper part of the respiratory tract is lined with small, hair-like structures called **cilia**. These cilia help to remove germs, dust, and other harmful particles from inhaled air. Smoking destroys these cilia, allowing germs, dust, smoke, and harmful chemicals to enter the lungs, causing infections, coughs, and even lung cancer.

### Questions

1.  What advantage over an aquatic organism does a terrestrial organism have with regard to obtaining oxygen for respiration?
    > **Answer:** Terrestrial organisms have a significant advantage over aquatic organisms in obtaining oxygen because:
    > *   **Higher Oxygen Concentration:** The concentration of oxygen in the atmosphere (air) is much higher (about 21%) than the concentration of dissolved oxygen in water (which is typically less than 1%).
    > *   **Easier Diffusion:** Gases diffuse much faster in air than in water.
    > *   **Less Energy Required:** Terrestrial organisms do not need to expend as much energy to move the respiratory medium (air) across their respiratory surfaces compared to aquatic organisms, which must constantly pump large volumes of water over their gills. This allows for more efficient oxygen uptake.

2.  What are the different ways in which glucose is oxidised to provide energy in various organisms?
    > **Answer:** Glucose (a 6-carbon molecule) is first broken down into pyruvate (a 3-carbon molecule) in the cytoplasm (glycolysis). The subsequent oxidation pathways depend on the availability of oxygen:
    > *   **Aerobic Respiration (Presence of Oxygen):**
    >     *   **Location:** Mitochondria.
    >     *   **Process:** Pyruvate is completely broken down into carbon dioxide (CO₂), water (H₂O), and a large amount of energy.
    >     *   **Organisms:** Most living organisms (e.g., humans, animals, plants).
    > *   **Anaerobic Respiration (Absence or Lack of Oxygen):**
    >     *   **Location:** Cytoplasm.
    >     *   **Pathways:**
    >         *   **Ethanol Fermentation:** Pyruvate is converted into ethanol (a 2-carbon alcohol) and carbon dioxide (CO₂).
    >             *   **Organisms:** Yeast, some bacteria.
    >         *   **Lactic Acid Fermentation:** Pyruvate is converted into lactic acid (a 3-carbon molecule).
    >             *   **Organisms:** Human muscle cells (during strenuous activity), some bacteria.
    >     *   **Energy Yield:** Produces a much smaller amount of energy compared to aerobic respiration.

3.  How is oxygen and carbon dioxide transported in human beings?
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Oxygen Transport:**
    >     *   Oxygen is primarily transported by the respiratory pigment **haemoglobin**, which is present in the red blood corpuscles (RBCs).
    >     *   Haemoglobin has a very high affinity for oxygen and binds with it in the lungs to form oxyhaemoglobin.
    >     *   This oxygenated blood is then pumped by the heart to all tissues and cells of the body, where oxygen is released due to lower oxygen partial pressure.
    > *   **Carbon Dioxide Transport:**
    >     *   Carbon dioxide is more soluble in water than oxygen.
    >     *   Most of the carbon dioxide (about 70%) is transported in the dissolved form as **bicarbonate ions** in the blood plasma.
    >     *   A smaller amount (about 23%) binds with haemoglobin to form carbaminohemoglobin.
    >     *   The remaining (about 7%) is transported as dissolved CO₂ in the plasma.
    >     *   Carbon dioxide is carried to the lungs, where it diffuses from the blood into the alveoli and is exhaled.

4.  How are the lungs designed in human beings to maximise the area for exchange of gases?
    > **Answer:** The human lungs are exceptionally well-designed to maximize the surface area for efficient gas exchange:
    > *   **Alveoli:** The lungs contain millions of tiny, balloon-like structures called *alveoli*. These numerous alveoli provide an enormous total surface area (approximately 80 square meters) for gas exchange.
    > *   **Thin Walls:** The walls of the alveoli are extremely thin (only one cell thick), allowing for rapid diffusion of gases between the air in the alveoli and the blood in the capillaries.
    > *   **Rich Blood Supply:** Each alveolus is surrounded by an extensive network of highly permeable blood capillaries. This rich blood supply ensures that a large volume of blood is constantly available for gas exchange.
    > *   **Residual Volume:** The lungs maintain a residual volume of air even after exhalation. This ensures that there is always sufficient time for oxygen to be absorbed into the blood and carbon dioxide to be released from the blood, even between breaths.
