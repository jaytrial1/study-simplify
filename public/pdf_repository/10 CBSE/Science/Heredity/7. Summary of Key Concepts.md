This section provides a concise summary of the main topics and concepts covered in the chapter, reinforcing the understanding of heredity, variation, and genetic inheritance.

# Summary of Key Concepts

*   *Variations* that arise during reproduction can be *inherited*.
*   These *variations* can contribute to the increased survival of individuals within a species, especially in changing environments.
*   *Sexually reproducing individuals* possess two copies of *genes* for the same *trait*. If these copies (alleles) are not identical, the *trait* that is expressed is termed the *dominant trait*, while the masked *trait* is the *recessive trait*.
*   *Traits* in an individual can be *inherited independently* of each other, leading to new combinations of *traits* in the offspring of *sexual reproduction* (Mendel's Law of Independent Assortment).
*   The *sex* of an individual is determined by different factors across various species. In human beings, the *sex* of the child is determined by the *paternal sex chromosome*: an *X chromosome* results in a *girl*, and a *Y chromosome* results in a *boy*.
