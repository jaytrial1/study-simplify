# Hormones in Animals (Endocrine System)

Animals also use chemical, or **hormonal**, means for information transmission, forming the **endocrine system**. This system is crucial for widespread and persistent changes in the body, especially in situations requiring a coordinated response from many tissue types.

Consider a scary situation for an animal, like a squirrel encountering a predator:
*   The body needs to prepare for "fight or flight."
*   This preparation requires integrating activities of many different tissues.
*   If only electrical impulses were used, the range of affected tissues would be limited.
*   A chemical signal, however, can reach *all cells* of the body, providing widespread changes.

This is achieved in many animals, including humans, by the hormone **adrenaline**.
*   **Secretion:** Adrenaline is secreted from the **adrenal glands** (located on top of the kidneys) directly into the blood.
*   **Transport:** It is carried by the blood to different parts of the body.
*   **Target Organs/Effects:**
    *   **Heart:** Beats faster, increasing oxygen supply to muscles.
    *   **Digestive system and skin:** Blood supply is reduced due to contraction of muscles around small arteries, diverting blood to skeletal muscles.
    *   **Breathing rate:** Increases due to contractions of the diaphragm and rib muscles.
*   **Overall Response:** All these responses together prepare the animal body to deal with stressful or dangerous situations by providing a burst of energy and heightened senses.

Animal hormones are part of the **endocrine system**, which is a second major way of control and coordination in our body, alongside the nervous system.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Hypothalamus:** Plays a crucial role in regulating the release of many hormones. For example, it releases **growth hormone releasing factor** to stimulate the pituitary gland to release growth hormone.
*   **Thyroxin and Iodine:**
    *   **Thyroxin** is a hormone produced by the **thyroid gland**.
    *   It regulates carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism for balanced growth.
    *   **Iodine** is essential for thyroxin synthesis.
    *   **Goitre** is a disease caused by iodine deficiency, characterized by a swollen neck due to an enlarged thyroid gland.
*   **Growth Hormone:**
    *   Secreted by the **pituitary gland**.
    *   Regulates the growth and development of the body.
    *   Deficiency in childhood leads to **dwarfism** (very short stature).
    *   Excess secretion can lead to **gigantism** (extremely tall stature).
*   **Sex Hormones (Puberty):**
    *   **Testosterone:** Secreted by the **testes** in males, responsible for changes associated with male puberty (e.g., development of secondary sexual characteristics).
    *   **Oestrogen:** Secreted by the **ovaries** in females, responsible for changes associated with female puberty (e.g., development of female sex organs, regulation of the menstrual cycle).
*   **Insulin and Diabetes:**
    *   **Insulin** is a hormone produced by the **pancreas**.
    *   It regulates **blood sugar levels** by helping cells absorb glucose from the blood.
    *   If insulin is not secreted in proper amounts, blood sugar levels rise, leading to **diabetes**. Patients with diabetes may require insulin injections as treatment.

### Regulation of Hormone Secretion (Feedback Mechanism)

Hormones must be secreted in precise quantities. This is regulated by **feedback mechanisms**.
*   **Example (Blood Sugar):** If blood sugar levels rise, cells in the pancreas detect this and respond by producing *more insulin*. As the blood sugar level falls, the secretion of insulin is *reduced*. This negative feedback loop ensures stable blood sugar levels.

### Q U E S T I O N S
1. How does chemical coordination take place in animals?
> **Answer:** Chemical coordination in animals takes place through the **endocrine system**, which involves specialized glands called **endocrine glands**. These glands secrete chemical messengers called **hormones** directly into the bloodstream. The blood then transports these hormones to specific target cells, tissues, or organs throughout the body. Hormones bind to specific receptors on these target cells, triggering a specific physiological response, thereby coordinating various long-term processes like growth, development, metabolism, and reproduction.

2. Why is the use of iodised salt advisable?
> **Answer:** The use of iodised salt is advisable because **iodine** is an essential mineral required by the **thyroid gland** to synthesize the hormone **thyroxin**. Thyroxin regulates carbohydrate, protein, and fat metabolism, which is crucial for balanced growth and development of the body. A deficiency of iodine in the diet can lead to a condition called **goitre**, characterized by an enlarged thyroid gland and impaired metabolic functions.

3. How does our body respond when adrenaline is secreted into the blood?
> **Answer:** When adrenaline is secreted into the blood, the body responds by preparing for a "fight or flight" situation. Key responses include:
> *   Increased heart rate, leading to more oxygen supply to muscles.
> *   Increased breathing rate.
> *   Diverted blood flow from the digestive system and skin to the skeletal muscles.
> *   Increased blood pressure.
> *   Increased glucose release into the blood for energy.
> These changes collectively enable the body to react quickly and effectively to stress or danger.

4. Why are some patients of diabetes treated by giving injections of insulin?
> **Answer:** Some patients with diabetes are treated by giving injections of insulin because their bodies either do not produce enough insulin (Type 1 diabetes) or do not effectively use the insulin they produce (Type 2 diabetes). Insulin is a hormone produced by the pancreas that is crucial for regulating blood sugar levels by facilitating the uptake of glucose from the blood into cells for energy or storage. Without sufficient or effective insulin, blood sugar levels remain high, leading to various harmful health effects. Insulin injections help to supplement the body's insulin, allowing glucose to be utilized properly and keeping blood sugar levels within a healthy range.
