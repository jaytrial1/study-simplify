# Introduction to Control and Coordination

In living organisms, movements are often associated with life. While some movements, like the growth of a seedling, are directly tied to *growth*, many others, especially in animals, are not. For instance, a cat running or children playing are movements independent of growth. These visible movements are typically a *response to a change in the environment*. Organisms use these responses to their advantage, such as plants growing towards sunshine or animals reacting to threats.

All such movements, in response to the environment, are *carefully controlled*. Each environmental change evokes an appropriate and precise movement. This implies that living organisms must possess systems for *control and coordination*, allowing them to recognize events and execute the correct response. In multicellular organisms, specialized tissues are employed for these vital functions.
