In your earlier classes, you learned that **power** is defined as the rate of doing work. In the context of electricity, it is also the rate of consumption of energy.

Equation (11.19) gives the rate at which electric energy is dissipated or consumed in an electric circuit. This is also termed as **electric power**. The power $P$ is given by:
$$
P=V I
$$
Using Ohm's Law ($V=IR$ or $I=V/R$), we can express power in other forms:
*   Substituting $V=IR$ into $P=VI$:
    $P = (IR)I = I^2R$
*   Substituting $I=V/R$ into $P=VI$:
    $P = V(V/R) = V^2/R$

So, the formulas for electric power are:
$$
P=V I = I^{2} R = V^{2} / R
$$

The SI unit of electric power is **watt (W)**. One watt is the power consumed by a device that carries 1 A of current when operated at a potential difference of 1 V.
Thus, $1 \mathrm{~W} = 1 \text{ volt} \times 1 \text{ ampere} = 1 \mathrm{~V} \mathrm{~A}$.

The unit 'watt' is very small for practical applications. Therefore, in actual practice, we use a much larger unit called **kilowatt (kW)**. It is equal to 1000 watts ($1 \mathrm{~kW} = 1000 \mathrm{~W}$).

Since electrical energy is the product of power and time ($E = P \times t$), the unit of electric energy is, therefore, **watt-hour (Wh)**. One watt-hour is the energy consumed when 1 watt of power is used for 1 hour.

The commercial unit of electric energy is **kilowatt-hour (kWh)**, commonly known as a 'unit' when referring to electricity bills.
We can convert kilowatt-hour to joules:
$1 \mathrm{~kW h} = 1000 \text{ watt} \times 3600 \text{ second}$
$1 \mathrm{~kW h} = 3,600,000 \text{ watt second}$
$1 \mathrm{~kW h} = 3.6 \times 10^{6} \text{ joule (J)}$

It's a common misconception that electrons are consumed in an electric circuit. This is incorrect! We pay the electricity board or electric company to provide the *energy* that moves electrons through electric gadgets like electric bulbs, fans, and engines. We pay for the electrical energy that we use, not for the electrons themselves.

## Example 11.12

An electric bulb is connected to a 220 V generator. The current is 0.50 A. What is the power of the bulb?

### Solution

Given:
Voltage $V = 220 \mathrm{~V}$
Current $I = 0.50 \mathrm{~A}$

Using the power formula $P = VI$:
$P = 220 \mathrm{~V} \times 0.50 \mathrm{~A}$
$P = 110 \mathrm{~W}$

The power of the bulb is 110 W.

## Example 11.13

An electric refrigerator rated 400 W operates 8 hours/day. What is the cost of the energy to operate it for 30 days at Rs 3.00 per kW h?

### Solution

Given:
Power rating of refrigerator $P = 400 \mathrm{~W}$
Operating time per day $= 8 \text{ hours/day}$
Number of days $= 30 \text{ days}$
Cost per kWh = Rs 3.00

First, calculate the total energy consumed in 30 days in watt-hours (Wh):
Energy consumed = Power $\times$ Total time
Total time $= 8 \text{ hours/day} \times 30 \text{ days} = 240 \text{ hours}$
Energy consumed $= 400 \mathrm{~W} \times 240 \text{ hours} = 96000 \mathrm{~W h}$

Now, convert the energy consumed from watt-hours to kilowatt-hours (kWh) by dividing by 1000:
Energy consumed in kWh $= 96000 \mathrm{~W h} / 1000 = 96 \mathrm{~kW h}$

Finally, calculate the total cost:
Cost = Energy consumed in kWh $\times$ Cost per kWh
Cost = $96 \mathrm{~kW h} \times \text{Rs } 3.00 \text{/kW h}$
Cost = Rs 288.00

The cost of energy to operate the refrigerator for 30 days is Rs 288.00.

## Questions

1.  What determines the rate at which energy is delivered by a current?
2.  An electric motor takes 5 A from a 220 V line. Determine the power of the motor and the energy consumed in 2 h.

> **Answer:**
> 1.  The rate at which energy is delivered by a current is determined by **electric power (P)**. Electric power is the product of the potential difference ($V$) across the circuit and the current ($I$) flowing through it ($P = VI$). It can also be expressed as $P = I^2R$ or $P = V^2/R$.
> 2.  **Calculations for electric motor:**
>     Given: Current $I = 5 \mathrm{~A}$
>     Voltage $V = 220 \mathrm{~V}$
>     Time $t = 2 \mathrm{~h}$
>
>     **Power of the motor ($P$):**
>     $P = VI = 220 \mathrm{~V} \times 5 \mathrm{~A} = 1100 \mathrm{~W}$
>
>     **Energy consumed ($E$):**
>     Energy $E = P \times t$
>     $E = 1100 \mathrm{~W} \times 2 \mathrm{~h} = 2200 \mathrm{~W h}$
>     To express in kWh: $E = 2200 \mathrm{~W h} / 1000 = 2.2 \mathrm{~kW h}$
>     To express in Joules: $E = 2.2 \mathrm{~kW h} \times 3.6 \times 10^6 \mathrm{~J/kW h} = 7.92 \times 10^6 \mathrm{~J}$
>
>     The power of the motor is **1100 W (or 1.1 kW)**, and the energy consumed in 2 hours is **2.2 kWh (or 7.92 MJ)**.
