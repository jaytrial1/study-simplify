# MINERALS AND ENERGY RESOURCES

Haban, a boy from a remote village, visits Guwahati with his father and is amazed by the moving "house-like objects" (buses and trains). His father explains that these are made of metals like iron and aluminum, which come from minerals and require energy to move. This highlights the widespread use of metals and the energy needed to power modern life.

We use different things in our daily life made from metal. Can you list a number of items used in your house made of metals. Where do these metals come from?

> **Answer:** Items used in the house made of metals include:
> *   **Utensils:** Spoons, forks, knives, pots, pans (often made of stainless steel, aluminum, copper).
> *   **Appliances:** Refrigerators, washing machines, ovens, microwaves (contain iron, steel, copper, aluminum components).
> *   **Furniture:** Metal chairs, bed frames, shelves (iron, steel, aluminum).
> *   **Tools:** Screwdrivers, hammers, wrenches (steel, iron).
> *   **Pipes and Fittings:** Water pipes, faucets (copper, iron, brass).
> *   **Electrical Wiring:** (Copper, aluminum).
>
> These metals come from *minerals* found in the Earth's crust. They are extracted from these minerals, known as *ores*, through processes like mining and then refined.

You have studied that the Earth's crust is made up of different minerals embedded in the rocks. Various metals are extracted from these minerals after proper refinement.

Minerals are an indispensable part of our lives. Almost everything we use, from a tiny pin to a towering building or a big ship, all are made from minerals. The railway lines and the tarmac (paving) of the roads, our implements and machinery too are made from minerals. Cars, buses, trains, aeroplanes are manufactured from minerals and run on power resources derived from the Earth. Even the food that we eat contains minerals. In all stages of development, human beings have used minerals for their livelihood, decoration, festivities, religious and ceremonial rites.

## A Bright Smile from Toothpaste and Minerals

Toothpaste cleans your teeth. Abrasive minerals like *silica*, *limestone*, *aluminium oxide*, and various *phosphate minerals* do the cleaning. *Fluoride*, which is used to reduce cavities, comes from a mineral called *fluorite*. Most toothpastes are made white with *titanium oxide*, which comes from minerals called *rutile*, *ilmenite*, and *anatase*. The sparkle in some toothpastes comes from *mica*. The toothbrush and tube containing the paste are made of plastics from petroleum.

Find out where these minerals are found?

> **Answer:**
> *   **Silica:** Abundantly found in quartz, sand, and various rocks globally.
> *   **Limestone:** Found in sedimentary rock formations worldwide, often in old marine environments.
> *   **Aluminium Oxide (Corundum/Bauxite):** Bauxite is the primary ore, found in tropical and subtropical regions like India (Amarkantak plateau), Australia, Brazil.
> *   **Phosphate Minerals:** Found in sedimentary phosphate rock deposits, e.g., in North Africa, USA, China.
> *   **Fluorite:** Found in veins in various rock types, often associated with metallic ores; major deposits in China, Mexico, Mongolia.
> *   **Titanium Minerals (Rutile, Ilmenite, Anatase):** Found in igneous and metamorphic rocks, often in beach sands. Major producers include Australia, South Africa, Canada.
> *   **Mica:** Found in igneous and metamorphic rocks, particularly in pegmatites and schist. Major deposits in India (Chota Nagpur plateau), Russia, Brazil.

Dig a little deeper and find out how many minerals are used to make a light bulb?

> **Answer:** A typical incandescent light bulb (the older type) uses several minerals:
> *   **Tungsten:** For the filament, known for its high melting point.
> *   **Glass (Silica):** For the bulb casing, made primarily from silica sand.
> *   **Aluminum:** For the screw base.
> *   **Copper:** For the electrical contacts and internal wiring.
> *   **Nickel/Iron:** For support wires inside the bulb.
> *   **Argon/Nitrogen:** (Gases, not minerals, but used to fill the bulb).
>
> Modern LED bulbs would involve a different set of materials, including various rare earth minerals and semiconductors.

## All Living Things Need Minerals

Life processes cannot occur without minerals. Although our mineral intake represents only about 0.3 per cent of our total intake of nutrients, they are so potent and so important that without them we would not be able to utilise the other 99.7 per cent of foodstuffs.

Dig a little deeper and collect "Nutritional Facts" printed on food labels.

> **Answer:** When collecting "Nutritional Facts" from food labels, you will typically find information on essential minerals like:
> *   **Calcium:** Important for bones and teeth.
> *   **Iron:** Crucial for blood formation and oxygen transport.
> *   **Potassium:** Essential for nerve and muscle function.
> *   **Sodium:** Plays a role in fluid balance and nerve signals (though often consumed in excess).
> *   **Zinc:** Important for immune function and cell growth.
> *   **Magnesium:** Involved in muscle and nerve function, blood sugar control.
>
> Many other trace minerals are also present in foods, reflecting their natural occurrence in the soil where plants grow or in the diets of animals.
