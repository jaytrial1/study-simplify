# Exercises

1.  Multiple choice questions.
    (i) Which one of the following minerals is formed by decomposition of rocks, leaving a residual mass of weathered material?
    (a) coal
    (b) bauxite
    (c) gold
    (d) zinc
    > **Answer:** (b) bauxite
    (ii) Koderma, in Jharkhand is the leading producer of which one of the following minerals?
    (a) bauxite
    (b) mica
    (c) iron ore
    (d) copper
    > **Answer:** (b) mica
    (iii) Minerals are deposited and accumulated in the stratas of which of the following rocks?
    (a) sedimentary rocks
    (c) igneous rocks
    (b) metamorphic rocks
    (d) none of the above
    > **Answer:** (a) sedimentary rocks
    (iv) Which one of the following minerals is contained in the Monazite sand?
    (a) oil
    (b) uranium
    (c) thorium
    (d) coal
    > **Answer:** (c) thorium

2.  Answer the following questions in about 30 words.
    (i) Distinguish between the following in not more than 30 words.
    (a) ferrous and non-ferrous minerals
    > **Answer:** **Ferrous minerals** contain iron (e.g., iron ore, manganese) and form the base for metallurgical industries. **Non-ferrous minerals** do not contain iron (e.g., copper, bauxite) and are used in electrical, engineering, and chemical industries.
    (b) conventional and non-conventional sources of energy
    > **Answer:** **Conventional energy sources** are traditional, exhaustible fuels like coal, petroleum, natural gas, firewood, and hydel/thermal electricity. **Non-conventional energy sources** are renewable and inexhaustible, such as solar, wind, tidal, geothermal, and biogas.
    (ii) What is a mineral?
    > **Answer:** A mineral is a homogenous, naturally occurring substance with a definable internal structure. They are found in various forms in nature and are the building blocks of rocks.
    (iii) How are minerals formed in igneous and metamorphic rocks?
    > **Answer:** In igneous and metamorphic rocks, minerals occur in cracks, crevices, faults, or joints. They form when liquid/molten and gaseous minerals are forced upwards through cavities, then cool and solidify as they rise, forming veins and lodes.
    (iv) Why do we need to conserve mineral resources?
    > **Answer:** Mineral resources are finite and non-renewable, taking millions of years to form. Current consumption rates are far too high for replenishment. Conserving them ensures future availability, reduces extraction costs, and minimizes environmental damage.

3.  Answer the following questions in about 120 words.
    (i) Describe the distribution of coal in India.
    > **Answer:** Coal in India is found in two main geological ages: Gondwana and Tertiary deposits.
    >
    > **Gondwana Coal (over 200 million years old):** These are the major metallurgical coal resources and are primarily located in the Damodar Valley in West Bengal and Jharkhand. Important coalfields include Jharia, Raniganj, and Bokaro. Other significant deposits are found in the Godavari, Mahanadi, Son, and Wardha valleys. This type of coal is crucial for heavy industries and thermal power generation.
    >
    > **Tertiary Coals (about 55 million years old):** These younger coal deposits are found in the northeastern states of Meghalaya, Assam, Arunachal Pradesh, and Nagaland.
    >
    > Due to coal being a bulky material that loses weight upon use (converting to ash), heavy industries and thermal power stations are often strategically located on or near these coalfields to minimize transportation costs and maximize economic viability.

    (ii) Why do you think that solar energy has a bright future in India?
    > **Answer:** Solar energy has a bright future in India for several compelling reasons:
    >
    > 1.  **Abundant Sunlight:** India is a tropical country, receiving ample sunlight throughout the year, making it ideal for solar energy generation.
    > 2.  **Energy Security:** It reduces dependence on fossil fuels, whose prices are volatile and supplies are finite, thereby enhancing India's energy security.
    > 3.  **Environmental Benefits:** Solar energy is clean and renewable, producing no greenhouse gas emissions during operation, which helps combat climate change and reduces pollution.
    > 4.  **Decentralized Power:** Photovoltaic technology can convert sunlight directly into electricity, making it suitable for decentralized power generation in rural and remote areas, minimizing reliance on traditional fuels like firewood and dung cakes. This also supports environmental conservation and ensures manure availability for agriculture.
    > 5.  **Cost Reduction:** Advances in solar technology and increasing production have led to a significant reduction in the cost of solar panels, making it more economically viable.
    > 6.  **Government Support:** The Indian government has ambitious targets and policies to promote solar energy, including large-scale solar power plants and incentives for rooftop solar installations.
