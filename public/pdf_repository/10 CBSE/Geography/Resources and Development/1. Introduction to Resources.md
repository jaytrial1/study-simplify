This section defines what resources are, explains the interdependent relationship between humans, nature, technology, and institutions in resource creation, and introduces the fundamental ways resources are classified.

# RESOURCES AND DEVELOPMENT

Can you identify and name the various items used in making life comfortable in our villages and towns. List the items and name the material used in their making.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Vehicles (Cars, Buses):** Made from steel, aluminum, rubber, plastic, glass.
> *   **Houses/Buildings:** Made from bricks, cement, steel, wood, glass.
> *   **Electricity:** Generated using coal, natural gas, hydro (water), solar, wind, nuclear materials. Transmitted via copper/aluminum wires.
> *   **Furniture:** Made from wood, metal, plastic, fabric.
> *   **Cooking Utensils:** Made from stainless steel, aluminum, cast iron.
> *   **Roads:** Made from asphalt (bitumen), concrete, gravel.
> *   **Water Supply:** Pipes made from PVC, cast iron, copper. Water itself is a natural resource.
> *   **Communication Devices (Phones, Computers):** Made from silicon, various metals (gold, silver, copper), plastic, glass.

Everything available in our environment which can be used to satisfy our needs, provided, it is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable can be termed as 'Resource'.

The diagram emphasizes that **resources are not merely natural endowments but become "resources" through the interactive process involving human beings, the technology they develop, and the institutions they create.** Human beings act as intermediaries, transforming elements from the physical environment with the aid of technology and within the framework of institutions to fulfill their needs and drive development. It highlights the dynamic and interdependent nature of human society with its environment, its technological advancements, and its organizational structures in the context of resource creation and utilization.*

The process of transformation of things available in our environment involves an interactive relationship between nature, technology and institutions. Human beings interact with nature through technology and create institutions to accelerate their economic development.

Do you think that resources are free gifts of nature as is assumed by many? They are not. Resources are a function of human activities. Human beings themselves are essential components of resources. They transform material available in our environment into resources and use them.
> **Answer:** No, resources are not free gifts of nature. This is a common misconception. Resources are a product of human interaction with nature. Humans use technology and create institutions to transform natural materials into something usable and valuable to satisfy their needs. Without human effort, technology, and organizational structures, many natural elements would remain just that – elements in the environment, not "resources" in the economic or functional sense.

These resources can be classified in the following ways:
*   On the basis of origin: **biotic** and **abiotic**
*   On the basis of exhaustibility: **renewable** and **non-renewable**
*   On the basis of ownership: **individual**, **community**, **national** and **international**
*   On the basis of status of development: **potential**, **developed stock** and **reserves**.

### Natural Resources

Natural Resources are further categorized into:
*   **Renewable Resources:** These are resources that can be replenished over a relatively short period.
    *   **Continuous or Flow Resources:** These are resources that are available continuously. Examples given are wind and water.
    *   **Biological Resources:** These are living resources.
        *   **Natural Vegetation (Forests):** This refers to plant life.
        *   **Wildlife:** This refers to animal life.
*   **Non-Renewable Resources:** These are resources that take a very long geological time to form or are finite and cannot be replenished once consumed.
    *   **Recyclable Resources:** These are non-renewable resources that can be processed and reused. An example given is metals.
    *   **Non Recyclable Resources:** These are non-renewable resources that cannot be recycled after use. An example given is fossil fuels.

### Human Resources

Human Resources are categorized into:
*   **Structures and Institutions:** This refers to man-made constructs and organizational frameworks.
*   **Quantity and Quality:** This refers to the number of people and their attributes (e.g., skills, health, education).*
