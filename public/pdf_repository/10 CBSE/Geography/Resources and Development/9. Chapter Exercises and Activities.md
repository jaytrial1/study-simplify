2.  Answer the following questions in about 30 words.
    (i) Name three states having black soil and the crop which is mainly grown in it.
    > **Answer:** Three states having black soil are **Maharashtra**, **Madhya Pradesh**, and **Gujarat**. The crop mainly grown in it is **cotton**.
    (ii) What type of soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast? Give three main features of this type of soil.
    > **Answer:** **Alluvial soil** is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast. Three main features are: (1) It is very **fertile**, ideal for various crops. (2) It consists of various proportions of **sand, silt, and clay**. (3) It is rich in **potash, phosphoric acid, and lime**.
    (iii) What steps can be taken to control soil erosion in the hilly areas?
    > **Answer:** To control soil erosion in hilly areas, methods like **contour ploughing** (ploughing parallel to contour lines), **terrace cultivation** (cutting steps on slopes), and **strip cropping** (leaving strips of grass between crops) can be adopted. Planting **shelter belts** of trees also helps.

3.  Answer the following questions in about 120 words.
    (i) Explain land use pattern in India and why has the land under forest not increased much since 1960-61?
    > **Answer:** Land use pattern in India is determined by both physical factors (topography, climate, soil types) and human factors (population density, technological capability, culture, traditions). The major categories include forests, land not available for cultivation (barren/waste, non-agricultural uses), other uncultivated land (pastures, tree groves, culturable waste), fallow lands (current and other), and net sown area.
    >
    > The total geographical area of India is 3.28 million sq km, but reporting data is only for 93%. The land under forest has not increased significantly since 1960-61 (from 18.11% to 23.41% in 2019-20) because:
    > 1.  **Competing Demands:** Despite afforestation efforts, there's continuous pressure on forest land for agriculture, urbanization, infrastructure development (roads, railways), and industrialization due to a growing population and economy.
    > 2.  **Slow Growth:** Forests take a very long time to grow and establish, so even consistent planting efforts show only gradual increases in percentage over decades.
    > 3.  **National Forest Policy Target:** The National Forest Policy (1952) aimed for 33% forest cover, indicating that the current level is still below the ecological ideal, highlighting the challenges in achieving rapid expansion.
    > 4.  **Encroachment and Degradation:** Despite legal protections, forest areas face issues of encroachment, illegal logging, and degradation, which counteract afforestation gains.
    (ii) How have technical and economic development led to more consumption of resources?
    > **Answer:** Technical and economic development have significantly increased resource consumption in several ways:
    > 1.  **Increased Production and Consumption:** Economic growth leads to higher incomes and living standards, which in turn drive greater demand for goods and services. Producing these goods (e.g., electronics, vehicles, processed foods) requires vast amounts of raw materials and energy.
    > 2.  **Industrialization:** Technical advancements facilitate the establishment of large-scale industries. These industries are often resource-intensive, requiring minerals, fossil fuels, and water as inputs for manufacturing processes.
    > 3.  **Urbanization and Infrastructure:** Development leads to rapid urbanization, necessitating the construction of houses, roads, railways, and other infrastructure. This consumes huge quantities of resources like cement, steel, sand, and aggregates.
    > 4.  **Technological Efficiency vs. Rebound Effect:** While technology can make resource use more efficient (e.g., fuel-efficient cars), this efficiency often leads to a "rebound effect" where the lower cost of use encourages more consumption (e.g., people drive more). New technologies also often require new and sometimes rare resources.
    > 5.  **Globalization and Trade:** Economic development enables countries to access resources from across the globe through international trade, facilitating the consumption of resources that might be scarce domestically.
    > 6.  **Mechanization of Agriculture:** Modern agriculture uses machinery, chemical fertilizers, and pesticides, all of which are products of industrial processes that consume significant energy and raw materials.

## Project/Activity

1.  Make a project showing consumption and conservation of resources in your locality.
    > **Answer:** (This is a project prompt, so I'll provide a framework for students)
    > **Project Title:** Resource Footprint and Conservation Efforts in [Your Locality Name]
    > **Objective:** To understand how resources are consumed and conserved in our immediate surroundings and identify areas for improvement.
    > **Methodology:**
    > 1.  **Resource Mapping:** Identify key resources used daily (water, electricity, food, fuel, paper, plastic) and their sources.
    > 2.  **Consumption Audit:**
    >     *   **Household Level:** Survey 5-10 households on their consumption habits (e.g., water usage for bathing/cleaning, electricity for appliances, waste generation).
    >     *   **Community Level:** Observe public resource use (streetlights, public taps, waste bins).
    > 3.  **Waste Analysis:** Categorize common waste types generated (organic, recyclable, non-recyclable).
    > 4.  **Conservation Measures:** Identify existing conservation practices (e.g., rainwater harvesting, waste segregation, energy-efficient appliances, public awareness campaigns).
    > 5.  **Interviews:** Talk to local residents, shopkeepers, or community leaders about their awareness and participation in conservation.
    > **Output:**
    > *   **Charts/Graphs:** Visualize consumption data (e.g., average water usage per household, percentage of waste recycled).
    > *   **Photographs:** Document resource use (e.g., overflowing bins, efficient lighting) and conservation efforts (e.g., composting pits, solar panels).
    > *   **Case Studies:** Highlight successful local conservation initiatives or challenges.
    > *   **Recommendations:** Propose practical steps for improving resource conservation in the locality (e.g., promoting responsible water use, advocating for more recycling bins, organizing awareness drives).
    > *   **Presentation/Report:** Compile findings into a report or a visual presentation.

2.  Have a discussion in the class- how to conserve various resources used in your school.
    > **Answer:** (This is a discussion prompt, so I'll provide discussion points)
    > **Discussion Points for Resource Conservation in School:**
    > *   **Electricity:**
    >     *   Switch off lights and fans when leaving classrooms.
    >     *   Use natural light as much as possible.
    >     *   Unplug chargers and electronic devices when not in use.
    >     *   Consider energy-efficient LED lighting.
    > *   **Water:**
    >     *   Turn off taps completely after use.
    >     *   Report leaky taps or pipes immediately.
    >     *   Use water judiciously in washrooms and labs.
    >     *   Implement rainwater harvesting for gardening or cleaning.
    > *   **Paper:**
    >     *   Use both sides of paper for notes and assignments.
    >     *   Recycle old notebooks and waste paper.
    >     *   Print only when necessary; use digital documents.
    >     *   Reuse rough sheets.
    > *   **Food:**
    >     *   Avoid food wastage in the canteen. Take only what you can eat.
    >     *   Compost food scraps for school gardens.
    > *   **Other Resources (e.g., lab chemicals, cleaning supplies):**
    >     *   Use chemicals efficiently and dispose of them responsibly.
    >     *   Choose eco-friendly cleaning products.
    > *   **General Practices:**
    >     *   Form a "Green Club" or "Eco-Warriors" team to monitor and promote conservation.
    >     *   Organize awareness campaigns, posters, and workshops.
    >     *   Conduct regular "resource audits" to track consumption and identify areas for improvement.

3.  Imagine if oil supplies get exhausted, how will this affect our life style?
    > **Answer:** (This question is a repeat of an earlier activity, so the answer will be similar.)
    > If oil supplies were to be exhausted, our lifestyle would undergo a radical and immediate transformation:
    > *   **Transportation Collapse:** Most modern transport (cars, buses, trucks, trains, airplanes, ships) would cease to function, bringing global trade, travel, and supply chains to a standstill. Food, goods, and people would be unable to move.
    > *   **Energy Crisis:** Power generation in many regions relies on oil. Widespread blackouts and energy shortages would occur, impacting homes, industries, and essential services like hospitals.
    > *   **Agricultural Disruption:** Farming machinery, fertilizers (derived from fossil fuels), and irrigation systems are heavily dependent on oil. Food production would drastically decline, leading to severe food shortages and potential famine.
    > *   **Manufacturing Halt:** Oil is a primary raw material for plastics, synthetic fibers, pharmaceuticals, and countless other everyday products. Factories would shut down, leading to massive shortages of essential goods.
    > *   **Economic Devastation:** The global economy, built on oil, would face an unprecedented collapse, resulting in mass unemployment, poverty, and widespread social unrest.
    > *   **Shift to Localized Living:** People would be forced to rely on local resources, manual labor, and non-motorized transport. There would be an urgent, but difficult, transition to renewable energy sources and electric alternatives, but the infrastructure for such a shift is not yet universally robust enough to handle the immediate vacuum left by oil.
