This section distinguishes between intensive subsistence farming, characterized by high population pressure and labor-intensive methods, and commercial farming, which focuses on higher productivity through modern inputs and includes plantation agriculture.

# Intensive Subsistence and Commercial Farming

## Intensive Subsistence Farming

This type of farming is practiced in areas with high population pressure on land. It is a **labour-intensive** farming system where high doses of **biochemical inputs** and **irrigation** are used to obtain higher production.

## Can you name some of the states of India where such farming is practised?
> **Answer:** Intensive subsistence farming is widely practiced in densely populated regions of India, including states like **Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, West Bengal, parts of Haryana, Punjab, and the coastal regions of Odisha, Andhra Pradesh, and Tamil Nadu.**

Despite the 'right of inheritance' leading to the division of land among successive generations, which has rendered land-holding sizes uneconomical, farmers continue to strive for maximum output from their limited land due to the absence of alternative sources of livelihood. This creates enormous pressure on agricultural land.

## Commercial Farming

The main characteristic of **commercial farming** is the use of higher doses of modern inputs, such as:
*   **High Yielding Variety (HYV) seeds**
*   **Chemical fertilizers**
*   **Insecticides**
*   **Pesticides**
These inputs are used to obtain higher productivity and yield. The degree of commercialization in agriculture varies from one region to another. For example, **rice** is a commercial crop in Haryana and Punjab, but in Odisha, it is primarily a **subsistence crop**.

## Can you give some more examples of crops which may be commercial in one region and may provide subsistence in another region?
> **Answer:**
> *   **Wheat:** Commercial in Punjab and Haryana, but can be subsistence in parts of Madhya Pradesh or Uttar Pradesh for small farmers.
> *   **Maize:** Commercial for fodder or industrial use in some regions, but subsistence for direct consumption in tribal or less developed areas.
> *   **Millets (Jowar, Bajra, Ragi):** Commercial in states like Rajasthan (Bajra) or Maharashtra (Jowar) for market sale, but subsistence for local consumption in many dryland farming areas.
> *   **Pulses:** Commercial for large-scale production and market, but subsistence for household protein needs for many farmers.

### Plantation Agriculture

**Plantation** is also a type of commercial farming. In this system, a single crop is grown on a large area. Plantation agriculture represents an interface between agriculture and industry. Plantations cover large tracts of land, using **capital-intensive inputs** with the help of **migrant laborers**. All the produce from plantations is used as raw material in respective industries.

In India, important plantation crops include:
*   **Tea**
*   **Coffee**
*   **Rubber**
*   **Sugarcane**
*   **Banana**

Examples of major plantation regions include **tea** in Assam and North Bengal, and **coffee** in Karnataka. Since the production is mainly for the market, a well-developed network of transport and communication connecting the plantation areas, processing industries, and markets plays a crucial role in the development of plantations.
