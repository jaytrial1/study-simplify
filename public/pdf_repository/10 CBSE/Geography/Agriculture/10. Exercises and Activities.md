# Exercises and Activities

## 1. Multiple choice questions.

(i) Which one of the following describes a system of agriculture where a single crop is grown on a large area?
(a) Shifting Agriculture
(b) Plantation Agriculture
(c) Horticulture
(d) Intensive Agriculture
> **Answer:** (b) Plantation Agriculture

(ii) Which one of the following is a rabi crop?
(a) Rice
(b) Gram
(c) Millets
(d) Cotton
> **Answer:** (b) Gram

(iii) Which one of the following is a leguminous crop?
(a) Pulses
(b) Jawar
(c) Millets
(d) Sesamum
> **Answer:** (a) Pulses

## 2. Answer the following questions in 30 words.

(i) Name one important beverage crop and specify the geographical conditions required for its growth.
> **Answer:** **Tea** is an important beverage crop. It requires a warm and moist, frost-free climate all year, deep and fertile well-drained soil rich in humus, and frequent, well-distributed showers.

(ii) Name one staple crop of India and the regions where it is produced.
> **Answer:** **Rice** is a staple food crop of India. It is primarily produced in the plains of north and north-eastern India, coastal areas, deltaic regions, and increasingly in Punjab and Haryana due to irrigation.

(iii) Enlist the various institutional reform programmes introduced by the government in the interest of farmers.
> **Answer:** Institutional reform programs include collectivization, consolidation of holdings, cooperation, abolition of zamindari, land reforms, crop insurance, establishment of Grameen banks/cooperative societies for loans, Kisan Credit Card (KCC), and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS).

## 3. Answer the following questions in about 120 words.

(i) Suggest the initiative taken by the government to ensure the increase in agricultural production.
> **Answer:** To increase agricultural production, the Indian government has undertaken several initiatives. Post-independence, land reforms like the abolition of zamindari and consolidation of holdings were prioritized. In the 1960s and 70s, the **Green Revolution** (using HYV seeds, fertilizers, irrigation) and the **White Revolution** (Operation Flood for dairy) were launched. Later, a comprehensive land development program in the 1980s and 90s included crop insurance against natural calamities, establishment of Grameen banks and cooperatives for low-interest loans, and schemes like Kisan Credit Card (KCC) and Personal Accident Insurance Scheme (PAIS). Additionally, weather bulletins, agricultural programs on media, and minimum support prices (MSP) were introduced to support farmers and ensure fair returns.

(ii) Describe the geographical conditions required for the growth of rice.
> **Answer:** Rice is a major Kharif crop requiring specific geographical conditions. It thrives in areas with **high temperatures**, typically above 25°C, and **high humidity**. Abundant **annual rainfall** exceeding 100 cm is crucial for its growth, making it suitable for monsoon-fed regions. In areas with less rainfall, effective **irrigation** systems, such as canals and tubewells, are essential for successful cultivation. Rice grows best in **alluvial soils** found in plains, coastal areas, and deltaic regions. The combination of high temperatures, ample water, and fertile soil makes it a dominant crop in the Indo-Gangetic plains, northeastern states, and coastal belts of India.
