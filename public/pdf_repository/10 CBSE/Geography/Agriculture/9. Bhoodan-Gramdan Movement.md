This section narrates the significant Bhoodan-Gramdan movement, led by Vinoba Bhave, which aimed at voluntary land redistribution, and highlights its impact as a 'Blood-less Revolution' in addressing landlessness.

# Bhoodan-Gramdan Movement

Mahatma Gandhi declared Vinoba Bhave as his spiritual heir. Vinoba Bhave participated in Satyagraha as one of the foremost satyagrahis and was a strong proponent of Gandhi's concept of *gram swarajya* (village self-rule). After Gandhiji's martyrdom, Vinoba Bhave undertook a *padyatra* (foot march) to spread Gandhiji's message, covering almost the entire country.

During one such lecture at Pochampalli in Andhra Pradesh, some poor landless villagers demanded land for their economic well-being. Vinoba Bhave could not promise it immediately but assured them he would talk to the Government of India regarding provision of land if they undertook cooperative farming.

Suddenly, Shri Ram Chandra Reddy stood up and offered 80 acres of land to be distributed among 80 landless villagers. This act was known as '**Bhoodan**' (land gift). Later, Vinoba Bhave traveled widely across India, introducing his ideas. Some zamindars (landowners), owners of many villages, offered to distribute some villages among the landless. This was known as '**Gramdan**' (village gift).

However, many landowners chose to provide some part of their land to the poor farmers primarily due to the fear of the **land ceiling act** (laws limiting the amount of land an individual or family could own). This Bhoodan-Gramdan movement, initiated by Vinoba Bhave, is also known as the **Blood-less Revolution**, as it achieved significant land redistribution without violence.
