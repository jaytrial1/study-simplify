# Major Food Crops: Grains

A variety of food and non-food crops are grown in different parts of the country, depending upon variations in soil, climate, and cultivation practices. Major crops grown in India include rice, wheat, millets, pulses, tea, coffee, sugarcane, oil seeds, cotton, and jute.

## Rice

*   **Significance:** Staple food crop for a majority of people in India.
*   **Global Rank:** Second largest producer of rice in the world after China.
*   **Crop Type:** Kharif crop.
*   **Geographical Conditions:**
    *   **Temperature:** High, above 25°C.
    *   **Humidity:** High.
    *   **Rainfall:** Annual rainfall above 100 cm.
    *   **Irrigation:** Required in areas of less rainfall.
*   **Producing Regions:**
    *   Plains of north and north-eastern India.
    *   Coastal areas and deltaic regions.
    *   Areas of less rainfall (e.g., Punjab, Haryana, western Uttar Pradesh, and parts of Rajasthan) due to the development of dense networks of canal irrigation and tubewells.

## Wheat

*   **Significance:** Second most important cereal crop; main food crop in the north and north-western parts of the country.
*   **Crop Type:** Rabi crop.
*   **Geographical Conditions:**
    *   **Growing Season:** Cool.
    *   **Ripening Season:** Bright sunshine.
    *   **Rainfall:** 50 to 75 cm of annual rainfall, evenly distributed over the growing season.
*   **Important Wheat-Growing Zones:**
    *   The Ganga-Satluj plains in the north-west.
    *   Black soil region of the Deccan.
*   **Major Producing States:** Punjab, Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Bihar, and Rajasthan.

## Millets

*   **Important Millets in India:** Jowar, bajra, and ragi.
*   **Nutritional Value:** Known as *coarse grains*, but have very high nutritional value. For example, **ragi** is very rich in iron, calcium, other micronutrients, and roughage.

### Jowar
*   **Significance:** Third most important food crop with respect to area and production.
*   **Nature:** Rain-fed crop, mostly grown in moist areas, hardly needs irrigation.
*   **Major Producing States:** Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, and Madhya Pradesh.

### Bajra
*   **Soil Preference:** Grows well on sandy soils and shallow black soil.
*   **Major Producing States:** Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Haryana.

### Ragi
*   **Nature:** Crop of dry regions.
*   **Soil Preference:** Grows well on red, black, sandy, loamy, and shallow black soils.
*   **Major Producing States:** Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, Jharkhand, and Arunachal Pradesh.

## Maize

*   **Usage:** Used both as food and fodder.
*   **Crop Type:** Kharif crop (in some states like Bihar, it is also grown in the rabi season).
*   **Geographical Conditions:**
    *   **Temperature:** Between 21°C to 27°C.
    *   **Soil Preference:** Grows well in old alluvial soil.
*   **Contributing Factors:** Use of modern inputs such as HYV seeds, fertilizers, and irrigation have contributed to increasing production.
*   **Major Producing States:** Karnataka, Madhya Pradesh, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Andhra Pradesh, and Telangana.

## Pulses

*   **Global Rank:** India is the largest producer as well as the consumer of pulses in the world.
*   **Nutritional Value:** Major source of protein in a vegetarian diet.
*   **Major Pulses Grown in India:** Tur (arhar), urad, moong, masur, peas, and gram.
*   **Growing Conditions:** Need less moisture and can survive even in dry conditions.
*   **Soil Fertility:** Being leguminous crops (except arhar), all these crops help in restoring soil fertility by fixing nitrogen from the air. Therefore, they are mostly grown in rotation with other crops.
*   **Major Producing States:** Madhya Pradesh, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, Uttar Pradesh, and Karnataka.
