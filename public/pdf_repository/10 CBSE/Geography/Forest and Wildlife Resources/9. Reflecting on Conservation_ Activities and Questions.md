    (i) Which of the following conservation strategies do not directly involve community participation?
    (a) Joint forest management
    (c) Chipko Movement
    (b) Beej Bachao Andolan
    (d) Demarcation of Wildlife sanctuaries

    > **Answer:** (d) Demarcation of Wildlife sanctuaries
    > **Explanation:** Joint forest management, Chipko Movement, and Beej Bachao Andolan are all examples explicitly mentioned as involving community participation. Demarcation of wildlife sanctuaries is primarily a government administrative action, though local communities might be affected or consulted, it's not a direct *participatory strategy* in the same sense as the others.

3.  Answer the following questions in about 30 words.
    (i) What is biodiversity? Why is biodiversity important for human lives?
    > **Answer:** Biodiversity, or biological diversity, refers to the immense variety of wildlife and cultivated species, diverse in form and function, yet closely integrated through complex interdependencies. It is crucial for human lives because it sustains our ecological systems, providing essential services like clean air, water, and fertile soil for food production, on which our very existence depends.

    (ii) How have human activities affected the depletion of flora and fauna? Explain.
    > **Answer:** Human activities have significantly depleted flora and fauna primarily through habitat destruction (e.g., shrinking tiger habitats), poaching and illegal trade (e.g., tiger skins, bones), pollution (e.g., toxic elements in Yamuna leading to bird deaths), and overexploitation of resources. These actions disrupt ecological balance and lead to species endangerment and extinction.

4.  Answer the following questions in about 120 words.
    (i) Describe how communities have conserved and protected forests and wildlife in India?
    > **Answer:** Communities in India have played a vital role in conserving forests and wildlife through various traditional and modern approaches. Recognizing their long-term livelihood depends on these resources, local people have actively protected habitats. For instance, villagers in Sariska Tiger Reserve fought against mining, while five villages in Alwar district declared 1,200 hectares as 'Bhairodev Dakav Sonchuri' with their own protection rules banning hunting. The famous Chipko Movement successfully resisted deforestation through community afforestation. Groups like Beej Bachao Andolan promote diversified crop production without chemicals. Additionally, the Joint Forest Management (JFM) program involves local communities in managing and restoring degraded forests, providing them with benefits like non-timber forest produce, demonstrating that local involvement is key to sustainable resource management.

    (ii) Write a note on good practices towards conserving forest and wildlife.
    > **Answer:** Good practices for conserving forest and wildlife involve a multi-faceted approach, integrating government policies, community participation, and individual responsibility. Implementing and enforcing strong legal protections like the Wildlife Protection Act, establishing national parks and wildlife sanctuaries, and launching species-specific projects (e.g., Project Tiger) are crucial. Equally important is fostering community involvement through initiatives like Joint Forest Management (JFM), which empowers local people to protect and restore forests while benefiting from their resources. Promoting traditional conservation methods, such as respecting sacred groves and indigenous species, and adopting sustainable agricultural practices like those advocated by Beej Bachao Andolan, also contribute significantly. Ultimately, conservation requires a shift towards people-centric, environment-friendly, and economically rewarding developmental activities, ensuring that human needs are met without compromising ecological integrity.
