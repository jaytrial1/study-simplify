This section critically examines the negative consequences and growing opposition to large multi-purpose river projects. It highlights their environmental impacts, social displacements, and unintended hydrological issues, including the paradoxical effect of triggering floods.

# Challenges and Criticisms of Multi-purpose Projects

In recent years, multi-purpose projects and large dams have come under great scrutiny and opposition for a variety of reasons.

## Environmental and Social Impacts

*   **Altered River Flow:** Regulating and damming rivers affect their natural flow, causing poor sediment flow and excessive sedimentation at the bottom of the reservoir. This results in rockier stream beds and poorer habitats for the rivers' aquatic life.
*   **River Fragmentation:** Dams fragment rivers, making it difficult for aquatic fauna to migrate, especially for spawning.
*   **Submergence:** The reservoirs created on the floodplains submerge existing vegetation and soil, leading to its decomposition over time.
*   **Induced Floods:** Ironically, dams constructed to control floods have sometimes triggered floods due to sedimentation in the reservoir. Big dams have mostly been unsuccessful in controlling floods during excessive rainfall.

*   **Other Problems:** Multi-purpose projects have also been observed to:
    *   Induce earthquakes.
    *   Cause water-borne diseases and pests.
    *   Lead to pollution resulting from excessive use of water.
*   **Agricultural Changes:** Irrigation has changed the cropping pattern of many regions, with farmers shifting to water-intensive and commercial crops. This has great ecological consequences, such as the *salinization of the soil*.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Sardar Sarovar Dam:** Built over the Narmada River in Gujarat, this is one of India's largest water resource projects, benefiting four states: Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, and Rajasthan. It aims to meet water requirements in drought-prone and desert areas, providing irrigation facilities to vast tracts of land, including drought-prone regions of Gujarat and Rajasthan, and tribal areas of Maharashtra.
*   **Pradhan Mantri Krishi Sinchayee Yojana (PMKSY):** This program aims to ensure access to protective irrigation for all agricultural farms in the country, fostering rural prosperity. Its broad objectives include:
    *   Enhancing physical access of water on the farm and expanding cultivable area under assured irrigation (*har khet ko pani* - water to every field).
    *   Improving on-farm water use efficiency to reduce wastage and increase availability (*per drop more crop*).
    *   Introducing sustainable water conservation practices.
*   **Inter-state Water Disputes:** Large dams and multi-purpose projects often lead to disputes between states over water sharing. For instance, the *Krishna-Godavari dispute* involves objections raised by Karnataka and Andhra Pradesh governments regarding Maharashtra's diversion of more water at Koyna for a multi-purpose project. This diversion reduces downstream flow, adversely affecting agriculture and industry in the lower riparian states.

## Activity: Floods and Water Disputes

1.  Collect information about floods that occurred in different parts of the country due to heavy rainfall in recent times.

    > **Answer:**
    > **Recent Flood Events in India (Illustrative Examples - as of early 2020s context):**
    >
    > *   **Assam Floods (Annual):** Assam frequently experiences severe floods, particularly during the monsoon season (June-September), due to the Brahmaputra River overflowing its banks. These floods regularly displace millions, cause extensive damage to crops, homes, and infrastructure, and lead to significant loss of life and livestock. Sedimentation and changing river courses exacerbate the problem.
    > *   **Kerala Floods (2018, 2019):** Kerala witnessed devastating floods in 2018, followed by significant floods in 2019. These events were largely attributed to unusually heavy monsoon rainfall combined with dam management issues (e.g., simultaneous opening of dam gates). The floods caused widespread destruction, landslides, and a humanitarian crisis, highlighting the vulnerability of the Western Ghats region to extreme weather events.
    > *   **Mumbai Floods (Annual):** Mumbai, being a coastal city with inadequate drainage infrastructure, experiences severe waterlogging and flash floods during heavy monsoon downpours each year. This disrupts daily life, transport, and economic activities, often leading to loss of life.
    > *   **Bihar Floods (Annual):** Similar to Assam, parts of Bihar, especially northern districts, face annual floods due to rivers originating in Nepal (like Kosi, Gandak, Bagmati) overflowing. These floods cause immense suffering, destroy agriculture, and displace large populations.
    > *   **Uttarakhand Flash Floods (2013, 2021):** The Himalayan state of Uttarakhand is prone to flash floods and landslides, often triggered by cloudbursts or glacial lake outburst floods. The 2013 Kedarnath tragedy and the 2021 Chamoli disaster, though different in origin, demonstrated the destructive power of water in mountainous terrains, affecting infrastructure, livelihoods, and causing significant casualties.
    >
    > These examples illustrate the recurring nature and varied impacts of floods across India, often linked to both natural rainfall patterns and anthropogenic factors like inadequate infrastructure or climate change.

2.  Make a list of inter-state water disputes.

    > **Answer:**
    > **Major Inter-State Water Disputes in India:**
    >
    > India has several long-standing inter-state water disputes, primarily over the sharing of river waters for irrigation, power generation, and drinking purposes. Some of the most prominent ones include:
    >
    > 1.  **Cauvery Water Dispute:** Between Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Puducherry. This is one of the oldest and most contentious disputes, revolving around the sharing of the Cauvery River waters.
    > 2.  **Krishna Water Dispute:** Between Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh (now Andhra Pradesh and Telangana). The dispute centers on the sharing of the Krishna River waters and the construction of dams by upstream states.
    > 3.  **Godavari Water Dispute:** Between Maharashtra, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh (now Andhra Pradesh and Telangana), Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha. This dispute involves the sharing of the Godavari River waters and its tributaries.
    > 4.  **Mahanadi Water Dispute:** Between Odisha and Chhattisgarh. This dispute concerns the sharing of the Mahanadi River waters and the construction of barrages and dams by Chhattisgarh.
    > 5.  **Narmada Water Dispute:** Between Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, and Rajasthan. This dispute mainly revolves around the height of the Sardar Sarovar Dam and the allocation of water from the Narmada River.
    > 6.  **Yamuna Water Dispute:** Between Haryana, Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi, and Uttarakhand. This dispute is about the allocation of Yamuna River waters, especially for drinking water supply to Delhi.
    > 7.  **Ravi-Beas Water Dispute:** Between Punjab, Haryana, and Rajasthan. This dispute concerns the sharing of surplus waters of the Ravi and Beas rivers.
    >
    > These disputes highlight the complex challenges of managing a vital shared resource among competing demands from different states.
