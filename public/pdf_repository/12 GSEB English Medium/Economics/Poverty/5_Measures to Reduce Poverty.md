5_Measures to Reduce Poverty

India has taken the following steps to reduce poverty during the planning period.

# To Increase Agricultural Productivity

In India, one of the basic reasons for poverty is low productivity in agriculture. With an increase in agricultural productivity and income for agricultural laborers, poverty can be reduced. Regarding this, through public programs, farmers are informed about upgraded technology, available resources at reasonable rates, improved infrastructural facilities, maximum prices, and regulated markets for their produce. Development of agriculture will increase productivity per laborer. Also, employment opportunities and prices of food grains will decrease. This will be more advantageous to the poor, and poverty can be reduced.

# Development of Small Scale Industries

In India, small and cottage industries have made immense contributions to the Gross National Income and employment. So, if small and cottage industries can be developed and encouraged, then poverty can be reduced on a larger scale.

# Development of Unorganized Sector

The unorganized sector includes vegetable vendors, masons in the construction sector, agricultural laborers, vendors, etc. To improve the conditions of these workers, the National Commission has recommended deciding the working conditions, Life Insurance, health, old age pension, and other social security measures. Moreover, for small and marginal farmers, it is recommended to provide them with irrigation facilities and loan facilities.

# Use of Appropriate Tax Policy

The government uses tax policy to reduce inequality of income and poverty by redistributing income. To fulfill this objective, the government makes such a policy wherein more tax is imposed on the rich class, which can afford that tax, and less tax is imposed on the poor class, with tax concessions given. This way, the government collects a fund by imposing taxes on rich people and makes expenses on welfare-oriented programs for the poor people. As a result, the socio-economic conditions of the poor improve, and a reduction in income inequality and poverty is seen.

# Rise in Human Capital Investment

Developed nations, where large-scale investment is made on education and skill development, have a very low level of unemployment. As a result, poverty is less. A high level of education satisfies the requirements of various employment opportunities and choices among workers. Skill development increases the productivity of workers, and that reflects in high wages. For that, at the workplace, continuous investment is required for training and research.

# Goods and Services at Reasonable Rates

To provide nutritive food and food security to poor people, it should be made available at reasonable rates, which will also directly reduce poverty. Under the public distribution system in India, we have ration shops where rural and urban poor are given basic utility goods at reasonable prices. During drought and scarcity, the responsibility of the public distribution system is more to satisfy the basic needs of the poor.
