3_Origin and Development of the Concept of Money

# Evolution of Money

In order to simplify the exchange process and introduce a universally acceptable medium that satisfies the store of value function under the barter system, commodities and animals were used as a medium of exchange in India.

## Agriculture's Role

Agriculture, being the major sector of the economy, resulted in a large production of crops. The required stock was stored, and the rest of the crops were sold to buy animals. When required, the animals could also be sold to get goods, services, or even crops in return. In this way, animals like horses, cows, and buffaloes became the means of exchange values for money and trade.

## Limitations of Animals as Currency

The use of animals as a standard measure and a common medium of trade also presented limitations and caused difficulties. Animals are mortal; they get sick and die. Therefore, storing wealth (in terms of value for money) in the form of animals was not advisable. Thus, valuable minerals and stones were used instead of animals.

## Introduction of Coins

At the onset of imperialism, the use of coins started as a means of trade and exchange of goods and services within the kingdom. The only limitation was that they were used in limited societies and regions.

## Modern Money

Democracy and industrialization inspired the need for modern money as a means of exchange. With the support of the central government, money became widely accepted as a means of exchanging goods and services. Money also succeeded in overcoming the limitation of storage of value. The advent of the banking system made it easier to transfer and store money.
