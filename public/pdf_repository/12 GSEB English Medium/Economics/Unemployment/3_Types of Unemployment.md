3_Types of Unemployment

# Open Unemployment
Meaning: Those individuals who are ready to work at the current wage rate and possess qualifications too, but do not get any job are said to be 'fully unemployed or openly unemployed'.

Commonly, in countries where labour supply increases fast and the process of urbanization is also fast, a high rate of full unemployment can be seen. This type of unemployment is seen more in cities than in villages. Openly unemployed people are those who migrate from villages to cities in search of jobs. People suffering from open unemployment are educated or less trained/skilled persons.

People with open unemployment cannot work and cannot get work, but such people consume goods and spend. Hence, they become a burden and a reason for low productivity. Open unemployment can be seen more among the age group of 15 to 25 years.

To get a proper number of openly unemployed people is very difficult, but there are three methods:
(1) Through Registration in Employment Exchange Centers (2) Through Sample Survey for Labour Supply (3) Through Census

# Underemployment
Meaning: When laborers cannot utilize their capabilities fully and for a certain period accept a less capable job, this is said to be underemployment.

When a laborer is willing and ready to work for a certain number of hours or days but gets a job for fewer hours or days, then he is said to be underemployed. For example, a laborer working in industry or on agricultural land gets work only for 5 hours instead of 8 hours, then he is said to be underemployed. According to this meaning, in rural India, seasonal unemployment in the agricultural sector is a type of underemployment because the laborer in the agriculture sector gets work only during the sowing and harvesting seasons and is unemployed the rest of the time. Indian agriculture is based on the monsoon, and due to limited irrigational facilities, the agricultural sector faces a seasonal nature of unemployment.

Similarly, many individuals do not get jobs according to their degrees; they accept degraded jobs, which is also called underemployment. E.g., a person with a degree in computer engineering works in a garage.

# Disguised Unemployment
Disguised unemployment means hidden unemployment. This type of unemployment is very common in developing economies like India.

## Meaning:
In any activity, if too many persons are employed at a given level of technology, and when a few persons are removed from work, the total production does not change, then there exists disguised unemployment.

It could also be defined as: Given the resources and technique of production, in overpopulated developing economies, the agriculture sector has zero marginal productivity; then such countries are said to have disguised unemployment.

In relation to the above definition, it can be said that disguised unemployment has zero marginal productivity.

In India, the population is constantly increasing. Hence, the number of people demanding employment is also increasing at a higher rate. But in India, poor development of sectors other than agriculture puts a heavy burden on the agriculture sector for employment. If the surplus laborers are removed from the agriculture sector, then agricultural production will not decrease. As the marginal productivity of these laborers is zero, they are said to be disguisedly unemployed. In cities also, in industries and businesses, this kind of disguised unemployment is seen. Commonly, in businesses where labor is borne by the families themselves and wages are not paid in monetary terms, in such a case, disguised unemployment takes place.
E.g., if 10 hectares of land are to be optimally used, then a maximum of 5 laborers can be employed. But due to the unavailability of work anywhere else, the other 3 members of the family also join the same work. But even after they join, the total production does not increase at all, then the other three laborers are called disguisedly unemployed. These laborers visibly do not seem to be unemployed, but because the marginal productivity is zero, there is disguised unemployment.

# Cyclical Unemployment
In a capitalist type of economy, investors and the saving class are distinct classes, and so that creates disequilibrium. As a result, sometimes the economy faces prosperity and sometimes depression. During prosperity, there is high investment, production, income, and employment in the economy. When the economy faces depression, then there is a reduction in the demand for goods and services. Due to a reduction in effective demand, industries have to reduce production or shut down production, and many laborers are retrenched from work. So here, depression becomes the reason for unemployment.

So this type of unemployment is called cyclical or depressive unemployment or trade cycle unemployment.

America experienced a severe depression during 1929-30, and its effect was felt by many countries of the world. That is why this depression was known as the Great Depression. Presently also, sometimes developed countries like America and England face this type of unemployment.

In India, due to the capitalist market mechanism, cyclical unemployment is seen every now and then. The Indian diamond industry faces this type of unemployment.

To solve the problem of cyclical unemployment, the state should invest in productive and developmental activities and provide employment to the maximum number of people and try to increase their income levels. With an increase in income, effective demand will increase, and production will increase. With an increase in production, employment will increase. As a result, the problem of cyclical unemployment will reduce.

# Frictional Unemployment
Meaning: When in the production process, because of changes in demand or production or due to changes in taste and preferences or new technology, new goods enter the market and unemployment arises, then this unemployment is called frictional unemployment.

In a developed nation, when the old production system is replaced by a new production system, then the units with the old production system face economic loss and shut down. As a result, the laborers working in those units remain unemployed until they learn the work according to the new technology. This unemployment is for a short term.
E.g., when smartphones replaced old mobile phones, then the laborers engaged in the production, sales, and service of old mobile phones became unemployed. This is frictional unemployment.
