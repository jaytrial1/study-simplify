4_Causes of High Birth-rate

Causes of high birth-rate in India can be classified under three heads: Social factors, Economic factors, and other factors.

# Social Factors

## (1) Universality of Marriage

In India, marriage is a religious ritual. The society looks unfavorably upon unmarried individuals. To avoid this, both men and women enter into the institution of marriage. Even disabled people are not exceptions. Compared to advanced countries, most women in India marry. This universality of marriage leads to a high birth-rate.

## (2) Early Marriage and Widow Remarriage

Child marriage is prevalent in many parts of the country despite laws banning it. As individuals get married at an early age, their fertility span is very long. This results in the birth of more children. The widow remarriage act in India, which has been supported by many, has made widow remarriage common. Therefore, it has resulted in a high birth-rate.

## (3) Preference for a Male Child

Indian society is male-dominated, and more importance is given to sons rather than daughters for the following three reasons:

### (1) Religious Beliefs

It is believed that there is a hell named 'poo,' and a son's birth can prevent parents from reaching this hell.

### (2) Procreation

Sons are desired for procreation.

### (3) Financial Support

Sons are expected to provide financial support during old age.

Due to these reasons, families give birth to more children expecting a son, and in the process, their family size becomes large.

## (4) Joint Family System

The joint family system is prevalent in the rural areas of India. As a result, the financial responsibility of the upbringing of a child is distributed among all the family members. Hence, a child does not become a burden, leading to a high birth-rate.

# Economic Factors

## (1) Low Level of Education

Education and population growth have a complex relationship. This is especially true regarding female education. Inadequate education makes it difficult to understand the need for small families, and as a result, the family size tends to become large. Education and the number of children in a family have an inverse relationship all over the world. It has been found that compared to illiterate women, women who have had primary education give birth to fewer children. This proves true for middle school-educated and secondary school-educated women as against primary school-educated women. From this experience, it can be said that the birth-rate is high because of illiteracy and low education levels.

## (2) Low Level Income

When the income level of a family is low, the birth of a child is considered to be an asset rather than a burden. It is commonly said, "the more, the merrier." It is expected that the child will also contribute to the income of the family in the future. Even today, we see children working in small eateries or tea stalls.

## (3) High Infant Mortality Rate

"Out of every 1000 children born in a given year, the number of child deaths before one year of age is known as the infant mortality rate."

The extent of the infant mortality rate is quite high in India compared to various other developed countries. In India, the infant mortality rate in 1951 was 146, which fell to 41.40 in 2011, but still, this rate is considered to be quite high. The main reasons for this high infant mortality rate are poverty, less care given to the girl child, a lack of nutritious food, frequent abortions among women, age-old practices in the upbringing of a child, inadequate medical facilities, and a small gap between two children, among others, which leads to a high tendency of birth-rate.

# Other Factors

## (1) High Fertility Rate

In a given year, the number of live children born per 1000 females in the age group of 15-49 years is known as the fertility rate. In the Indian population structure, high fertility is a characteristic feature. Let us analyze this feature with reference to women in the age group of 15-49 years.

In 1961, women in this age group gave birth to an average of 6 children, which fell to 3 in 2011. Still, this is considered to be high, and two reasons can be attributed to this high rate:

### (1) Early Marriage

Early marriage leads to a longer fertility period for women.

### (2) Low Proportion of Unmarried Women

The proportion of unmarried women in the total number of women in the fertile age group is very low.

## (2) Lack of Family Planning Information

Family planning refers to decisions on the size of the family and maintaining a gap between two children based on proper understanding, i.e., planned parenthood. In India, poverty, social customs, and religious beliefs, combined with a low level of education, have acted as obstacles to family planning. Moreover, a lack of knowledge regarding the instruments of contraception and sometimes the scarcity of those leads to a high birth-rate.
