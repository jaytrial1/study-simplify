8_Selective (Qualitative) Measures of Credit Control

Besides the general measures, the RBI uses certain selective measures to direct credit for the development of specific sectors or sections of the economy.

These measures have a unique impact on some sectors and are not meant to impact all sectors similarly. Some of these measures are:

# Security Requirement

Banks must ensure that the public returns the loans given to them. Hence, they lend money against some security deposits from the borrowers. In case the borrower is not able to repay the loan, the bank uses the security to recover its dues. For example, a bank can take jewelry, deposits, a car, a house, land, etc., as security.

Security specifications are different for different segments of the population so that all sections of the population can have the opportunity to obtain bank credit and all sectors, including the agricultural sector, develop well. Hence, the RBI directs commercial banks regarding the amount of security which they may ask for in the case of different types of loans or from different sections of the people. A poor farmer may be granted a loan without much security, and a rich business person may be required to keep more as security with the bank for a loan.

# Margin Requirement

The RBI also sets margins for granting loans against security. An individual is given only a certain percentage as a loan of the total value of assets offered as security. Banks are directed to selectively keep different margins for different purposes.

# Ceiling on Credit

The RBI also prescribes ceilings for credits for different purposes.

# Discriminatory Interest Rate

The RBI suggests differential rates of interest for different types of lending. This is called the policy of discriminatory interest rates. For example, a relatively lower rate of interest is charged from needy farmers than what is charged from rich business persons to buy a car or a house.
