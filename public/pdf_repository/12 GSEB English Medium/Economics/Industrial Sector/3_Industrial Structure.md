3_Industrial Structure
Industrial structure of India has remained progressive during the planning period. It is important to understand the industrial structure to know various aspects of the industrial sector. Industrial structure varies depending upon the size of investment, ownership, and produced products. This can be explained by the following factors:

# Types of Industries on the Bases of Investment Size:

## Cottage Industries
Industries mainly run by family members and with simple equipment and with negligible use of electricity, machines, and investment are known as cottage industries. E.g., Khadi, Papad, Khakhra, incense stick industries, etc.

## Tiny Industries
Industries run on labor-intensive production techniques along with an investment limit of up to 25 lakhs are known as tiny industries. E.g., Industries of Artistic products made from metal, leather, clay, etc.

## Small Scale Industries
Industries that have an investment of more than ₹ 25 lakhs and less than 5 crores and utilize labor-intensive production techniques and ancillary industries to big industries are known as small-scale industries. E.g., units producing tools and simple consumer goods, auto repair units, etc.

## Medium Scale Industries
Industries that have an investment limit of more than ₹ 5 crores and less than ₹ 10 crores, utilize labor-intensive or capital-intensive production techniques are known as medium-scale industries. E.g., Industries of machinery, chemicals, electronic equipment, etc.

## Large Scale Industries
Industries that have an investment of more than ₹ 10 crores and utilize capital-intensive production techniques are known as large-scale industries. E.g., Industries related to equipment for railways, big vehicles, iron, etc.

# Types of Industries on the Basis of Ownership

## Public Sector Units
Industries that have ownership and administration under government control are known as public sector units. For example, Railways, Telephone, Post, etc. Public sector units are classified into three categories:

### Departmental Industries
When the government runs industrial units under its direct observation as its department and includes its income and expenditure provisions in the budget is known as departmental industries. E.g., Railways, Post, etc.

### Public Corporations
Units that are owned by the central or state government but administration is under the independent control of the corporation wherein administration and the decision process is under strong influence of the government are known as public corporations. E.g., Life Insurance Corporation, State Transport Corporation, Air India, and fertilizer producing and selling units (GSFC and GNFC) are known as public corporations.

### Joint Stock Companies
Units that are managed by the government like private companies within the framework of prevailing company laws, for which the government raises capital by issuing and selling shares to the people or institutes are known as joint stock companies. These units are not working under direct controls of the government. These types of units are different from departmental industries and public corporations. E.g., Bharat Heavy Electricals Limited (BHEL), Oil and Natural Gas Corporation Limited (ONGC), Indian Oil Corporation (IOC), etc.

## Private Sector Industries
Industrial units that are owned and managed by the private sector are known as private sector industries. Here, it is notable that the management of such units is under personal ownership or partnership. E.g., Car, TV, Shoe making units.

## Joint Sector Industries
There is a difference between joint stock companies and joint sector industries. Units in which the government has given up ownership rights of industry to people and institutes $51 \%$ or more are joint sector industries. Thus though industry is joint sector industry, it still remains in the control of the government. E.g., GSPC.

## Co-operative Sector Industries
Industries run on co-operative activities to stop the exploitation of small (marginal) owners, to stop the exploitation of laborers, or to stop the exploitation of consumers and to provide benefit to all are known as industries of the co-operative sector. Shops of daily used (essential) commodities, dairies, several banks have administration on co-operative bases. E.g., IFFCO, KRIBHCO.

# Types of Industries on the Bases of Products

## Consumers Goods Industries
Goods that directly satisfy the requirements of people are known as consumer goods. Industries producing these types of commodities are known as consumer goods industries. E.g., ghee, oil, soap, shampoo, powder making industries, etc.

## Intermediate Goods Industries
Units that have semi-finished production. It means, the types of goods that are semi-processed in nature and still one stage of production is remaining are known as intermediate goods industries. E.g., yarn, steel sheets, machines, etc.
