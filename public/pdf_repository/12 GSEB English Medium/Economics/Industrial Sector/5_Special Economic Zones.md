# Introduction to Special Economic Zones

Special Economic Zones (SEZs) were introduced on April 1, 2000, with the primary goal of attracting foreign investment and fostering a control-free environment conducive to exports. The aim is to elevate Indian manufacturing sectors to global standards.

# Purpose of SEZs

## Attracting Foreign Investment

Tax incentives are legally provided to special economic zones to attract foreign investors.

## Development Model

These special economic zones are modeled after China's successful SEZs, which have significantly contributed to developing export-oriented manufacturing sectors with foreign direct investment.

# Characteristics of SEZs

## Tax-Free Zones

Special economic zones establish tax-free zones, creating distinct geographical areas within a nation where economic regulations differ.

## Global Adoption

Countries like China, India, Jordan, Poland, the Philippines, Russia, and North Korea have implemented special economic zones.

# SEZs in India

## Existing SEZs

India has established eight Special Economic Zones:

*   Santacruz (Maharashtra)
*   Kochi (Kerala)
*   Kandla and Surat (Gujarat)
*   Chennai (Tamil Nadu)
*   Visakhapatnam (Andhra Pradesh)
*   Falta (West Bengal)
*   Noida (Uttar Pradesh)

## Proposed SEZs

In addition to the existing zones, eighteen new special economic zones are proposed.

# Establishing an SEZ

## Eligible Entities

Any private individual, government entity, joint venture, state government, or their representative bodies can initiate a special economic zone. Foreign entities are also eligible to establish SEZs.

## Regulatory Oversight

All types of special economic zones may be subject to government control.
