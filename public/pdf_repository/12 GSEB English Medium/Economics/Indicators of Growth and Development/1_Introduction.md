# Economic Development After World War II

The primary goal for most countries globally, especially after World War II, has been achieving economic development. This concept has become central to economic thought. The terms "Economic Growth" and "Economic Development" gained popularity following the Second World War (1939-1944) as developing nations sought to address issues such as unemployment, poverty, famine, income inequality, and wealth disparity. Developing countries have become increasingly aware of the need for development, striving to narrow the per capita income gap with developed nations and enhance living standards through economic advancement.

It is important to note that improving economic development is crucial not only for developing countries but also for developed nations, albeit for different reasons. Developing countries aim to raise living standards by reducing poverty and unemployment, while developed countries focus on maintaining their existing high standards. Consequently, maintaining economic growth is the goal of developed countries, while achieving rapid economic development is the primary need of developing countries.

# Economic Growth vs. Economic Development

Generally, "economic development" and "economic growth" are often used synonymously.

## Historical Perspective

In the past, the terms 'growth' and 'development' were used interchangeably.

## Distinction by Economists

However, some economists have consistently viewed economic growth and economic development as distinct concepts.

### Ursula Hicks' Perspective

According to Professor Ursula Hicks, economic development is the solution to the economic challenges faced by developing countries, whereas economic growth is the solution for developed countries.
