3_Present Situation of Agricultural Sector in India (Importance)

India has been an agriculture-oriented economy before, during, and after British rule. During the planning period, industrialization has been the main focus, leading to abnormal changes in the present scenario of agriculture compared to the past. Currently, the agriculture sector provides more employment, production, and export income than in the past, but changes have occurred in these areas. Although the agriculture sector provides maximum employment to the economy, its contribution to the total revenue (National Income) is the least compared to other sectors. To understand these changes, let's examine several factors of the present scenario of agriculture.

The income of the agricultural sector is also known as the income of the primary sector. It includes agriculture crops, poultry farming, fisheries, and cattle rearing. According to the Economic Survey of 2011-12, the contribution of the agriculture sector to national income (GDP) was 53.1% in 1950-51 and decreased to 13.9% (at constant prices) in the year 2011-12 due to greater emphasis on industrialization since 1956. This fall in the contribution of the agriculture sector to national income is due to the rapid progress of the non-agricultural sector. The development of the agricultural sector in India has become a complex issue.

# Employment

The agricultural sector provides maximum employment in India. At the time of independence, 72% of the population was engaged in agriculture and allied agricultural activities (cattle rearing, fisheries, forest products, poultry farming, etc.). After the independence of India, the development process has become speedier, especially the development of industries and the service sector, which achieved much faster growth than the agriculture sector. Therefore, employment dependency on agriculture has been reduced. In the year 2001-02, it was 58%, which came to 49% in the year 2014-15 as an employment-providing sector.

# Export Income

Indian agriculture helps our country earn foreign exchange by exporting necessary exports and importing necessary goods that are not produced or produced less in the country. During the independence period, 70% of India's total export earnings were obtained from agriculture. However, due to the development process, industries and the service sector are at the top, and thus the contribution of the agriculture sector to export earnings has reduced. In the year 2013-14, the contribution of agriculture in the total export earnings was 14.2%.

# Living Standard

Agriculture is the basic support of the world's population and has continuously improved the lives of people in India. The agriculture sector produces two types of crops: Food grains and Cash crops. All cereals are included in food grains. Due to the production of these food grains, India became self-sufficient. Cash crops are cotton, jute, groundnut, oilseeds, sugarcane, etc., which are used as raw materials. Food items are mainly included in food grain crops, and these have shown a rise in production. Present farmers are also engaged in the production of vegetables, fruits, flowers, etc. Therefore, it can be said that the agriculture sector is satisfying the requirement of agricultural goods for people. The average food grain availability was 395 grams per head per day in the year 1951, which increased to 511 grams in the year 2013, even though there was a rise in population. Therefore, it can be said that the agriculture sector is satisfying the requirement of people in enough quantity, and due to that, the average life span of people has also increased.

The total production of agriculture in India has increased. As shown in the schedule, the production of food grains was 51 metric tons in 1951, which increased to 264.4 metric tons in 2013-14. That shows about a 2.5 times rise. Whereas the production of sugarcane was 69 metric tons in 1951, which increased to 348 metric tons in 2013-14. That also shows a rise of about five times. Here, oilseeds and cotton show a tremendous rise. Oilseeds were 5.1 metric tons in 1951, which rose to 32.4 metric tons in 2013-14. That means a rise of 6.35 times, and cotton was 2.1 metric bales in 1950-51. It rose to 36.5 metric bales in 2013-14. That means a rise of 17 times. That becomes possible only due to the rise of cultivated land and the rise of hectare-wise productivity.

# Base for Industrial Development

Agriculture is a base for the industrial sector of India. The agriculture sector provides the required raw material to the industrial sector to do production as per the capacity of the industrial sector to attain possible development. In addition, rural areas are becoming a big market for industrial products because about 69% of the population of India lives in rural areas. It is noticeable that 69% of the rural population has the main source of income from agriculture. Due to that, only rural areas are demanding industrial products like television, freezer, bike, mobile, etc.
