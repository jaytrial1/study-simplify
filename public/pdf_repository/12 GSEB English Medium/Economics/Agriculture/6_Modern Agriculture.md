6_Modern Agriculture

Indian traditional agriculture uses organic manure, seeds, simple ploughs, bullocks, and primary instruments. These instruments provide very little productivity, and their use was unable to satisfy the agricultural requirements of the entire nation. Therefore, since 1966, modern agriculture has emerged in India. Modern agriculture uses modern inputs like hybrid seeds, chemical fertilizers, pesticides, new agricultural machines, and irrigation. Due to the use of these modern inputs, agricultural production has increased extensively. This was considered a revolution. This revolution was in agriculture; therefore, it was called the Green Revolution.

# Green Revolution

The use of new agricultural technology started in the year 1960-61 at seven districts of India as a 'pilot project.' This was initially known as IADP (Intense Agricultural District Program), which means an intensive agricultural program for districts. Due to its wonderful success, with the passing of time, it was applied to the whole nation. When it was implemented nationwide, it was known as HYVP (High Yielding Varieties Program), which means a program of high-yield products, also known as the "Green Revolution." It is also known as the "Modern agricultural technology program" or the "Program of seeds, fertilizer, and water technology."

# Multiple Cropping

Multiple cropping can be obtained by growing different crops on a cultivated land area. Multiple cropping shows the nature of agricultural work. Normally, two types of crops are seen: (1) Food grains and (2) Non-food grains, which are also known as cash crops. Wheat, rice, coarse grains (millet/bajra, sorghum/juvar, maize, etc.), and pulses are included in food grain crops, whereas different oilseeds (groundnut, sesame, castor, soybean, linseed, sunflower, etc.) and sugarcane, rubber, cotton, jute, etc., are included in cash crops.

Reasons for multiple cropping are twofold: (1) Technological factors and (2) Economic factors.

## Technological Factors

Multiple cropping in an area depends on its soil, environment, rain, etc., elements. For example, in Madhya Pradesh, after taking a crop of millet/bajri, they take a crop of sugarcane, tobacco, etc., due to irrigation facilities. This way, multiple cropping is possible due to capital, new seeds, fertilizers, and credit facilities.

## Economic Factors

Economic factors are also important for multiple cropping. These economic factors are as follows:

(1) To maximize price and income (2) Availability of agricultural instruments (3) Size of farm (4) Protection of insurance (5) Tenure (Tenure available from landlord), etc.

The availability or scarcity of these factors is responsible for the selection of crops.

In 1950-51, approximately 75% of crops were food grains, and 25% were cash crops. After 1966, multiple crops have been seen due to the use of inputs from the 'Green Revolution'. In the year 1970-71, about 74% were food grains, whereas about 26% were cash crops. It became 64% of food grains and 36% of cash crops in the year 2006-07. But then again, due to the change in multiple crops in the year 2010-11, about 66% were food grains and about 34% were cash crops. That can be said based on the details of Agricultural Statistics at a glance of 2010-11.

# Crop Protection

Agricultural productivity should increase with the use of pesticides, but India has very little use of pesticides per hectare. The Economic Survey 2015-16 shows that India has only 0.5 kg per hectare use of pesticides, where America has 7.0 kg, Europe has 2.5 kg, Japan has 12 kg, and Korea has 6.6 kg use of pesticides. This is the reason why 15 to 25% of Indian crops get spoiled by insects, diseases, weeds, cattle, and birds, which may be saved.

Improper information about pesticides, poor quality pesticides, and a lack of information on the proper use of pesticides are big issues in India. Improper use of pesticides in India creates a big danger to mankind and the environment.

CIBRC (Central Insecticide Board and Registration Committee) is appointed to inform Indian farmers about various types of pesticides and their level of poison. They publish different booklets to guide farmers, which explain the quantity of pesticides, time of usage, and level of poison. This information should be spread among farmers to make pesticides environment-friendly. Pesticides should be effective but non-poisonous and cheaper to encourage small and marginal farmers to increase agricultural productivity.

# Agriculture Research

ICAR (Indian Council of Agricultural Research) is the only institute that manages agricultural research. It provides arrangements for research and also gives adequate help. It is also spreading awareness of Horticulture, Fisheries, and Cattle rearing science. ICAR has done pioneer work for the expansion of the green revolution. It has made appropriate efforts to have national food availability and nutrition.
