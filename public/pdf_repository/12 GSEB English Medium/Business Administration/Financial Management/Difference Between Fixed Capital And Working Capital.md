# Difference between Fixed Capital and Working Capital

| Sr. <br> No. | Points of <br> Differences | Fixed Capital | Working Capital |
| :--: | :--: | :-- | :-- |
| (1) | Meaning | Capital invested in fixed <br> assets such as land, building, <br> machinery, furniture is called <br> fixed capital. | Capital invested in current <br> assets such as stock of raw <br> materials and finished goods, <br> debtors, bills receivable etc. <br> is called working capital. |
| (2) | Period | Blocked up for a long <br> period in business. | Blocked up for a short <br> period in business. |
| (3) | Liquidity | Ratio of liquidity is less as it <br> is invested for a long period <br> in fixed assets. | Ratio of liquidity is high as <br> converted into cash easily. |
| (4) | Risk | Ratio of risk is high. | Ratio of risk is low. |
| (5) | Requirement | Required to purchase fixed <br> assets such as land, building, <br> plant and machinery. | Required for day-to-day <br> expenses like wages, salary <br> and to purchase rawmaterials. |
| (6) | Sources | This capital is obtained <br> through issue of shares and <br> debentures or obtained from <br> financial institutions. | This capital is obtained <br> through trade credit, bank <br> overdraft, indigenous bankers. |
| (7) | Depreciation | Depreciation is calculated on <br> fixed assets. | Depreciation is not calculated <br> on working capital |
