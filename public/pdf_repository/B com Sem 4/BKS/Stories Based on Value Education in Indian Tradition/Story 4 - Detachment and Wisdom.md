STORY-4 Detachement and Wisdom : The Story of King Janaka and Sage Yajnavalkya (Brihadaranyakopanishad)

In the heart of ancient India, amidst fertile plains and wise whispers, unfolds the legendary tale of King Janaka and Sage Yajnavalkya which is found in the pages of the Upanishads, shimmering with profound meaning.

King Janaka was a great king who ruled the prosperous kingdom of Mithila with grace and justice. He was a wise ruler who was known for his spiritual wisdom. His true quest did not lay in earthly conquests but in the uncharted territories of the soul. He desired for the ultimate knowledge, the essence of Brahman, the eternal Self.

Yajnavalkya, was a sage who roamed across the land and was valued for his wisdom and persistent pursuit of truth. He explored into

the deepest questions of existence, challenging rituals and beliefs, his path illuminated by the unwavering flame of inner inquiry. Rishi Yajnavalkya was a friend and intimate of King Janaka. Yajnavalkya was considered to be the greatest sage of that time, a supreme knower of Brahman.

One day King Janaka challenged sage Yajnavalkya to a debate, and the two engaged in a fierce intellectual battle. King Janaka asked very relevant and important question related to the knowledge of Self. King Janaka :"O revered sage, what is the light that illumines a person, the light that awakens and impels him to perform all that he does?"
Yajnavalkya : gave a very straightforward answer: "The sun, O king, for it is the sun alone that is the source of all light and it is for this light that man sits, moves about, does all his work and returns."
King Janaka :"That's right $O$ venerable sage, but when the sun has set, what is it that helps man as light?"
Yajnavalkya : "The moon; it is the moon that is the light of the man when the sun is not there".
King Janaka :"All right, I agree, but when the sun is not there, the moon is also not there, then what is it that guides man as his light?"
Rishi Yajnavalkya : "When the sun has set and the moon is not there, fire is our light, for by that we sit, work, go out and come back."
King Janaka :"I am all in agreement with you, but what then is the light when there is no sun, no moon and no fire?"
Yajnavalkya :"Speech, indeed, is the light when all these are absent. Even though we cannot see our hand in the dark we can hear the voice and move towards the sound." Janaka was happy but had still one more question. He asked, "O revered sage, when sun sets, moon is not there, fire is absent and there is no speech, then what is the light?"

Yajnavalkya : "The Self, indeed, is our light O king, for by that we sit, move, work, go out and come back." Janaka was deeply touched by this but wanted to know from Yajnavalkya more about the Self that he referred to as the light of all lights. Yajnavalkya continued to impart the knowledge of the Self to king Janaka. He kept unraveling the mystery after mystery of the Self Yajnavalkya spoke to king Janaka about the Self is like this:
Yajnavalkya : "The Self is the pure awareness that shines as the light within the heart, surrounded by the senses. It is this Self that is one with the Sole Reality, the Brahman. This Self is free from desire, from evil and from fear. The man who is union with the Self sees without seeing, smells without smelling, tastes without tasting, speaking without speaking, hears without hearing, touches without touching, thinks without thinking, knows without knowing, for there is nothing separate from him. This state of not having another is the state of unity, one without a second and that is the world of Brahman. This is the supreme goal of life, the supreme treasure, the supreme joy."
In the end, Yajnavalkya was emerged victorious and Janaka recognized him as a true master of spiritual knowledge.

# Moral of the story The Story of King Janaka and Sage Yajnavalkya from Brihadaranyakopanishad 

- The story symbolizes the eternal quest for self-realization, the yearning to break free from the limitations of the ego and merge with the infinite.
- The story reminds us that true wisdom lies not in external knowledge, but in the silent depths of our own being.
- Look beyond the veil of illusion to discover the unchanging reality within.
- Approach the quest for truth with unwavering curiosity and an open mind.

- True knowledge is not found in books or rituals, but through direct experience and self-inquiry.
- The path to liberation is paved with challenges, but the rewards are immeasurable.
