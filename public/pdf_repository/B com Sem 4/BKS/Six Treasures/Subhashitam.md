# SUBHASHITAM

## 1. नाभिषेको न संस्कार: सिंहस्य क्रियते बने । विक्रमाजितराज्यस्य स्वयमेव मृगोद्वता ॥
This verse describes the natural power and prowess of a lion: "A lion is neither crowned nor formally consecrated in the forest. It becomes the king of animals by virtue of its own valor and strength."
Explanation : The verse teaches that greatness or leadership is not achieved through ceremonies or rituals but through one's deeds

and courage. Just as the lion doesn't need a coronation to be recognized as the king of the jungle, individuals too can earn respect and authority through their effort, determination, and achievements. It emphasizes self-reliance, hard work, and bravery as the true markers of success and leadership.

## 2. . विद्या विवादाय धनं मदाय शक्ति: परेषां परिपीडनाय । खलय्य साधोद् विपरीतमेतद् ज्ञानाय दानाय च रक्षणाय ॥ 

This verse contrasts the qualities of a wicked person (khala) with those of a virtuous person (sadhu):
"For a wicked person, knowledge is used for argument, wealth leads to arrogance, and strength is used to oppress others. For a virtuous person, the opposite holds true: knowledge is for wisdom, wealth for charity, and strength for protection."
Explanation : The verse highlights the differing mindsets and purposes between the wicked and the virtuous:

1. A wicked person misuses knowledge to engage in disputes, uses wealth to show off or indulge in pride, and exploits strength to harm others.
2. In contrast, a virtuous person utilizes knowledge for gaining deeper understanding and spreading wisdom, uses wealth to help others through charity, and applies strength to safeguard and protect those in need.
This teaching encourages the ethical use of one's resources and abilities, aligning them with values of compassion, wisdom, and service.

## 3. अति तृष्णा न कर्तव्या तृष्णां नैव परित्यजेत् । ज्ञाने: ज्ञानेक्ष भोक्तव्यं स्वयं वित्तमुपार्जितम् ॥

This verse provides a balanced perspective on desire and the enjoyment of wealth :
"Excessive greed should not be indulged in, but desire should not be completely abandoned either. One should gradually enjoy the wealth that has been earned by one's own effort."

Explanation : The verse teaches the importance of moderation and balance in life:

1. Avoid excessive greed (अति तृष्णा) as it leads to dissatisfaction and suffering.
2. At the same time, do not completely suppress desire (तृष्णा नैव परित्यजेत), as it is natural and a motivator for action.
3. Wealth earned through one's own efforts should be enjoyed gradually (शनैः शनैः), ensuring it brings sustained happiness and well-being.
This wisdom encourages a balanced approach to life, advocating for neither overindulgence nor asceticism, but a thoughtful and mindful enjoyment of one's resources.

## 4. साहित्यसंगीतकलाविहीनः साक्षात्पशुः पुच्छविषापाहीनः | तृणं न खादन्नपि जीवमानस्तद्धागधेयं परमं पशूनाम् ॥
This verse emphasizes the importance of art, music, and literature in human life:
"A person devoid of literature, music, and the arts is like a beast, though without a tail or horns. Even if such a person does not eat grass and lives among humans, their life is no better than that of animals."

Explanation : The verse compares a person who lacks appreciation or knowledge of literature, music, and the arts to an animal, suggesting that:

1. Human life is elevated by cultural and intellectual pursuits. Without these, a person's existence is comparable to that of an animal.
2. The absence of artistic and cultural refinement diminishes the essence of humanity, even if the person appears human in physical form.
3. Such a person may not indulge in animal behavior like eating grass, but their life lacks the higher joys and purposes that come from engagement with literature, music, and arts.

The verse highlights that true human living involves more than mere survival; it includes cultivating and enjoying the finer aspects of culture and intellect.

## 5. सुखस्य मूलं धर्मः । धर्मस्य मूलं अर्धः । अर्थस्य मूलं राज्यं । राज्यस्य मूलं इन्द्रिय जयः । इन्द्रियाजयस्य मूलं विनयः । विनयस्य मूलं वृद्धोपसेवा । वृद्धोपसेवया विज्ञानम् । विज्ञानेन आत्मानं संपाद्येत् । सेपादितात्मा जितात्मा भवति । जितात्मा सर्वाधैः संयुज्येत ॥
This verse outlines a profound chain of values and principles that lead to ultimate fulfillment and mastery of life:
The root of happiness is dharma (righteousness).
The root of dharma is artha (material prosperity or resources).
The root of artha is governance (good administration or a wellrun state).
The root of governance is self-control over the senses (indriyajaya).
The root of self-control is humility (vinaya).
The root of humility is service to the wise and the elderly (vriddhopaseva).
Through service to elders, one gains knowledge and wisdom (vijnana).
With knowledge, one realizes the self (atma).
One who realizes the self becomes a master of oneself (jit-atma).
A self-mastered individual achieves success in all aspects of life (sarv-artha-samyujyata).
Explanation : This progression emphasizes the interconnectedness of values, actions, and outcomes. It begins with practical aspects like wealth and governance, rooted in discipline and humility, and ascends to spiritual self-mastery and universal success.
Service to elders and humility are portrayed as the foundation for growth, showing respect for experience and learning as essential for wisdom.
