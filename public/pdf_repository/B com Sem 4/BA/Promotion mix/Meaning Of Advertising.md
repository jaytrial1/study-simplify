4\. MEANING OF ADVERTISING:

It is difficult to give an exact definition of advertisement. We find advertisements on all sides. It is a term that has a very wide meaning. It is derived from Latin words 'Ad' and 'verto'. The meaning of 'Ad' is towards and "Verto" means to turn, to attract. In short, advertisement means to attract the attention of people. Broadly, advertising means to notify or communicate to the people, some special information about a product. To understand the meaning more clearly, it is necessary to be familiar with some definitions of advertising.

(1) According to Lasker, 'Advertisement is salesmanship in print'.

(2) Frank Presbrey is of the opinion that, "Advertising is a printed, written, oral and illustrated art of selling. Its object is to encourage sales of the advertiser's products and to create in the minds of people individually or collectively an impression in favour of the advertiser's interests".

(3) Hepner defines advertising as 'creative communicative activity conducted through such mass educational media as newspapers, magazines, radio and television to make people informed and to encourage them to buy.

(4) According to Chambers Encyclopaedia "Advertising includes those activities by which, visual or oral messages are addressed to the people for the purpose of informing them and influencing them either to buy merchandise or to act or inclined favourably towards ideas, institutions."

(5) In the words of Carl Richard, "Advertising is a general term for any and all forms of publicity, from the cry of the street boy selling newspapers to the most elaborate attention attracting device. The object always is to bring to public notice some article or service, to create a demand, to stimulate buying, and in general to bring together the man with something to sell and the man who has the means or desire to buy."

(6) The American Marketing Association has defined Advertisement thus, "Any paid form of non-personal presentation and promotion of ideas, goods or services by an identified sponsor."

4.1 CHARACTERISTICS OF ADVERTISING:

The main features that follow from the above definitions are as below:

(1) Impersonal Salesmanship: Though advertising is an art of selling goods, it is impersonal. The salesmen personally contact the customers and sell goods in the area of personal selling, while through various media of advertising people are informed of goods or services and are motivated to buy.

(2) Handwritten, Printed or Oral: The message about a commodity or service may be handwritten (as written on blackboards) printed (i.e. handbills, posters, circulars and leaflets etc). oral (i.e. drumbeats, shouts, announcements on T.V. and radio) or pictorical (pictures and designs).

(3) An Art: Advertising is a highly developed tool at present, thanks to the growth of printing, newspapers, magazines, radio and television. It must not be forgotten, however, that advertising activity is as old as the activity of exchange and trade. In the old days, people were motivated to assemble in one place through shouts and drum beating etc. and then a message was conveyed to them about goods to be sold.

(4) Activity Addressed to a Mass of People: Advertising is not an activity concerning a single individual. Instead, it is an activity through which information and message is communicated to a large number of persons collectively so that a group impact may be created, in favor of goods or services.

(5) Objective of Sales Promotion: The objective of advertising is not only to give a message or information about a product. In addition it aims at inducing the people to purchase it. It attempts to explain the uses and significance of the product, so that misunderstanding about the product may be removed. The result is that sales expand, markets widen and profitability of business improves.

(6) Use of Various Media: The old and new media are both used in the current advertising activities. The examples are newspapers, circulars, posters, writings on the wall, magazines, vehicles, boards, samples, gifts, cinema slides, electric signs, radio and television etc.

(6) More Elements of Truth: Advertising was misleading in most cases during the last quarter of the nineteenth century and also in the beginning of the present century. Now, advertising agencies associations have come into existence to regulate advertising activity. Also, laws have been enacted by the government for the same purpose. Now advertisements contain more elements of truth than before.

(8) Huge Expenditure: The annual advertisement expenditure in countries like the U.S.A. amounts to Rs. 13,000 crores, which is about three times the annual budget of the Government of India. Procter and Gamble, a soap manufacturing company in the U.S.A. spends about Rs. 210 crores, on advertisements and sells goods worth Rs. 1,900 crores every year. In India alone, this company spends Rs. 26 crores on T.V. advertisements. The rate for a full page advertisement only once in the most popular American monthly 'look' was Rs. 3,75,000. In the U.S.A. the advertising charge per minute is $ 2000 on radio and $ 20000 on the T.V. screen. In India a 30 minutes T.V. serial can be at the cost of a few lacs.
