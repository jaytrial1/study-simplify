3\. INDUSTRIAL OR PRODUCT MARKET-PRODUCERS MARKET:

"Industrial or producers market consists of all the individuals and organisations that acquire goods and services to use in the production or other products or services that are sold, rented or supplied to others."

The main industries buying in this manner are: Agriculture, mines, manufacturing, construction, transportation, communication, banks, insurance companies etc. Similarly, the buying value of the industrial market is higher than the buying made by the consumer market.

The Characteristics of Industrial Market are as under:

(1) Fewer Buyers: The industrial marketer normally deals with far fewer buyers than does the consumer marketer. This is because one single large company may buy crores of rupees worth of products from a single supplier for further production.

(2) Large Buyers: In this market a large share of total buying is made by a few large producers. e.g. In the area of car production, 70% to 80% of the total raw material is purchased by four to five big car manufacturers.

(3) Close Supplier-Customer Relationship: The buying is made on a very large scale in this market, which leads to inevitable close relationships between suppliers and buyers. Supplier is supposed to manufacture keeping in to consideration the needs of the producers. By arranging meetings with producers their needs have to be understood.

(4) Geographically Concentrated Buyers: The buyers of different products are concentrated mostly in certain regions only. e.g. buying of groundnut is mainly in the area of Saurashtra, Andhra Pradesh, Chennai and Mumbai. Still however the geographical locations may change some times. e.g. now major buying of cotton has shifted from Ahmedabad to Mumbai and Chennai.

(5) Derived Demand: The demand of the industrial market is not for direct consumption but it is derived from the consumer market demand. If consumer demand goes down, then its dependent industry's demand may also go down.

(6) Inelastic Demand: In certain industrial goods and services the price fluctuation of raw materials has no impact e g. in spite of rise in cotton prices the textile mills producing cotton cloth do not reduce their buying quantity or otherwise do not buy more if prices go down

(7) Fluctuating Demand: The demand for the industrial goods and services tends to be more volatile than the demand for consumer goods. Sometimes an increase in consumers' demand by just 10% may result in a 200% increase in the demand for machinery in the industrial market.

(8) Professional Purchasers: The purchasers handling purchase portfolios in the industries are expert on buying as well as technically qualified. They have received special training. As a result whatever they buy is economical from an investment view point and it is technically sound. This is because they can evaluate technical matters in a better manner.

(9) Buying influenced by Several Factors: The consumer very rarely discusses with somebody before buying while in industrial buying mostly the decision is taken by the purchase committee as well as senior manager. In industry it is necessary to have detailed discussion with the senior manager and purchase committee before deciding to purchase a certain product.

In the industrial market mostly purchasers are purchasing directly from the producer without approaching the intermediaries. In certain cases the purchase is made with an intention to reciprocate advantage.

Major Influences on industrial buyers: In the industrial market following factors are having impact on the behaviour of buyers:

1\. Environmental factors like level of demand, rate of interest, the economic outlook, competition etc. 2\. Organisational factors like organisational objectives, policies, procedures, organisational structure etc. 3\. Interpersonal factors like status, the authority level etc. 4\. Individual factors like age, income, education, location, attitude of taking risk etc.
