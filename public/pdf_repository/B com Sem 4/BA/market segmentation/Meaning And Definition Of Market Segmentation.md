2\. MEANING AND DEFINITION OF MARKET SEGMENTATION :\*

To grasp the meaning of market segmentation, let us examine some definitions:

(1) In simple terms 'market segmentation may be defined as a process of dividing a market into different sub-markets on the basis of some definite criteria.'

(2) Another definition may be put as follows: "Market segmentation is a process of dividing buyers into various groups with a common need or want, into different classes on the basis of region, age, sex, preference, habit, prejudice etc.'

(3) According to Philip Kotler, "Market segmentation is the sub- dividing of a market into distinct subsets of customers, where any subset may conceivably be selected as a market target to be reached with a distinct marketing mix."

In his latest definition Philip Kolter has stated, "The process of classifying customers into groups exhibiting different needs, characteristics or behaviour is called market segmentation. Every market is made up of market segments".\*

(4) According to Stanton, "In market segmentation, the total heterogeneous market for a product is divided into several segments, each of which tends to be homogeneous in all significant aspects".

(5) In the words of Cundiff, Still and Govoni, "Market segmentation refers to the analysis of a total market in terms of its component segments, each being made up of prospective purchasers, who share common characteristics as buyers."

(6) Definition given by Frank Jefkins, "Market segmentation is the identification of numerous sub-groups which make up the mass market. Thus, analysis is made of the total market to place consumers in homogeneous groups so as to determine the most profitable segments on which to concentrate market strategy.”

It follows from the above definitions of market segmentation that

(i) Market is basically a group of buyers.

(ii) But these buyers differ in terms of quantity to be bought, needs and urgency, preferences and buying motives etc.

(iii) Hence, all buyers can be successfully influenced through sales effort of the same kind.

(iv) Producers divide the whole market into different groups of buyers on the basis of age, sex, income, habit, prejudice etc. so that a sales promotion program suitable to each of them may be devised and launched. This is known as market segmentation.

For instance, (1) On the basis of region, a market can be divided into rural customers and urban customers. (2) On the basis of age, it can be divided into children, young and aged customers (3) On the basis of income, a market can be segmented into poor, middle class and rich customers. (4) On the basis of preferences, a group of buyers can be divided into different segments according to the colour of the product, its size, taste and shape etc. (5) On the buying habits, customers can be grouped into those who buy in retail, those who buy in wholesale, those who buy on credit and those who buy for cash payment etc. (6) On the basis of prejudices, customers can be divided into fashionable and conservative.

To take one more example, instead of a single market for shoes a distinction is made between different sub-markets consisting of different classes of customers such as school children, college students, doctors, administrators etc. Shoes are designed to suit their distinct needs and preferences. Sales campaigns are also adjusted to influence each class of buyers separately. Similarly, producers of cloth also have to examine such questions as what are the needs and whose needs are to be met before they produce to serve those needs.
