5\. GEOGRAPHIC BASES:

Here markets are divided into different groups on such bases as states, regions, cities etc. On the basis of region, markets can be divided into (1) local market, (2) city ma riket, (3) village market, (4) regional market, (5) national market and (6) international market. The Italian Bakery's market is a local market as its products sell in Ahmedabad city only. Market for an expensive, fashionable and better quality cloth is an urban market as it is sold mainly in cities, while the market for coarse, durable and less expensive cloth is a rural market as it is sold mainly in villages. There is a regional market for Vadilal icecream and Rajat note books because they are in demand in big cities in the Western India. The products such as Vimal cloth, Bata shoes, Bajaj Scooters. Thums-up, Parle Glucose biscuits, Kisan Tomato Catch-up, Godrej Refrigerators, Usha fans and Prestige cookers enjoy national markets as they sell all over India. The precious metals like silver and gold are bought and sold all-over the world, and hence theirs is an international market.

(2) On the basis of area or density, markets are divided into city markets, sub-urban markets and rural markets. (3) Markets can be divided on the basis

of weather ie. whether the climate in the area is cold or hot. For instance, in western part of North India and in hilly areas like Jammu and Kashmir, Simla, Matheran, Mahabaleshwar, Utakamand etc. the climate is cold and hence they serve as a good market for woollen cloth. As against this, the climate is hot in Gujarat, Madhya Pradesh, Bengal, Chennai etc. and hence they offer good opportunities for sale of cotton and thin cloth.

The producers have to adopt a proper marketing strategy keeping in mind the geographic nature of the market for their products. For rural markets, a traditional medium of publicity would be more effective, while for the national markets, a comparatively new medium such as television network programmes would be more advisable.
