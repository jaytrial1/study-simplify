The national market of any country is very wide e.g. there are wide national markets for such goods as sugar, kerosene and petrol etc. The producers of such goods need not concentrate on a specific group of buyers or a distinct market. This may be termed as 'mass marketing'. But in respect of items like cloth, different customer groups buy different types of cloth according to their preferences, needs and buying motives. The producers of such products must be clear whether they want to make a general purpose cloth which can be bought by any person in the nation-wide market or a distinct kind of cloth to serve the needs of a particular group of buyers only. It is possible to divide buyers of cloth in distinct groups. The customers of poor and low income groups buy the coarse cheap quality cloth, the middle class persons are interested in polyester and tere cotton cloth; the rich end higher middle class persons like to go for expensive quality of polyester and teriwool cloth such as top quality cloth made by Raymond, Digjam, Vimal, Dinesh etc. The manufacturers of sarees prefer to serve a particular group of female buyers because the saree market can also be divided into such groups of buyers as poor and middle class women, rich and well-to-do women, young and old women etc.

This suggests that the cloth market is not a single market. But it is a composite of various sub-markets distinguished on the basis of age, sex, preferences, income, geographical area, buying habits etc. Each firm prefers to serve only one or two of these sub-markets. The process of dividing a market into distinct groups of buyers is called market segmentation. This division helps producers to concentrate on a definite group of buyers with a view to promoting sales of his product. This may be called "Target Marketing.'
