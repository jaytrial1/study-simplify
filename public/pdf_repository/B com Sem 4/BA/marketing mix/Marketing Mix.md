## **1\. INTRODUCTION :**

That four basic elements of marketing together make up a marketing mix, was first indicated by Prof. Neil Borden of Harvard University. These our P's of marketing are product, physical distribution, price and promotion. Marketing is likely to be a success if these four P's are mixed properly. We shall discuss them in this chapter. There are a number of functions or processes of marketing. Marketing includes not only the function of selling; but also includes assembling, processing, standardisation and grading, storing, transporting, insurance, advertising, selling and providing after-sale services. In the present chapter, we shall explain all these marketing functions. The Id belief that marketing means selling goods anyhow has become outdated. There are five concepts of marketing production concept, product concept, selling concept, marketing concept and societal marketing concept. ## **2\. 'FOUR Ps' IN MARKETING / '4P' OF MARKETING MIX :**

Product is the point of origin of marketing in any business unit. It is only when a product is produced by a producer or purchased for resale by a trader, that the problem of marketing arises. Hence, 'product' is the most significant element of marketing. The product may be a physical commodity or it may be a service. The next question is what price to charge for this product. Obviously that price is considered as reasonable which is profitable to the producer and reasonable to the buyers. Hence, the other important element in marketing is price. When a product is ready for sale and price is also fixed, the problem is how to send it to the innumerable buyers scattered over vast areas. To solve this problem, a proper system of physical distribution is necessary. The marketing manager has to decide whether the company itself should sell the product through its retail stores, or sell it through wholesalers, or through retailers or through some other means. Once a product is placed in the market, it is necessary to take some steps for sales promotion also. In other words, sales promotion is also an important element in marketing. There are four well-known methods to promote sales; advertisement, personal selling, sales promotion and publicity.

Philip Kolter writes. "Marketing mix is the set of marketing tools that the firm uses to pursue its marketing objectives in the target market."

The other definition given by him is, "Marketing mix is the set of controllable variables that the firm can use to influence the buyers' responses." According to R. S. Davar, "The policies adopted by the manufacturers to attain success in the market constitute the Marketing Mix."

In short, there are four P's which constitute a marketing mix. They are as follows:\*

(1) Product

(2) Price

(3) Physical Distribution

(4) Promotion

The group of these four P's marketing-mix. Prof. Neil Borden of Harvard University propounded the idea of marketing mix in an article entitled 'The Concept of Marketing Mix' published in the Journal of Advertising Research, in June, 1964\. A marketing mix is a combination of product, distribution, sales promotion and price in an optimum proportion so that the company can re	 ach effectively that segment of the market to which it wants to sell goods.

(1) Product Product is a basic element of marketing. The marketing organisation has to take some important decisions concerning the product, which together form the product strategy of the company. Two important aspects of this strategy are once to place new product in the market ie. product innovation and, two, to make changes in the existing product i.e. product planning and development.

The producer has to decide what should be the nature of the new product and whether it will be acceptable to the market. He has to inquire whether the new product will satisfy the needs and wants of the customers and whether customers will be able to pay the price. If proper investigation is not made and the problem has not been given due consideration, the new product is likely to fail.

The other decision is with regard to product mix. Generally, a company prefers to produce a product related to its main product. For example, the Bush Company makes T.V. sets along with its radio sets. The D.C.M. The company makes cloth, but along with it, it also produces cement and chemicals. Thus, the company has to examine the nature and profitability of its product mix.

The third decision is concerning the policy to be adopted at different stages of the life cycle of the product. In the initial stage, the price of a newly introduced product is high generally, and yet its demand tends also to remain high due to its novelty. For instance, when T.V. sets were introduced in the market in 1982, the demand for colour T.V. The set was high, and its market price was also quite high. Then follows the stage of its growth in the sense that its demand increases as the number of buyers increases gradually. The competition among the producers also increases and consequently its price goes down. The third stage is that of maturity, when demand for the product gets stabilised at a definite level. This stage continues for a long period. The television market in India is in the third stage of its life cycle at present. In the final stage, when the buyers get fed up with the product and turn to some other product, it enters into its last stage of life cycle. i.e. its declining stage.

(2) Physical Distribution: This is the second important element of the marketing-mix. A proper channel of distribution should be selected so that the product of the company can easily reach into the hands of its final consumers. The mode of transportation and storing facilities are to be selected carefully.  
Of course, the choice of the channel of distribution depends on the nature of the product, financial resources and the business traditions etc. But it must be so selected that in combination with other elements of marketing mix it helps in the realisation of goals of the company such as maximisation of profit, economic stability and long term growth of the business etc. The decision about physical distribution means decisions about whether the product is to be marketed through wholesalers or retailers, through company's own stores or through sole selling agents etc.

(3) Price The third element of the marketing mix is about pricing of the product. The price should be fixed at such a level that it will create an optimum marketing-mix along with other elements. The price should be fixed keeping in view the channels of distribution selected so that the company can realise its long run objective of profit maximisation, even after allowing a satisfactory return to the intermediaries. Along with prices, discounts, freight charges and other problems related to price must also be considered carefully.

To fix the price of the product, three criteria are available cost, competition and demand. Sometimes, a fixed percentage of profit is just added to the average cost of production to determine the price of the product. Sometimes, the price charged by the rivals is fixed, because a price which is even slightly higher than that of the rivals may be harmful. Most of the time, price is fixed according to the demand for the product. Sometimes it is possible to follow the policy of price discrimination, i.e. charging different prices to different customers for the same product.

(4) Promotion: Mere production of a commodity is not enough. To sell it, consumers must be informed of its existence and advantages, they must be induced to purchase it and if necessary, special incentives must be offered to them. Thus, sales promotion efforts are also necessary. There are four ways by which sales can be promoted: advertisement, personal selling, sales promotion and publicity. The decision to combine these in the best possible manner determines the success of the business. The best combination can be decided on the basis of the nature of the product, financial position of the company, the number of customers and the group to which they belong etc. The other equally important decisions involved at this stage are with regard to the size of advertisement budget, timing of the sales promotion efforts, training of the salesmen and planning and controlling of their activities and use of such media as radio, television and newspapers etc.

Thus, the four P's stated above are the cornerstone of marketing activity. They must be well coordinated so that an integrated system of marketing can be developed.

## **3\. Elements of Marketing Mix :**

Dr. R. S. Davar in his book 'Modern Marketing Management' has given following eight elements of marketing mix:

(1) General Planning: The enterprise has to clarify the acceptable and possible rate of growth in general planning. The environment in which the business has to function is studied in an organised manner for this purpose. Then it has to determine the size and extent of the market where business may function profitably. Besides, it has also to determine the costs and expenses needed for the business operations and also estimate the finances needed for the purpose. Of course, an eye has to be constantly kept on minimum size and area, as any reduction in it would result in reduced profitability. Such general planning is essential to make business successful. It helps in policy formation to achieve the objectives.

(2) Product Planning Product planning signifies the development of a product in such a way that it will give maximum satisfaction to the consumers in the context of their needs and wants. It includes all aspects relating to the product like design, colour, alternative uses, quality, packing etc. The marketing research makes product planning more meaningful. The product planning includes (1) Determining the product or service offered (2) The possible improvement and innovation in the product. (3) The brand and packaging policies (4) The importance of servicing and after-sale services.

(3) Pricing: The work of determining the price of a product is a very delicate task. Generally, this is done by the sales manager in consultation with top management. While determining price the factors which are considered are cost of production, competitors' prices, condition of demand, appropriate margin, resale price maintenance, government control, if any etc.

(4) Channel of Distribution: There are mainly two methods of distribution direct and indirect. Under direct method, the producer sells directly to consumers for which he opens his own retail shop. It also includes selling through mail. Under the indirect system, the producer sells through wholesalers or through some other intermediaries.

(5) Sales Force This includes recruitment, training, remuneration, control and incentive to salesmen. Sales quotas are fixed for each salesman taking into consideration the potential sales of each territory. The total requirement of sales force depends upon the extent of personal selling, the extent of approach to wholesalers and retailers and extent of approach to consumers directly.

(6) Advertising and Sales Promotion: In the modern era of cut-throat competition, the effective advertising and efforts of sales promotion assumes special significance. In determining the importance of these aspects, the management has to take into account. (1) The advertisement programme (2) Importance given to display and point-of-sale material (3) Extent of sales promotion aimed at consumers, and (4) Extent of sales promotion to dealers.

(7) Physical Handling: This factor includes such aspects as transportation, warehousing, inventory prices, and possible reduction in costs. To prevent unnecessary increase in cost, it is essential that transportation and warehousing costs should be properly controlled.

(8) Marketing Research: This includes research about products, customers, efficiency of marketing set-up, distribution channels, effectiveness of advertisement, extent and nature of competition etc. This includes sales analysis field surveys and use of outside agencies.

It is an outstanding example of dynamism in business. In the U.S.A., Black and Decker Co. was established in 1910, which makes drills for cutting holes in wood and metals. When it put its first electric drill in the market in 1910\. it weighed 50 lbs (pounds 454 gms.) Two persons were required to hold it and a third person was required to control the electric switch. This instrument was costly and very inconvenient. The company thought that the market requires a convenient electric drill, which can be handled by one person only. Hence, it prepared a design of light weight portable drill and put a small motor and electric switch in the same small machine and it became an uncrowned king in the field of portable power tools. Why? Because it concentrated on customers' needs and not on sales or advertisement.

(2) Price: Another important element of four P is the price. While pricing a product, it should not be assumed that the customer always wants a cheap item. He wants value for money spent by him. What is proper value for money is a subjective concept and is in the mind of the customer. A manufacturer who charges arbitrarily high prices will ultimately ruin himself. The multi-national companies have come to India to capture the Indian market but they charge very high prices and so their sales estimates have gone wrong.

Their sales forecasts have been proved wrong as they had not been able to understand Indian customers' psychology. There may be different motives of pricing like maximising profits, obtaining market leadership, to increase market share, to defeat the competitors and to achieve different motives, different pricing policies have to be adopted. In order to increase profit in the short run, high prices have to be fixed but to defeat competitors, lower prices are required to be charged.

(3) Place: Here the third element of marketing mix is place meaning thereby either place where goods are to be sold or it may also mean distribution channel. An important factor contributing to the success of a company's business is its distribution channel. How to send the goods to customers, how to reach them in distant small places or villages? For this purpose the company must prepare its distribution network. Dealers and stockists have to be appointed and given proper remuneration in the form of commission. The dealers have to dispatch goods to the retailers. Some companies avoid these intermediaries and reach directly to customers. But this arrangement requires a great deal of skill and expertise. In India, Tatas are selling their vacuum cleaners directly to customers. But generally, there are a number of intermediaries in distribution channels like wholesalers, semi- wholesalers, stockists, retailers etc. This distribution system requires a great deal of skill on the part of management. The Hindustan Lever Limited and Pan Parag have the best distribution system in India.

(4) Promotion In promotion, the main emphasis is on advertisement and sales promotion techniques. Advertisement has now become the main tool of sales promotion and all major companies spend crores of rupees on advertisement, particularly on television advertisement. They send a message to customers through such advertisements and so they treat advertisement as marketing communication, e.g. formerly, an outstanding advertising company of Ahmedabad was named Mudra Advertising, which is now known as Mudra Communication. Advertising science requires deep study, as each advertisement has to send a message to customers. A good deal of professional competence and skill is required in preparing such a message. The second part of promotion includes sales promotion techniques. It embraces discounts, commissions, prizes, competition among customers, gifts to customers etc. The third one is personal selling and mail order selling. Goods are sold through television programmes tele Asian Skyshop.

Some authors argue that as packaging is a part of a product, it should not be treated as a separate element.
