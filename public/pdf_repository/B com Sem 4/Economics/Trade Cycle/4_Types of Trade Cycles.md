Types of Trade Cycles

According to James Arthur Estey, there are four main types of trade cycles :

(1) Secular Trends
(2) Seasonal Fluctuations
(3) Random Fluctuations
(4) Cyclical Fluctuations.

# (1) Secular Trends ( Long - term Cycles )

The secular trends or long term cycles refer to a persistent movement in the same direction over a long period of time. Such trends generally show upward movements of economic activity spread over a long period, though they may as well relate to sustained downward movement of economic activity. So far only two long - wave cycles have been studied. These are the cycles between 1789 - 1814 and 1814 - 1896. The first half of the third wave has also been studied by some economists. It was Russian economist N.D Kondratieff who undertook a study of the long period trends and therefore these secular trends are also known as Kondratieff Cycles. He found that there was a very long period rise of economic activity followed by a very long period fall in economic activity all these years. According to Schumpeter, Kondratieff's first cycle was due to the industrial revolution, the second one was due to the use of steam and steel and the third was due to the discovery of electricity, motors and mechanical power.

# (2) Seasonal Fluctuations

Seasonal fluctuations refer to the variations in business activity that take place due to changing seasons within the period of a calender year. Seasonal flctuations are seen in various business and economic phenomenon

like prices of agricultural and industrial products, bank clearings, interest rates, demand for certain commodities, etc. The leading causes of seasonal variations are climate and customs. Thus, for example, there is a spurt in demand for woolen clothes in winter, for electric fans in summer and for umbrellas in monsoon. Likewise, festivals, social and religious functions etc. influence the demand for many goods and services. For example, fire crackers and sweets during Diwali, kites during Makar Sankranti, dresses for 'garbas' during Navratri Festival, cakes in Christmas, etc. Generally, these seasonal fluctuations have regular periodicity and as such they are predictable. Therefore, they do not cause any serious social or economic problems.

# (3) Random Fluctuations

These refer to changes in economic activity brought about by certain random factors which are unexpected and unanticipated. Random factors are non - recurring and irregular and affect the economy as a whole. Thus, for example, earth quakes, famines, floods, fires, strikes, revolution, war etc. may be termed as random factors which bring about fluctuations in economic activity. These factors, in fact are accidental and have no sequence or rhythm.

# (4) Cyclical Fluctuations
Cyclical fluctuations are wave - like fluctuations of economic activity characterised by recurring phases of expansion and contraction. They contain oscillating movements in the form of waves from peak to trough and from trough to peak. One complete period of such oscillations is called a cycle which have a free rhythm and are irregular. But they have some recognised pattern of recurrent expansions and contractions. These cyclical fluctuations are spread over a relatively longer period than that of seasonal fluctuations, although their life - span is much shorter than that of secular trends. The life of a normal business cycle is about 7 to 11 years. Then, there are long - period cycles called Kondratieff Cycles and minor cycles of about 40 months life - span called Kitchin Cycles.
