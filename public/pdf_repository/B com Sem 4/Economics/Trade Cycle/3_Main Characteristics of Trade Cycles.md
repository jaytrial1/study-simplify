3. Main Characteristics of Trade Cycles

The following are the main features of trade cycles :

(1) A trade cycle is a wave-like movement. It is characterised by alteration of expansion (prosperity) and contraction (depression) in economic activity. A period of prosperity is followed by a period of depression and vice-versa.

(2) All trade cycles are more or less similar in nature, but they are not exactly the same. As has been aptly said, "all the recorded cycles are members of the same family, but amongst them, there are no twins."

(3) Trade cycles are recurrent in nature, that is, they are repetitive. All trade cycles are rhythmic and have some recognised patterns.

(4) Although businees cycles are recurrent in nature, still they are not periodical and regular. Some cycles are very severe while some others are mild.

(5) Trade cycles are cumulative in character. Once started, they go on sustaining themselves in a particular direction; they are self reinforcing in nature.

(6) A trade cycle is self-generating in nature. The self-generating forces terminate one phase and start another phase. There cannot be an indefinite boom or an indefinite depression period.

(7) Trade cycles are all pervasive in their effects; they affect practically every segment of the economy.

(8) Business cycles are international in character, but all countries are not equally affected by trade cycles.

(9) In trade cycles, the points of prosperity and depression are not quite symmetrical. The movement from expansion to contraction or from up-turn point to down-turn point is more sudden, violent, sharp and steep than the movement from contraction to expansion or from down-turn point to up-turn point.

(10) Fluctuations are more marked in capital goods industries as compared to consumer goods industries.

(11) In trade cycles, profits fluctuate more than any other factor income.

(12) Like wise, in trade cycles, prices, production and employment move together in the same direction.
