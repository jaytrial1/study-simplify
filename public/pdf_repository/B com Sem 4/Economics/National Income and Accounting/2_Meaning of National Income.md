As a result of various economic activities performed by people of a country, various types of goods and services are produced in the country. Thus, the total value of goods and services produced in the country during a year, is known as Total National Income. Thus, in order to derive the Total National Income of a country, the following should be kept in mind :

(i) Value of goods and services produced during a year.
(ii) Values of new capital goods such as machines, instruments, vehicles etc. produced during a year.
(iii) Difference between export value and import value.
(iv) Values of produced goods and services are to be calculated at some fixed prices, not at current prices.

# Net National Product
One can find out Net National Product by subtracting depreciation from the total national product. This is because, depreciation of the capital goods occuring during the production of the Total Net Production has not been considered here. In reality, however, capital goods also depreciate over a time period. i.e., these goods also have a certain level of life and after that, they have to be replaced. So, from the total National Product, this 'depreciation' has to be subtracted and then one is able to get the 'Net National Product'. Thus, Total (Gross) National Product = Gross National Product - Depreciation.

Question of Avoiding Double Counting :

While caluculating National Income, there is a possibility that value of some items are counted twice. Suppose we first calculate values of cash crops like Sugarcane and Tobacco amd alongwith it, we also add the values of things which are produced by these goods i.e. say cloth, sugar, cigarattee etc. then values of sugarcane and tobacco are counted twice and so final figure is misleading as it does not give the correct and reliable value of National Income. So, in order to avoid such problems, usually the following two methods are adopted :

(i) To take into account price of the readymade or the last good produced : In this method, to avoid double counting, the values of Kachcha product, half ready product and intermediate goods are not taken into account, but the values of ready goods only are considered. e.g. while calculation the National Income, values of cotton or sugarcane is not taken into account, but only the values of the last product i.e. cloth, or sugar etc. are taken into account.
(ii) To add rise in value at every stage of production : In this method, the increase in value of product at each production level is taken into account. Thus, the process of production is divided into several steps and addition to the value of the commodity at each stage is added to it.

# Calculation of National Income is done at Constant Prices
It should be remembered here that while calculating the National Income, value of goods and services is calculated not at current prices but at constant prices. It so happens sometimes in actual practice, that due to inflationary situation in the economy or due to taxes imposed on the goods, prices rise and even when there is no rise in production, this way of calculation, result in artificial higher National Income. This is misleading, because even when there is no rise in production, there is illusary rise in the National Income.

Therefore, to obtain the correct and reliable picture of National Income, calculation is performed at constant prices and taking into account, the effects of taxes, subsidies etc. which influence prices.

# Monetary Income and Real Income
It is necessary keep in mind, the difference between Monetary Income and Real Income, which is theoretical in nature. When the goods and services produced i nthe country in one year, are expressed in terms of production, it is called National Production, and when it is expressed in monetary terms, it is called National Income. So when the phrase 'National Income' is used, it means it is Monetary Income.

Now when National Income is evaluated at current market prices, it is known as 'Monetary Income', but when National Income is evaluated at constan prices, it is called 'Real Income'.

It is to be remembered here that one can not say with confidence that when monetary income rises, real income also rises. e.g. when price level rises, monetary income also rises, but the production of goods and services might have remanined constant or might have gone down. Thus even wifen production of goods in the nation has remained constant or has reduced, then even when monetary income has increased real income might have remained constant or might have fallen.

Thus, to know the actual economic progress of a nation, the idea of Real Income is more useful than that of the Monetary Income. Eventhough National Income is measured in monetary terms money is just a medium of exchange. The importance of National Income depends upon, how much goods and services can be purchased through money. e.g. suppose that due to inflation, prices of all goods and services rise to three times of the original levels, and the remaining things remain the same. Then one can see that the National Income has risen to three times the original value. However, there is no addition to the real prosperity. When income of all have increased three times, but all have to pay three times the original prices, the Reai Income will remain the same. So it is not sufficient that there is rise in the monetary income. Alognwith monetary income, there is rise in real income. One has to take into account increase in Real Income. Then only one can know the actual rise in income. Increase in monetary income due to price rise is misleading and deceptive.

In short, if one wants to have a clear idea about happiness and welfare of people of any country, then he should know its real income. One can not get a clear and true idea through monetary income only. Moreover, the purpose of monetary income is to buy goods and services. One can not get happiness through money. But the goods and services that are purchased through that money, provides pleasure and satisfaction. Therefore, the concept of real income is important.
