**Introduction**: The system of national accounts has been the most important tool for policy making in the last fifty years. It is an economic interpretation of changes in the wealth and resources of a nation. With the help of this concept, an estimate of the current income flow of the economy and a measure of the financial and resource flows can be obtained.

**Explanation**: This systematic national accounting system is the process of economic growth. Economic activity observed during the period may not reflect the depletion of natural resources and environmental damage. In this situation a correct estimate of environmentally adjusted net domestic product cannot be obtained. Implicitly this question is a question of measuring economic development in an environmentally sustainable manner. In this context, UNO's Department of Statistics has introduced an alternative national accounting system known as the Environmental and Economic Accounts System. This is also known as satellite accounting. These satellite accountings are complementary accounts that are linked to the 'environment' and show the value of the environment. Green accounting is prepared by linking satellite accounts with national accounts.

The EADP (Environmentally Adjusted Domestic Product) method can be estimated from the environmental point of view (adjusted) domestic product. In the formula, EADP = Net Domestic Product - Depletion of Natural Resources - Environmental Degradation

E.C. 178 nations of the world participated in the 'Earth summit of 1992. These nations are expected to adopt this method of calculation.

EADP aims to measure national welfare taking into account the damage to the natural environment.

Thus, environmental accounting is the systemsatic description of the interaction of the environment and the economy through an accounting framework. This framework aims to provide information about the impact of the economy on the environment and the contribution of the environment to economic output, in a given period and beyond.

As we have seen earlier, the established system of national accounts ignores the depreciation of natural resources. The impact on human health and human welfare due to environmental degradation cannot be underestimated. Inadequate attention is paid to the details of the conservation costs involved in stopping this process. The concept of EADP has been introduced to solve such a problem and there are three points of view :
(1) Include environmental impacts in national accounts.
(2) To prepare satellite accounts with systematic national accounts,
(3) Independent natural environment and natural resource accounts integrated with national accounts.

The first two perspectives relate the economic evaluation of environmental damage and regional resource depletion. (One suggests that the value of environmental services should also be taken into account, but such a calculation seems almost impossible).

A third view points to keeping inventories of physical and financial flows and quantities of natural resources.

Why should environment and natural resources be covered in national accounts? And what kind of difficulties can arise in doing this calculation? A discussion of these two questions seems present here.

In answer to the first question, it can be stated that such accounts provide useful information for the analysis of policy decisions. Such policy decisions may touch on the impact of environmental protection on economic growth and the interrelationships between business, environment and natural resource policy.

The second question relates to the methodology of preparation of accounts. First of all, estimate the economic value of non-renewable natural resources like petroleum, coal etc. By doing so, national income will provide a basis for measuring the extent to which natural resources have been depleted.

These types of calculations are difficult to perform as previously indicated. It is important to know some examples of how problems can arise in caluclations, such as -
(1) Natural resources sold in organized markets are easy to account for but how to account for groundwater resources, wildlife resources which do not directly enter the sales chain and do not directly support production?

(3) It is also difficult to estimate the contribution of man-made equipment in reducing pollution damage, like air conditioners reduce pollution damage to respiratory patients but are all air conditioners purchased for this purpose?

**Conclusion**: The question is sometimes raised whether the inclusion of natural resources and environment in national accounts mislead policy makers? Is green accounting really useful to policy makers?

As the previous discussion points out these questions are now irrelevant. While Inclusion in national accounts is essential, it seems to require some special care, such that value addition through environmental services should be recognized in national accounts. As such services are invisibly included in the measurement of national income, they should clearly indicate the cost of reducing the harm caused by pollution. Also, national accounts ignore the capacity of the environment to absorb pollution. The value of equipment used to prevent pollution damage is included in national accounts eg C.N.G. Fuel use is also not classified differently in the national accounts than other investments or consumption.

In fact, insufficient, incomplete information on natural resources and the environment has become a concern for policymakers regarding the. interrelationships between business, economic growth, and the environment.

The fact is that changes in environmental quality and natural resource stocks should be fully reflected in national accounts, information on the amount of investment and depreciation costs informs policy makers about whether or not the natural means of production are maintained, but in fact ign6res most changes in natural means and the environment.

Thus, the methodology for incorporating environmental accounting into national accounts is still in its infancy. It has a lot of room for improvement. But its utility and necessity is beyond controversy, it is self-evident.
