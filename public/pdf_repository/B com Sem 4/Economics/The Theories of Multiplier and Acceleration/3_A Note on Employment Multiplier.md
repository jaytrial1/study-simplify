
The concept of multiplier discussed above is known as investment multiplier and was developed by Keynes. However, as said in the begining of this chapter, Keynes has developed this concept of investment multiplier on the basis of Kahn's employment multiplier.

R. F. Kahn in his article, "The Relation of Home Investment to unemployment" published in the Economic Journal of June 1931 pointed out that if during periods of depression, the government undertakes a massive programme of public investments, the total employment in the economy would increase by many more times than the primary or the initial employment which varies directly with government investment.

Kahn's concept of employment multiplier measures the ratio of an increase in total employment and the initial increase in primary employment in the investment goods industries. It may be expressed symbolically as follows :

$$
K^{\prime}=\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta N_{1}}
$$

Where $\mathrm{K}^{\prime}$ is employment multiplier, $\Delta \mathrm{N}$ is increase in total employment and $\Delta N_{1}$ is increase in primary employment.

Thus, Kahn's employment multiplier is the coefficient relating to an increment of primary employment to the resulting increment of toial employment.

It can, therefore, be said that while the employment multiplier of Kahn is the ratio of increase in total employment to the increase in primary employment, the investment multiplier of Keynes, on the other hand, is the ratio of increase in total income to increase in initial investment.

Keynes, however, is of the opinion that these two multipliers investment multiplier ( K ) and employment multiplier ( $\mathrm{K}^{\prime}$ ) are not identical. According to him, "there is no reason in general to suppose that $\mathrm{K}=\mathrm{K}^{\prime}$." This is because it may so happen that income in terms of wage-units may rise more than employment, if in the process non-wage earners' income rise in greater propertion than wage-earners', income. Likewise, under conditions of diminishing returns, the total output would rise less proportionately than employment.

Hansen, on the other hand is of the view that, if we assume that $\mathrm{K}^{\prime}=\mathrm{K}$ in the short run, the level of income and employment would tend to rise or fall together along with changes in investment.

According to Dillard, the concept of employment multiplier is useful in showing the relation between primary employment and secondary employment from public works. It indicates the number of persons who will be added to total employment for every one person that is directly employed. Thus, for example, if the employment multiplier is 3 , this implies that every person newly employed in capital goods induustries will cause two other persons to be newly employed in the consumer goods industries giving a total increase in employment of 3 persons.
