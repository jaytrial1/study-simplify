3. Difference Between Inter-regional and International Trade

Theoretically speaking, there should not be any difference between inter-regional (internal) trade and international trade because, as said above, the basis of all trade is division of labour and specialisation. Still, the study of international trade forms a separate part of study in economic literature. The classical economists have indicate important differences, between these two types of trade and therefore, they were justified in propounding a separate theory of international trade. In other words, theoretically speaking both internal trade and inter national trade appear to be similar, still there are differences between the two. These differences are not only of kind but of degree too. Therefore, why international trade arises requires a separate presentation and a separate theory. The following are the main differences between internal and international trade :

# Differences in the Mobility of the Factors of Production
The classical economists had assumed that the various factors of production are perfectly mobile within a country, while they are immobile or less less mobile as between different countries of the world. The factors can move freely within the different parts or regions of a country while such free movement does not take place between one country and another country. Thus, for example, a worker would accept work any where within his country, but he is not easily ready and willing to go in an other country. It is in this context that Adam Smith - father of Economics had said, `Of all the luggage, man is the most difficult luggage to be transported'. The main factors which restrict international mobility are differences in luggage, customs and traditions, altogether new environment, immigration restrictions, huge cost of setting in foreign countries, differences in food habits etc.

Besides, immigration controls in different foreign countries also tend to restrict mobility. Likewise, mobility of capital gets restricted because of various factors like risk involved in investing capital in foreign countries, longer of nationalisation, discriminating policies towards foreign capital and foreign investment, controls and restrictions of profit repatriation etc. Thus, according to classical economists, factors of production are perfectly mobile in internal trade while they are immobile or less mobile in international trade.

It is because of the differences in the mobility of factors of production that there arises fundamental differences between internal and international trade. Due to the perfect mobility of the factors of production within a country, their rewards tend to be the same, while in international trade there may arise differences in the rewards of the factors of production. It is due to the immobility of the factors of production that there is the existence of 'non-competing groups' in international trade.

Again, due to the differences in internal and international mobility, separate laws have been deduced for international trade. According to Kindleberger, in case of internal trade when fall in demand in one region leads to a rise in demand in another region, the factors would tend to move in that region, but in the case of international trade, such changes in demand would necessitate different types of changes. ${ }^{+}$

# Differences in the Currency System
Another difference between inter-regional and international trade arises due to the existence of different currency systems in the different countries of the world. In the case of internal trade payments are made in one and the same currency and so it does not pause any difficulty. However, in international trade, payments are to be made in different currencies and so there arises various complex and intricate problems. For example, if we import from America, we cannot make payment in rupees, for this we need. dollards. Likewise, if we export to Japan, it cannot pay in yen. Thus, in international trade there arise the problem of converting local currency into foreign currency at a particular exchange rate. In this way, the problem of foreign exchange arises in international trade, while there are no such issues in internal trade.

In short, the problems of exchange control and balance of payments arise in the context of international trade. Changes in the exchange rate have a profound impact on international trade and many a times, instead of fundamental principle, it becomes the result of other factors.

Over - valuation or under valuation of currency has a deep impact on exports and imports and it also affects international movements of capital. Besides, changes in the external value of currency also has its impact on the productivity capacity of the nation and also on the level of employment.

# Sovereignty of the State
The third reason for a separate discussion on international trade is the sovereignity of each state. The various countries in international trade are independent sovereign states and can pursue their own independent policies

[^0] with respect of movement of goods, money matters, wages and prices, fiscal matters, banking laws, foreign capital etc. Several kinds of restrictions may be placed on the movement of goods beyond their frontiers by the states.

# Different Economic Policies
Within the same countries the various economic policies are the same and apply uniformly through out the country. There no differences in the application and implementation of economic policies, like the monetary policy, fiscal policy, trade policy etc. in the different regions of the country.

In the case of international trade, however, different countries have different economic policies. Similarly, differences in policies relating to wages, social security measures, taxation, trade restrictions, foreign trade etc. may exist amongst countries. Thus, for example, one country may adopt the policy of monetary expansion or rising interest rates, while other country may like to follow a policy of monetary contraction or deflationary policy or falling interest rates. These problems hardly in case of internal trade and hence the need for a separate theory of international trade.

# Differences in Countries, Regulations and Restrictions
There are controls and restrictions in both internal and international trade, but there are difference in the controls and regulations between internal and international trade, In the case of international trade, the number of controls restrictions as also the degree of these controls and restrictions are special as compared to internal trade. Thus, for example, in the case of international trade, there are various types of controls and regulations like the protectionist policy to safeguard the interest of domestic industry against foreign competition, imports and export controls, quota system etc. The objective is also to earn revenue and deal with the problem of shortage of foreign exchange etc. Likewise, in case of internal trade too there certain controls and restrictions on the movement of goods as between different regions of the same country, but there are great differences in the number and degree of such controls and restrictions.

Controls and restrictions in international trade are mainly due to economic and politiacal reasons. Custom duties are an important source of revenue for the state as also it is used as an instrument to protect the domestic industry against undue foreign competition. Besides, such controls and restrictions are imposed due to scarcity of foreign exchange.

# Different Markets
International market is more complex and intricate than internal market. Apart from different currency systems and various types of controls and regulations, there are many other factors which account for differences, between different market. Legal systems consumers' preferences and likings, differences in language, existence of different social institutions etc., differents systems of weights and measures, different techniques of consumption, food habits, differences in fashions etc. account for differences in different market.

The feeling of national unity and patriotism widens these differences. As Prof. Eredic List aptly points out. 'domestic trade is amongst us while international trade is between us and them.'
