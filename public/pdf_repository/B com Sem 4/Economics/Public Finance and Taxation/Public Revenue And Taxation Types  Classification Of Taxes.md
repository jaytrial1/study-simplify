# 3_Public Revenue and Taxation Types (Classification of Taxes)
Public revenue means revenue of the State and other Public Institutions. Modern State obtains its revenue through various sources like taxes, fee,

penalty, revenue from the public enterprises etc. However, in modern times the most important sources of revenue for the Government is taxation about which we shall discuss in the following paras.

## Meaning of Tax
Taxation, by far, is the most important source of revenue for the government. According to Seligman : "A tax is a compulsory contribution from the person to the government to defray the expenses incrurred in the common interests of all, without reference to special benefits conferred." This means that:
(i) A tax is a compulsory payment made to the State by every citizen of the country; non-payment of a tax is a punishable offence.
(ii) Tax is a payment for meeting certatin expenses incurred by the State for conferring common benefits to the society; that is, it is a contribution for the general or common use and not for any specific purpose or individual benefit.
(iii) A tax is not the cost of the benefit recieved, that is, the payment of tax by an individual does not entitle him to receive any direct benefits or services from the government in return for the tax. An indivdual cannot expect that the State should render him a specific service or a special benefit in return for the tax paid by him. Of course, the State does confer certain benefits on the tax payers, but these are common benefits meant for all and not any special benefit meant for a particular tax payer.
(iv) There is no relationship between the amount of tax paid by an individual and the benefits which he may receive as a result of the government expenditure. As Taussing has very aptly put it, "The essence of a tax as distinguished from other charges by the Government, is the absence of a direct 'quid-pro-quo' between the tax payer and the public authority."
## Classification of Taxes
Taxes have broadly been classified under three major heads :
(1) Direct and Indirect Taxes
(2) Proportional, Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes
(3) Specific and Advalorem Taxes

### (1) Direct and Indirect Taxes :
#### (a) Direct Tax :

A direct tax is a that tax whose burden is borne by the person on whom it is levied. He cannot shift or transfer the burden of the tax to some other

person. J.S.Mill defines a direct tax as "one which is demanded from the very person who, it is intended or desired, should pay it." Thus, the impact, that is, the initial or first burden - and the incidence, that is, the ultimate burden of a direct tax, is on the same person. The tax-payer is the taxbearer. For example, income tax, wealth tax, gift tax etc are examples of direct taxes.

#### (b) Indirect Tax : 

An indirect tax is that tax which is paid by one person but the burden of which is borne by another person, The person who pays the tax in the first instance shifts its burden on to the other person. In other words, an indirect tax is a tax the burden of which can be shifted to others. Thus, the impact and incidence of an indirect tax are on different persons. An indirect tax is levied and collected from a person who manages to pass its burden on to some other person or persons on whom the real burden of the tax falls. Hence in the case of indirect taxes, the tax-payer is not tax bearer. Commodity taxes like excise duties and sales tax are generally regarded as indirect taxes as they are imposed on the producers or sellers, but their incidence falls on the consumers as such taxes are wrapped up in the prices.

Many modern writers however, have distinguished between direct and indirect taxes on the basis of assessment "rather than on the point of assessment". According to this view, taxes are genrally assessed on the basis of income received or expenditure incurred. Thus, taxes which are based on income are known as direct taxes, while those which are levied on outlays are known as indirect taxes.
Advantages and Disadvantages of Direct and Indirect Taxes:

(a) Advantages of Direct Taxes :

The following are the main advantages of direct taxes :
(i) Direct taxes honour the principle of equality. Direct taxes can be made sufficiently progressive so as to secure equal sacrifice of princes and paupers. Such taxes prove to be an effective instrument of social justice by reducing inequality of income and wealth.
(ii) Direct taxes are most certain. The tax-payer knows pretty well how much he has to pay. Besides the Government can also estimate accurately the revenue yield of such taxes.
(iii) As direct taxes are paid directly to the government, the cost of their collection is much less. In other words, direct taxes are economical.
(iv) Such taxes are productive. The income from these taxes swell almost automatically as income and wealth of citizens increase. They easily satisfy the changing needs of the state.

(v) Direct taxation commands the virtue of elasticity. Such taxes are flexible. Their rates can be comfortably changed to procure necessary finance.
(vi) Direct taxes generate civic consciousness. Such consciousness is very necessary for the smooth and efficient working of a democracy. Every tax-payer experiences the pinch of direct taxes. This compels him to take interest in policies and programmes of the Government. The educational value of such taxes is therefore great.

(b) Disadvantages of Direct Taxes : 

Direct taxes are criticsed on the following grounds :
(i) One of the great drawbacks of direct taxes is that such taxes are cynically referred to as "tax on honesty" that they can be easily dodged or evaded. Tax-evasion is a big menace in India to-day. Moreover, it is very diffcicult to bring irregular income within its net. Tax evasion create the problem of black money (which is not black in colour). Black money has assumed dangerous proportions (almost a parellel economy) in India.
(ii) Direct taxes are highly inconvenient. As pointed above, they have to be paid in lump sum at an inconvenient time. Such taxes create discontent.
(iii) Direct taxes are arbitrary. As it is difficult if not impossible to determine one's ability to pay tax, rates of direct taxes on income and property are arbitrarily fixed.
(iv) Direct taxes may adversely influence saving and investment in an economy. Steeply progressive direct taxes endanger production and kill incentives to work, save and invest. Such direct taxes increase to be economical. The Indian income tax law is a piece of highly progressive direct tax. Shri N. A. Palkhiwala therefore criticised India as the highest taxes nation."

(c) Advantages of Indirect Taxes :

Indirect taxes are favoured on the grounds mentioned below :
(i) Indirect taxes are difficult to evade. It is possible only if there is a collusion among all persons involved. This is not easy.
(ii) Indirect taxes are very convenint, as they are paid in very small amount. They are collected without much bitterness and irritation. Such taxes are hardly felt as they are wrapped up in the price of the commodity. Therefore, they are euphemistically called "taxation in the dark". Such taxes raise income without creating discontent or frustration.
(iii) Indirect taxes can also be made progressive. Luxuries can be taxes heavily. High taxes on luxuries and semi-luxuries narrow down inequalities of income.

(iv) One of the chief advantages of indirect taxes is that they are the way to reach a large number of middle class and poor people, not covered by indirect taxes. Hence indirect taxes broaden the tax-base.
(v) Indirect taxes also serve one social purpose. Such taxes prevent persons from consuming injurious drinks or drugs. Taxes on liquor, opium and such other intoxicants or narcotic drugs add to human welfare.
(vi) Indirect taxes are also used as contra-cyclical instrument to control inflation and slump.

(d) Disadvantages of Indirect Taxes : 

Indirect taxes at disfavoured because of their following weaknesses:
(i) Indirect taxes mostly impose a disproportionately heavieg burden on the poor than on the rich. The rich and the poor have to pay the same tax e.g. a tax on a packet of cigarette. Thus such taxes tend to be regressive.
(ii) The cost of collecting indirect taxes is relatively more. Thus they violate the canon of economy.
(iv) Indirect taxes are not much felt as they are paid as a part of the price of the commodity or service. The pinch of payment is conspicuous by its absence in indirect taxation. They fail therefore to arouse civic consciousness.
(v) It is difficult to trace the exact incidence in indirect taxes as their incidence is shiftable.

Conclusion : discussion gives rise to one pertinent question as to which of the two types of taxation is desirable. The answer is no government can rely completely on any one. Direct and indirect taxes are complementary, not competitive. Indirect taxes act as the police to catch those who escape from direct taxes. It is desirable to have a prudent admixture of both of them to subserve different social and economic objectives. The discussion can be best summed up using Gladstone's analogy that direct and indirect taxes are like two charming sisters and that one can do no better than be impartial between them.
### (2) Proportional, Progressive, Regressive and Degressive Taxes:
#### (a) Proportional Tax :

A proportional tax is one in which all incomes are taxed at a single uniform rate, no matter whether the income is high or low, Thus, in proportional taxes, the rate of tax remains constant though the tax base changes. For example, under this system, if an individual with an annual income of say Rs. 50,000/- pays Rs. 5000 as tax, then an individual with an annual income of Rs. 1,00,000/- will pay Rs. 10,000 as tax. annual income of Rs. 1,00,000/- will pay Rs. 10,000 as tax.

In a proportional tax system, therefore, taxes vary in direct proportion to the changes in income. If income is doubled, the tax amount is also doubled. A proportional tax extracts a constant proportion of rising income Such a tax, on the face of it, appears to be just, but infact it is unjust. Proportional taxes ensure equality of rate but not equality of sacrifice; they are not consistent with the principles of justice and equity taxaion.

#### (b) Progressive Tax : 

When the rate of tax increases with an increase in income it is known as a progressive tax This tax has two important features :
(a) An exemption limit is fixed and all those whose income is below this limit are granted exemption from the payment of the tax
(b) The rate of tax goes on incresing with an increase in income. Thus, under a progressive tax system, higher the level of income, higher the rate of taxation and vice versa. For example, if a person with an annual income of say Rs. 50,000/- pays the tax at the rate of say 10 percent, a person with an annual income of say Rs $1,00,000 /-$ will pay the tax at the rate of say 15 percent. Thus, in a progresive tax, the rate of tax increases as the tax - base increases. Sometimes, it is also known as a graduated tax.

Progressive taxes are equitable because they make the rich people (who have a greater ability) to pay more. The rates of progressive taxes can be so fixed as to secure justice and equity in taxation.

The Case for Progressive Taxes :

In the modern tax system, progressive taxes occupy a very important place and have been widely accepted all over the globe. The case for progressive taxes mainly rests on the following ground :
(i) In order to implement the canon of equity, it is progressive taxes and not poroportion taxes which help to achieve this canon of equity. It is worthwhile to remember that even in the sphere of income the law of diminishing marginal utility does operate. An increase in income tends to reduce or diminish marginal utility of money and therefore it has been held
that lower income groups should be taxed at relatively lower rate. Progressive taxes are justified also on the grounds of sacrifice. Prof. Marshall, therefore, considers progressive taxes justified on grounds of justice and equality. In order to achieve the goal of economic equality and social justice, the richer class needs to be taxed at a higher rate and hence the progressive taxes.
(ii) Progressive taxes are also advocated on the ground of maximum social welfare. If a higher tax rate is levied on higher income groups, the sacrifice which they have to undergo is relatively less and the same higher tax revenue is trasferred to lower income groups through public expenditure, the gain to the lower income groups is much more and thus to achieve the objective of maximum social advantage, progressive taxes are used.
(iii) Progressive taxes are useful from the point of view of productivity too. These taxes help in meeting the ever - increasing need for revenue of a modern state.
(iv) Progressive taxes are economical; an increase in their rates lead to substantial increase in revenue without a corresponding increase in the cost of tax collection.
(v) Progressive taxes impart flexibility to the tax system. In times of emergency or any other difficult situation, it can be met by a slight increase in progressive taxes and can yield more revenue to the government to tide over the emergent situation.
(vi) According to Prof. Hopson the `surplus element' in individual's income indicates his tax paying capacity and as income increases, the 'surplus element' exceeds the 'element of expenditure.' The surplus income is that part of total income which does not diminish under the effect of taxation and so there is no harm on taxing it; hardly affects upper income groups and industry. On the other hand, the form of indirect taxes on goods and commodities is quite different from this. Thus, taxes which are levied on the element of expenditure adversely affect the consumers in the long run and taxes levied on the surplus element do not have such adverse effects. Still however, while levying taxes on surplus element of income care will have to be taken to see that they do not adversely affect savings, investment and enterprise. An indiscriminate high rate of taxes is likely to have an adverse impact on savings, investment and enterprise is the opinion of many economists.
(vii) According to Prof. Keynes, in order to attain full employment in the income redistribution should be done in such a way that it leads to reduction

in income inequalities. If, in order to achieve the goal of full employment it becomes necesssary to maintain the high propensity to consume, then the richer section of the society whose propensity to consume is low should be taxed at a higher progressive rate and the tax revenue so accrued may be distributed through progressive public expenditure amongst lower income groups whose propensity to consume tends to be relatively high.

Limitations of Progressive Taxes : 

The following are the main limitations of progressive taxes :
(i) There appears to be an element of arbitrainess in the imposition of progressive taxes. There is no specific standard or scientific basis for determining the level of progressiveness. It all depends on the whims and fancies of the Government in power.

However, this argument is not against progressive taxes but against determining the level of progressive taxes. If it is not possible to evolve any scientific standard, the government, through its experience can have an estimate of the element of progressiveness. Besides, in all democracies there are legislative assemblies which can control and regulate the arbritariness of the government.
(ii) It has also been said that the argument in favour of progressive taxes for increasing the welfare of the society does not cut much ice, because the concept of welfare is 'subjective' and cannot be exactly quantitatively measured.
(iii) Looked at from the broader view point, progressive taxes may prove to be harmful. An indiscriminate high rate of progressive taxes is likely to have an deterious effect on saving, investment and enterprise. However, if such taxes are levied reasonably, there is little likelihood of their adverse impact.
(iv) The basic assumption behind the progressive taxes cannot be verified scientifically. With an increase in income, the satisfaction derived from income tends to decline cannot be tested scientifically. Satisfaction derived from income is relatively a subjective concept and cannot be specifically or exactly measured. It has, therefore, been held that progressive taxes can hardly be justified from the scientific point of view.
(v) As said earlier, excessive high rates of progressive taxes is likely to have an adverse impact on savings, investment and enterprise. Production mainly depends on investment and investment on a large scale takes when there are no strict control, regulations, and restrictions on it and there is a favourable atmosphere to plough back profits for investment. Besides,

unduly high rates of progressive taxes scultle productive enterprise and acts as a deterrent to investment and enterprise. Apart from this high progressive taxes tend to encourage tax evasion and other unethical practices and may cause a flight of capital.

Considering both the above arguments, we may say that a judicious and discriminate use of progressive taxes may play an important role in achieving the goal of maximum social welfare.
#### (c) Regressive Tax
A regressive tax is one whose burden falls more heavily on the poor than on the rich. It is just the opposite of a progressive tax. Under such a tax system, as the income of an individual increases, the rate of tax goes on diminishing, In other words, when the rate of tax decreases as the tax base increases, it is called a regressive tax.

It should also be noted here that in regressive taxation, although the total amount of tax increases on higher incomes in the absolute sense, the tax rate declines on higher income in the relative sense. Thus, a regresive tax extracts a declining proportion of the rising income. Generally, a tax on necessaries is regressive as it takes away a greater proportion of lower income away as compared to higher income groups.

A system of regressive taxation is unjust and inequitable; it is not in conformity with the canon of equity and tends to widen inequalities of income in the society.

#### (d) Degressive Tax
A degressive tax is mildly progressive upto a certain limit and after that it is charged at a flat-rate. In other words, the rate of tax goes on increasing mildly with an increase in income upto a certain limit, but after that limit the rate of tax becomes uniform or flat for all slabs of income. Thus, a degressive tax is an admixture of progressive- cum- proportional tax.

### (3) Specific and Ad-Valorem Taxes

#### (a) Specific Tax
When the tax is imposed on a commodity according to its weight, size, number, length or volume, it is known as a specific tax. Thus, for example when excise duty is levied on tea on the basis of its weight - or duty on cloth is levied according to its length (per meter), it is referred to as a specific tax.

Specific taxes are easy to calculate and administer because these are levied according to the weight of the commodity or according to the size of the unit of the commodity But these taxes do not satisfy the canon of equity and are regressive in nature, because when the same specific tax is revied on diffrent qualities of goods, a relatively greater burden falls upon low quality cheap goods which are generally consumed by the poor.

#### (b) Ad-Valorem Tax
When the tax is levied entirely on the money value of a commodity it known as advalorem tax or advalorem duty. Thus, for example, sales tax on a commodity levied at the rate of say three percent of its selling price is an advalorem tax; or a 10 percent advalorem custom duty on the imports of machinery means that the importer has to pay an amount equal to 10 percent of the value of machinery as tax.

Advalorem taxes are more equitable in incidence as they are based upon the value of the article so that the canon of ability to pay is fulfilled, Being in proportion to the prices, such taxes impose a greater burden on the richer sections. However, advalorem taxes are difficult to administer and collect as they involve the complex procedure of assessing the value of the commodities. Genrally the business men tend to under value the commodities in their invoices to avoid the burden of paying advalorem tax.

As a matter of fact it is difficult to choose between a specific tax and an advalorem tax, Sometimes, therefore, a compromise is attempted between these two taxes under which commodities are graded according to their quality and different rates of specific taxes are levied on them. Thus, for example, while levying excise duty on textiles, a specific tax of say 50 paise per meter may be imposed on superior quality of textiles, while the medium and coarse variety of textiles may be charged at the rate of 30 paise and 20 paise respectively.
