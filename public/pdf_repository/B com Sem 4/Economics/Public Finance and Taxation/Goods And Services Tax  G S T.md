# 4_Goods and Services Tax (G.S.T)
Goods and services tax is an excellent idea and concept about which there can hardly be two opinions. This is a tax which is in existence in more than 160 countries of the world.

The Goods and Services Tax which seeks to create a seamless national market in the country by replacing the multiple Central and State taxes on various goods and commodities with a single levy is an unprecedented tax reform. The said tax could unfold dramatic changes in the realm of tax reform.

As an integral part of economic reforms and tax reform, both the Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha passed the Constitutional Amendment Bill for the introduction of Goods and Services Tax Bill in August 2016 and thus paving the way for the implementation of the Goods and Services Tax with effect from July 01, 2017. With this, the taxes on various goods and services levied by the Central and State Governments have been replaced by one uniform tax system leading to the achievement of long cherished dream of 'One Nationa, One Tax and One Market'.

With the implementation of Goods and Services Tax with effect from July 01, 2017 the following central taxes have ceased to operate :
(i) Central/Union excise duty.
(ii) Additional central excise duty.
(iii) Service Tax
(iv) Additional custom duty
(v) Special additional custom duty
(vi) Central sales tax etc.

Likewise, the state taxes have also ceased to operate :
(i) State value added tax
(iv) Luxuray tax
(ii) Octroi and Entry tax
(v) Entertainment tax
(iii) Purchase tax
(vi) Lottery, horse-racing, batting and gambling tax etc.

Merits : 

The following are some of the main advantages claimed for the Goods and Services Tax :
(i) It will give a great boost to 'make-in India' compaign and lead to an increase in exports.
(ii) There will be a single uniform tax market in the country.
(iii) Instead of multiple taxes by the Centre and the States, there will now be one uniform tax in the country.
(iv) It will end the problem of tax on tax.
(v) There will be uniformity in tax rate and tax composition.
(vi) For the tax payers, it will be easier to comply and that too at less cost.
(vii) Possibility of an increase of about 1.5 percent to 2.0 percent in our gross domestic product.

(iv) Benefit of exemption/composition scheme for large segment of small scale suppliers to make their products cheaper.

It should be noted here that for the efficient implementation of Goods and Service Tax, the Government has constituted a Good and Services Tax Council - G. S. T. council with the Union Finance Minister as its Chairman and the finance ministers of all the 29 states of the country as also the representatives of all the 7 Union Territories who all are the members of the said council. This council meets from time to time in the different parts of the country and discusses the various issues and problems arising out of the implementation of the Goods and Services Tax and takes appropriate decisions. Thus, for example, at its $23^{\text {rd }}$ meeting held at Guwahati (Assam) held on November 10, 2017, the council moved 178 items out from the list of 228 products from 28 percent slab to 18 percent and less. Now only 50 items remain in the highest slab of 28 percent; rate for restaurants except luxury hotels was too reduced to 05 percent minus input tax credit. Besides, certain procedures too were simplified.

Demerits : 

Very High Tax Burden on SMEs

As per the structure of the previous tax system, only those businesses whose yearly sales were more than Rs. 1.5 crore were required to pay excise duty. But as per the new tax structure, it is mandatory for all businesses whose annual sales are more than Rs. 40 lakh to pay GST.

Compliance Burden
GST compliance is quite high due to the filing of 3 tax returns every single month. Besides, now it is mandatory for the companies to register for the GST in all states wherever they perform business.

The whole procedure of registering with the regulatory body, producing GST-compliant invoices, maintaining digital records, and filing returns have put a huge stress burden on SMEs and others.

Increased Costs : 

It is seen that GST compels businesses to convert their present accounting software to ERP or GST-compliant software with a view to keeping their operations running. But one also has to remember that the businesses may incur substantial expenses for buying, installing, and then training employees to use GST-compliant software.

In addition to this, the costs of doing business have increased considerably not only for big businesses but also for small ones since they have to hire tax professionals in order to become GST-compliant.

IT Software Expenditure :

Keeping in view the GST regime, all businesses either have to update their current accounting software or ERP software to make it GST compliant or purchase new GST software to prop up their businesses. This results in an increase in IT expenses of the businesses in terms of purchasing the GST software and training the staff to use the software efficiently. However, Masters India, a firm that is one of the leading GST SuvidhaProviders(GSP) has successfully developed customized GST software and APIs in order to ease the compliance procedures for different business users.

Loss in the real estate sector :

The advent of the GST has had a significant impact on the real estate industry. It has resulted in an $8 \%$ increase in real estate prices. This has resulted in a $12 \%$ drop in property demand. However, it is possible that this is a short-term trend that may not persist forever.

Standard Tax Rates and Multiple Rates of CESS :

Instead of a simpler tax system, India's GST Council implemented GST with five standard rates. Many economists believe that this complicates rather than simplifies the structure.

Given India's many states, each had its own challenges with GST rates. Each wants lower rates to be implemented for certain items produced. As a result, the GST Council was forced to introduce numerous tax rates under GST. Furthermore, GST was initially implemented with a tax rate as high as $28 \%$. Despite this, the GST Council has been steadily lowering rates, and most items of daily use now fall into the $0 \%$ to $5 \%$ tax category.

Dual Control : 

GST is referred to as a single taxation system, but in reality, it is a dual tax because both the state and the center will collect separate taxes on a single sale and service transaction.

Hurried Implementation of GST :

GST was implemented on July 1, 2017, in the midst of the fiscal year. This made it difficult for firms to swiftly transition to a new tax framework. Following the prior regime's tax laws for the first quarter of 2017 and sticking to the newly implemented GST for the remaining quarters posed compliance challenges.
