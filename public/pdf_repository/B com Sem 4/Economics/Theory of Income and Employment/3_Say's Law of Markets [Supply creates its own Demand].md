
J. B. Say (1767-1822) a French economist propounded a principle of markets in a dogmatic form, according to which 'supply creates its own demand.' This means that whenever any product is produced, the demand
for that product is also simultaneously genereated on account of or through the process of payment of remuneration to the factors of production. In other words, every output produced results in an equivalent demand being generated which leads to its sale so that there is no surplus output or over production. Say's law is applicable both in the barter economy as well as in the money economy.

**(a) Say's Law in Barter Economy :**

It is easy to understand how Say's law holds good in a barter economy where an individual receives goods in exchange for goods. In such an economy. people undertake to produce goods either to consume or to exchange them for other goods which they need for consumption. In any case, they create a demand for goods which is equal to the supply of goods they have produced. According to Say, it is production which creates market for goods; no sooner a product is created, from that instant it affords a market for other products to the full extent of its value Thus, a producer himself becomes a consumer of his own product or of some body else's product. In the words of J. S. Mill 'all sellers are inevitably and by the meaning of the word buyers'

**(b) Say's Law in a Money Economy :**

The existence of money does not alter the basic law of markets. In a money economy, people receive money in exchange for goods which they have produced. The money income they receive equals the value of goods which they have produced. This implies that the supply of a product, through the process of production, generates necessary income- accruing to the factors of production in the form of rent, wages, interest, profits, etc. to demand the goods produced so that an equivalent demand is created in accordance with the supply. It has been conceived here that the main source of demand is the flow of factor incomes generated from the process of production itself.

However, in a money economy, it may be that people may not spend all the income which they have received, they may decide to save a part of their income. But according to classical writers, the interest rate mechanism would ensure the conversion of saving into investment to ensure full employment. Thus, for example, if people decide to save more than before, total savings in the economy would increase leading to a fall in interest rate which would encourage entrepreneurs to invest more. Investment, thus will increase until it becomes equal to saving. It follows, that if the rate of interest is flexible, total saving will always be equal to total investment. the equality of saving and investment implies that the part of income which is not spent on consumption goods by the people gets spent on investment goods by the entrepreneurs. Thus, according to classical economists, if the
rate of interest is flexible, economy can always operate at full employment irrespective of any level of saving.

# Assumption of Say's Law

The main assumptions of Say's law of market are as follows :
(a) Perfect competition prevails in the market. The price mechanism in the free market economy brings about automatic adjustment between supply and demand, savings and investment, etc. The 'invisible hand' of the market acts as a "supreme equilibrator" in a free economy.
(b) Demand expands as and when production and supply expands; thus demand is a function of supply. Supply creates its own demand.
(c) No rational individual holds idle cash balances, because money is just a medium of exchange.
(d) Saving and investment is a single continuous process. All savings are channelised into investment, via, changes in the rate of interest. Equality of saving and investment is brought about through changes in the rate of interest.
(e) The government follows the policy of laissez-faire and does not interfere in the working of the market forces.

# Criticisms of Say's Law
The main criticisms of Say's Law are, as follows :
(i) The assumption of free competition is unrealistic. Modern economy lacks smooth adjustment. Existence of monopolies, trade unions, market imperfections, etc. prevent price and wage flexibility and consequently automatic adjustment.
(ii) According to Keynes, saving and investment is not a continuous process, a part of the saving may simply be hoarded, that is, it may be diverted to satisfy the liquidity preference of the people.
(iii) Equilibrium between saving and investment is not brought about by changes in the rate of interest but through changes in the level of income as pointed out by Keynes. Besides, investment is not so very sensitive to changes in the rate of interest, it is mainly influenced by the marginal efficiency of capital.
(iv) Keynes has also criticised the classical concept of full employment equilibrium. He does not accept that price or wage or interest flexibility can ensure full employment equilibrium. According to him, equilibrium can be attained at several points below full
employment. In fact his concept of "under - employment equilibrium" is an important contribution to monetary theory.
