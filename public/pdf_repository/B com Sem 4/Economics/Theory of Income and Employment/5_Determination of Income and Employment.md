
[Theory of Effective Demand] :

According to Keynes, income depends on employment, that is to say, income is a function of employment. If employment increases, income also increases and if employment decreases, income also decreases. Keynes has explained this with the help of his concept of effective demand. Broadly speaking, effective demand is the total demand for goods and services by the people at a particular time. In a money economy, this effective demand indicates spending of income or flow of expenditure. The flow of expenditure, in turn, determines the flow of income, as one man's expenditure becomes the income of another. In other words, the total expenditure indicates effective demand.

Now, this total expenditure or aggregate expenditure comprises of two elements : (a) Consumption expenditure (b) Investment expenditure.

According to Keynes, as income increases, consumption expenditure also increases, but after a certain point it does not increase in the same proportion as increase in income. In other words, beyond a certain stage, as income increases, consumption expenditure increases, but at a diminishing rate, and hence, there arises a gap between income and consumption which may be bridged by investment expenditure. If investment expenditure does not increase to this extent, the total expenditure in the economy will be less and so the effective demand will be low leading to unemployment in the economy. In other words, employment depends on investment and this is the crux of the theory of effective demand. In order to increase employment, effective demand should be increased by increasing investment.

# [5.1] Theory of Effective Demand
As stated above, effective demand refers to the total demand for goods and services by the people at a particular time. According to Keynes, this effective demand is the sole determinant of employment and as such it is an important element of the theory of employment. Effective demand is determined by two factors :
(i) Aggregate Demand Price (ii) Aggregate Supply Price.

(i) Aggregate Demand Price (ADP):

Aggregate demand price is a schedule of various amounts of money which the entrepreneurs in an economy expect from the sale of their output at varying levels of employment. It refers to the receipts which the entrepreneurs taken together expect from the sale of output. Thus, the aggregate demand price represents the receipts of the entrepreneurs in an economy.

Let us explain it in detail. We know that the aim of the entrepreneurs is to earn maximum profits and so they have to consider the receipts or the income which they will get by selling their output. This income depends on the demand for goods at a particular price. Now, the income which the entrepreneurs expect from the sale of their output at a certain level of employment is known as aggregate demand price. It is obvious, therefore, that the expected income of the entrepreneurs or the aggregate demand price depends on the expected expenditure of the people on various goods and commodities, that is, how much people spend on these goods and services. Thus, as employment increases, aggregate demand price also increases because with an increase in employment, production increases and so the aggregate demand price also increases.

(ii) Aggregate Supply Price (ASP):

Aggregate supply price is a schedule of the various amounts of money which the entrepreneurs in an economy must receive from the sale of output at varying levels of employment. Aggregate supply price, thus, represents the costs.

Let us explain it in detail. For producing goods and commodities, the entrepreneurs have to make investment and make payments to the factors of production. The price which is paid to the factors is generally known as cost of production. Every enterpreneur will expect at least a minimum income from his sales so as to cover his cost of production. In other words, at a particular level of employment, the entrepreneur must get a minimum income form the sale of his output or else he will stop production. This expectation of minimum sale price is known as the aggregate supply price. Thus, the aggregate supply price is the minimum income which an entrepreneur must get to provide a certain level of employment.

Like the aggregate demand price, the aggregate supply price also increases with an increase in employment, but there is a difference. Aggregate demand price indicates expected income or receipts of the entrepreneur; aggregate supply price on the other hand, increases more or less at a constant rate. It is only in the long run that it shows wide changes; in the short run, however, increase in employment brings about increase in aggregate supply price at the same rate.

It should now be clear that the aggregate supply price representing the costs must in no case be more than the aggregate demand price representing the receipts. If at any particular level of employment, the entrepreneurs find that the receipts are less than their costs, they shall stop production and refuse to offer more employment to that particular number of workers. However, so long as the costs remain less than the receipts, the employment in the economy shall go on increasing till both the costs and the receipts, that is the aggregate supply price and the aggregate demand price are equalised.

The whole proposition can, thus, be summarised as follows :
Employment depends upon effective demand. Effective demand in turn is governed by aggregate demand price and aggregate supply price. Effective demand is the point where the two are equal. Under - employment Equilibrium : 

As said above, at point E , the aggregate demand price is equal to aggregate supply price and the economy is in equilibrium. But this does not mean that the economy is having full employment at this point. The economy can be in equilibrium even before full employment, that is, at a point of less than full employment.

According to classical economists, the economy attains equilibrium only at a level of full employment but according to Keynes the economy can be in equilibrium at different levels of employment.

It should be noted that in his General Theory, Keynes has assumed the aggregate supply price as given, In the short run, the aggregate supply price remains constant and therefore, it is the aggregate demand price which acts as an important determinant of employment. If aggregate demand price falls, employment also falls and if it rises, employment also rises. This will be clear from a study of following diagram :

Thus, according to Keynes, the economy can attain equilibrium at a point of less than full employment and this is known as underemployment equilibrium. Normally, the point of intersection of effective demand indicates under employment equilibrium. It is of course, true that aggregate demand price and aggregate supply price can be equal at full employment, but the condition is that the investment demand should be high enough to fill the gap between consumption and income to attain full employment. But according to Keynes, the typical investment demand is generally inadequate to fill the gap between the two and hence the economy operates at a point of less than full employment or at under employment equilibrium.
