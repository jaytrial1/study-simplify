Keynes first introduced the concept of consumption function in 1936 to indicate the relationship between consumption and income. The consumption function or the propensity to consume denotes a functional relationship between consumption and income, where consumption is a dependent variable and income an independent variable. It may be expressed as :

$$
C=f(Y)
$$

Where $C$ is consumption, $Y$ is income and $f$ denotes "a function of " or "depends upon."

Thus, consumption is a function of income or depends on income.
It may, however be noted here that when we speak of consumption function, we refer to aggregate macro function, that is, consumption function for the whole economy.

Consumption function is also alternatively known as propensity to consume, as it shows consumption at different levels of income. Thus, "the whole schedule relating to consumption at various levels of income is what Keynes calls "propensity to consume or simply the consumption function."

The consumption function enables us to measure the changes in consumption due to changes in income. Hence it is a strategic macrorelationship. As savings represent income which is not consumed, saving function is a complement of the consumption function.
