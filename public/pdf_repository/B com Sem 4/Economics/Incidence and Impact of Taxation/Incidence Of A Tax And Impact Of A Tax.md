# 2_Incidence of a Tax and Impact of a Tax
It would be here interesting to note the difference between incidence of a tax and impact of a tax. The impact of a tax is said to be on the person who pays the tax in the first instance, that is to say, the impact of a tax is on the person who is legally liable to pay the tax and therefore may be known as 'defacto tax-payer', but the incidence is said to fall on a person who finally bears the direct money burden of a tax and therefore he may be finally bears the direct money burden of a tax and therefore he may be

known as 'de dejure tax-payer'. Incidence, thus, refers to the location of the direct money burden of a tax-the ultimate point of resting place of a tax.

It would thus be clear from the above discussion that the 'incidence' and the 'impact' of a tax may not fall on the same person. Impact refers to the original money burden of a tax, while incidence refers to the ultimate money burden of a tax. Thus, for example, when the government levies an excise duty on say sugar, it is paid in the first instance by the mill owner. Thus, the impact of sugar duty is on the mill owner, but this does not mean that the incidence of this duty will also fall on the mill owner. The mill owner will pass on the burder of the excise duty on to the wholesale trader who in turn will pass on to the retail trader and ultimately the burden will be passed on to the consumer in the form of higher prices of sugar. Thus, the impact of the excise duty on sugar is on the mill owner while its incidence is on the consumer.

## Incidence of a Tax and Effects of a Tax
Incidence of a tax is also different from effects of a tax. Tax incidence, as said above, refers to the direct money burden of a tax; here we study on whom the direct money burden of the tax falls. But the term effects of taxation is used in a much wider sense. The effects of a tax refer to incidental results of the tax. There are several consequences of the imposition of a tax which are quite distinct from the problem of incidence. Thus, for example, when the excise duty is levied on sugar, its direct money burden, no doubt, falls on the consumer, but besides that there are several other effects of this excise duty. Thus, for example, a duty on suger by raising its price may lead to fall in its consumption and concequently its production. A fall in production may lead to fall in employment and income, while reduction in consumption may mean sacrifice on the part of consumers and some loss of economic welfare. All these are the effects of a tax - which is a much broader term and includes all economic and non- economic effects arising out of the imposition of a tax. The incidence of a tax, on the other hand, is a narrow and special problem of finding who ultimately bears the direct money burden of a tax.

## Formal Incidence and Effective Incidence
Mrs. Ursula Hicks has sought to explain the difference between tax incidence and effects of a tax through her new concepts of formal incidence' and 'effective incidence'.

Formal Incidence :

According to Mrs. Hicks, formal incidence means the direct money burden of a tax. It is measured in terms of money paid by the tax payers to the government under the provisions of the tax laws of the country.

Effective Incidence : 

Effective incidence means the over-all economic repercussions of a particular tax on consumption and production of a commodity on which the tax is levied, besides its effects on the distribution of income in the society.

## Incidence of a Tax and Shifting of a Tax
Incidence of a tax is also different from shifting of a tax. According to Musgrave shifting refers to the process by which the direct money burden of a tax is pushed, through price adjustments, from the point of impact to the final resting place. In other words, shifting means the process of transfer, that is, the passing of the tax from one who pays it to the one who finally bears it. It is through this process of shifting that the incidence of a tax comes finally to rest some where. Thus, the process by which the direct money burden of a tax is passed from the producer down to the ultimate consumer is known as shifting.

The burden of an indirect tax can be shifted in two ways :
(i) Forward Shifting (ii) Backward Shifting
(i) Forward Shifting :

In forward shifting, the burden of tax is pushed forward from the main producers or manufacturers further down to the final consumer in the chain of adjustment. This is done by raising the market price of the commodity. Thus, for example, when the producer shifts the burden of the tax on to the trader and the trader in turn passes on the burden to the final consumer in the form of rise in price, it is a case of forward shifting.
(ii) Backward Shifting :

In the case of backward shifting, the incidence is pushed back on to the producers by the traders and consumers. Thus, for example, if the commodity taxed has a relatively elastic demand, it may not be possible for the traders to pass on the tax burden on to the consumers. In such cases, the trader would try to push back or shift back the burden of the tax to the producer. He will pressurise the producer to reduce the price of the commodity and if the producer reduces the price under the pressure of the trader, the tax burden inevitably will fall on him. This, then, is a case of backward shifting.

Whether there would forward shifting of a tax or backward shifting depends mainly on the nature of the demand for the product. Generally speaking it may be said that, if the demand for the product is relatively inelastic, the burden of the tax would be shifted forward on to the consumers. On the other hand, if the demand for the product is relatively elastic, the burden of the tax would be shifted backward on to the

producer. Forward shifting, however, is more common than backward shifting. Of course, in certain cases, the burden of the tax may be shifted both forward as well as backward. Thus, for example, if the tax is levied on a producer, he may partly shift it forward by raising the price of the product and partly shift it backward by cutting down the remuneration payable to the various factors of production.

Forms of Shifting : 

A trader can shift the burden of a tax on to the consumer mainly in two ways :
(a) By raising the price of the product in such a case; the entire burden shall fall on the consumer.
(b) In case the producer or the trader finds it difficult to raise the price of the product, he shall try to lower the quality of the product or its size and thus pass on the inferior stuff or small size of the product.

## Shifting of a Tax and Evasion of a Tax
Tax shifting is different from tax evasion or tax avoidance.In the case of tax shifting, the tax payer - the producer or the trader - pays the tax to the government, but shifts the burden on to the consumers. As against this, tax evasion means escaping from the tax by concealing correct statements of accounts. Tax evasion is illegal and is resorted to by understating or concealing tax liability. Tax avoidance, on the other hand refers to avoiding the payment of a tax by not purchasing a taxed product or by not earning more.

It can then be said that the shifting of a tax depends upon the pricing process. And pricing, as we know, is a function of demand and supply forces. It is from this stand point that we consider the modern theory of tax incidence as an integral part of the theory of pricing.
