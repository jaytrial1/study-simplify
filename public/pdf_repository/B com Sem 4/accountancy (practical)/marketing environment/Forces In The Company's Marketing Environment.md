3\. FORCES IN THE COMPANY'S MARKETING ENVIRONMENT:

The company's marketing environment is made up of forces which affect the company's capacity to achieve its goals and develop its capacity to maintain relations and transactions with the consumers.

These forces are of two categories:

(A) Micro Environment

(B) Macro Environment

The figure shown above describes both the forces.

(A) Micro or Internal Environment:

1\. The businessmen supplying material to the company Suppliers

2\. The Company itself.

3\. Marketing intermediaries.

4\. Customers

5\. Competitors

6\. Public

(B) Macro or External Environment:

1\. Demographic

2 Economic

3\. Physical or Natural

4\. Technological

5\. Political and Legal

6\. Social and Cultural

(A) Micro Environment Forces :\*

These forces are as under:

1\. Company: The company creates a separate department for marketing which prepares all the strategies for marketing. It is responsible for the development of new products or new brands. The top managers of the company decide the goal, main strategy and policies of the company. The marketing manager takes decisions keeping the same into consideration. The marketing manager is supposed to work in cooperation with other departmental heads. Such other departmental heads are Finance Manager, Research and Development Manager, Production Manager, Accountant etc. All these departments affect the strategies and functions of the marketing department. Coordination among all these is essential. All of these departments have an impact on the marketing department's plans and actions.

2\. Suppliers: The business companies and individuals supplying materials and other necessary equipment are the suppliers. They include electric power provider Electricity company, raw material suppliers, packing material suppliers etc. All these make a notable impact on the company's marketing. e.g. if raw material prices increase, then the marketing department has to increase its product prices; hence the importance of supply planning is increasing.

3\. Marketing Intermediaries: Marketing intermediaries are those that help the company in promoting, selling and distributing the goods to final buyers. Such intermediaries are of two types: Agent middlemen and merchant middlemen... The agent intermediaries only do the job of bringing customers while merchant intermediaries are wholesalers, retailers etc. Such middlemen car function better than the company in the field. Such intermediaries develop utility of place, time, volume and ownership. It is not easy to select such intermediaries and get the work done through them. It is only because of this rather than working with such small multiple intermediaries; it is preferable to work with big intermediaries who are in small numbers. For all these marketing managers have to learn how to get work from and with such intermediaries.

4\. Customers: The company works with its suppliers and intermediaries in such a manner that it can provide necessary goods and services to the customers of the targeted market efficiently. There are five types of such targeted markets. They are: Consumers' market, Industrial markets, Resale markets, Government and non-profitable organisations' markets and international markets. Each type of customer has certain special characteristics. The marketing manager of the company has to formulate policy and decide strategy keeping all these into consideration.

5\. Competitors: The company is not all alone in its customers' market to sell goods or services. There are a host of other competitors present in the market. The marketing manager has to know such competitors. He is further supposed to study the strategy of competitors, the needs of customers and with all these he has to procure larger market share simultaneously. For this not only the present market has to be explored and developed but also the present product has to be developed and grown so as to ensure more and more customers to be tempted to use the same.

6\. Public: Barring competitors there are other parties also which affect the company's market. Hence, companies are keeping the public relation department which remains in contact with all these parties and try to keep them satisfied. Such parties are: (1) Common public, whose likes and dislikes etc. about company's product are important for the company. (2) The financial institutions like banks, insurance companies, share brokers' firms etc. who can help the company in raising the funds. (3) Public media, whercin specifically newspapers, magazines, television channel owners etc. are included. The marketing manager has to maintain good relations with all these. (4) Government is an important force as a company is supposed to follow statutes, its rules and legal procedures. (5) Vigilant parties, who give critical review of the company's product and its policies. (6) Lastly, the internal public, i.e. company's executives and employees, with whom also it is necessary to maintain good relations.

(B) Macro Environment Forces :\*

We have seen forces which can affect a single company and it is important for the company while macro environment forces are such that it affects the entire economy and its business units. Such forces are as under:

1\. Demographic Forces Population is one of the important macro environment forces. The market is formed of people. The marketing manager has to study demographic forces. (1) Marketing managers have to keep in mind factors of total population. He has to consider its expansion and density of population. (2) He has to keep into consideration age distribution. (3) Birth rate, marriage rate and death rate are also important. (4) Within different countries and within the country, what types of religious groups prevail is important for the marketing manager.

2\. Economic Forces: Economic forces are such that it depicts purchasing power.and pattern of spending for purchases. A marketing manager is interested in people and their purchasing power. The purchasing power is dependent on a lot of factors as narrated below.

(a) Present income, (b) changing income, (c) method of spending by the customers for purchasing and changes into the same, (d) savings rate of the society, (e) increase or decrease into savings rate, (f) expenditure for livelihood, (g) savings quantum, (h) rate of interest etc.

Hence, the marketing manager is supposed to take care of all these factors while preparing a marketing strategy.

3\. Natural Environment: Natural atmosphere is an important force for the business. Many businesses generate pollution. Pollution can be air, water and noise. If managers do not take care of all these, then a situation may arise that business has to be closed down. In India now High Courts and Supreme Court also compel the businessmen or managers to close down business for pollution causing.

Over and above this: (a) If there is shortage of raw materials, then the matter is serious for the marketing manager. (b) Increasing fuel cost is also a point of worry, e.g. hike of prices of petroleum products disturbs the harmony of business. International hike in petroleum prices per barrel of crude oil may adversely affect business parameters. (c) The increasing pollution rate at times provides a bigger market to the industry manufacturing pollution control equipment (d) At last Government steps against pollution also affect the business. Its impact is notable.

4\. Technological Forces: The technological forces are having a notable impact on peoples' life. With the development of new technology certain old businesses have closed down and new businesses have developed. The effect on the economy is dependent on how fast the technological changes take place. The means of luxury available today were not available 100 years ago. (1) Marketing managers have to consider technological changes because it has a direct impact over the consumption pattern of people. Accordingly, marketing strategy may change. (2) Because of fast changing technology every company has to keep the research and development department and a special budget is allocated for that. (3) The company which cannot allocate a large share has to keep on making minor changes. (4) The changes in technology affect the natural environment, human life and its privacy. As a result, the government has to put certain restrictions. The Marketing Manager has to keep on studying all these matters.

5\. Political and Legal Factors: Political environment has a notable impact over the marketing decisions. It includes government government laws, government institutions and social pressurising groups. (1) For the last few years, the statutes restricting business activity have come into force. Especially laws protecting consumers are higher in it. To protect society against the mal practices of the businessmen, the statutes are formulated. The marketing manager has to study such statutes effective at national level or state level. For the implementation of different laws, different institutions are coming into establishment. Out of which the activity of some is directly affecting marketing. Certain institutions remain busy in making money rather than implementing the statutes. At certain places to protect the interest of the public the citizen councils are created. It has a notable impact on the strategy of the marketing manager.

6\. Cultural Forces: The society to which people belong are influenced by its basic beliefs and values. (1) Each society has certain basic beliefs. e.g. In India people spend precious time regularly in performing religious rites (seva-puja) and then behave dishonestly in daily activity. They keep on practising tricks to earn by cheating others. Still however certain beliefs go on changing. Hence marketing managers should try to change that and increase the sale of their own products.
