2\. CONCEPT OF PRODUCT:

(a) Meaning of Product: There are various synonyms for the word

'product' such as a commodity, goods service etc. To clarify the meaning of the product, we shall look at its various definitions.

(1) In common usage. 'Anything that is capable of satisfying human needs is a product.'

(2) In simple terms, 'Product is what a seller has to sell and what a buyer has to buy.'

(3) In economic parlance, 'Production means creation of utility through transformation of a material into product, i.e. making cloth out of raw cotton, utensils out of metal and furniture out of wood. Thus, to create utility is to create a product.'

(4) According to W. Alderson, 'Product is a bundle of utilities, consisting of various product features and accompanying services".\*

(5) In the words of Philip Kotler, "A product is anything that can be offered to a market for attention, acquisition, use or consumption; that might satisfy a want or need, it includes physical products, services, persons, places, organizations and ideas." \+

(6) William J. Stanton is of the opinion that, "Product is a complex of tangible and intangible attributes, including packaging, colour, price, manufacturer's prestige, retailer's prestige and retailer's services which buyer may accept as offering satisfaction of wants or needs."

It follows that 'product' is a term which is substituted for the term 'goods' or 'commodity'. Some prefer to use the word 'production' in place of product. In any case, a product is a bundle of utilities which enable the consumers to satisfy their needs. It is rightly said by someone that, 'It is utility, not product, which is sold and bought.’  
(b) Characteristics of Products: Following are the main characteristics of the product:

(1) Creation of Utility: A product is a result of the transformation activity. Utility is created by changing the form of an article and it satisfies human needs. Thus, creation of utility is an important feature of a product.

(2) Addition of Value: When a raw-material is transformed into a final product, it becomes consumable, and hence it commands a greater value than the raw material. For instance, the value of cloth is greater than that of raw cotton used in making it. The factors of production like land, labour, capital and entrepreneurship can be easily rewarded for their services out of this positive difference of value.

(3) Use of Factors of Production By the factors of production, we mean inputs or resources which are used in making a final product. In Economics, these factors are divided into four groups: (i) land, (ii) labour, (iii) capital and (iv) entrepreneurship. It is the entrepreneur who brings land, labour and capital under the same roof, combines them and produces a commodity.

(4) Commodity or Service: A product is either material or immaterial. In other words, goods and services both are included in the definition of product. The service supplied by an electricity company, gas company and post and telegraph departments is also regarded as a product.

(5) Process Production is a process which is divided into various stages. The input has to pass from a number of stages before it turns into output. For instance, in between raw cotton and cotton shirts are such processes or stages as spinning, weaving, dyeing, bleaching, stitching etc.

(C) Classification of Products: Products available in the markets are divided into two groups (1) Industrial products and (2) Consumer products.

1\) Industrial Products: These are the products which help in producing other goods and services. Industrial products are defined by the American Marketing Association as "goods or products, which are destined to be sold primarily for use in producing other goods or rendering services, as opposed with goods destined to be sold primarily to the utility-mate consumer.

Following goods are included in the definition of industrial products:

(i) Big, expensive and long lasting machines which do not enter into the final product.

(ii) Ancillary equipment or apparatus like lathes. drills, lift trucks etc. which do not enter into the final product.

(iii) Raw-materials which are processed and become a part of the final product.

(iv) Component parts.

(v) Industrial services which smoothen mechanical processes.

(2) Consumer Products: These products are meant for final use or consumption. Consumer products are defined by the American Marketing Association as products intended for use by ultimate household consumers for personal, non-business purposes. The customer derives satisfaction or utility by consuming these products, e.g. cloth, furniture, radio, foodgrains, television sets, refrigerators, ballpen, shoes, medicine, bicycle, scooters etc. The other important feature of these products is that they are used by persons or families.
