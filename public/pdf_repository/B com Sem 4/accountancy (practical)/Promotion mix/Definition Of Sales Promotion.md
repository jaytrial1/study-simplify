8\. DEFINITION OF SALES PROMOTION:

To grasp the meaning of the term sales promotion, let us examine some of its definitions.

(1) According to A. H. R. Delens, sales promotion means. "any steps that are taken, for the purpose of obtaining or increasing sales. Often, this term refers specially to selling efforts that are designed to supplement personal selling and advertising and, by coordination, help to become effective."\!

(2) More clear is the definition given by Harold Whitehead. Sales promotion includes the activities of providing information to wholesalers, retailers, actual buyers, potential buyers and firm's own salesmen. Its object is to make retailer's job easier by arousing a desire for the product among buyers, to help firm's own salesmen in preparing their briefs and to make dealers better businessmen.2

(3) Sales promotion is defined by the American Marketing Association to mean, "those marketing activities other than personal selling, advertising and publicity, that stimulate consumer purchasing, and dealer effectiveness, such as display, shows and expositions, demonstrations and various no- recurrent selling efforts not in the ordinary routine."

Thus, following points are implicit in sales promotion activities   
(i) Its basic objective is to increase sales through special efforts. (ii) These are the activities through which the efforts of the sales personnel, advertising department, distributors and dealers are supplemented, co-ordinated and made more effective. (iii) It encourages consumers to be enthusiastic in purchase by other means also.

Sales promotion efforts grow more intense with the intensification of competition in the product market. In India, the Sunlight Soap colour contest organised by the Hindustan Lever Limited, the prize contest organised by the manufacturers of incense sticks in Ahmedabad, prize contest from the manufacturers of cold drinks, 'Made for each other' contest from the cigarette manufacturing companies, 'Man of the Series' contest from the Wills Cigarette Company are some of the examples of sales promotion activities. These efforts strengthen salesmen's enthusiasm and zeal, encourage dealers to increase the stock of the product concerned and make the product more popular among the people. Yet these efforts cannot take the place of advertising and other marketing activities. They cannot arrest the constantly declining sales. Repeated offers of prizes and gifts cannot attract the 'people to buy again and again. And there are also the efforts of the competitors in the same direction. Hence these efforts may have a casual impact on sales, but they cannot be expected to affect sales favourably every time.
