8\. BEHAVIOURISTIC BASES:\*

In behaviouristic segmentation, the major criteria are occasion rate of consumption, product loyalty, firm loyalty, place faculty, product benefits, user status, readiness to buy and attitudes.

(1) Occasions Occasions give rise to need for products. On certain occasions, demand for certain products moves up. It is therefore possible to distinguish buyers according to occasions when they need a certain product. For example, (i) Demand for air-plane, S. T. Bus and train services expand considerably during vacations. (ii) There is a heavy rush at bus service and suburban trains at office hours. (iii) There is brisk demand for stationery and books when schools and colleges reopen. (iv) The period during which marriages take place in large number is also a period of high demand for clothes, jewellery, stainless steel utensils, flowers and bouquets, services of the music parties and music bands etc. (v) In summary, demand for ice cream and cold drinks shoots up. (vi) In winter, tea and coffee are in demand particularly at morning hours.

(2) Rate of Consumption: Market segmentation is possible on the basis of the rate of consumption of a product also. For example, there can be such groups of buyers as those who consume less, those who consume moderate amounts and those who consume considerably large amount of the product.

(3) Product Loyalty: Some buyers insist on consuming a definite product. all the time. For example, some are loyal to Lux soap, while some are fond of Hamam soap. Some people are loyal to more than one product. The people who shift from Lux to Hamam, and vice versa according to their convenience belong to this group. There are buyers who show no loyalty to any brand. These buyers provide a good market to the producers desirous of introducing new products.

Firm Loyalty: Some buyers show loyalty to a definite firm or company instead of a definite product. These buyers insist on using only that product which the firm of their choice puts in the market. For instance, the fans of the Godrej company prefer to use its cithol soap. If this company introduces a new talcum powder, it will be bought by this group of buyers without hesitation. If the Godrej Company introduces a refrigerator in the market, they will buy it gladly.

Place Loyalty: It is found that some customers insist on buying a product belonging to a definite place only. They prefer, for example, shoes made in Kanpur, wool of Kashmir, hosiery of Ludhiana, Crockery of Than, watches of Morbi and ghee of Jamkhambhalia. Hence, new producers select those areas to set up production units which have carned prestige in the product in question. This is the reason why the number of watch making factories has increased in Morbi recently. Sometimes, the producers exploit the name of such famous areas to promote the sales of their products.

(4) Product Benefits: A powerful form of market segmentation is the classification of customers according to the benefits that they seek from the product. The customers purchase a product on the expectation of certain benefits from its consumption. The Colgate tooth-paste has captured a large market perhaps because of the fact that its benefits such as decay prevention and removal of bad smell etc. are forcefully pointed out in its advertisements. The producers of other brands of tooth-paste are also compelled to offer such benefits to consumers. It is found that each benefit- seeking group possesses certain common characteristics. For example, decay prevention seekers generally have large families. The producer should decide the benefit which he wants to offer and produce a brand that delivers this benefit. The Balsara company, which makes Promise toothpaste, gave priority to the removal of toothache and succeeded in its attempt to attract the customers with large family to its product.

(5) User Status Market can be segmented into different groups of buyers according to their status as consumers. For example, there can be such groups as non-users, ex-users, potential users, first-time users, and regular users of a product. The producers can concentrate on each group's status as consumers. For instance, high-market-share companies are interested in converting potential users into actual users. The small firms try to get users of rival firm's brands to switch to their own brands. Potential users and regular users require different marketing approaches.

(6) Readiness to Buy Actual purchase depends on whether people are ready to buy or not. On the basis of the stage of readiness to buy a product, customers can be grouped into such classes as unaware buyers, aware buyers, interested buyers, desirous buyers and potential buyers. The marketing programme must be adjusted to the stage of readiness of buyers. It should aim at converting the potential buyers into intending buyers.

(7) Attitudes: A market can be divided on the basis of customers' attitudes into such groups as enthusiastic buyers, positive byers, indifferent buyers, negative buyers and hostile buyers. The marketing strategy should be strong enough to convert indifferent buyers into enthusiastic buyers.
