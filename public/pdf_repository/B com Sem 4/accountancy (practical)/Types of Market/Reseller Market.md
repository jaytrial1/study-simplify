4\. RESELLER MARKET:\*

"The reseller market consists of all the individuals and organisations that acquire goods for the purpose of reselling or renting them to others at a profit."

Among resellers, wholesalers and retailers are included. They themselves do not manufacture the goods but after buying from producers they sell those goods to ultimate consumers.

The resellers buy many products for reselling. Especially barring products which are preferred for direct selling by the producers, they (resellers) sell all other products Thus most of the products reach the hands of the end consumers through such intermediaries.

The resellers are supposed to solve certain issues which are as under:

1\. What assortment to carry? (How many products to be kept for reselling?)  
For this there are four alternatives available:

1\. Exclusive assortment: Carrying the line of only one producer. e.g. to sell only television sets of Videocon Company only. 2\. Deep assortment:I Carrying a product family in depth. i.e. to keep the particular product of all the producers. e.g. a particular businessman may decide to sell T.V. of all the t producers. 3\. Broad assortment: Carrying several product lines that fall within the normal scope of the reseller's type of business. e.g. to keep for sale C

all the products of the same line like T.V., V.C.R., Video Camera, Cassette etc. 4\. Scrambled assortment: Carrying many unrelated product lines. e.g. to sell stove, refrigerator, crockery, toys etc.

II. Who participates in the reseller buying process ?  
In small firms the function of buying is directly under the control of the proprietor himself. The firms making business on a very large scale employ whole time employees with specialised knowledge. Different methods of buying are seen in departmental stores, supermarkets etc. In super markets many times one expert purchaser is appointed and one purchase committee helps him. Sometimes, the purchase officer is vested with such an authority that he can discuss with the salesman or producer and decide to purchase or reject the product. While in certain stores his authority is limited to submit the report for this to the purchasing committee. It is the job of marketing manager to decide the person who has the required authority to purchase and by contacting him he has to ensure that he takes the decision in his favour.

III. Major influences on the reseller's buying:  
We have seen previously that a number of factors influencing buyers in the market do influence the reseller's buyers. e.g. Environmental, organisational, interpersonal and individual factors etc.

At this stage we should know which types of buyers are resellers. According to Dickinson such buyers are of seven types:  
Loyal Buyers who are generally loyal to a source year after year.  
Opportunistic Buyers who generally enter into contract with those who do the same in their favour.  
Advertising Buyer who attempts to obtain advertising money from the producer as a part of every deal.  
Creative Buyer: This buyer tells the seller what he wants in the way of product, service and prices.  
The Chiseler Buyer: This buyer always negotiates extra concessions in price.  
Best Deal Buyer: This buyer selects the best deal available at the time.  
Nuts and holts buyer This buyer selects the merchandise offering the best value.

IV. How do resellers make their buying decisions ?  
The resellers put the order of the standard product as per routine, once the stock level reaches reorder level. Still however if the selling expense increases then he will try to get further discount from the producer. This is because in certain products the rate of commission is hardly 1% to 2% which is eroded if the administrative expense increases.

Now, they are trying to develop buying skills. They forecast the demand. Which product should be provided how much space? They adopt methods like inventory control. Thus producers are supposed to work with such resellers who are becoming more and more organised.

GOVERNMENT MARKET:\*

In the Government market, all the government units like Central government, State government and local authority are included, who are buying necessary material for observing government duties. According to one estimate the American Government had bought goods worth 90,000 crore dollars in the year 1987\. (Meaning thereby purchase worth 32,40,000 crore Indian rupees.) It is almost equal to 30% of the gross national product. Thus, the Government is the biggest customer in the country. In India the government spends about Rs. 75,000 crores of rupees after defence equipment. Thus, main purchase of government is of defence equipments

Who is covered in the government market ? Among the government buyers; government departments, Boards, (Railway board, Gujarat Electricity Board), Commission (Forward market commission), Municipality, Nagar Panchayat, Gram Panchayat etc. are included. All these institutions delegate authority to certain executives and form a purchase committee for purchases above a certain amount. The marketing manager has to understand characteristics of these people and their buying methods and then try to contact them.

The government buying is made by two methods:

1\. Open Bid Buying and 2\. Negotiated Contracts.

Most of the purchases are made through bidding. In this method the government gives advertisements and asks for bids. Therein the item to be purchased is described in detail. The lowest quote wins the contract of the bid. The supplier has to think about whether he would be in a position to provide the material as per description and quantity mentioned. For standard product there is no question, but for specialised product all this is important

The purchase through a negotiated contract is for a specific project. For this, the government enters into negotiations with the companies having specialised knowledge. Then it determines the price. This type of contract can be cost plus pricing, fixed price or fixed price and incentive. At times. Such contracts are based on cost. There are provisions of automatic escalation in case of price rise. In America, in the area of defence, large construction projects in the areas of science, astrological research etc. negotiated contracts are preferred. Similarly, if contracts are very large then the small companies are given sub-contracts.

The special feature of the government market is that there is hardly any marketing skill needed. There is little scope for development of such markets. How much funds are to be deployed is decided by ministers and government executives. Hence, there is no question of developing this type of market through advertisement such markets cannot be developed.

Still however, many companies maintain separate departments for the government companies. Such companies forecast estimates for government needs and projects. It collects all the information including competition. Then they participate in tendering. They try to create a good impression about the company before government executives.
