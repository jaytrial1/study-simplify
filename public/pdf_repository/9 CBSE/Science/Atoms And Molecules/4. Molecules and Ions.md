This section defines molecules and ions, explaining how atoms combine to form these particles and how they constitute matter. It covers molecules of elements, molecules of compounds, and the formation of ions.

# Molecules and Ions

## What is a Molecule?

A **molecule** is generally a group of two or more atoms that are chemically bonded together by attractive forces. A molecule is the smallest particle of an element or a compound that can exist independently and retains all the properties of that substance. Atoms of the same element or different elements can join to form molecules.

## Molecules of Elements

Molecules of an element are made up of the same type of atoms. Molecules of some elements, like argon (Ar) and helium (He), consist of only one atom and are called **monoatomic**. However, many nonmetals exist as molecules containing multiple atoms. For example, an oxygen molecule consists of two oxygen atoms ($\mathrm{O}_{2}$) and is called a **diatomic** molecule. If three oxygen atoms combine, they form ozone ($\mathrm{O}_{3}$). The number of atoms constituting a molecule is called its **atomicity**.

Metals and some other elements like carbon do not form simple molecules but consist of a large, indefinite number of atoms bonded together in complex structures.

## Molecules of Compounds

Atoms of different elements combine in definite proportions to form molecules of compounds.

### Table: Molecules of some compounds

| Compound                          | Combining Elements   | Ratio by Mass |
| :-------------------------------- | :------------------- | :-----------: |
| Water ($\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{O}$) | Hydrogen, Oxygen     |      1:8      |
| Ammonia ($\mathrm{NH}_{3}$)       | Nitrogen, Hydrogen   |     14:3      |
| Carbon dioxide ($\mathrm{CO}_{2}$) | Carbon, Oxygen       |      3:8      |

### Activity: Finding the Ratio by Number of Atoms

Refer to the table for the ratio by mass of atoms in compounds and the table for atomic masses. Find the ratio by number of atoms of elements in the molecules given in the table above.

The ratio by number of atoms for a water molecule can be found as follows:

| Element | Ratio by mass | Atomic mass (u) | Mass ratio / atomic mass | Simplest ratio |
| :-----: | :-----------: | :-------------: | :----------------------: | :------------: |
|    H    |       1       |        1        |     $\frac{1}{1}=1$      |       2        |
|    O    |       8       |       16        |    $\frac{8}{16}=\frac{1}{2}$    |       1        |

Thus, the ratio by number of atoms for water is H:O = 2:1.

## What is an ION?

Compounds formed from metals and nonmetals typically contain charged particles called **ions**. Ions are single charged atoms or groups of atoms with a net charge. A negatively charged ion is an **anion**, and a positively charged ion is a **cation**. For example, sodium chloride (NaCl) is made of positively charged sodium ions ($\mathrm{Na}^{+}$) and negatively charged chloride ions ($\mathrm{Cl}^{-}$). A group of atoms carrying a charge is known as a **polyatomic ion**.
