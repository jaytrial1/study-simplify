# Introduction to Atoms and Early Ideas about Matter

Ancient Indian and Greek philosophers pondered the unknown and unseen form of matter. The concept of matter's divisibility was considered in India around 500 BC. An Indian philosopher, Maharishi Kanad, proposed that dividing matter (*padarth*) would yield smaller and smaller particles, eventually reaching a stage of the smallest, indivisible particles, which he named *Parmanu*. Another Indian philosopher, Pakudha Katyayama, expanded on this, suggesting these particles normally exist in combined forms, creating various types of matter.

Around the same time, ancient Greek philosophers Democritus and Leucippus also suggested that matter could be divided until an indivisible particle was reached. Democritus called these indivisible particles *atoms* (meaning indivisible). These ideas were based purely on philosophical thought, with little experimental validation until the eighteenth century.

By the end of the eighteenth century, scientists had distinguished between elements and compounds and became interested in *how* and *why* elements combine and the results of such combinations.

Antoine L. Lavoisier is credited with laying the groundwork for chemical sciences by establishing two crucial laws of chemical combination.
