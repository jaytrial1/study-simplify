This section details Newton's universal law of gravitation, describing how the force between two objects depends on their masses and the distance between them.

# The Universal Law of Gravitation

Every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that follows a specific rule:

*   The force is **directly proportional** to the **product of their masses**.
*   The force is **inversely proportional** to the **square of the distance** between them.

This force acts along the line connecting the centers of the two objects.

Let's consider two objects, A and B, with masses $M$ and $m$, separated by a distance $d$. The force of attraction $F$ between them is:

$F \propto M \times m$

$F \propto \frac{1}{d^2}$

Combining these proportionalities, we get:

$F \propto \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$

Introducing a constant of proportionality, G, we get the formula for the Universal Law of Gravitation:

$F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$

Here, G is known as the **universal gravitation constant**. Its value was determined by Henry Cavendish and is accepted as $6.673 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$.

The SI unit of G is $\mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$.

The term "inverse-square" means that if the distance ($d$) between two objects increases by a certain factor, the gravitational force ($F$) decreases by the square of that factor. For example, if the distance doubles (factor of 2), the force becomes $1/2^2 = 1/4$ times smaller. If the distance triples (factor of 3), the force becomes $1/3^2 = 1/9$ times smaller.

This law is called universal because it applies to all objects, regardless of their size or whether they are on Earth or in space.

### Example: Gravitational Force between Earth and Moon

Calculate the force exerted by the Earth on the Moon given:
Mass of Earth ($M$) = $6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$
Mass of Moon ($m$) = $7.4 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~kg}$
Distance between Earth and Moon ($d$) = $3.84 \times 10^5 \mathrm{~km} = 3.84 \times 10^8 \mathrm{~m}$
Universal Gravitation Constant ($G$) = $6.7 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$

Using the formula $F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$:

$F = \frac{6.7 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2} \times 6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg} \times 7.4 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~kg}}{(3.84 \times 10^8 \mathrm{~m})^2}$
$F = 2.02 \times 10^{20} \mathrm{~N}$

The force exerted by the Earth on the Moon is approximately $2.02 \times 10^{20} \mathrm{~N}$.

### Questions

1. State the universal law of gravitation.
> **Answer:** The universal law of gravitation states that every object in the universe attracts every other object with a force that is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them. The force is along the line joining the centers of the two objects.

2. Write the formula to find the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and an object on the surface of the earth.
> **Answer:** The formula is $F = G \frac{M \times m}{R^2}$, where $F$ is the gravitational force, $G$ is the universal gravitation constant, $M$ is the mass of the Earth, $m$ is the mass of the object, and $R$ is the radius of the Earth (representing the distance between the object and the Earth's center).
