This section defines free fall and introduces the concept of acceleration due to gravity, explaining how it affects falling objects.

# Free Fall and Acceleration due to Gravity

When an object falls towards the Earth under the influence of the gravitational force *alone*, it is said to be in **free fall**.

Consider throwing a stone upwards. It goes up, reaches a peak height, and then falls back down. This downward motion is due to the Earth's gravitational force. While falling, the direction of motion is downwards, but the *magnitude* of the velocity changes due to the Earth's attraction. A change in velocity involves acceleration.

The acceleration experienced by an object falling towards the Earth due to gravitational force is called the **acceleration due to gravity**. It is denoted by the symbol $g$. The unit of $g$ is the same as acceleration, which is $\mathrm{m} \mathrm{s}^{-2}$.

According to Newton's second law ($F=ma$), the gravitational force ($F$) acting on an object of mass ($m$) during free fall is $F = m \times g$.

We also know from the universal law of gravitation that the force between the Earth (mass $M$) and the object (mass $m$) at a distance $d$ is $F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$.

Equating these two expressions for force:
$m \times g = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$

We can cancel the mass of the object ($m$) from both sides:
$g = G \frac{M}{d^2}$

For objects on or very near the surface of the Earth, the distance $d$ is approximately equal to the Earth's radius ($R$). So, for objects on the surface:
$g = G \frac{M}{R^2}$

This equation shows that the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) is independent of the mass of the falling object. This means that all objects, regardless of their mass or composition, should fall at the same rate in a vacuum.

The Earth is not perfectly spherical; its radius is slightly larger at the equator than at the poles. Since $g$ is inversely proportional to the square of the radius, the value of $g$ is slightly greater at the poles than at the equator. However, for most calculations near the Earth's surface, $g$ is considered approximately constant.

### Calculating the value of $g$

Using the accepted values for G, M, and R:
$G = 6.7 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$
$M$ (Mass of Earth) = $6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$
$R$ (Radius of Earth) = $6.4 \times 10^6 \mathrm{~m}$

$g = \frac{6.7 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2} \times 6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}}{(6.4 \times 10^6 \mathrm{~m})^2}$
$g \approx 9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$

The average value of acceleration due to gravity on the Earth's surface is approximately $9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.

### Motion of Objects Under Gravity

Since the acceleration due to gravity ($g$) is nearly constant near the Earth's surface, the equations of motion for uniformly accelerated motion can be applied to objects in free fall. We replace the acceleration $a$ with $g$.

The equations become:
*   $v = u + gt$
*   $s = ut + \frac{1}{2} gt^2$
*   $v^2 = u^2 + 2gs$

Here, $u$ is initial velocity, $v$ is final velocity, $s$ is displacement, and $t$ is time.

When applying these equations:
*   Take $g$ as positive when acceleration is in the direction of motion (e.g., falling downwards).
*   Take $g$ as negative when acceleration opposes the motion (e.g., moving upwards).

### Activity: Paper vs. Stone

Dropping a sheet of paper and a stone from the same height shows the stone hits the ground first. This is because of **air resistance**, which affects the larger surface area of the paper more than the compact stone. In a vacuum (where there is no air), both would fall at the same rate, as demonstrated by Galileo's experiments (according to a story) from the Leaning Tower of Pisa.

### Example: Falling Car

A car falls off a ledge and drops to the ground in 0.5 s. Let $g=10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.
(i) What is its speed on striking the ground?
(ii) What is its average speed during the 0.5 s?
(iii) How high is the ledge from the ground?

Given: $t = 0.5 \mathrm{~s}$, $u = 0 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$ (starts from rest), $a = g = +10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$ (downward motion).

(i) Using $v = u + at$:
$v = 0 + (10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2})(0.5 \mathrm{~s})$
$v = 5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$

(ii) Using average speed = $(u+v)/2$:
Average speed = $(0 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} + 5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}) / 2$
Average speed = $2.5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$

(iii) Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2} at^2$:
$s = (0 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1})(0.5 \mathrm{~s}) + \frac{1}{2} (10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2})(0.5 \mathrm{~s})^2$
$s = 0 + \frac{1}{2} (10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2})(0.25 \mathrm{~s}^2)$
$s = 1.25 \mathrm{~m}$

Results: (i) Speed on striking ground = $5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$, (ii) Average speed = $2.5 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$, (iii) Height of ledge = $1.25 \mathrm{~m}$.

### Example: Object Thrown Upwards

An object is thrown vertically upwards and rises to a height of 10 m. Calculate (i) the velocity with which it was thrown and (ii) the time taken to reach the highest point.

Given: $s = 10 \mathrm{~m}$, $v = 0 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$ (at highest point), $a = g = -9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$ (upward motion).

(i) Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
$(0 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1})^2 = u^2 + 2(-9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2})(10 \mathrm{~m})$
$0 = u^2 - 196 \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$
$u^2 = 196 \mathrm{~m}^2 \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$
$u = \sqrt{196} \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$
$u = 14 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$

(ii) Using $v = u + at$:
$0 = 14 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} + (-9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2})t$
$9.8t = 14$
$t = \frac{14}{9.8} \mathrm{~s}$
$t \approx 1.43 \mathrm{~s}$

Results: (i) Initial velocity = $14 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}$, (ii) Time to reach highest point = $1.43 \mathrm{~s}$.

### Questions

1. What do you mean by free fall?
> **Answer:** Free fall refers to the motion of an object falling towards the Earth under the influence of only the Earth's gravitational force, with no other forces (like air resistance) acting on it.

2. What do you mean by acceleration due to gravity?
> **Answer:** Acceleration due to gravity is the acceleration experienced by an object when it is in free fall near the Earth's surface. It is caused by the Earth's gravitational attraction and is denoted by $g$.
