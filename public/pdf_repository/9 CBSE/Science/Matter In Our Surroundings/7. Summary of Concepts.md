# Summary of Key Concepts

Here is a summary of the main points learned in this chapter:

*   Matter is composed of small particles.
*   Matter around us exists in three primary states: solid, liquid, and gas.
*   The forces of attraction between particles are strongest in solids, intermediate in liquids, and weakest in gases.
*   The spaces between particles and the kinetic energy of particles are smallest in solids, intermediate in liquids, and largest in gases.
*   The arrangement of particles is most ordered in solids. In liquids, particles are in layers that can slide over each other. In gases, particles move randomly with no fixed order.
*   The states of matter can be inter-converted by changing temperature or pressure.
*   *Sublimation* is the direct change from solid to gas.
*   *Deposition* is the direct change from gas to solid.
*   *Boiling* is a bulk phenomenon where particles from the entire liquid change to vapour at the boiling point.
*   *Evaporation* is a surface phenomenon where particles from the liquid surface change to vapour at any temperature below the boiling point.
*   The rate of evaporation is influenced by surface area, temperature, humidity, and wind speed.
*   Evaporation causes cooling.
*   *Latent heat of vaporisation* is the energy needed to change 1 kg of liquid to gas at its boiling point at atmospheric pressure.
*   *Latent heat of fusion* is the energy needed to change 1 kg of solid into liquid at its melting point at atmospheric pressure.
