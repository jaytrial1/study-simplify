This section introduces evaporation as a surface phenomenon where liquid changes to vapour below its boiling point and discusses the factors affecting its rate and how it causes cooling.

# Evaporation

Do we always need to heat or change pressure to change the state of matter? Can you think of examples from everyday life where a liquid changes into vapour without reaching its boiling point? Water left uncovered slowly turns into vapour. Wet clothes dry up. What happens to the water in these cases?

We know that particles of matter are always in motion and never at rest. At any given temperature, in any state of matter (gas, liquid, or solid), there are particles with varying amounts of kinetic energy. In liquids, a small fraction of particles at the surface have higher kinetic energy than others. These energetic surface particles are able to overcome the forces of attraction from surrounding particles and escape into the vapour state. This process, where a liquid changes into vapour at any temperature below its boiling point, is called *evaporation*.

## Factors Affecting Evaporation

Let's understand this with an activity.

### Activity: Factors Affecting Evaporation Rate

*   Take 5 mL of water in a test tube and place it near a window or under a fan.
*   Take 5 mL of water in an open china dish and place it near a window or under a fan.
*   Take 5 mL of water in an open china dish and place it inside a cupboard or on a shelf in your class.
*   Record the room temperature.
*   Record the time or days it takes for the water to evaporate completely in each case.
*   Repeat these steps on a rainy day and record your observations.
*   What do you infer about the effect of temperature, surface area, and wind velocity (speed) on evaporation?

> **Answer:**
> *   The water in the open china dish near a window/fan evaporates fastest.
> *   The water in the test tube near a window/fan evaporates slower than the china dish but faster than the cupboard.
> *   The water in the china dish inside the cupboard evaporates slowest.
> *   On a rainy day (higher humidity), evaporation is generally slower in all cases.
> *   From this, we infer that the rate of evaporation increases with:
>     *   Increasing surface area (china dish vs. test tube).
>     *   Increasing wind speed (near fan/window vs. cupboard).
>     *   Increasing temperature (implied by comparing sunny vs. rainy day conditions, though room temp is constant in this specific activity unless varied).
>     *   Decreasing humidity (rainy day vs. normal day).

You must have observed that the rate of evaporation increases with:

*   **An increase of surface area:** Evaporation is a surface phenomenon. A larger surface area exposed to the atmosphere means more particles at the surface can escape into the vapour state, thus increasing the rate of evaporation. For example, we spread clothes out when drying them.
*   **An increase of temperature:** Higher temperature means more particles gain enough kinetic energy to overcome the forces of attraction and transition into the vapour state, increasing the evaporation rate.
*   **A decrease in humidity:** Humidity is the amount of water vapour in the air. Air can only hold a limited amount of water vapour at a given temperature. If the air is already high in humidity, it holds less capacity for additional water vapour, so the rate of evaporation decreases.
*   **An increase in wind speed:** On a windy day, clothes dry faster. Increased wind speed carries away the water vapour particles from the surface of the liquid, decreasing the amount of water vapour in the surroundings. This helps to increase the rate of evaporation.

## How Does Evaporation Cause Cooling?

In an open container, liquid continuously evaporates. The particles of the liquid absorb energy from the surroundings to compensate for the energy they lose during evaporation. This absorption of energy from the surroundings makes the surroundings feel cool.

What happens when you pour some acetone (nail polish remover) on your palm? The acetone particles quickly gain energy from your palm or the surroundings and evaporate. This process draws heat away from your palm, causing it to feel cool.

After a hot sunny day, people often sprinkle water on the roof or open ground. The water evaporates, and the large amount of latent heat of vaporisation absorbed from the hot surface helps to cool it down.

Can you provide more examples from daily life where we experience cooling due to evaporation?

> **Answer:** Examples include:
> *   Sweating: When sweat evaporates from our skin, it absorbs heat from our body, causing us to feel cool.
> *   Water kept in an earthen pot (matka): Water seeps through the pores of the pot and evaporates from the outer surface, taking heat from the remaining water inside, thus cooling it.
> *   Wearing cotton clothes in summer: Cotton absorbs sweat, which then evaporates from the fabric surface, providing a cooling effect.
> *   Cooling effect of a desert cooler: A desert cooler works by evaporating water; the air passing through wet pads loses heat to the evaporating water and becomes cool.

Why should we wear cotton clothes in summer?

During summer, we sweat more as our body's mechanism to stay cool. Evaporation is the process where liquid particles at the surface gain energy from the surroundings or body surface and change into vapour. Heat energy equivalent to the latent heat of vaporisation is absorbed from the body, leaving the body cool. Cotton is a good absorber of water (sweat), helping to absorb the sweat and expose it to the atmosphere for easier evaporation, thereby enhancing the cooling effect.

Why do we see water droplets on the outer surface of a glass containing ice-cold water?

If we take a glass with ice-cold water, we soon observe water droplets on its outer surface. This happens because the water vapour present in the air surrounding the cold glass comes into contact with the cold surface. The cold surface causes the water vapour to lose energy and condense into liquid water droplets, which appear on the outside of the glass.

### Questions

1.  Why does a desert cooler cool better on a hot dry day?

> **Answer:** A desert cooler works by evaporating water. Evaporation is faster on a hot day (higher temperature) and a dry day (lower humidity). Increased evaporation rate leads to more absorption of heat from the surrounding air (latent heat of vaporisation), resulting in more effective cooling.

2.  How does the water kept in an earthen pot (matka) become cool during summer?

> **Answer:** Earthen pots have tiny pores in their walls. Water seeps through these pores to the outer surface of the pot. This water then evaporates from the surface. Evaporation requires energy, which is absorbed from the water remaining inside the pot, thus cooling the water.

3.  Why does our palm feel cold when we put some acetone or petrol or perfume on it?

> **Answer:** Acetone, petrol, and perfume are volatile liquids, meaning they evaporate quickly. When placed on the palm, their particles rapidly gain energy from the palm (which is part of the surroundings) to overcome the forces of attraction and change into vapour. This rapid absorption of heat from the palm causes the palm to feel cold.

4.  Why are we able to sip hot tea or milk faster from a saucer rather than a cup?

> **Answer:** A saucer has a larger surface area compared to a cup. Increasing the surface area increases the rate of evaporation. Faster evaporation from the saucer cools the hot tea or milk more quickly than in a cup, making it possible to sip it faster without getting burned.

5.  What type of clothes should we wear in summer?

> **Answer:** We should wear cotton clothes in summer. Cotton is a good absorber of sweat, and because it allows sweat to evaporate easily from its surface into the atmosphere, it helps to cool the body. Also, light-coloured and loose-fitting clothes are preferable as they absorb less heat and allow for better air circulation, promoting evaporation.
