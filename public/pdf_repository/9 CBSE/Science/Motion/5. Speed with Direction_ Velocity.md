# Speed with Direction: Velocity

The rate of motion of an object can be more comprehensive if we specify its direction of motion along with its speed. The quantity that specifies both these aspects is called *velocity*. Velocity is the speed of an object moving in a definite direction.

The velocity of an object can be uniform or variable. It can be changed by changing the object's speed, direction of motion or both. When an object is moving along a straight line at a variable speed, we can express the magnitude of its rate of motion in terms of *average velocity*. It is calculated in the same way as we calculate average speed:

$$
\text { average velocity }=\frac{\text { Total displacement }}{\text { Total time taken }}
$$

In case the velocity of the object is changing at a uniform rate, then average velocity is given by the arithmetic mean of initial velocity and final velocity for a given period of time. That is,

$$
\text { average velocity }=\frac{\text { initial velocity }+ \text { final velocity }}{2}
$$

Mathematically, $v_{av}=\frac{u+v}{2}$

where $v_{av}$ is the average velocity, $u$ is the initial velocity and $v$ is the final velocity of the object.

Speed and velocity have the same units, that is, m s⁻¹ or m/s.

## Activity

Measure the time it takes you to walk from your house to your bus stop or the school. If you consider that your average walking speed is 4 km h⁻¹, estimate the distance of the bus stop or school from your house.
> **Answer:** To estimate the distance, you would use the formula: Distance = Average Speed × Time. Convert your measured time into hours (if measured in minutes or seconds) and multiply it by the average speed (4 km/h). For example, if it takes you 15 minutes (0.25 hours), the estimated distance is 4 km/h * 0.25 h = 1 km.

## Activity

At a time when it is cloudy, there may be frequent thunder and lightning. The sound of thunder takes some time to reach you after you see the lightning. Can you answer why this happens? Measure this time interval using a digital wrist watch or a stop watch. Calculate the distance of the nearest point of lightning. (Speed of sound in air = 346 m s⁻¹).
> **Answer:** This happens because light travels much faster than sound. You see the lightning almost instantaneously, but the sound of thunder takes a noticeable amount of time to travel the distance from the lightning strike to your location. To calculate the distance, measure the time interval (t) between seeing the lightning and hearing the thunder. The distance (s) can then be calculated using the formula: Distance = Speed of sound × Time interval. So, s = 346 m/s * t.

## Questions

1. Distinguish between speed and velocity.
> **Answer:** Speed is the rate of motion and is a scalar quantity (has only magnitude). Velocity is the rate of motion in a specific direction and is a vector quantity (has both magnitude and direction).

2. Under what condition(s) is the magnitude of average velocity of an object equal to its average speed?
> **Answer:** The magnitude of average velocity is equal to its average speed when the object moves along a straight line in a single direction without changing its direction. In this case, the magnitude of displacement is equal to the total distance traveled.

3. What does the odometer of an automobile measure?
> **Answer:** The odometer of an automobile measures the total distance traveled by the vehicle.

4. What does the path of an object look like when it is in uniform motion?
> **Answer:** When an object is in uniform motion, its path is a straight line.

5. During an experiment, a signal from a spaceship reached the ground station in five minutes. What was the distance of the spaceship from the ground station? The signal travels at the speed of light, that is, 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.
> **Answer:**
> Time taken (t) = 5 minutes = 5 * 60 seconds = 300 s.
> Speed of the signal (v) = 3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.
> Distance (s) = Speed × Time
> s = (3 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹) × (300 s)
> s = 900 × 10⁸ m
> s = 9 × 10¹⁰ m
> The distance of the spaceship from the ground station was 9 × 10¹⁰ meters.

### Example 7.2

The odometer of a car reads 2000 km at the start of a trip and 2400 km at the end of the trip. If the trip took 8 h, calculate the average speed of the car in km h⁻¹ and m s⁻¹.

#### Solution:

Distance covered by the car, $s = 2400 \text{ km} - 2000 \text{ km} = 400 \text{ km}$
Time elapsed, $t = 8 \text{ h}$
Average speed of the car is,

$$
\begin{aligned}
v_{av} & =\frac{s}{t}=\frac{400 \mathrm{~km}}{8 \mathrm{~h}} \\
& =50 \mathrm{~km} \mathrm{~h}^{-1}
\end{aligned}
$$

To convert km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹:
1 km = 1000 m
1 hour = 3600 seconds

$$
\begin{aligned}
50 \mathrm{~km} \mathrm{~h}^{-1} & =50 \frac{\mathrm{~km}}{\mathrm{~h}} \times \frac{1000 \mathrm{~m}}{1 \mathrm{~km}} \times \frac{1 \mathrm{~h}}{3600 \mathrm{~s}} \\
& =\frac{50 \times 1000}{3600} \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} \\
& =\frac{500}{36} \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1} \\
& =13.89 \text{ m s}^{-1} \text{ (approximately)}
\end{aligned}
$$

The average speed of the car is 50 km h⁻¹ or approximately 13.89 m s⁻¹.

### Example 7.3

Usha swims in a 90 m long pool. She covers 180 m in one minute by swimming from one end to the other and back along the same straight path. Find the average speed and average velocity of Usha.

#### Solution:

Total distance covered by Usha in 1 min is 180 m.
Total time taken = 1 minute = 60 seconds.

Average speed = $\frac{\text{Total distance covered}}{\text{Total time taken}} = \frac{180 \text{ m}}{60 \text{ s}} = 3 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.

Displacement of Usha in 1 min: She starts at one end, swims to the other end (90 m), and swims back to the starting end (another 90 m). Her final position is the same as her initial position.
Displacement = 0 m.

Average velocity = $\frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Total time taken}} = \frac{0 \text{ m}}{60 \text{ s}} = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.

The average speed of Usha is 3 m s⁻¹ and her average velocity is 0 m s⁻¹.
