This section explains how to plot and interpret velocity-time graphs for uniform and accelerated motion, and how the area under the graph represents displacement.

# Velocity-Time Graphs

The variation in velocity with time for an object moving in a straight line can be represented by a velocity-time graph. In this graph, time is represented along the $x$-axis and the velocity is represented along the $y$-axis.

*Image Description: A velocity-time graph with time on the x-axis (h) and velocity on the y-axis (km/h). A horizontal straight line is shown at 40 km/h from time t1 to t2. The rectangular area under this line between t1 and t2 is shaded and labeled ABDC.*

Fig. 7.5: Velocity-time graph for uniform motion of a car

If the object moves at uniform velocity, the height of its velocity-time graph will not change with time (Fig. 7.5). It will be a straight line parallel to the $x$-axis. Fig. 7.5 shows the velocity-time graph for a car moving with uniform velocity of 40 km h⁻¹.

We know that the product of velocity and time give displacement of an object moving with uniform velocity. The area enclosed by velocity-time graph and the time axis will be equal to the magnitude of the displacement.

To know the distance moved by the car between time $t_1$ and $t_2$ using Fig. 7.5, draw perpendiculars from the points corresponding to the time $t_1$ and $t_2$ on the graph. The velocity of 40 km h⁻¹ is represented by the height AC or BD and the time $(t_2 - t_1)$ is represented by the length CD.

So, the distance $s$ moved by the car in time $(t_2 - t_1)$ can be expressed as

$$
\begin{aligned}
s & = \text{AC} \times \text{CD} \\
& = [ (40 \text{ km h}^{-1}) \times (t_2 - t_1) \text{ h} ] \\
& = 40(t_2 - t_1) \text{ km} \\
& = \text{area of the rectangle ABDC (shaded in Fig. 7.5).}
\end{aligned}
$$

We can also study about uniformly accelerated motion by plotting its velocity-time graph. Consider a car being driven along a straight road for testing its engine. Suppose a person sitting next to the driver records its velocity after every 5 seconds by noting the reading of the speedometer of the car. The velocity of the car, in km h⁻¹ as well as in m s⁻¹, at different instants of time is shown in table 7.3.

You can also determine the distance moved by the car from its velocity-time graph. The area under the velocity-time graph gives the distance (magnitude of displacement) moved by the car in a given interval of time. If the car would have been moving with uniform velocity, the distance travelled by it would be represented by the area ABCD under the graph (Fig. 7.6). Since the magnitude of the velocity of the car is changing due to acceleration, the distance $s$ travelled by the car will be given by the area ABCDE under the velocity-time graph (Fig. 7.6).
That is,
$s =$ area ABCDE
$=$ area of the rectangle ABCD $+$ area of the triangle ADE

$$
=\mathrm{AB} \times \mathrm{BC}+\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{AD} \times \mathrm{DE})
$$

> **Answer:**
> **Graph (a):** This graph shows that the velocity is decreasing linearly with time. This represents motion with *uniform deceleration* or *uniform negative acceleration*.
> **Graph (b):** This graph shows that the velocity is changing in a non-linear way over time. The slope (which represents acceleration) is changing constantly. This represents motion with *non-uniform acceleration*. The object is speeding up, slowing down, and speeding up again at varying rates.

## Activity

The times of arrival and departure of a train at three stations A, B and C and the distance of stations B and C from station A are given in Table 7.4. Plot and interpret the distance-time graph for the train assuming that its motion between any two stations is uniform.

## Table 7.4: Distances of stations B and C from A and times of arrival and departure of the train

| Station | Distance <br> from A <br> (km) | Time of <br> arrival <br> (hours) | Time of <br> departure <br> (hours) |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| A | 0 | 08:00 | 08:15 |
| B | 120 | 11:15 | 11:30 |
| C | 180 | 13:00 | 13:15 |

> **Answer:**
> To plot the graph:
> - Use time on the x-axis and distance from A on the y-axis.
> - Plot points for the train's position at the departure time from each station: (08:15, 0 km), (11:30, 120 km), (13:15, 180 km).
> - Since the motion between stations is assumed uniform, draw straight lines connecting these points.
> - Also, represent the time spent at each station (layover) as a horizontal line segment at the station's distance. For example, at station B, a horizontal line would be drawn from 11:15 to 11:30 at the 120 km mark.
>
> Interpretation:
> - The segments connecting the departure points will be straight lines with positive slopes, indicating uniform speed between stations.
> - The slope of the line segment between A and B represents the train's speed from A to B.
> - The slope of the line segment between B and C represents the train's speed from B to C.
> - The horizontal segments represent periods where the train is at rest at a station.
> - The graph will show increasing distance from A over time, with periods of rest at stations B and C.

## Activity

Feroz and his sister Sania go to school on their bicycles. Both of them start at the same time from their home but take different times to reach the school although they follow the same route. ## Questions

1. What is the nature of the distance-time graphs for uniform and non-uniform motion of an object?
> **Answer:** For uniform motion, the distance-time graph is a straight line. For non-uniform motion, the distance-time graph is a curved line.

2. What can you say about the motion of an object whose distance-time graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis?
> **Answer:** If the distance-time graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis, it means the distance of the object from the origin is not changing over time. This indicates that the object is at rest.

3. What can you say about the motion of an object if its speed-time graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis?
> **Answer:** If the speed-time graph is a straight line parallel to the time axis, it means the speed of the object is constant over time. This indicates that the object is moving with uniform speed. If the motion is in a straight line, this also implies uniform velocity.

4. What is the quantity which is measured by the area occupied below the velocity-time graph?
> **Answer:** The area occupied below the velocity-time graph measures the magnitude of the displacement (or distance travelled if motion is in a straight line in one direction) of the object.
