# Improvement in Crop Yields

Cereals (wheat, rice, maize, millets, sorghum) provide carbohydrates for energy. Pulses (gram, pea, black gram, green gram, pigeon pea, lentil) provide protein. Oil seeds (soyabean, ground nut, sesame, castor, mustard, linseed, sunflower) provide fats. Vegetables, spices, and fruits provide vitamins, minerals, and small amounts of other nutrients. Fodder crops (berseem, oats, sudan grass) are raised as food for livestock.

## Questions

1. What do we get from cereals, pulses, fruits and vegetables?

> **Answer:** Cereals provide carbohydrates. Pulses provide protein. Fruits and vegetables provide vitamins and minerals, along with small amounts of proteins, carbohydrates, and fats.

Different crops need specific climatic conditions, temperature, and *photoperiods* (duration of sunlight) for growth and life cycle completion. Plant growth and flowering depend on sunlight for photosynthesis.

Crops are grown in different seasons:
*   **Kharif season:** Rainy season (June to October). Examples: Paddy, soyabean, pigeon pea, maize, cotton, green gram, black gram.
*   **Rabi season:** Winter season (November to April). Examples: Wheat, gram, peas, mustard, linseed.

India has seen a four-fold increase in food grain production from 1952 to 2010 with only a 25% increase in cultivable land. This increase was achieved through various farming practices, which can be divided into three stages:
1.  Choosing seeds for planting.
2.  Nurturing the crop plants.
3.  Protecting growing and harvested crops from loss.

Thus, improving crop yields involves three major groups of activities:
*   Crop variety improvement
*   Crop production improvement
*   Crop protection management.
