# Cropping Patterns

Different methods of growing crops can be used for maximum benefit.

*Mixed cropping* involves growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same piece of land (e.g., wheat + gram, groundnut + sunflower). This reduces risk and provides insurance against the failure of one crop.

*Inter-cropping* is growing two or more crops simultaneously in a definite pattern on the same field, with rows of one crop alternating with rows of another (e.g., soyabean + maize, bajra + cowpea) (Fig. 12.2). Crops are chosen with different nutrient needs to ensure maximum nutrient utilisation. This also helps prevent pests and diseases from spreading easily throughout one crop type. This method can lead to better returns from both crops.

*Crop rotation* is growing different crops on the same land in a pre-planned sequence. The duration and crop combinations vary. Availability of moisture and irrigation influences the choice of the next crop. Proper crop rotation can allow growing two or three crops a year with good harvests.
