# Poultry farming

Poultry farming raises domestic fowl for eggs and meat. Improved breeds are developed for layers (egg production) and broilers (meat).

Cross-breeding Indian (indigenous, e.g., Aseel) and foreign (exotic, e.g., Leghorn) breeds aims for desirable traits:
*   Number and quality of chicks.
*   Dwarf broiler parents for commercial chick production.
*   Tolerance to high temperature (summer adaptation).
*   Low maintenance requirements.
*   Reduced size of egg-laying birds that can use cheaper, fibrous diets from agricultural by-products.

1. Discuss the implications of the following statement:
"It is interesting to note that poultry is India's most efficient converter of low fibre food stuff (which is unfit for human consumption) into highly nutritious animal protein food."

> **Answer:** This statement implies that poultry farming is highly valuable for food security and resource efficiency in India. Poultry birds can consume agricultural by-products and low-fibre feed materials that humans cannot digest or use directly. They then convert these less valuable inputs into high-quality protein (eggs and meat), which is essential for human nutrition. This makes poultry farming an efficient way to produce protein using resources that would otherwise be wasted, contributing significantly to the availability of nutritious food for the population.

## EGG AND BROILER PRODUCTION

*Broiler chickens* are fed vitamin-rich supplements for fast growth and good feed efficiency. Care focuses on minimizing mortality and maintaining feathering and carcass quality for meat production.

Good management practices are vital for poultry production, including maintaining proper temperature and hygiene in housing, providing suitable feed, and preventing/controlling diseases and pests.

Housing, nutrition, and environmental needs differ for broilers and layers (egg producers).

Broilers require a protein-rich diet with adequate fat and high levels of vitamins A and K.

Poultry birds are susceptible to diseases from viruses, bacteria, fungi, parasites, and nutritional deficiencies. Regular cleaning, sanitation, disinfectant spraying, and appropriate vaccination prevent infectious diseases and reduce losses during outbreaks.

## uestions

1. What management practices are common in dairy and poultry farming?

> **Answer:** Common management practices include providing proper shelter and housing, maintaining hygiene and sanitation, ensuring balanced and nutritious feeding, implementing disease prevention measures like vaccination, and controlling pests and parasites.

2. What are the differences between broilers and layers and in their management?

> **Answer:** Broilers are raised for meat, while layers are raised for egg production. Their management differs in feeding and housing. Broilers require protein-rich feed for rapid growth and emphasis is on feed efficiency and meat quality. Layers require feed formulated for egg production, and management focuses on maximizing egg laying duration and quality. Housing and environmental conditions may also be adjusted differently to optimize meat or egg production.
