This section covers the discovery of the third sub-atomic particle, the neutron, and its location within the atom.

## Neutrons

In 1932, **J. Chadwick** discovered another subatomic particle which had no charge and a mass nearly equal to that of a proton. It was eventually named as **neutron**. Neutrons are present in the nucleus of all atoms, except hydrogen (specifically, the most common isotope, protium). In general, a neutron is represented as 'n'. The mass of an atom is therefore given by the sum of the masses of protons and neutrons present in the nucleus.

## Questions

1. Name the three sub-atomic particles of an atom.
> **Answer:** The three sub-atomic particles of an atom are electrons, protons, and neutrons.

2. Helium atom has an atomic mass of 4 u and two protons in its nucleus. How many neutrons does it have?
> **Answer:** The mass number of an atom is the sum of protons and neutrons. For Helium, mass number is 4 u and it has 2 protons. Number of neutrons = Mass number - Number of protons = 4 - 2 = 2. Helium atom has 2 neutrons.
