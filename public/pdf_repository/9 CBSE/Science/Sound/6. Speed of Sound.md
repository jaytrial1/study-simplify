This section defines the speed of sound and its relationship to wavelength and frequency, including a solved example.

# Speed of Sound

The **speed of sound** is defined as the distance which a point on a wave, such as a compression or a rarefaction, travels per unit time.
We know,
speed, $v=$ distance / time

$$
=\frac{\lambda}{T}
$$

Here $\lambda$ is the wavelength of the sound wave. It is the distance travelled by the sound wave in one time period (T) of the wave. Thus,

$$
v=\lambda v\left(\frac{1}{T}=v\right)
$$

or $\quad v=\lambda v$
That is, speed $=$ wavelength $\times$ frequency.
The speed of sound remains almost the same for all frequencies in a given medium under the same physical conditions.

Example 11.1 A sound wave has a frequency of 2 kHz and wave length 35 cm . How long will it take to travel 1.5 km ?

## Solution:

Given,
Frequency, $v=2 \mathrm{kHz}=2000 \mathrm{~Hz}$
Wavelength, $\lambda=35 \mathrm{~cm}=0.35 \mathrm{~m}$
We know that speed, $v$ of the wave
$=$ wavelength $\times$ frequency
$v=\lambda v$
$=0.35 \mathrm{~m} \times 2000 \mathrm{~Hz}=700 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$
The time taken by the wave to travel a distance, $d$ of 1.5 km is
$\mathrm{t}=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{v}}=\frac{1.5 \times 1000 \mathrm{~m}}{700 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-1}}=\frac{1500}{700} \mathrm{~s}=2.14 \mathrm{~s}$ (approx).
Thus sound will take approximately 2.1 seconds to travel a distance of 1.5 km.

## Questions

1. What are wavelength, frequency, time period and amplitude of a sound wave?
> **Answer:**
> - **Wavelength (λ):** The distance between two consecutive compressions or rarefactions of a sound wave.
> - **Frequency (v):** The number of complete oscillations (compressions and rarefactions) of the medium's density or pressure per unit time.
> - **Time period (T):** The time taken for one complete oscillation of the medium's density or pressure.
> - **Amplitude (A):** The magnitude of the maximum disturbance or displacement in the medium on either side of the mean (equilibrium) value.

2. How are the wavelength and frequency of a sound wave related to its speed?
> **Answer:** The speed ($v$) of a sound wave is related to its wavelength ($\lambda$) and frequency ($v$) by the equation: $v = \lambda \times v$.

3. Calculate the wavelength of a sound wave whose frequency is 220 Hz and speed is $440 \mathrm{~m} / \mathrm{s}$ in a given medium.
> **Answer:**
> Given: Frequency ($v$) = 220 Hz, Speed ($v$) = 440 m/s
> Using the relationship $v = \lambda v$, we can find the wavelength ($\lambda$):
> $\lambda = \frac{v}{v} = \frac{440 \mathrm{~m/s}}{220 \mathrm{~Hz}} = 2 \mathrm{~m}$
> The wavelength of the sound wave is 2 meters.

4. A person is listening to a tone of 500 Hz sitting at a distance of 450 m from the source of the sound. What is the time interval between successive compressions from the source?
> **Answer:** The time interval between successive compressions is equal to the time period ($T$) of the wave. The time period is the reciprocal of the frequency ($v$).
> Given: Frequency ($v$) = 500 Hz
> $T = \frac{1}{v} = \frac{1}{500 \mathrm{~Hz}} = 0.002 \mathrm{~s}$
> The time interval between successive compressions from the source is 0.002 seconds. The distance to the source (450 m) is not needed to answer this question.

The amount of sound energy passing each second through unit area is called the **intensity of sound**. We sometimes use the terms "loudness" and "intensity" interchangeably, but they are not the same. Loudness is a measure of the response of the ear to the sound. Even when two sounds are of equal intensity, we may hear one as louder than the other simply because our ear detects it better.

## Question

1. Distinguish between loudness and intensity of sound.
> **Answer:** Intensity of sound is the objective measure of the amount of sound energy passing through a unit area per second. Loudness, on the other hand, is a subjective measure of how the human ear perceives the intensity of sound. Loudness depends on intensity but also on the sensitivity of the ear and frequency.
