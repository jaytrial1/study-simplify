This section explores how energy can change from one form to another and introduces the fundamental law of conservation of energy, stating that total energy remains constant during transformations.

# Energy Transformation and Conservation

### Are various energy forms INTERCONVERTIBLE?

Can energy be converted from one form to another? Yes, nature provides many examples of energy conversion.

## Activity

10.13

- Sit in small groups.
- Discuss the various ways of energy conversion in nature.
- Discuss following questions in your group:
(a) How do green plants produce food?
(b) Where do they get their energy from?
(c) Why does the air move from place to place?
(d) How are fuels, such as coal and petroleum formed?
(e) What kinds of energy conversions sustain the water cycle?

> **Answer:**
> (a) Green plants produce food through **photosynthesis**.
> (b) They get their energy from **sunlight** (light energy).
> (c) Air moves from place to place (wind) because of differences in air pressure caused by uneven heating of the Earth's surface by the Sun. This is a conversion of **heat energy** (from the sun) into **kinetic energy** (of moving air).
> (d) Fuels like coal and petroleum are formed over millions of years from the buried remains of plants and animals. The energy stored in them originally came from sunlight captured by plants through photosynthesis. This is stored **chemical energy**.
> (e) The water cycle involves:
>     *   Evaporation: **Light/Heat energy** (from the Sun) converts into **kinetic energy** of water molecules, changing liquid water to water vapour.
>     *   Condensation: Water vapour cools and condenses into liquid water (clouds), releasing some **heat energy**.
>     *   Precipitation: Water falls.
>     *   Flowing water (rivers): **Gravitational potential energy** (of water at height) converts into **kinetic energy** (of flowing water).

### LAW OF CONSERVATION OF ENERGY

Activities 10.13 and 10.14 show that energy can change form. What happens to the total energy? The **law of conservation of energy** states that whenever energy is transformed, the total energy remains unchanged. Energy can only be converted from one form to another; it cannot be created or destroyed. The total energy before and after transformation is always the same. This law applies in all situations and transformations.

Consider an object of mass, $m$ falling freely from a height, $h$.
*   At the start (height $h$): Potential energy is $mgh$, kinetic energy is zero (velocity is zero). Total energy = $mgh + 0 = mgh$.
*   As it falls: Potential energy decreases, and kinetic energy increases. If $v$ is the velocity at a height $h'$ (where $h'<h$), potential energy is $mgh'$, kinetic energy is $\frac{1}{2}mv^2$. Total energy = $mgh' + \frac{1}{2}mv^2$.
*   Just before hitting the ground (height $h \approx 0$): Potential energy is almost zero, velocity $v$ is highest, kinetic energy is $\frac{1}{2}mv_{max}^2$. Total energy = $0 + \frac{1}{2}mv_{max}^2$.

According to the law of conservation of energy, the total energy is constant at all points during the fall:
Potential energy + Kinetic energy = Constant
$mgh + \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \text{constant}$ (Eq. 10.7)

The sum of an object's kinetic and potential energy is its **total mechanical energy**.

During free fall, the decrease in potential energy at any point equals the increase in kinetic energy (ignoring air resistance). There is a continuous conversion of gravitational potential energy into kinetic energy.

## Think it over !

What would have happened if nature had not allowed the transformation of energy? There is a view that life could not have been possible without transformation of energy. Do you agree with this?

> **Answer:**
> If energy could not be transformed, life as we know it would be impossible. Life processes, like photosynthesis, respiration, movement, and growth, fundamentally rely on converting energy from one form (like light or chemical energy in food) into other forms (like chemical energy in ATP or kinetic energy for movement). Without these transformations, organisms could not obtain, process, or use energy to survive and function. So, yes, it is reasonable to agree that life could not have been possible without energy transformation.
