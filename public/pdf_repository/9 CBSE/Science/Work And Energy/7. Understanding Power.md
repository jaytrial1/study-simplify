This section introduces the concept of power as the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. It defines the unit of power, the watt, and explains the concept of average power.

# Rate of Doing Work

Do people work at the same rate? Do machines transfer energy at the same rate? Agents that transfer energy or do work operate at different rates.

## Activity

- Consider two children, say A and B. Let us say they weigh the same. Both start climbing up a rope separately. Both reach a height of 8 m . Let us say A takes 15 s while B takes 20 s to accomplish the task.
- What is the work done by each?
- The work done is the same. However, A has taken less time than B to do the work.
- Who has done more work in a given time, say in 1 s ?

> **Answer:**
> *   The work done by each child is the same because they both lift their body weight (force) through the same vertical height (displacement). Work = Weight $\times$ Height.
> *   Since A takes less time to do the same amount of work, A has done more work per unit time. In 1 second, A has done a larger fraction of the total work than B. This rate of doing work is called power.

A stronger person or a more powerful vehicle can do work or transfer energy faster. **Power** measures the speed of work done or the rate of energy transfer.
Power is defined as the rate of doing work or the rate of transfer of energy.
If an agent does work $W$ in time $t$, then power $P$ is:

Power $=$ work / time
$P = \frac{W}{t}$ (Eq. 10.8)

The unit of power is the **watt (W)**, named after James Watt. 1 watt is the power of an agent that does work at the rate of 1 joule per second. Power is 1 W when energy is consumed at a rate of 1 J per second.
$1 \text{ watt} = 1 \text{ joule} / \text{second}$ or $1 \mathrm{~W} = 1 \mathrm{~J \ s}^{-1}$.

Larger rates of energy transfer are expressed in kilowatts (kW).
$1 \text{ kilowatt} = 1000 \text{ watts}$
$1 \mathrm{~kW} = 1000 \mathrm{~W}$
$1 \mathrm{~kW} = 1000 \mathrm{~J \ s}^{-1}$

The power of an agent can change over time. In such cases, the concept of **average power** is useful. Average power is the total energy consumed divided by the total time taken.

**Example 10.7** Two girls, each of weight 400 N climb up a rope through a height of 8 m. We name one of the girls A and the other B. Girl A takes 20 s while B takes 50 s to accomplish this task. What is the power expended by each girl?

**Solution:**
Work done by each girl = Force $\times$ displacement = Weight $\times$ Height
Work done, $W = 400 \mathrm{~N} \times 8 \mathrm{~m} = 3200 \mathrm{~J}$.

(i) Power expended by girl A:
Time taken, $t_A = 20 \mathrm{~s}$.
Power, $P_A = \frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Time taken}} = \frac{W}{t_A} = \frac{3200 \mathrm{~J}}{20 \mathrm{~s}} = 160 \mathrm{~J/s} = 160 \mathrm{~W}$.

(ii) Power expended by girl B:
Time taken, $t_B = 50 \mathrm{~s}$.
Power, $P_B = \frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Time taken}} = \frac{W}{t_B} = \frac{3200 \mathrm{~J}}{50 \mathrm{~s}} = 64 \mathrm{~J/s} = 64 \mathrm{~W}$.

Power expended by girl A is 160 W. Power expended by girl B is 64 W. Girl A is more powerful as she does the same work in less time.

**Example 10.8** A boy of mass 50 kg runs up a staircase of 45 steps in 9 s. If the height of each step is 15 cm, find his power. Take $g=10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.

**Solution:**
Mass of the boy, $m = 50 \mathrm{~kg}$.
Weight of the boy, $mg = 50 \mathrm{~kg} \times 10 \mathrm{~m/s^2} = 500 \mathrm{~N}$.
Number of steps = 45.
Height of each step = 15 cm = 0.15 m.
Total height of the staircase, $h = \text{Number of steps} \times \text{Height per step}$
$h = 45 \times 0.15 \mathrm{~m} = 6.75 \mathrm{~m}$.
Time taken, $t = 9 \mathrm{~s}$.

Work done by the boy against gravity = Force $\times$ displacement = Weight $\times$ Total height
Work done, $W = mgh = 500 \mathrm{~N} \times 6.75 \mathrm{~m} = 3375 \mathrm{~J}$.

Power, $P = \frac{\text{Work done}}{\text{Time taken}} = \frac{W}{t} = \frac{3375 \mathrm{~J}}{9 \mathrm{~s}} = 375 \mathrm{~J/s} = 375 \mathrm{~W}$.
The power of the boy is 375 W.

## uestions

1. What is power?
2. Define 1 watt of power.
3. A lamp consumes 1000 J of electrical energy in 10 s . What is its power?
4. Define average power.

> **Answer:**
> 1.  Power is the rate of doing work or the rate of transfer of energy.
> 2.  1 watt (W) is the power of an agent that does 1 joule (J) of work in 1 second (s). ($1 \mathrm{~W} = 1 \mathrm{~J/s}$).
> 3.  Energy consumed, $E = 1000 \mathrm{~J}$.
>     Time taken, $t = 10 \mathrm{~s}$.
>     Power, $P = \frac{\text{Energy consumed}}{\text{Time taken}} = \frac{1000 \mathrm{~J}}{10 \mathrm{~s}} = 100 \mathrm{~J/s} = 100 \mathrm{~W}$.
>     The power of the lamp is 100 W.
> 4.  Average power is defined as the total work done or total energy consumed divided by the total time taken.

## Activity

- Take a close look at the electric meter installed in your house. Observe its features closely.
- Take the readings of the meter each day at 6.30 am and 6.30 pm .
- Do this activity for about a week.
How many 'units' are consumed during day time?
- How many 'units' are used during night?
Tabulate your observations.
- Draw inferences from the data.
Compare your observations with the details given in the monthly electricity bill (One can also estimate the electricity to be consumed by specific appliances by tabulating their known wattages and hours of operation).

> **Answer:**
> This activity involves practical observation and data collection. Students would need to record meter readings at the specified times for a week.
> *   **'Units' consumed during day time:** Calculate the difference between the 6:30 pm reading and the 6:30 am reading for each day and sum these differences over the week.
> *   **'Units' used during night:** Calculate the difference between the 6:30 am reading of a day and the 6:30 pm reading of the previous day and sum these differences over the week.
> *   **Inferences:** Students might observe patterns in energy consumption, such as higher usage during certain times of the day (e.g., evening) or on certain days.
> *   **Comparison:** Comparing with the electricity bill helps understand how the 'units' measured on the meter correspond to the billed consumption and cost. Estimating appliance usage helps correlate specific activities with energy consumption.
>
> (Note: A 'unit' of electricity typically refers to a kilowatt-hour (kWh), which is the energy consumed by a 1 kW device running for 1 hour. 1 kWh = 3.6 million Joules).
