This section provides a concise summary of the main concepts covered in the chapter, including definitions of work, energy, power, and the law of conservation of energy.

# Summary of Key Concepts

-   Work done on an object is the magnitude of the force multiplied by the distance moved by the object in the direction of the applied force.
-   The unit of work is the **joule (J)**: 1 joule = 1 newton $\times$ 1 metre.
-   Work done on an object by a force is zero if the displacement of the object is zero.
-   An object capable of doing work possesses **energy**. Energy has the same unit as work (joule).
-   An object in motion has **kinetic energy**. An object of mass $m$ moving with velocity $v$ has kinetic energy $E_k = \frac{1}{2} m v^2$.
-   Energy possessed by a body due to its position or shape is called **potential energy**. The gravitational potential energy of an object of mass $m$ raised through a height $h$ is $E_p = mgh$.
-   The **law of conservation of energy** states that energy can only be transformed from one form to another; it cannot be created or destroyed. The total energy before and after transformation remains constant.
-   Energy exists in forms like kinetic energy, potential energy, heat energy, chemical energy, etc.
-   The sum of an object's kinetic and potential energies is its **mechanical energy**.
-   **Power** is the rate of doing work. The SI unit of power is the **watt (W)**. $1 \mathrm{~W} = 1 \mathrm{~J/s}$.
