This section introduces the concept of potential energy, focusing on gravitational potential energy due to position and elastic potential energy due to configuration (shape). It provides an equation for gravitational potential energy.

# Potential ENERGY

## Activity

10.8

- Take a rubber band.
- Hold it at one end and pull from the other. The band stretches.
- Release the band at one of the ends.
- What happens?
- The band will tend to regain its original length. Obviously the band had acquired energy in its stretched position.
- How did it acquire energy when stretched?

> **Answer:**
> *   When released, the rubber band snaps back towards its original shape.
> *   It acquired energy when stretched because work was done on it to change its shape. This stored energy, due to its changed shape, is called elastic potential energy.

## Activity

10.10

- Take a toy car. Wind it using its key.
- Place the car on the ground.
- Did it move?
- From where did it acquire energy?
- Does the energy acquired depend on the number of windings?
- How can you test this?

> **Answer:**
> *   Yes, the toy car moved.
> *   It acquired energy from the winding action. Winding tightens a spring inside the toy, storing potential energy in the spring's compressed or twisted state.
> *   Yes, the energy acquired depends on the number of windings. More windings typically store more energy in the spring.
> *   You can test this by winding the car different amounts (e.g., 5 turns, 10 turns, 20 turns) and observing how far the car travels each time. More windings should result in the car traveling a greater distance, indicating more energy was stored and released.

## Activity

10.11

- Lift an object through a certain height. The object can now do work. It begins to fall when released.
- This implies that it has acquired some energy. If raised to a greater height it can do more work and hence possesses more energy.
- From where did it get the energy? Think and discuss.

> **Answer:**
> *   The object got the energy from the work done on it to lift it against the force of gravity. This energy is stored in the object due to its position relative to the ground and is called gravitational potential energy.

In the above situations, energy is stored due to the work done on the object. This stored energy is called **potential energy** if it is not used to change the object's velocity or speed.

Stretching a rubber band transfers energy to it, stored as its potential energy. Winding a toy car key transfers energy to the spring inside, stored as potential energy. The potential energy of an object is the energy it possesses by virtue of its **position** or **configuration** (shape).

### Potential energy of an object AT A HEIGHT

An object increases its energy when raised through a height because work is done on it against gravity. This energy is called **gravitational potential energy**.

The gravitational potential energy of an object at a height above the ground is defined as the work done in raising it from the ground to that height against gravity.

**Image Description**
The image illustrates the concept of potential energy or gravitational potential energy. There is a cube shown in two different positions. One position is on the ground (labeled "Ground") and the other is at a height *h* above the ground. An arrow points upwards from the cube on the ground, implying it could be raised. A dashed line marked *h* shows the height difference between the ground level and the elevated cube. The image essentially shows an object being raised from the ground to a height *h*. This suggests that the cube at height *h* possesses potential energy due to its position relative to the ground.

Let's find an expression for gravitational potential energy. Consider an object of mass, $m$ raised through a height, $h$ from the ground. A force is needed to do this. The minimum force required to lift the object is equal to its weight, $mg$. The object gains energy equal to the work done on it against gravity.
Work done, $W = \text{force} \times \text{displacement}$
$W = mg \times h$
$W = mgh$

Since the work done is $mgh$, the potential energy ($E_p$) gained by the object is:
$E_p = mgh$ (Eq. 10.6)

The potential energy depends on the chosen zero level (like the ground). An object at a certain position has different potential energy values relative to different reference levels.

The work done by gravity depends only on the vertical height difference between the initial and final positions, not on the path taken. Fig. 10.8 shows two paths to raise a block from A to B, a vertical height $h$. In both cases, the work done against gravity is $mgh$.

**Example 10.5** Find the energy possessed by an object of mass 10 kg when it is at a height of 6 m above the ground. Given, $g=9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.

**Solution:**
Mass of the object, $m=10 \mathrm{~kg}$.
Displacement (height), $h=6 \mathrm{~m}$.
Acceleration due to gravity, $g=9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.
From Eq. (10.6),
Potential energy, $E_p = mgh$
$E_p = 10 \mathrm{~kg} \times 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s^2} \times 6 \mathrm{~m}$
$E_p = 98 \mathrm{~N} \times 6 \mathrm{~m} = 588 \mathrm{~N m} = 588 \mathrm{~J}$.
The potential energy is 588 J.

**Example 10.6** An object of mass 12 kg is at a certain height above the ground. If the potential energy of the object is 480 J, find the height at which the object is with respect to the ground. Given, $g=10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.

**Solution:**
Mass of the object, $m=12 \mathrm{~kg}$.
Potential energy, $E_p = 480 \mathrm{~J}$.
Acceleration due to gravity, $g=10 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$.
Using the formula $E_p = mgh$:
$480 \mathrm{~J} = 12 \mathrm{~kg} \times 10 \mathrm{~m/s^2} \times h$
$480 \mathrm{~J} = 120 \mathrm{~N} \times h$
$h = \frac{480 \mathrm{~J}}{120 \mathrm{~N}}$
$h = 4 \mathrm{~m}$.
The object is at the height of 4 m.
