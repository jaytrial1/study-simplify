This section details the negative impacts of mechanization and the post-war economic depression on smaller farmers and agricultural laborers in the USA.

## What Happened to the Poor?

For poorer farmers, machines brought misery. Many bought machines, expecting high wheat prices and profits. If they lacked money, banks offered loans. Those who borrowed struggled to repay debts. Many abandoned their farms and sought jobs elsewhere.

But jobs were scarce. Mechanization reduced the need for labour. The boom of the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries ended by the mid-1920s. After that, most farmers faced trouble. Production had expanded so rapidly during and after the war that there was a large surplus. Unsold stocks piled up, storehouses overflowed, and vast amounts of grain were used as animal feed. Wheat prices fell, and export markets collapsed. This set the stage for the **Great Agrarian Depression of the 1930s**, which ruined wheat farmers everywhere.
