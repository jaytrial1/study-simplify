This chapter introduces the historical experiences of peasants and farmers in different parts of the world as modern agriculture developed and integrated regions into the global capitalist market. It focuses on England, the USA, and India (specifically Bengal) to compare their distinct but sometimes similar histories.

# Peasants and Farmers

In previous chapters, we learned about people who depended on pastures and forests, such as shifting cultivators, pastoral groups, and tribals. We saw how modern governments regulated access to these resources and how this affected the lives of those who used them.

This chapter shifts focus to peasants and farmers in three different countries: small cottagers in England, wheat farmers in the USA, and opium producers in Bengal. We will explore what happens to various rural groups with the advent of modern agriculture and the integration of different world regions into the capitalist market. Comparing these histories reveals differences despite similar underlying processes.

We begin in England, where the agricultural revolution first took place.
