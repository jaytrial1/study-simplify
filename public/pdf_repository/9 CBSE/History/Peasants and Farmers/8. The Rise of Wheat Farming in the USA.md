## The Wheat Farmers

From the late nineteenth century, wheat production in the USA expanded dramatically. The urban population grew, and the export market increased. Rising demand led to higher wheat prices, encouraging farmers to produce more. The spread of railways facilitated transporting grain from wheat regions to the eastern coast for export. Demand grew even higher in the early twentieth century, booming during World War I as Russian wheat supplies were cut off and the USA needed to feed Europe. US President Wilson urged farmers, "Plant more wheat, wheat will win the war."

In 1910, about 45 million acres in the USA were under wheat. Nine years later, this increased to 74 million acres, a 65% rise. Most of this expansion occurred in the Great Plains, where new areas were ploughed. Large farmers, known as **wheat barons**, sometimes controlled 2,000 to 3,000 acres individually.
