# Major Causes of Deforestation Under Colonial Rule

Several factors contributed to the rapid and systematic deforestation during the colonial era.

## Land for Cultivation

Around 1600, about one-sixth of India's land was cultivated. This figure has since risen to roughly half. As the population grew over centuries, increasing the demand for food, peasants expanded cultivation by clearing forests. During the colonial period, cultivation grew even faster for several reasons.

First, the British actively promoted the production of **commercial crops** like jute, sugar, wheat, and cotton. Demand for these crops surged in nineteenth-century Europe to feed growing urban populations and supply raw materials for industries.

## Timber for Railways and Ships

By the early nineteenth century, oak forests in England were vanishing, creating a timber shortage for the **Royal Navy**. Ensuring a steady supply of strong, durable timber was vital for building ships to protect and maintain imperial power. By the 1820s, search parties were dispatched to explore India's forest resources. Within a decade, trees were being felled extensively, and vast quantities of timber were exported from India.

The expansion of **railways** from the 1850s created a new demand for timber. Railways were crucial for colonial trade and troop movements. Wood was needed as fuel for locomotives, and **sleepers** (wooden planks) were essential to hold railway tracks in place. Each mile of track required between 1,760 and 2,000 sleepers.

Adivasis were hired by the forest department to cut trees, and make smooth planks which would serve as sleepers for the railways. At the same time, they were not allowed to cut these trees to build their own houses.

The railway network grew rapidly from the 1860s, reaching about 25,500 km by 1890 and over 765,000 km by 1946. This expansion led to the felling of an increasing number of trees. In the Madras Presidency alone, 35,000 trees were cut annually for sleepers as early as the 1850s. The government issued contracts to suppliers, who often cut trees indiscriminately, causing forests near railway tracks to disappear quickly.

## Plantations

Large areas of natural forests were also cleared to establish **tea, coffee, and rubber plantations**. These were created to satisfy Europe's increasing demand for these commodities. The colonial government acquired forest lands and granted vast tracts to European planters at low prices. These areas were fenced off, cleared of natural forests, and planted with single commercial species like tea or coffee.
