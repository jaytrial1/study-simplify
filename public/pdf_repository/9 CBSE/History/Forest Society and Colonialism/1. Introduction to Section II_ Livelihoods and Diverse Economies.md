# Introduction to Section II: Livelihoods and Diverse Economies

In this section, we will explore livelihoods and economies beyond traditional focus on factories and cities. We will examine how the lives of **forest dwellers**, **pastoralists**, and **peasants** were transformed by the modern world and how they actively shaped these changes.

Often, studies of the modern world concentrate on industrial and agricultural sectors serving the market, neglecting other vital economies and communities. The lives of pastoralists, forest dwellers, **shifting cultivators**, and **food gatherers** might seem disconnected from the contemporary world, as if they are relics of the past. However, this section argues for the necessity of understanding their lives, their world organization, and their economies. These communities are very much part of today's modern world, not merely survivors from a bygone era.

Chapter IV will delve into forests, describing how communities used them in diverse ways. It will show how the growth of industries, urban centers, ships, and railways in the nineteenth century created new demands for timber and forest products. These demands led to new rules and organization of forest use. We will see how **colonial control** was established, forests were mapped, trees classified, and plantations developed. These changes significantly affected local communities who relied on forest resources, forcing them into new systems and life reorganizations. However, they also resisted these rules, influencing state policies. The chapter will cover these developments in India and Indonesia.

Chapter V will trace the movements of pastoralists in India and Africa, across mountains, deserts, plains, and plateaus. These communities form a significant population segment but are rarely studied, and their histories are often absent from textbooks. Chapter V will show how their lives were impacted by forest controls, agricultural expansion, and the decline of grazing lands. It will detail their movement patterns, relationships with other communities, and adaptations to changing situations.

Chapter VI will discuss changes in the lives of peasants and farmers in India, England, and the USA. Over the past two centuries, agriculture has undergone major transformations due to new technology, demands, rules, laws, and ideas of property, fundamentally altering rural life. The rise of **capitalism** and **colonialism** has also changed rural existence. This chapter will introduce these changes and show how different groups—poor and rich, men and women, adults and children—were affected distinctly.

Understanding the making of the contemporary world requires recognizing changes in the lives of diverse communities. Similarly, comprehending the problems of modernization necessitates examining its impact on the environment.
