This section describes the events of February 1917 in Petrograd that led to the abdication of the Tsar and the establishment of the Provisional Government.

# The February Revolution in Petrograd

In the winter of 1917, the capital city, Petrograd, faced severe conditions. The city's layout reflected social divisions, with workers' quarters and factories on the right bank of the River Neva and fashionable areas, official buildings, and the Winter Palace on the left bank. Food shortages were particularly acute in the workers' quarters, compounded by an exceptionally cold winter. Parliamentarians, concerned about preserving elected government, opposed the Tsar's intention to dissolve the Duma.

On 22 February, a lockout occurred at a factory on the right bank. The following day, workers in fifty factories went on strike in solidarity. Women workers played a leading role in many strikes, and this day came to be known as **International Women's Day**. Demonstrators from the factory districts crossed into the city centre, the Nevskii Prospekt. Although no political party was actively organising the movement at this point, the government imposed a curfew as workers surrounded fashionable areas and official buildings. Demonstrators dispersed by evening but returned on the 24th and 25th, prompting the government to deploy cavalry and police.

On Sunday, 25 February, the government suspended the Duma, a move criticised by politicians. Demonstrators returned to the streets on the left bank in greater numbers on the 26th. On the 27th, the Police Headquarters were ransacked. Crowds filled the streets, chanting slogans demanding *bread, wages, better hours, and democracy*. The government again called out the cavalry, but this time, the cavalry refused to fire on the demonstrators. An officer was shot, and three regiments mutinied, siding with the striking workers. By that evening, soldiers and striking workers had formed a **'soviet' or 'council'** in the same building where the Duma met. This council was named the **Petrograd Soviet**.

The next day, a delegation visited the Tsar. Military commanders advised him to abdicate, which he did on **2 March**. The leaders of the Soviet and the Duma formed a **Provisional Government** to administer the country. They decided that Russia's future structure would be determined by a constituent assembly elected by universal adult suffrage. The events in Petrograd constituted the **February Revolution**, which successfully ended the monarchy in February 1917.

## Women in the February Revolution

'Women workers, often ... inspired their male co-workers ... At the Lorenz telephone factory, ... Marfa Vasileva almost single handedly called a successful strike. Already that morning, in celebration of Women's Day, women workers had presented red bows to the men ... Then Marfa Vasileva, a milling machine operator stopped work and declared an impromptu strike. The workers on the floor were ready to support her ... The foreman informed the management and sent her a loaf of bread. She took the bread but refused to go back to work. The administrator asked her again why she refused to work and she replied, "I cannot be the only one who is satiated when others are hungry". Women workers from another section of the factory gathered around Marfa in support and gradually all the other women ceased working. Soon the men downed their tools as well and the entire crowd rushed onto the street.'
From: Choi Chatterji, Celebrating Women (2002).
Description: This box provides an account of how women workers, particularly Marfa Vasileva, initiated and inspired strikes during the February Revolution, demonstrating their crucial role in the movement.
