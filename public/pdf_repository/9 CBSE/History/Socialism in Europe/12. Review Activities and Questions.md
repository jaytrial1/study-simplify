# Questions

1.  What were the social, economic and political conditions in Russia before 1905?

> **Answer:** Before 1905, Russia was an autocracy ruled by the Tsar, who was not subject to a parliament. Socially, the population was largely agricultural (85%), with a small industrial sector concentrated in cities like St Petersburg and Moscow. Society was divided, with the nobility, crown, and Church owning vast lands, while peasants cultivated most of the land but desired redistribution. Workers faced long hours, low wages, and poor conditions. Politically, all parties were illegal, and there was limited freedom, although liberals, socialists, and nationalists were campaigning for change and a constitution. The economic situation for workers was deteriorating, with falling real wages.

2.  In what ways was the working population in Russia different from other countries in Europe, before 1917?

> **Answer:** The Russian working population differed in several ways:
> 1.  **Agricultural Majority:** A much higher proportion of the population (85%) were agriculturists compared to industrialised European countries (40-50% in France/Germany).
> 2.  **Peasant Commune (Mir):** Russian peasants uniquely pooled their land periodically in a commune (mir) and divided it according to family needs.
> 3.  **Peasant-Noble Relations:** Unlike some parts of Europe (like France), Russian peasants generally had little respect for the nobility and desired their land, frequently refusing rent and sometimes resorting to violence.
> 4.  **Industrial Workforce:** While industrialisation was growing, the industrial workforce was smaller than in leading European nations and was divided by skill and origin (ties to villages vs. permanent city dwellers).
> 5.  **Political Context:** All political parties and unions were illegal before 1914, forcing workers' organisations to operate underground or unofficially, unlike the more established labour movements in some European democracies.

3.  Why did the Tsarist autocracy collapse in 1917?

> **Answer:** The Tsarist autocracy collapsed in 1917 due to a combination of factors:
> 1.  **Long-standing discontent:** The Tsar's autocratic rule, lack of political rights, and failure to address the grievances of workers and peasants created deep dissatisfaction.
> 2.  **Impact of World War I:** The war caused devastating casualties, economic collapse (industry breakdown, railway failure), severe shortages of food and essentials in cities, and a massive refugee crisis. This discredited the Tsar and his government and demoralised the army.
> 3.  **Tsar's isolation:** The Tsar's refusal to consult the Duma and the unpopularity of his advisors (like Rasputin) and the Tsarina further eroded support.
> 4.  **February Revolution:** Food shortages and harsh winter conditions in Petrograd sparked strikes and demonstrations. When the government attempted to suppress these protests, the army mutinied and sided with the workers, leading to the Tsar's abdication.

4.  Make two lists: one with the main events and the effects of the February Revolution and the other with the main events and effects of the October Revolution. Write a paragraph on who was involved in each, who were the leaders and what was the impact of each on Soviet history.

> **Answer:**
> **February Revolution (Petrograd, February 1917):**
> *   **Main Events:** Lockout and strike at a factory on Feb 22, sympathy strikes spread, women lead strikes on Feb 23 (International Women's Day), demonstrators reach city centre, government suspends Duma on Feb 25, protests continue, cavalry refuses to fire on Feb 27, army regiments mutiny and join strikers, formation of the Petrograd Soviet, military commanders advise Tsar to abdicate on March 2.
> *   **Effects:** Abdication of the Tsar and collapse of the monarchy, establishment of the Provisional Government, removal of restrictions on public meetings and associations, formation of Soviets across Russia, decision to elect a Constituent Assembly based on universal adult suffrage.
> **October Revolution (Petrograd, October 1917):**
> *   **Main Events:** Lenin proposes socialist seizure of power (April Theses), Bolshevik influence grows while Provisional Government's power weakens, Provisional Government represses Bolsheviks in July, Bolsheviks plan uprising in September/October, Military Revolutionary Committee formed, uprising begins Oct 24, Bolsheviks seize government buildings/points, Cruiser Aurora shells Winter Palace, ministers surrender, All Russian Congress of Soviets approves Bolshevik action, Bolsheviks gain control of Moscow-Petrograd area by December.
> *   **Effects:** Overthrow of the Provisional Government, Bolshevik seizure of state power, nationalisation of industries and banks, declaration of land as social property and peasant land seizures, establishment of a one-party state (Russian Communist Party), dismissal of the Constituent Assembly, making peace with Germany (Treaty of Brest Litovsk), beginning of the Russian Civil War, formation of the USSR.
>
> **Involvement, Leaders, and Impact:**
> The **February Revolution** involved workers (men and women), soldiers, and politicians (liberals, socialists in the Duma, and Soviet leaders). While it was largely a spontaneous uprising driven by popular discontent, figures like Father Gapon (in 1905, preceding the 1917 events he helped trigger) and various Duma politicians and Soviet leaders played roles. Its main impact was ending the Tsarist autocracy and establishing a dual power structure (Provisional Government and Soviets), opening the door for more radical change. The **October Revolution** was led primarily by the Bolshevik Party, with key figures like Vladimir Lenin and Leon Trotskii (heading the Military Revolutionary Committee). It involved Bolshevik supporters in the army, Soviets, and factories. Its impact was the establishment of a socialist state under Bolshevik control, fundamentally restructuring Russian society, economy, and politics, leading to the Civil War and shaping the course of 20th-century history by creating the world's first major communist state (the USSR).

5.  What were the main changes brought about by the Bolsheviks immediately after the October Revolution?

> **Answer:** Immediately after the October Revolution, the Bolsheviks brought about several main changes:
> 1.  Nationalisation of most industry and banks.
> 2.  Declaration of land as social property and allowing peasants to seize noble land.
> 3.  Enforcement of partition of large houses in cities based on family needs.
> 4.  Banning of old aristocratic titles.
> 5.  Renaming the Bolshevik Party to the Russian Communist Party (Bolshevik).
> 6.  Dismissal of the Constituent Assembly when it did not support their measures.
> 7.  Making peace with Germany (Treaty of Brest Litovsk).
> 8.  Beginning the process of establishing a one-party state under Bolshevik control.

6.  Write a few lines to show what you know about:
    *   kulaks
    *   the Duma
    *   women workers between 1900 and 1930
    *   the Liberals
    *   Stalin's collectivisation programme.

> **Answer:**
> *   **Kulaks:** In the Russian context, kulaks were the name given to well-to-do peasants. Stalin's government accused them of hoarding grain and speculating on prices. They were targeted during collectivisation, often raided, and seen as enemies of the state who needed to be eliminated to facilitate the establishment of collective farms.
> *   **The Duma:** The Duma was an elected consultative parliament created by Tsar Nicholas II during the 1905 Revolution. Although intended to be consultative, the Tsar limited its powers and dismissed the first two Dumas quickly, packing the third with conservatives, showing his unwillingness to share power or be subject to a parliament.
> *   **Women workers between 1900 and 1930:** Women constituted a significant portion of the factory labour force in Russia (31% by 1914) but were paid less than men. They were active in strikes and played a leading role in initiating protests during the February Revolution (e.g., International Women's Day strike). After the revolution, the Soviet government established crèches in factories to support women workers with childcare.
> *   **The Liberals:** Liberals were a political group in 19th and early 20th century Europe and Russia who desired a nation that tolerated all religions, opposed the uncontrolled power of dynastic rulers, and wanted to safeguard individual rights through a representative, elected parliamentary government and an independent judiciary. However, they were not democrats and did not support universal adult suffrage, generally favouring voting rights only for men of property.
> *   **Stalin's collectivisation programme:** This was a policy initiated by Stalin from 1929 to force peasants to give up their individual farms and cultivate in large collective farms (kolkhoz). It aimed to modernise agriculture and solve grain shortages by eliminating private landholding and kulaks. The programme involved confiscating land and tools, met strong peasant resistance (including destroying livestock), and was enforced through severe punishment, deportation, exile, and led to a devastating famine in the early 1930s.
