This section describes the period after the February Revolution, the struggles between the Provisional Government and the Bolsheviks, and the Bolshevik seizure of power in October 1917.

## After February

Following the February Revolution, the **Provisional Government** was formed, with influential figures from the army, landowning class, and industry. However, liberals and socialists within the government aimed for an elected government. Restrictions on public meetings and associations were lifted, leading to the establishment of 'Soviets' across the country, although without a uniform election system.

In April 1917, the Bolshevik leader **Vladimir Lenin** returned from exile. He and the Bolsheviks had opposed the war since 1914. Believing the time was right for the Soviets to take power, Lenin issued his **'April Theses'**, demanding:
1.  The war be brought to a close.
2.  Land be transferred to the peasants.
3.  Banks be nationalised.
He also proposed renaming the Bolshevik Party the **Communist Party** to signal its radical goals. Initially, many Bolsheviks were surprised by these demands, believing the Provisional Government should still be supported as Russia was not yet ready for a socialist revolution. However, subsequent events shifted their perspective.

Throughout the summer of 1917, the workers' movement gained momentum. Factory committees formed in industrial areas, challenging the authority of industrialists. Trade unions grew, and soldiers' committees emerged in the army. In June, the All Russian Congress of Soviets received representatives from about 500 Soviets. As the Provisional Government's authority waned and Bolshevik influence increased, it adopted repressive measures. The government resisted workers' attempts to control factories and began arresting leaders. A large pro-Bolshevik demonstration in July 1917 was suppressed, forcing many Bolshevik leaders into hiding or exile.

Meanwhile, in the countryside, peasants, encouraged by the Socialist Revolutionaries, pushed for land redistribution. Land committees were formed to manage this process. Between July and September 1917, peasants seized land belonging to nobles.

## The Revolution of October 1917

As the conflict between the Provisional Government and the Bolsheviks intensified, Lenin feared the Provisional Government would establish a dictatorship. In September, he initiated discussions within the party for an armed uprising. Bolshevik supporters in the army, Soviets, and factories were mobilised.

On **16 October 1917**, Lenin convinced the Petrograd Soviet and the Bolshevik Party to support a socialist seizure of power. A **Military Revolutionary Committee**, led by Leon Trotskii, was appointed by the Soviet to plan and execute the takeover. The date of the uprising was kept secret.

The uprising commenced on **24 October**. Prime Minister Kerenskii, anticipating trouble, left Petrograd to gather loyal troops. Early that morning, government loyalists seized the offices of two Bolshevik newspapers. Pro-government forces were dispatched to secure telephone and telegraph offices and protect the Winter Palace. The Military Revolutionary Committee responded swiftly, ordering its supporters to seize government offices and arrest ministers. Later that day, the cruiser *Aurora* shelled the Winter Palace. Other ships sailed along the Neva, taking control of military points. By nightfall, the Military Revolutionary Committee controlled the city, and the Provisional Government ministers had surrendered. The Bolshevik action was approved by the majority at a meeting of the All Russian Congress of Soviets in Petrograd. Uprisings also occurred in other cities, with heavy fighting, particularly in Moscow. By December, the Bolsheviks had secured control over the Moscow-Petrograd area.

## Date of the Russian Revolution

Russia used the Julian calendar until 1 February 1918, when it adopted the Gregorian calendar, which is 13 days ahead. Consequently, the 'February' Revolution occurred on 12th March (Gregorian), and the 'October' Revolution occurred on 7th November (Gregorian).
