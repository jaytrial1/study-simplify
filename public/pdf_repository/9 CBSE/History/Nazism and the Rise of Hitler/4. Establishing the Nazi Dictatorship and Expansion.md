This section details how the Nazi regime consolidated power, implemented economic recovery programs, and pursued aggressive foreign policies leading to territorial expansion and the outbreak of World War II.

### Reconstruction

Hitler assigned economic recovery to economist Hjalmar Schacht, who aimed for full production and employment through state-funded work programs. This led to the famous German superhighways and the *Volkswagen* (people's car).

Hitler also achieved rapid successes in foreign policy. He withdrew from the League of Nations in 1933, reoccupied the Rhineland in 1936, and integrated Austria and Germany in 1938 under the slogan, 'One people, One empire, and One leader.' He then took the German-speaking Sudetenland from Czechoslovakia and eventually absorbed the entire country. England's unspoken support, viewing the Versailles terms as too harsh, facilitated these moves. These quick successes domestically and internationally seemed to reverse the country's destiny.

Hitler did not stop there. Schacht advised against massive rearmament investment due to deficit financing, but cautious voices were unwelcome. Schacht had to leave. Hitler chose war as the solution to the economic crisis, planning to accumulate resources through territorial expansion. In September 1939, Germany invaded Poland, starting the war with France and England. In September 1940, the *Tripartite Pact* was signed with Italy and Japan, boosting Hitler's international standing. Puppet regimes supporting Nazi Germany were installed across much of Europe. By the end of 1940, Hitler was at the peak of his power.

Hitler then pursued his long-term goal of conquering Eastern Europe to gain food supplies and *Lebensraum* (living space) for Germans. He attacked the Soviet Union in June 1941, a strategic error that exposed Germany's western front to British bombing and the eastern front to powerful Soviet armies. The Soviet Red Army inflicted a crushing defeat at Stalingrad and pushed German soldiers back to Berlin, establishing Soviet dominance over Eastern Europe for fifty years.

Meanwhile, the USA initially avoided the war due to economic concerns from WWI. However, Japan's expansion in the east, including occupying French Indo-China and planning attacks on US Pacific bases, drew them in. When Japan supported Hitler and bombed Pearl Harbor, the US entered World War II. The war ended in May 1945 with Hitler's defeat and the US dropping atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
