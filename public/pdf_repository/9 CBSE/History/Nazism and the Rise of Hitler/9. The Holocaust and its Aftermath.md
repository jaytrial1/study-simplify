This section focuses on the systematic mass murder carried out by the Nazis against Jews and other groups, known as the Holocaust. It discusses how information about these atrocities emerged and how the history and memory of the Holocaust are preserved.

### Knowledge about the Holocaust

Information about Nazi practices gradually emerged from Germany during the regime's final years. However, the full horror became widely known only after the war ended and Germany was defeated. While Germans faced their own struggles as a defeated nation, Jews wanted the world to remember the atrocities and suffering of the Nazi killing operations – the *Holocaust*. A ghetto inhabitant wished to survive the war for just half an hour to tell the world what had happened. This determination to bear witness led many ghetto and camp inhabitants to keep diaries, notebooks, and archives. Conversely, as defeat seemed imminent, Nazi leaders distributed petrol to destroy incriminating evidence.

Despite efforts to hide the truth, the history and memory of the Holocaust persist through memoirs, fiction, documentaries, poetry, memorials, and museums globally. These serve as tributes to those who resisted, embarrassing reminders to collaborators, and warnings to those who remained silent.
