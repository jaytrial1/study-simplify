# Mahatma Gandhi writes to Hitler

## LETTER TO ADOLF HITLER

AS AT WARDHA, C. P., INDIA, July 23, 1939

HERR HITLER
BERLIN
GERMANY

## DEAR FRIEND,

Friends have been urging me to write to you for the sake of humanity. But I have resisted their request, because of the feeling that any letter from me would be an impertinence. Something tells me that I must not calculate and that I must make my appeal for whatever it may be worth.
It is quite clear that you are today the one person in the world who can prevent a war which may reduce humanity to the savage state.
Must you pay that price for an object however worthy it may appear to you to be? Will you listen to the appeal of one who has deliberately shunned the method of war not without considerable success? Anyway
I anticipate your forgiveness, if I have erred in writing to you.
I remain,
Your sincere friend,
M. K. GANDHI

THE COLLECTED WORKS OF MAHATMA GANDHI VOL. 76.

## LETTER TO ADOLF HITLER

WARDHA,
December 24, 1940
We have found in non-violence a force which, if organised, can without doubt match itself against a combination of all the most violent forces in the world. In non-violent technique, as I have said, there is no such thing as defeat. It is all 'do or die' without killing or hurting. It can be used practically without money and obviously without the aid of science of destruction which you have brought to such perfection. It is a marvel to me that you do not see that it is nobody's monopoly. If not the British, some other power will certainly improve upon your method and beat you with your own weapon. You are leaving no legacy to your people of which they would feel proud. They cannot take pride in a recital of cruel deed, however skilfully planned. I, therefore, appeal to you in the name of humanity to stop the war....
I am,
Your sincere friend,
M. K. GANDHI

THE COLLECTED WORKS OF MAHATMA GANDHI VOL. 79.

## Questions

1. Describe the problems faced by the Weimar Republic.
> **Answer:** The Weimar Republic faced numerous problems: it was blamed for Germany's defeat in World War I and the harsh Treaty of Versailles, which imposed significant territorial losses, demilitarization, and massive reparations (£6 billion), causing national humiliation and financial crippling. It suffered from political instability due to constitutional defects like proportional representation, leading to weak coalition governments, and Article 48, which allowed the President to rule by decree, undermining democracy. The republic was also challenged by political radicalism, including uprisings like the Spartacist League, creating deep divisions between left-wing groups. Severe economic crises, such as hyperinflation in 1923 and the devastating impact of the Great Depression after 1929, led to mass unemployment, business failures, and widespread poverty, eroding public confidence in the government.
2. Discuss why Nazism became popular in Germany by 1930.
> **Answer:** Nazism became popular by 1930 primarily due to the severe economic and political crises facing Germany. The Great Depression after 1929 caused mass unemployment, poverty, and despair, making people desperate for solutions. The Weimar Republic was seen as weak, unstable, and unable to manage these crises, leading to a loss of faith in democracy. Hitler and the Nazi Party offered a seemingly strong, decisive leadership and promised to restore national dignity, undo the Versailles Treaty, provide employment, and secure the future. Their propaganda skillfully exploited people's fears and anxieties, scapegoating Jews and other groups for Germany's problems. Hitler's powerful speaking skills and the Nazi Party's use of spectacle and rallies mobilized mass support, projecting an image of strength and hope amidst widespread suffering and disillusionment.
3. What are the peculiar features of Nazi thinking?
> **Answer:** Peculiar features of Nazi thinking include a rigid racial hierarchy, placing Nordic German Aryans at the top and Jews at the bottom as an 'anti-race' and ultimate enemy. It embraced the concept of 'survival of the fittest' applied to races, believing the 'Aryan race' must remain pure, strong, and dominate others. Another key feature is the geopolitical concept of *Lebensraum* (living space), the belief that Germany needed to conquer new territories, especially in the east, for settlement and resource acquisition. Nazi thinking also involved extreme nationalism, a rejection of democracy and equality, and the glorification of aggression, violence, and war as means to achieve national goals and racial purity.
4. Explain why Nazi propaganda was effective in creating a hatred for Jews.
> **Answer:** Nazi propaganda was effective in creating hatred for Jews because it was systematic, pervasive, and targeted. It built upon existing traditional anti-Jewish sentiments but amplified them using pseudoscientific racial theories. Propaganda outlets like media, films (e.g., *The Eternal Jew*), posters, and even school textbooks consistently stereotyped Jews as evil, greedy, and physically repulsive ('hooked noses', looking like the number six). They were depicted as *vermin*, *rats*, and *pests*, associated with negative forces like Bolshevism and Big Capitalism, and blamed for Germany's economic problems and national humiliation. This constant, dehumanizing portrayal, combined with state control over information and the suppression of alternative views, worked on people's emotions, tapping into existing fears and redirecting anger towards Jews as a convenient scapegoat, making hatred seem justified and widespread.
5. Explain what role women had in Nazi society. Return to Chapter 1 on the French Revolution. Write a paragraph comparing and contrasting the role of women in the two periods.
> **Answer:** In Nazi society, women's primary role was defined by motherhood and maintaining the purity of the 'Aryan race'. They were told that their world was distinct from men's and that their contribution was through childbearing ("Every child... is a battle"). Girls were taught to be good mothers, manage the home, instill Nazi values, and avoid contact with 'undesirables'. While Hitler called the mother the "most important citizen," this status was conditional on bearing racially 'desirable' children; mothers of 'undesirable' children were punished, while those with many 'Aryan' children received rewards and privileges (Honour Crosses, concessions). Women who deviated, especially by associating with Jews or other persecuted groups, faced severe public humiliation and punishment. Thus, the Nazi state imposed a strict, racially-defined, and subservient role on women, primarily valuing them for their biological capacity to reproduce the 'master race'. 6. In what ways did the Nazi state seek to establish total control over its people?
> **Answer:** The Nazi state sought total control through multiple means:
> *   **Political Suppression:** Banning all political parties and trade unions except the Nazi Party, eliminating opposition.
> *   **Legal Dictatorship:** The Enabling Act gave Hitler power to rule by decree, bypassing and ultimately sidelining Parliament.
> *   **Surveillance and Terror:** Creating special security forces like the Gestapo, SS, and SD with extra-constitutional powers, enabling arbitrary detention, torture, and arrest without legal procedures.
> *   **Control of Institutions:** Establishing state control over the economy, media, army, and judiciary.
> *   **Propaganda and Ideology:** Using media (radio, film, posters) and language to spread Nazi worldview, control information, and manipulate public opinion, promoting loyalty to Hitler and hatred of enemies.
> *   **Education and Youth:** Indoctrinating children from a young age through rewritten school curricula (including racial science) and mandatory youth organizations (Jungvolk, Hitler Youth) to instill Nazi values and loyalty.
> *   **Social Engineering:** Defining and enforcing rigid social roles (e.g., for women) and persecuting any deviation.
> *   **Elimination of 'Undesirables':** Systematically excluding, segregating, and ultimately annihilating groups deemed racially or politically 'undesirable', creating an atmosphere of fear that discouraged dissent among the remaining population.

## Some important dates

*   August 1, 1914: First World War begins.
*   November 9, 1918: Germany capitulates, ending the war.
*   November 9, 1918: Proclamation of the Weimar Republic.
*   June 28, 1919: Treaty of Versailles.
*   January 30, 1933: Hitler becomes Chancellor of Germany.
*   September 1, 1939: Germany invades Poland. Beginning of the Second World War.
*   June 22, 1941: Germany invades the USSR.
*   June 23, 1941: Mass murder of the Jews begins.
*   December 8, 1941: The United States joins Second World War.
*   January 27, 1945: Soviet troops liberate Auschwitz.
*   May 8, 1945: Allied victory in Europe.
