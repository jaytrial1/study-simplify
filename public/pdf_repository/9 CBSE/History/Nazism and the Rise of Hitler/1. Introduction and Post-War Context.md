This section introduces the human cost of Nazism through a personal story and sets the scene with the end of World War II, the Nuremberg Tribunal, and the initial understanding of Nazi crimes. It also briefly touches upon the context of Germany after World War I as a precursor to understanding the rise of Nazism.

# Nazism and the Rise of Hitler

In the spring of 1945, a young German boy named Helmuth overheard his parents discussing serious matters. His father, a physician and Nazi supporter, feared retaliation from the Allies and contemplated killing his family or himself. The father later committed suicide. This traumatic event deeply affected Helmuth, who for years feared being poisoned by his mother.

Nazism was not merely a few isolated actions but a complete *system* of ideas about the world and politics. Understanding this system helps explain the fear that led Helmuth's father to take his own life.

In May 1945, Germany surrendered to the Allies. Hitler, his propaganda minister Goebbels, and their families committed suicide in April. An International Military Tribunal at Nuremberg was established to prosecute Nazi war criminals for *Crimes against Peace*, *War Crimes*, and *Crimes Against Humanity*.

Germany's actions during the war, particularly the *Crimes Against Humanity*, caused serious moral and ethical questions and were widely condemned. These acts included a *genocidal* war resulting in the mass murder of specific groups of European civilians. Millions were killed: 6 million Jews, 200,000 Gypsies, 1 million Polish civilians, 70,000 Germans deemed mentally or physically disabled, and countless political opponents. The Nazis used unprecedented methods, such as gassing people in killing centers like Auschwitz.

The Nuremberg Tribunal sentenced only eleven leading Nazis to death, imprisoning many others for life. While retribution occurred, the punishment seemed minor compared to the brutality and scale of the crimes. The Allies were hesitant to be as harsh on defeated Germany as they had been after the First World War.

Many felt that the rise of Nazi Germany was partly rooted in Germany's experience at the end of the First World War.

## New words

**Allies** - The Allied Powers were initially led by the UK and France. In 1941 they were joined by the USSR and USA. They fought against the Axis Powers, namely Germany, Italy and Japan.
**Genocidal** - Killing on a large scale leading to the destruction of large sections of people.
