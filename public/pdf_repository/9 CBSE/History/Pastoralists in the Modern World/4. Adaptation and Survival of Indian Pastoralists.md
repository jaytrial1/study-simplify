This section examines the various strategies adopted by pastoral communities in India to cope with the challenges posed by colonial policies and the changing modern world, demonstrating their resilience and adaptability.

# How Did the Pastoralists Cope with these Changes?

Pastoralists reacted to these changes in various ways.

*   Some reduced herd sizes due to pasture shortages.
*   Others found new pastures when old ones became inaccessible. After 1947, Raikas could no longer move into Sindh (Pakistan) and had to find new areas, migrating to Haryana where sheep graze on post-harvest fields and provide manure.
*   Some wealthier pastoralists bought land and settled down, becoming settled peasants or extensive traders.
*   Many poor pastoralists borrowed money, lost cattle, and became labourers on fields or in towns.

Despite challenges, pastoralists continue to survive, and their numbers have grown in some regions. When pastures were closed, they changed movement paths, reduced herd size, combined pastoralism with other income sources, and adapted. Many ecologists see pastoral nomadism as ecologically viable in dry and mountainous regions.

Similar changes affected pastoral communities globally. New laws and settlement patterns forced alterations in Africa as well.
