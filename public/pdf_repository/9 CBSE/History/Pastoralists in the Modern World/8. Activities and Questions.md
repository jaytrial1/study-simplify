## Questions

1.  Explain why nomadic tribes need to move from one place to another. What are the advantages to the environment of this continuous movement?
    > **Answer:** Nomadic tribes need to move primarily to find sufficient pasture and water for their herds, which are essential for their survival and livelihood. Pasture availability changes seasonally and can be quickly depleted in one area. The advantages of this continuous movement to the environment are that it allows pastures to recover. By not grazing the same area continuously, the vegetation has time to regrow, preventing overuse and degradation of the land.

2.  Discuss why the colonial government in India brought in the following laws. In each case, explain how the law changed the lives of pastoralists:
    > **Answer:** The colonial government introduced these laws mainly to increase its revenue, control resources, and establish administrative control over the population.
    > *   **Waste Land Rules:** Introduced to expand cultivation, primarily for revenue generation through land tax and to produce cash crops needed in England. These rules took over uncultivated lands, which were often traditional pastoral grazing grounds, reducing the area available for animals and forcing pastoralists into smaller, degraded areas.
    > *   **Forest Acts:** Enacted to control commercially valuable timber forests for revenue and industrial needs. By declaring forests 'Reserved' or 'Protected,' they restricted or banned pastoralists' access, limiting their forage sources and regulating their movements within forests with permits and time limits.
    > *   **Criminal Tribes Act:** Passed out of suspicion towards mobile populations, whom the British found difficult to control and tax compared to settled villagers. This act labeled many pastoral and nomadic communities as inherently criminal, forcing them into settlements, restricting their movement, and subjecting them to constant police surveillance, severely disrupting their traditional way of life and social status.
    > *   **Grazing Tax:** Introduced to increase state revenue by taxing animals that grazed on pastures. This imposed a financial burden on pastoralists, increasing their expenses and making it harder to maintain large herds, especially as tax rates rose and collection became more efficient.

3.  Give reasons to explain why the Maasai community lost their grazing lands.
    > **Answer:** The Maasai community lost their grazing lands primarily due to colonial policies. In the late nineteenth century, their territory was divided between British Kenya and German Tanganyika. Subsequently, large areas of their best grazing lands were taken over for white settlement. Additionally, the colonial government encouraged local peasants to expand cultivation, converting more pastureland into fields. Finally, significant portions of their land were turned into game reserves and national parks, from which pastoralists were excluded.

4.  There are many similarities in the way in which the modern world forced changes in the lives of pastoral communities in India and East Africa. Write about any two examples of changes which were similar for Indian pastoralists and the Maasai herders.
    > **Answer:**
    > 1.  **Loss of Grazing Lands:** Both Indian pastoralists (like the Gujjars, Gaddis, Raikas, Dhangars) and the Maasai in East Africa suffered significant loss of their traditional grazing lands. In India, this was due to the conversion of 'waste lands' into cultivated fields and the reservation of forests. In Africa, Maasai lands were taken for white settlement, expanded cultivation by peasants, and the creation of game reserves. This reduced the area available for grazing and forced communities into smaller, often less fertile, tracts.
    > 2.  **Restrictions on Movement:** Both groups faced severe restrictions on their traditional movements. Indian pastoralists' movements were regulated by Forest Acts requiring permits and setting time limits, and by the Criminal Tribes Act confining some communities to settlements. Maasai were confined to reserves with fixed boundaries and needed permits to move their stock, making their traditional nomadic response to finding pasture difficult or impossible.
