# Threats to Wildlife and Conservation Efforts

The main threats to nature include:

*   Hunting by greedy hunters for commercial purposes.
*   Pollution from chemical and industrial waste, and acid deposits.
*   Introduction of alien species.
*   Reckless cutting of forests for cultivation and settlement.

These factors contribute to ecological imbalance and threaten biodiversity.

To protect the country's flora and fauna, the government has implemented several measures:

*   **Biosphere Reserves:** Fourteen biosphere reserves have been established to protect diverse ecosystems. Four of these are included in the world network of Biosphere reserves:
    *   Sunderbans (West Bengal)
    *   Nanda Devi (Uttarakhand)
    *   Gulf of Mannar (Tamil Nadu)
    *   The Nilgiris (Kerala, Karnataka and Tamil Nadu)

*   **Botanical Gardens:** Financial and technical assistance is provided to many Botanical Gardens since 1992.
*   **Conservation Projects:** Projects like Project Tiger, Project Rhino, Project Great Indian Bustard, and other eco-development projects have been introduced.
*   **Protected Areas:** 89 National Parks, 490 Wildlife Sanctuaries, and Zoological Gardens have been set up to safeguard natural heritage.

We must all recognise the importance of the natural ecosystem for our survival and stop the indiscriminate destruction of the natural environment immediately.

# Migratory Birds

Some Indian wetlands are popular with migratory birds, such as the Siberian Crane, which arrive in large numbers during winter. The Rann of Kachchh is one such place, where the desert meets the sea. Flamingoes with their brilliant pink plumage come in thousands to build nest mounds from salty mud and raise their young, creating an extraordinary sight.
