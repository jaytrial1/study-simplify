# Importance of Forests and Ecosystems

Forests are vital renewable resources that enhance environmental quality. They influence local climate, prevent soil erosion, regulate water flow, support industries, provide livelihoods, and offer recreational opportunities. They control wind and temperature, cause rainfall, add humus to the soil, and provide shelter for wildlife.

India's natural vegetation has been significantly altered by human activities like increasing demand for cultivated land, industrial and mining development, urbanisation, and over-grazing. Much of the vegetation cover, except in remote areas like the Himalayas and parts of central India, has been modified or degraded by human presence.

## ECOSYSTEM

Plants grow in distinct communities in areas with similar climatic conditions. The type of plants largely determines the animal life. When vegetation changes, animal life also changes. All plants and animals in an area are interdependent and interrelated within their physical environment, forming an **ecosystem**. Humans are also part of the ecosystem. Human activities like over-utilisation of resources (cutting trees, killing animals) cause ecological imbalance, leading to some plants and animals becoming endangered or extinct.

A very large ecosystem on land with distinct vegetation and animal life is called a **biome**. Biomes are identified based on the dominant plant types.
