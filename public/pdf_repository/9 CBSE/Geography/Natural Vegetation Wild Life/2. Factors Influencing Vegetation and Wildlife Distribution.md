# Factors Affecting Natural Vegetation and Wildlife

The distribution and characteristics of natural vegetation and wildlife are primarily determined by relief and climate.

## Relief

### Land

Land directly and indirectly affects natural vegetation. Different types of land support different vegetation. Fertile plains are typically used for agriculture. Undulating and rough terrains are often covered by grasslands and woodlands, providing shelter for various wildlife.

### Soil

Soil types vary geographically and support different vegetation. Sandy soils in deserts support cactus and thorny bushes. Wet, marshy, and deltaic soils support mangroves and deltaic vegetation. Hill slopes with sufficient soil depth are home to conical trees.

## Climate

### Temperature

Temperature, along with humidity, precipitation, and soil, is a major determinant of vegetation type and growth. In mountainous regions like the Himalayas and the hills of the Peninsula above 915 metres, temperature changes with altitude lead to changes in vegetation, from tropical to subtropical, temperate, and alpine types.

### Photoperiod (Sunlight)

The duration of sunlight varies with latitude, altitude, season, and day length. Longer sunlight duration, especially in summer, promotes faster tree growth.

### Find out

Why are the southern slopes in Himalayan region covered with thick vegetation cover as compared to northern slopes of the same hills?
> **Answer:** The southern slopes of the Himalayas face the sun, receiving more direct sunlight (photoperiod) and are often warmer, leading to more luxuriant vegetation growth compared to the northern slopes, which are steeper and receive less direct sunlight. The southern slopes also receive more precipitation from the monsoon winds.

### Precipitation

Most rainfall in India comes from the southwest monsoon (June to September) and retreating northeast monsoons. Areas with heavy rainfall tend to have denser vegetation than areas with less rainfall.

### Find out

Why have the western slopes of the Western Ghats covered with thick forests and not the eastern slopes?
> **Answer:** The western slopes of the Western Ghats face the Arabian Sea branch of the southwest monsoon winds. They receive heavy rainfall as the moist winds are forced to rise along the slopes. The eastern slopes, lying in the rain shadow area, receive significantly less rainfall, resulting in sparser vegetation.
