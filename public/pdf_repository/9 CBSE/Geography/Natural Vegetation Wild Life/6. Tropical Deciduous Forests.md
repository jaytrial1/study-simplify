# Tropical Deciduous Forests

These are the most common forests in India, also known as **monsoon forests**. They are found in regions receiving rainfall between 200 cm and 70 cm. The trees in these forests shed their leaves for about six to eight weeks during the dry summer.

Based on water availability, they are divided into:

*   **Moist Deciduous Forests:** Found in areas with rainfall between 200 cm and 100 cm. Locations include northeastern states, foothills of the Himalayas, Jharkhand, West Orissa, Chhattisgarh, and the eastern slopes of the Western Ghats.
    *   **Dominant species:** Teak.
    *   **Other commercially important species:** Bamboos, sal, shisham, sandalwood, khair, kusum, arjun, mulberry.
*   **Dry Deciduous Forests:** Found in areas with rainfall between 100 cm and 70 cm. Locations include the rainier parts of the peninsular plateau and the plains of Bihar and Uttar Pradesh. These areas often have open stretches with trees like Teak, Sal, Peepal, and Neem. Large parts have been cleared for cultivation or used for grazing.
