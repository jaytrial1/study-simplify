2. Answer the following questions briefly.
(i) Define an ecosystem.
> **Answer:** An ecosystem is a system formed by the interdependence and interrelation of all plants and animals in an area with each other and with their physical environment.

(ii) What factors are responsible for the distribution of plants and animals in India?
> **Answer:** The main factors responsible for the distribution of plants and animals in India are relief (land and soil) and climate (temperature, photoperiod/sunlight, and precipitation/rainfall).

(iii) What is a bio-reserve? Give two examples.
> **Answer:** A bio-reserve is a large area designated to protect flora and fauna and the ecosystems they inhabit. It aims at conservation as well as sustainable use of resources and research. Examples from the text include Sunderbans, Nanda Devi, The Nilgiris, Gulf of Mannar, Manas, Simlipal, etc.

3. Distinguish between
(i) Flora and Fauna
> **Answer:** Flora refers to the plant species of a particular region or period, while fauna refers to the animal species of a particular region or period.

(ii) Tropical Evergreen and Deciduous forests
> **Answer:** Tropical Evergreen forests are found in areas with heavy rainfall (>200 cm) and a short dry season. Trees remain green throughout the year as they shed leaves at different times. They are very dense and multi-layered. Tropical Deciduous forests (monsoon forests) are found in areas with rainfall between 200 cm and 70 cm. Trees shed their leaves for 6-8 weeks in the dry summer. They are the most widespread forests in India and are less dense than evergreen forests.

4. Name different types of Vegetation found in India and describe the vegetation of high altitudes.
> **Answer:** The different types of vegetation found in India are Tropical Evergreen Forests, Tropical Deciduous Forests, Tropical Thorn Forests and Scrubs, Montane Forests, and Mangrove Forests.
> The vegetation of high altitudes (Montane vegetation) changes with increasing height. Between 1000-2000m, wet temperate forests with broad-leaf trees like oaks are found. Between 1500-3000m, temperate forests with coniferous trees like pine and deodar are common. Above 3600m, Alpine vegetation is found, including trees like silver fir and junipers, which become stunted near the snow-line and merge into Alpine grasslands. Mosses and lichens form tundra vegetation at the highest altitudes.

5. Quite a few species of plants and animals are endangered in India. Why?
> **Answer:** Several plant and animal species are endangered in India due to excessive exploitation by human beings, causing ecological imbalance. The main causes include hunting for commercial purposes, pollution from industrial and chemical waste, introduction of alien species, and the reckless cutting of forests for agriculture, mining, and urbanisation.

6. Why has India a rich heritage of flora and fauna?
> **Answer:** India has a rich heritage of flora and fauna because it is one of the twelve mega biodiversity countries in the world. This is due to its vast geographical area and diverse climatic and relief conditions (mountains, plateaus, plains, coasts, islands, varying temperatures and rainfall) which support a wide variety of ecosystems and habitats for different plant and animal species.
