This section covers the period when the monsoon withdraws from India, the transition season, and the spatial patterns of rainfall distribution across the country.

# Retreating/Post Monsoons (The Transition Season)

During October-November, the monsoon trough over the northern plains weakens due to the sun's apparent southward movement. It is replaced by a high-pressure system. The south-west monsoon winds weaken and withdraw gradually, starting from the Northern Plains by early October.

October-November is a transition period from hot rainy conditions to dry winter.
*   The retreat is marked by clear skies and rising temperatures.
*   Day temperatures are high, but nights are cool and pleasant. The land is still moist.
*   The combination of high temperature and humidity makes the weather oppressive during the day, known as '**October heat**'.
*   In the second half of October, temperatures fall rapidly in northern India.
*   Low-pressure conditions from northwestern India transfer to the Bay of Bengal by early November.
*   This shift is associated with cyclonic depressions originating over the Andaman Sea. These tropical cyclones usually cross the eastern coasts, bringing heavy, widespread, and often destructive rain.
*   The deltas of the Godavari, Krishna, and Kaveri are frequently hit by these cyclones, causing significant damage. Orissa, West Bengal, and Bangladesh coasts are also affected.
*   The Coromandel Coast (Tamil Nadu) receives the bulk of its rainfall from these depressions and cyclones during this season.

## Distribution of Rainfall

Rainfall distribution across India varies significantly:
*   Parts of the western coast and northeastern India receive over 400 cm annually.
*   Western Rajasthan and adjoining parts of Gujarat, Haryana, and Punjab receive less than 60 cm.
*   Rainfall is also low in the interior Deccan plateau and east of the Sahyadris (Western Ghats) because these regions lie in the rain shadow area of the Western Ghats.
*   Another low precipitation area is around Leh in Jammu and Kashmir.
*   The rest of the country receives moderate rainfall.
*   Snowfall is limited to the Himalayan region.

Due to the nature of monsoons, annual rainfall is highly variable year-to-year. Variability is high in low-rainfall regions (Rajasthan, Gujarat, leeward Western Ghats). Areas with high rainfall are prone to floods, while low-rainfall areas are drought-prone.
