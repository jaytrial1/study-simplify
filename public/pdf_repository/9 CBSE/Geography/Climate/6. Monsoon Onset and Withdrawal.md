This section describes the timing and pattern of the monsoon's arrival in India and its subsequent retreat.

# The Onset of the Monsoon and Withdrawal

Unlike steady trade winds, monsoons are pulsating, affected by atmospheric conditions over warm tropical seas. The monsoon season lasts about 100-120 days (early June to mid-September).

## Onset of Monsoon

Around its arrival time, rainfall increases suddenly and continuously for several days. This is the '**burst**' of the monsoon, distinct from pre-monsoon showers.

The monsoon arrives at the southern tip of India usually by the first week of June. It then splits into two branches:
*   **Arabian Sea branch:** Reaches Mumbai around June 10th. Advances rapidly over Saurashtra-Kuchchh and central India by mid-June.
*   **Bay of Bengal branch:** Advances rapidly, arriving in Assam in the first week of June. Lofty mountains deflect these winds westward over the Ganga plains.

The two branches merge over the northwestern Ganga plains. Delhi usually receives monsoon showers from the Bay of Bengal branch by late June (around June 29th). By the first week of July, western Uttar Pradesh, Punjab, Haryana, and eastern Rajasthan get the monsoon. By mid-July, it reaches Himachal Pradesh and the rest of the country.

## Withdrawal of Monsoon

The withdrawal or retreat of the monsoon is a gradual process.
*   It begins in northwestern states by early September.
*   By mid-October, it completely withdraws from the northern half of the peninsula.
*   Withdrawal from the southern half is fairly rapid.
*   By early December, the monsoon has withdrawn from the rest of the country.
