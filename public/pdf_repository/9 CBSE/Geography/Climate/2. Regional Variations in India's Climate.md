This section examines how temperature and precipitation, key elements of weather and climate, vary across different regions and seasons within India.

# Regional Variations in Climate

India's climate is described as the '**monsoon**' type. The word 'monsoon' is derived from the Arabic word 'mausim', meaning season. Monsoon refers to the seasonal reversal in the wind direction during a year. In Asia, this climate type is mainly found in the south and southeast.

Despite a general pattern, there are noticeable regional variations in climatic conditions within India. Let's look at temperature and precipitation variations:

## Temperature Variations

*   In summer, temperatures can reach *50°C* in parts of the Rajasthan desert, while simultaneously being around *20°C* in Pahalgam, Jammu and Kashmir.
*   On a winter night, the temperature at Drass in Jammu and Kashmir can drop as low as *minus 45°C*, whereas Thiruvananthapuram might have a temperature of *22°C*.

### Day and Night Temperature Differences

In certain places, there's a wide difference between day and night temperatures.
*   In the Thar Desert, the day temperature can rise to *50°C* and drop to near *15°C* the same night.
*   In contrast, there is hardly any difference in day and night temperatures in the Andaman and Nicobar islands or in Kerala.

## Precipitation Variations

Precipitation varies in form, type, amount, and seasonal distribution.
*   Precipitation is mostly in the form of **snowfall** in the upper Himalayas.
*   Over the rest of the country, it occurs as **rain**.
*   Annual precipitation varies greatly, from over *400 cm* in Meghalaya to less than *10 cm* in Ladakh and western Rajasthan.
*   Most parts of the country receive rainfall from June to September.
*   However, some parts, like the Tamil Nadu coast, receive a large portion of their rain during October and November.

## Impact of Variations

Generally, coastal areas experience less temperature contrast due to the moderating influence of the sea. Seasonal contrasts are more pronounced in the interior of the country. Rainfall in the Northern Plains generally decreases from east to west. These variations lead to diversity in people's lives, including their food, clothing, and housing styles.

## Find out

*   Why the houses in Rajasthan have thick walls and flat roofs?
> **Answer:** Houses in Rajasthan have thick walls to insulate against the extreme heat of the desert day and cold nights, while flat roofs are suitable in areas with very low rainfall, allowing for potential use of the rooftop space.
*   Why is it that the houses in the Tarai region and in Goa and Mangalore have sloping roofs?
> **Answer:** Houses in the Tarai region (foothills of Himalayas) and coastal areas like Goa and Mangalore have sloping roofs to allow heavy rainfall to drain off easily, preventing water accumulation and damage.
*   Why houses in Assam are built on stilts?
> **Answer:** Houses in Assam are often built on stilts to protect them from flooding, which is common during the heavy monsoon season, and also from pests and animals.
