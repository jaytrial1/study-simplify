2. Answer the following questions briefly.
(i) What are the controls affecting the climate of India?
> **Answer:** The controls affecting the climate of India are latitude, altitude, pressure and wind system, distance from the sea (continentality), ocean currents, and relief features.
(ii) Why does India have a monsoon type of climate?
> **Answer:** India has a monsoon type of climate because its climate is strongly influenced by monsoon winds, which are characterised by a seasonal reversal in wind direction. This is due to the differential heating of land and water, the shift of pressure belts, and the influence of upper air circulation patterns like jet streams.
(iii) Which part of India does experience the highest diurnal range of temperature and why?
> **Answer:** The northwestern part of India, particularly the Thar Desert, experiences the highest diurnal range of temperature. This is because deserts have clear skies and dry land, which heats up quickly during the day and cools down rapidly at night due to the absence of moisture and cloud cover to retain heat.
(iv) Which winds account for rainfall along the Malabar coast?
> **Answer:** The Southwest Monsoon winds account for rainfall along the Malabar coast.
(v) What are Jet streams and how do they affect the climate of India?
> **Answer:** Jet streams are narrow belts of high-altitude westerly winds in the troposphere. Over India, the subtropical westerly jet stream influences the winter weather by bringing western cyclonic disturbances from the Mediterranean region, which cause winter rainfall in the north and northwestern parts. In summer, the movement of this jet stream northwards and the presence of the tropical easterly jet stream over the peninsula influence the monsoon circulation.
(vi) Define monsoons. What do you understand by "break" in monsoon?
> **Answer:** Monsoons refer to the seasonal reversal in the wind direction during a year. A "break" in monsoon refers to the phenomenon where the monsoon rains take place only for a few days at a time, interspersed with rainless intervals. These breaks are related to the movement of the monsoon trough.
(vii) Why is the monsoon considered a unifying bond?
> **Answer:** The monsoon is considered a unifying bond because it influences the entire Indian landscape, agricultural cycles, life, and festivals. The annual arrival of the monsoon is eagerly awaited across the country, providing water for agriculture and connecting the country through its river valleys that carry the monsoon water.

3. Why does the rainfall decrease from the east to the west in Northern India.
> **Answer:** Rainfall decreases from east to west in Northern India because the monsoon winds (Bay of Bengal branch) that bring rain to this region gradually lose moisture as they travel further inland towards the west.

4. Give reasons as to why.
(i) Seasonal reversal of wind direction takes place over the Indian subcontinent?
> **Answer:** Seasonal reversal of wind direction takes place over the Indian subcontinent primarily due to the differential heating and cooling of the vast Indian landmass and the surrounding oceans. In summer, the land gets much hotter than the sea, creating a strong low-pressure area over the land which attracts winds from the high-pressure areas over the oceans (southwest monsoon). In winter, the land is cooler than the sea, creating a high-pressure area over the land, causing winds to blow from land to sea (northeast monsoon).
(ii) The bulk of rainfall in India is concentrated over a few months.
> **Answer:** The bulk of rainfall in India is concentrated over a few months (June to September) because it is predominantly brought by the Southwest Monsoon winds. These winds blow for a specific period during the year, bringing heavy moisture from the oceans to the land.
(iii) The Tamil Nadu coast receives winter rainfall.
> **Answer:** The Tamil Nadu coast receives winter rainfall from the Northeast trade winds. While these winds blow from land to sea over most of the country in winter, they blow from sea to land on the Tamil Nadu coast, picking up moisture from the Bay of Bengal.
(iv) The delta region of the eastern coast is frequently struck by cyclones.
> **Answer:** The delta region of the eastern coast is frequently struck by cyclones because tropical cyclones originating in the Bay of Bengal during the transition seasons (October-November) often move towards and cross the eastern coast of India, particularly affecting these low-lying delta areas.
(v) Parts of Rajasthan, Gujarat and the leeward side of the Western Ghats are drought-prone.
> **Answer:** Parts of Rajasthan and Gujarat are drought-prone due to very low rainfall received from the monsoon winds. The leeward side of the Western Ghats is drought-prone because it lies in the rain shadow area; the monsoon winds, after shedding most of their moisture on the windward side of the mountains, bring very little rain to the leeward side.

5. Describe the regional variations in the climatic conditions of India with the help of suitable examples.
> **Answer:** Regional variations in India's climate are significant. For example, temperature in summer can reach 50°C in the Rajasthan desert while being 20°C in Pahalgam. In winter, Drass can have temperatures as low as -45°C, while Thiruvananthapuram is around 22°C. Precipitation also varies greatly, from over 400 cm in Meghalaya (Mawsynram) to less than 10 cm in Ladakh and western Rajasthan. Coastal areas have less temperature contrast than the interior.

6. Discuss the mechanism of monsoons.
> **Answer:** The mechanism of monsoons is influenced by several factors: (a) Differential heating of land and water creates low pressure over land and high pressure over seas in summer, attracting winds. (b) The ITCZ shifts north over the Ganga plain in summer. (c) A high-pressure area east of Madagascar affects monsoon intensity. (d) Intense heating of the Tibetan plateau creates low pressure. (e) The movement of the westerly jet stream northwards and the presence of the tropical easterly jet stream over the peninsula in summer influence circulation. These factors combine to cause the seasonal reversal of winds, bringing moisture-laden Southwest Monsoons in summer.

7. Give an account of weather conditions and characteristics of the cold season.
> **Answer:** The cold weather season (winter) in northern India is from mid-November to February. Characteristics include decreasing temperature from south to north, warm days and cold nights, frost in the north, and snowfall in the Himalayas. Northeast trade winds prevail, making it mostly dry, except for the Tamil Nadu coast. A feeble high-pressure exists in the north, with light winds. A key feature is the inflow of western cyclonic disturbances from the west, causing winter rain ('mahawat') in the plains and snow in the mountains, important for rabi crops. Peninsular India has milder winters due to the sea's moderating influence.

8. Give the characteristics and effects of the monsoon rainfall in India.
> **Answer:** Characteristics of monsoon rainfall include its arrival around early June, its pulsating nature with 'breaks' (alternating wet and dry spells), and its high spatial variability (heavy rain in some areas, scanty in others). It is also uncertain in arrival and retreat. Effects include providing the bulk of India's annual rainfall, being crucial for agriculture (setting the farming calendar), causing floods in areas of heavy rain, and leading to droughts in areas of low or variable rain. The monsoon binds the country by providing water and influencing life and culture.

4. Now find out
(i) Why are Thiruvananthapuram and Shillong rainier in June than in July?
> **Answer:** Thiruvananthapuram receives the monsoon early (first week of June) as the Arabian Sea branch hits the Kerala coast first, leading to peak rain early in the season. Shillong is in the northeast, receiving the Bay of Bengal branch early in June. The intensity might be higher in June as the initial surge hits these areas directly, while by July the monsoon trough's position or movement might slightly alter rainfall distribution.
(ii) Why is July rainier in Mumbai than in Thiruvananthapuram?
> **Answer:** Mumbai receives the monsoon later than Thiruvananthapuram (around June 10th). The monsoon strengthens and becomes more widespread as it progresses northwards along the west coast, leading to peak rainfall in Mumbai in July.
(iii) Why are southwest monsoons less rainy in Chennai?
> **Answer:** Chennai lies on the eastern coast and is in the rain shadow area of the Western Ghats during the Southwest Monsoon season. The winds blow parallel to the coast or are obstructed by the mountains, resulting in less rainfall compared to the west coast.
(iv) Why is Shillong rainier than Kolkata?
> **Answer:** Shillong is located in the Khasi Hills in Meghalaya, a region known for extremely high rainfall. Its higher altitude and location in the path of the Bay of Bengal monsoon branch, which is forced to rise (orographic lifting), cause it to receive significantly more rainfall than Kolkata, which is in the plains.
(v) Why is Kolkata rainier in July than in June unlike Shillong which is rainier in June than in July?
> **Answer:** Kolkata receives the Bay of Bengal monsoon branch, but the peak intensity often occurs slightly later than in the areas where the monsoon first hits the northeast (like Shillong). The monsoon flow over the plains might take until July to reach its peak intensity in Kolkata, whereas Shillong, being directly in the path and benefiting from early orographic lift, gets very heavy rain right from the onset in June.
(vi) Why does Delhi receive more rain than Jodhpur?
> **Answer:** Delhi receives rain from the Bay of Bengal branch of the monsoon, which travels westward across the Ganga plains. Jodhpur is located further west in Rajasthan, and the monsoon winds lose much of their moisture by the time they reach this region, resulting in significantly less rainfall compared to Delhi.

*   Thiruvananthapuram has equable climate?
> **Answer:** Thiruvananthapuram has an equable climate because it is a coastal city located near the equator. Coastal areas experience the moderating influence of the sea, which keeps temperatures relatively uniform throughout the year, preventing extremes.
*   Chennai has more rains only after the fury of monsoon is over in most parts of the country?
> **Answer:** Chennai receives the bulk of its rainfall during the retreating monsoon season (October-November). This is when low-pressure systems and cyclones originating in the Bay of Bengal move towards the eastern coast, bringing rain to the Tamil Nadu coast, after the Southwest Monsoon season (June-September) has ended in most other parts of India.
*   Jodhpur has a hot desert type of climate?
> **Answer:** Jodhpur has a hot desert type of climate due to its location in western Rajasthan, far from the moderating influence of the sea, and in a region that receives very low rainfall from the monsoon winds. It also lies within or near the subtropical high-pressure belt during certain times of the year, which is associated with dry, descending air.
*   Leh has moderate precipitation almost throughut the year?
> **Answer:** Leh is in a high-altitude cold desert region behind the Greater Himalayas. The Himalayas act as a barrier to the moisture-laden Southwest Monsoon winds. Precipitation is low throughout the year, occurring mainly as snowfall in winter and light rain/snow in other months, influenced by western disturbances or local convective activity, but it is generally low and distributed rather than concentrated in a monsoon season. (Note: The table shows very low annual rainfall for Leh, only 8.5 cm, suggesting "moderate precipitation" might be relative to other arid regions, but it's very low compared to most of India).
*   while in Delhi and Jodhpur most of the rain is confined to nearly three months, in Thiruvananthapuram and Shillong it is almost nine months of the year?
> **Answer:** In Delhi and Jodhpur, rainfall is concentrated during the Southwest Monsoon season (roughly June-September), which lasts about three months in these regions. Thiruvananthapuram receives rain from the early onset of the Southwest Monsoon and also from the retreating monsoon and pre-monsoon showers, extending its rainy period. Shillong, located in a high rainfall zone in the northeast, receives heavy rainfall from the Bay of Bengal monsoon branch for several months starting from June, and also gets pre-monsoon showers, leading to a much longer rainy season compared to Delhi and Jodhpur.
