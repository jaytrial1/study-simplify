# East Flowing Peninsular River Basins

## The Godavari Basin
The Godavari is the largest Peninsular river, rising from the Western Ghats slopes in Nasik, Maharashtra. It is about 1500 km long and drains into the Bay of Bengal. Its basin is the largest among Peninsular rivers, covering parts of Maharashtra (about 50%), Madhya Pradesh, Odisha, and Andhra Pradesh. Tributaries include the Purna, Wardha, Pranhita, Manjra, Wainganga, and Penganga (the last three are very large). Due to its size, it is known as the **'Dakshin Ganga'** (Southern Ganga).

## The Mahanadi Basin
The Mahanadi rises in the highlands of Chhattisgarh, flows through Odisha, and reaches the Bay of Bengal. It is about 860 km long. Its basin is shared by Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, and Odisha.

## The Krishna Basin
Rising from a spring near Mahabaleshwar, the Krishna flows about 1400 km to the Bay of Bengal. Tributaries include the Tungabhadra, Koyana, Ghatprabha, Musi, and Bhima. Its basin is shared by Maharashtra, Karnataka, and Andhra Pradesh.

## The Kaveri Basin
The Kaveri rises in the Brahmagiri range of the Western Ghats and reaches the Bay of Bengal south of Cuddalore, Tamil Nadu. It is about 760 km long. Its main tributaries are Amravati, Bhavani, Hemavati, and Kabini. Its basin drains parts of Karnataka, Kerala, and Tamil Nadu.

### Key Concept: Sivasamudram Waterfall
The river Kaveri forms the second biggest waterfall in India, known as Sivasamudram. Hydroelectric power from these falls is supplied to Mysore, Bangalore, and the Kolar Gold Field.

Besides these major rivers, some smaller rivers flow east, such as the Damodar, Brahmani, Baitarni, and Subarnarekha.
