## Short Answer Questions
(i) What is meant by a water divide? Give an example.
> **Answer:** A water divide is an elevated area, such as a mountain or upland, that separates two adjacent drainage basins. An example is the upland area that separates the Indus and Ganga river systems near Ambala.
(ii) Which is the largest river basin in India?
> **Answer:** The largest river basin in India is the Ganga basin.
(iii) Where do the rivers Indus and Ganga have their origin?
> **Answer:** The Indus river originates near Lake Mansarowar in Tibet. The Ganga river originates from the Gangotri Glacier (as Bhagirathi) and Satopanth Glacier (as Alaknanda) in the Himalayas.
(iv) Name the two headstreams of the Ganga. Where do they meet to form the Ganga?
> **Answer:** The two headstreams of the Ganga are the Bhagirathi and the Alaknanda. They meet at Devaprayag in Uttarakhand to form the Ganga.
(v) Why does the Brahmaputra in its Tibetan part have less silt, despite a longer course?
> **Answer:** The Brahmaputra river carries less water and silt in its Tibetan part because Tibet is a cold and dry area with less rainfall and sparse vegetation compared to the region it flows through in India.
(vi) Which two Peninsular rivers flow through trough?
> **Answer:** The two Peninsular rivers that flow through a trough (rift valley) are the Narmada and the Tapi.
(vii) State some economic benefits of rivers and lakes.
> **Answer:** Rivers and lakes provide water for irrigation, support navigation and transportation, are sources for hydroelectric power generation, supply drinking water, moderate climate, support fisheries, and promote tourism and recreation.
