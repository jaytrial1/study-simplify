# Drainage Systems in India
The drainage systems of India are mainly controlled by the broad relief features of the subcontinent. Accordingly, the Indian rivers are divided into two major groups:
- The **Himalayan rivers**
- The **Peninsular rivers**

Apart from originating from two major physiographic regions, the Himalayan and Peninsular rivers differ in many ways. Most Himalayan rivers are **perennial**, meaning they have water throughout the year, receiving it from rain and melted snow. The Indus and Brahmaputra originate north of the mountains and cut through them, forming gorges. Himalayan rivers have long courses and perform intensive erosional activity in their upper courses, carrying huge loads of silt and sand.

Peninsular rivers are largely **seasonal**, dependent on rainfall. During the dry season, their flow reduces. They have shorter and shallower courses compared to Himalayan rivers. However, some originate in the central highlands and flow westwards.

> **Answer to question about west-flowing Peninsular rivers:** Two large rivers that originate in the central highlands and flow towards the west are the Narmada and the Tapi.
