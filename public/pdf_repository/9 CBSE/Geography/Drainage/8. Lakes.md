# Lakes
Lakes are significant for tourism and are useful in many ways. India has many lakes varying in size and characteristics. Most are permanent, while some in semi-arid regions are seasonal, containing water only during the rainy season. Lakes form due to various processes: glacial action (digging out basins filled by snowmelt), wind, river action, tectonic activity, and human activities.

A meandering river on a flood plain can form **ox-bow lakes** from cut-offs. Spits and bars in coastal areas form **lagoons**, such as the Chilika lake, Pulicat lake, and Kolleru lake. Lakes in inland drainage regions are sometimes seasonal, like the Sambhar lake in Rajasthan, a saltwater lake used for salt production.

Most freshwater lakes are in the Himalayan region and are of glacial origin. The Wular lake in Jammu and Kashmir, however, is a result of tectonic activity and is the largest freshwater lake in India. Other important freshwater lakes include Dal lake, Bhimtal, Nainital, Loktak, and Barapani.

Lakes are of great value:
*   They regulate river flow, preventing floods during heavy rain and maintaining flow in the dry season.
*   They can be used for developing hydel power.
*   They moderate the climate of surroundings.
*   They maintain aquatic ecosystems.
*   They enhance natural beauty, help develop tourism, and provide recreation.
