# River Pollution and Conservation
Growing domestic, municipal, industrial, and agricultural demand affects the quality of river water. More water is drained out, reducing river volume. Simultaneously, untreated sewage and industrial effluents are dumped into rivers, affecting water quality and the river's self-cleansing capacity. For example, with adequate flow, Ganga water can dilute pollution within 20 km of large cities, but increasing urbanization and industrialization prevent this, causing pollution levels to rise.

Concern over rising river pollution led to various action plans.
> **Answer to question about action plans:** Yes, there have been action plans to clean rivers, such as the Ganga Action Plan (GAP) and the National River Conservation Plan (NRCP).

### National River Conservation Plan (NRCP)
The Ganga Action Plan (GAP) phase-I (1985-2000) aimed to improve Ganga's water quality. Based on lessons learned, GAP Phase-II was merged with the expanded National River Conservation Plan (NRCP). The NRCP now covers 152 towns along 27 interstate rivers in 16 states, undertaking pollution abatement works like intercepting, diverting, and treating sewage.

> **Answer to question about health effects of polluted water:** Polluted river water can cause various health problems, including waterborne diseases like cholera, typhoid, dysentery, and jaundice, due to pathogens and harmful chemicals present in the water.
