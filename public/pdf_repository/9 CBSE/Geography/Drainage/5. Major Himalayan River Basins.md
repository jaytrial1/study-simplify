# Major Himalayan River Basins

## The Indus River System
The river Indus rises in Tibet, near Lake Mansarowar. It flows west, entering India in Ladakh (Jammu and Kashmir), forming a picturesque gorge. Tributaries like the Zaskar, Nubra, Shyok, and Hunza join it in the Kashmir region. The Indus flows through Baltistan and Gilgit, emerging from the mountains at Attock. The Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, and Jhelum join the Indus near Mithankot in Pakistan. The Indus then flows south to the Arabian Sea, east of Karachi. The Indus plain has a very gentle slope. With a total length of 2900 km, the Indus is one of the world's longest rivers. A little over a third of the Indus basin is in India (Jammu and Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab), and the rest is in Pakistan.

### Key Concept: Indus Water Treaty
According to the regulations of the Indus Water Treaty (1960), India can use only 20 percent of the total water carried by the Indus river system. This water is primarily used for irrigation in Punjab, Haryana, and parts of Rajasthan.

## The Ganga River System
The headwaters of the Ganga are the **Bhagirathi** (fed by the Gangotri Glacier) and the **Alaknanda**. They join at Devaprayag in Uttarakhand to form the Ganga.

At Haridwar, the Ganga emerges from the mountains onto the plains.

Many Himalayan tributaries join the Ganga, including major rivers like the Yamuna, Ghaghara, Gandak, and Kosi. The Yamuna rises from the Yamunotri Glacier, flows parallel to the Ganga, and joins it as a right bank tributary at Allahabad. The Ghaghara, Gandak, and Kosi rise in the Nepal Himalaya. These rivers often flood the northern plains annually, causing damage but also enriching the soil for agriculture.

Peninsular tributaries like the Chambal, Betwa, and Son rise from semi-arid areas, have shorter courses, and carry less water.
> **Answer to question about where Peninsular tributaries join Ganga:** The Chambal, Betwa, and Son rivers join the Yamuna river, which is a major tributary of the Ganga, or they join the Ganga directly downstream.

Enlarged by tributaries, the Ganga flows east to Farakka in West Bengal, the northernmost point of the Ganga delta. Here, the river bifurcates: the Bhagirathi-Hooghly (a distributary) flows south to the Bay of Bengal, while the mainstream flows south into Bangladesh, where it is joined by the Brahmaputra. Further downstream, it's known as the Meghna and flows into the Bay of Bengal. The combined delta is the **Sunderban delta**.

### Key Concept: Sunderban Delta
The Sunderban Delta is derived from the Sundari tree that grows there. It is the world's largest and fastest-growing delta and is home to the Royal Bengal tiger.

The Ganga's length is over 2500 km.
> **Answer to question about Ganga drainage pattern:** Looking at Figure 3.4, the Ganga river system, especially its tributaries joining the main river, shows a predominantly **dendritic** drainage pattern, resembling tree branches.

Ambala is on the water divide between the Indus and Ganga systems. The plains from Ambala to the Sunderban stretch nearly 1800 km with a small slope drop (1m per 6km), causing the river to develop large meanders.

## The Brahmaputra River System
The Brahmaputra rises in Tibet east of Lake Mansarowar, near the sources of the Indus and Satluj. It's slightly longer than the Indus, but most of its course is outside India. It flows east parallel to the Himalayas. Near Namcha Barwa (7757 m), it takes a 'U' turn through a gorge and enters India in Arunachal Pradesh, called the Dihang. It's joined by tributaries like the Dibang and Lohit to form the Brahmaputra in Assam.

### Key Concept: Brahmaputra Names
The Brahmaputra is known as the **Tsang Po** in Tibet and the **Jamuna** in Bangladesh.

In Tibet, the river carries less water and silt due to the cold, dry climate. In India, it passes through a high rainfall region, carrying a large volume of water and considerable silt. The Brahmaputra has a braided channel in Assam, forming many riverine islands.
> **Answer to question about largest riverine island:** The world's largest riverine island formed by the Brahmaputra is **Majuli**.

During the rainy season, the river overflows, causing floods in Assam and Bangladesh. Unlike other north Indian rivers, the Brahmaputra accumulates huge silt deposits on its bed, causing it to rise and shift its channel frequently.
