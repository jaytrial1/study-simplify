# Theory of Plate Tectonics

This section explains the fundamental theory of plate tectonics as the basis for understanding the formation of Earth's physical features, including those in India.

Earth scientists use theories based on evidence to explain the formation of physical features. One widely accepted theory is the "**Theory of Plate Tectonics**". According to this theory, the Earth's crust (the upper layer) is composed of seven major and several minor plates.

The movement of these plates creates stresses within the plates and the continental rocks above them, leading to geological activities such as *folding*, *faulting*, and *volcanic activity*. Plate movements are broadly classified into three types:

*   **Convergent Boundary:** Plates move towards each other. They may collide and crumble, or one plate may slide under the other (subduction).
*   **Divergent Boundary:** Plates move away from each other.
*   **Transform Boundary:** Plates move horizontally past each other.

The image depicts three primary types of plate boundaries found in plate tectonics. Each boundary is represented by a simplified diagram showing two adjacent tectonic plates and their relative movement. The mantle beneath the plates is also indicated.

**Key Elements of Each Boundary Type:**

*   **Convergent Boundary:** Two plates are moving towards each other. This is indicated by arrows pointing inwards. At a convergent boundary, one plate typically subducts (slides) beneath the other. This process can lead to various geological features such as mountain ranges, volcanoes, and deep-sea trenches.

*   **Divergent Boundary:** Two plates are moving away from each other. Arrows point outward. This creates a space where molten material from the mantle can rise, forming new crust. Divergent boundaries are commonly found at mid-ocean ridges.

*   **Transform Boundary:** Two plates are sliding past each other horizontally. Arrows indicate movement in opposite directions along the boundary. Transform boundaries are characterized by frequent earthquakes due to the friction and pressure that build up as the plates grind against each other.
