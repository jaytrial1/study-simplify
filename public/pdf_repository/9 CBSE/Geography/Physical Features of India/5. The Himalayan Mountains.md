# The Himalayan Mountains

This section details the characteristics, divisions, and features of the Himalayan mountain range, which forms India's northern border.

The Himalayas are geologically young and structurally *fold mountains* stretching along the northern borders of India. They run from the Indus River in the west to the Brahmaputra River in the east, forming one of the world's loftiest and most rugged mountain barriers. The range forms an arc covering about 2,400 Km. Its width varies significantly, from 400 Km in Kashmir to 150 Km in Arunachal Pradesh. The eastern half of the Himalayas shows greater altitudinal variations compared to the western half.

The Himalayas consist of three parallel ranges running longitudinally:

1.  **The Great or Inner Himalayas or 'Himadri':** This is the northernmost and most continuous range. It contains the loftiest peaks with an average height of 6,000 metres, including all the prominent Himalayan peaks.

2.  **The Himachal or Lesser Himalaya:** Located south of the Himadri, this range forms the most rugged mountain system. It is mainly composed of highly compressed and altered rocks. Altitudes range between 3,700 and 4,500 metres, with an average width of 50 Km. Important ranges include the Pir Panjal (the longest and most important), Dhaula Dhar, and Mahabharat ranges. This region is famous for beautiful valleys like Kashmir, Kangra, and Kullu, and is well known for its hill stations.

    > **Answer (Find Out):**
    > -   Mussoorie, Nainital, and Ranikhet are famous hill stations located in the state of Uttarakhand.

3.  **The Shiwaliks:** This is the outermost range of the Himalayas, extending over a width of 10-50 Km with altitudes between 900 and 1100 metres. These ranges are formed from unconsolidated sediments brought down by rivers from the higher Himalayan ranges. The valleys in the Shiwaliks are covered with thick gravel and alluvium. The longitudinal valleys lying between the Lesser Himalaya and the Shiwaliks are called **Duns**, such as Dehra Dun, Kotli Dun, and Patli Dun.

Besides these longitudinal divisions, the Himalayas are also divided regionally from west to east based on river valleys:

*   Between Indus and Satluj rivers: **Punjab Himalaya** (also known regionally as Kashmir and Himachal Himalaya).
*   Between Satluj and Kali rivers: **Kumaon Himalayas**.
*   Between Kali and Tista rivers: **Nepal Himalayas**.
*   Between Tista and Dihang rivers: **Assam Himalayas**.
