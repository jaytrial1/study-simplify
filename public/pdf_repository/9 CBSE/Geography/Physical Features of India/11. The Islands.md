# The Islands

This section describes the two main groups of islands belonging to India: Lakshadweep in the Arabian Sea and Andaman & Nicobar in the Bay of Bengal.

Besides the vast mainland, India also has two main groups of islands.

1.  **Lakshadweep Islands:** This group lies close to the Malabar coast of Kerala in the Arabian Sea. It is composed of small **coral islands**. Originally known as Laccadive, Minicoy, and Amindive, they were renamed Lakshadweep in 1973. The group covers a small area of 32 sq km. **Kavaratti island** is its administrative headquarters. This island group is known for its great diversity of *flora* (plant life) and *fauna* (animal life). Pitti island, which is uninhabited, has a bird sanctuary.

    ### Key Concepts & Examples

    *   **Corals:** Short-lived microscopic organisms that live in colonies in shallow, mud-free, warm waters. They secrete calcium carbonate, forming coral deposits as reefs. Types include Barrier reefs (like the Great Barrier Reef of Australia), fringing reefs, and atolls (circular or horse-shoe shaped reefs).

2.  **Andaman and Nicobar Islands:** This is an elongated chain of islands located in the Bay of Bengal, extending from north to south. They are larger in size, more numerous, and scattered compared to Lakshadweep. The group is divided into the Andaman in the north and the Nicobar in the south. These islands are believed to be the elevated parts of submarine mountains. They hold great strategic importance for the country. Like Lakshadweep, they have great diversity of flora and fauna. Located close to the equator, they experience an *equatorial climate* and have thick forest cover.

    ### Key Concepts & Examples

    *   **Barren Island:** Located in the Andaman and Nicobar group, Barren Island is India's only active volcano.
