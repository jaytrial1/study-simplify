# Exercises and Activities

This section contains questions and prompts designed to test understanding and encourage further exploration of India's physical geography.

## Exercise Questions

1.  Choose the right answer from the four alternatives given below.
    (i) A landmass bounded by sea on three sides is referred to as
        (a) Coast
        (c) Peninsula
        (b) Island
        (d) none of the above
    > **Answer:** (c) Peninsula
    (ii) Mountain ranges in the eastern part of India forming its boundary with Myanmar are collectively called as
        (a) Himachal
        (c) Purvachal
        (b) Uttarakhand
        (d) none of the above
    > **Answer:** (c) Purvachal
    (iii) The western coastal strip, south of Goa is referred to as
        (a) Coromandel
        (c) Kannad
        (b) Konkan
        (d) Northern Circar
    > **Answer:** (c) Kannad
    (iv) The highest peak in the Eastern Ghats is
        (a) Anai Mudi
        (c) Mahendragiri
        (b) Kanchenjunga
        (d) Khasi
    > **Answer:** (c) Mahendragiri

2.  Answer the following questions briefly.
    (i) What are tectonic plates?
    > **Answer:** Tectonic plates are large, rigid slabs that make up the Earth's crust and upper mantle. They are constantly moving slowly.
    (ii) Which continents of today were part of the Gondwana land?
    > **Answer:** India, Australia, South Africa, South America, and Antarctica were part of the Gondwana land.
    (iii) What is the bhabar?
    > **Answer:** Bhabar is a narrow belt, 8 to 16 km wide, lying parallel to the slopes of the Shiwaliks. It consists of pebbles deposited by rivers, and streams disappear in this belt.
    (iv) Name the three major divisions of the Himalayas from north to south.
    > **Answer:** The three major divisions from north to south are the Great or Inner Himalayas (Himadri), the Lesser Himalaya (Himachal), and the Shiwaliks.
    (v) Which plateau lies between the Aravali and the Vindhyan ranges?
    > **Answer:** The Malwa plateau lies between the Aravali and the Vindhyan ranges.
    (vi) Name the island group of India having coral origin.
    > **Answer:** The Lakshadweep Islands are of coral origin.

3.  Distinguish between
    (i) Converging and diverging tectonic plates
    > **Answer:** Converging tectonic plates move towards each other, often leading to collision, subduction, mountain building, and volcanic activity. Diverging tectonic plates move away from each other, creating new crust and often associated with mid-ocean ridges and rift valleys.
    (ii) Bhangar and Khadar
    > **Answer:** Bhangar is the older alluvial soil found above the floodplains of rivers, forming a terrace-like feature and containing calcareous deposits (kankar). Khadar is the newer, younger alluvial soil deposited in the floodplains every year, making it very fertile and suitable for intensive agriculture.
    (iii) Western Ghats and Eastern Ghats
    > **Answer:** The Western Ghats lie parallel to the western coast, are continuous, higher (average 900-1600m), cause orographic rain, and their height increases from north to south. The Eastern Ghats lie parallel to the eastern coast, are discontinuous and irregular, lower (average 600m), and are dissected by rivers flowing into the Bay of Bengal.

4.  Describe how the Himalayas were formed.
    > **Answer:** The Himalayas were formed due to the collision of the northward-drifting Indo-Australian plate with the Eurasian plate. This collision folded and uplifted the sedimentary rocks that had accumulated in the Tethys Sea, resulting in the formation of the vast Himalayan mountain system.

5.  Which are the major physiographic divisions of India? Contrast the relief of the Himalayan region with that of the Peninsular plateau.
    > **Answer:** The major physiographic divisions of India are the Himalayan Mountains, the Northern Plains, the Peninsular Plateau, the Indian Desert, the Coastal Plains, and the Islands.
    > **Contrast:**
    > -   **Himalayan Region:** Geologically young and unstable fold mountains with youthful topography (high peaks, deep valleys, fast-flowing rivers). Formed recently from the collision of tectonic plates.
    > -   **Peninsular Plateau:** One of the Earth's oldest and most stable landmasses composed of ancient crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks. Features include broad and shallow valleys and rounded hills, representing a much older and eroded landscape.

6.  Give an account of the Northern Plains of India.
    > **Answer:** The Northern Plains are a vast, fertile plain formed by the deposition of alluvial soil by the Indus, Ganga, and Brahmaputra rivers and their tributaries. They cover about 7 lakh sq km, are 2400 km long, and 240-320 km wide. They are agriculturally productive and densely populated. The plains can be divided into regions like Bhabar (pebble belt), Terai (wet, marshy land), Bhangar (older alluvium above floodplains), and Khadar (newer alluvium in floodplains).

7.  Write short notes on the following.
    (i) The Indian Desert
    > **Answer:** The Indian Desert (Thar Desert) is an arid region west of the Aravali Hills, characterized by an undulating sandy plain covered with sand dunes. It receives less than 150 mm of rainfall annually and has sparse vegetation. Luni is the only significant river, and streams disappear into the sand during the dry season. Barchans (crescent dunes) are common.
    (ii) The Central Highlands
    > **Answer:** The Central Highlands are the northern part of the Peninsular Plateau, north of the Narmada river, covering much of the Malwa plateau. They are bounded by the Vindhyan range to the south and the Aravalis to the northwest. Rivers here flow southwest to northeast. They are wider in the west and include eastern extensions like Bundelkhand, Baghelkhand, and the Chotanagpur plateau.
    (iii) The Island groups of India
    > **Answer:** India has two main island groups. Lakshadweep in the Arabian Sea is a group of small coral islands near the Malabar coast, known for flora/fauna diversity and Kavaratti as its capital. The Andaman and Nicobar Islands in the Bay of Bengal are an elongated chain, larger and more scattered, believed to be elevated submarine mountains. They have high biodiversity, strategic importance, equatorial climate, and thick forests, and are home to India's only active volcano (Barren Island).
