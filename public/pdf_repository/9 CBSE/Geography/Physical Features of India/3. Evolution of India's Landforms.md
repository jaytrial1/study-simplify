# Evolution of India's Landforms

This section explains how the Indian subcontinent's current physical features, particularly the Himalayas and Northern Plains, were formed through the movement of tectonic plates.

The oldest landmass, the Peninsular part of India, was originally part of a giant ancient continent called **Gondwana land**. Gondwana land included present-day India, Australia, South Africa, South America, and Antarctica as a single connected landmass. Convectional currents within the Earth's mantle caused the crust to split into pieces. This led to the *Indo-Australian plate* breaking away from Gondwana land and drifting northward.

The northward drift resulted in a collision between the Indo-Australian plate and the much larger *Eurasian Plate*. This powerful collision caused the sedimentary rocks that had accumulated in a large depression (geosyncline) known as the *Tethys Sea* to fold and uplift, forming the extensive mountain systems of western Asia and the **Himalaya**.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Gondwana land:** The ancient southern supercontinent that included India, Australia, South Africa, South America, and Antarctica.
*   **Angara Land:** The northern part of the ancient supercontinent Pangea, located north of the Tethys Sea.

The uplift of the Himalayas from the Tethys Sea and the sinking (subsidence) of the northern edge of the Peninsular plateau created a large basin. Over time, this depression was gradually filled with sediments carried by rivers flowing from the Himalayas in the north and the Peninsular plateau in the south. This deposition of vast amounts of *alluvium* formed the flat and fertile **Northern Plains of India**.

Geologically, the Peninsular Plateau is one of the Earth's oldest and most stable landmasses, composed of ancient crystalline, igneous, and metamorphic rocks. In contrast, the Himalayas and the Northern Plains are much younger landforms. The Himalayan mountain system is considered an unstable zone, displaying a youthful topography characterized by high peaks, deep valleys, and fast-flowing rivers. The Northern Plains are formed by extensive alluvial deposits, while the Peninsular plateau features gently rising hills and wide valleys formed from older, harder rocks.
