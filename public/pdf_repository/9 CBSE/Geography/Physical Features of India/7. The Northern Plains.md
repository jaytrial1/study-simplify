# The Northern Plains

This section describes the formation, extent, and characteristics of the fertile plains formed by the major river systems of North India.

The Northern Plain was formed by the combined action of three major river systems: the Indus, the Ganga, and the Brahmaputra, along with their tributaries. This vast plain is composed of *alluvial soil*, deposited over millions of years in a large basin at the foothills of the Himalayas.

The plain covers an area of 7 lakh sq. km, is about 2400 Km long, and ranges from 240 to 320 Km in width. Due to its rich soil, adequate water supply, and favorable climate, it is a highly agriculturally productive and densely populated region of India.

Rivers flowing from the northern mountains are involved in depositional work. In their lower course, the gentle slope causes the river velocity to decrease, leading to the formation of **riverine islands**.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Majuli:** Located in the Brahmaputra River, Majuli is the largest inhabited riverine island in the world.

In their lower course, rivers often split into numerous channels called **distributaries** due to the deposition of silt.

The Northern Plain is broadly divided into three sections:

1.  **The Punjab Plains:** The western part, formed by the Indus and its tributaries (Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Satluj), which originate in the Himalaya. A larger part of this plain lies in Pakistan. This section is characterized by **doabs**.

    ### Key Concepts & Examples

    *   **Doab:** A term made of two words - 'do' meaning two and 'ab' meaning water, referring to the land between two rivers.
    *   **Punjab:** Made of two words - 'Punj' meaning five and 'ab' meaning water, referring to the land of five rivers.

2.  **The Ganga Plain:** Extends between the Ghaggar and Teesta rivers, covering states like Haryana, Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, parts of Jharkhand, and West Bengal.

3.  **The Brahmaputra Plain:** Lies mainly in Assam, to the east of the Ganga plain.

Although often described as flat, the Northern Plains have diverse relief features. Based on these variations, they can be divided into four regions:

1.  **Bhabar:** A narrow belt (8-16 km wide) parallel to the Shiwalik slopes where rivers deposit pebbles after descending from the mountains. Streams disappear in this belt.
2.  **Terai:** South of the Bhabar belt, streams and rivers re-emerge, creating a wet, swampy, and marshy region. Originally thickly forested and rich in wildlife, much of it has been cleared for agriculture and settlement.

    > **Answer (Find Out):**

3.  **Bhangar:** The largest part of the northern plain, formed of older alluvium. It lies above the floodplains and shows a terrace-like feature. The soil in this region contains calcareous deposits called **kankar**.
4.  **Khadar:** The newer, younger alluvial deposits of the floodplains. These are renewed almost every year, making them very fertile and ideal for intensive agriculture.
