This section highlights the contributions of cooperative societies and non-governmental organizations in ensuring food security, particularly at the local level.

# Role of Cooperatives in Food Security

**Cooperatives** also play a significant role in food security in India, especially in the southern and western parts of the country. Cooperative societies establish shops that sell low-priced goods to poor people. For example, in Tamil Nadu, around 94% of fair price shops are run by cooperatives.

In Delhi, **Mother Dairy** is a cooperative making strides in providing milk and vegetables to consumers at controlled rates set by the government. **Amul** in Gujarat is another successful example of a cooperative in milk and milk products, credited with bringing about the **White Revolution**. These are examples of many cooperatives across the country helping ensure food security for different sections of society.

In Maharashtra, the **Academy of Development Science (ADS)** has facilitated a network of NGOs to set up **grain banks** in various regions. ADS provides training and capacity building on food security for these NGOs. Grain Banks are developing in different parts of Maharashtra. ADS's efforts to establish grain banks, encourage replication by other NGOs, and influence government policy on food security have been fruitful. The ADS Grain Bank programme is recognized as a successful and innovative intervention for food security.

# Summary

Food security in a nation requires that all citizens have enough nutritious food available, the capacity to buy food of acceptable quality, and no barriers to accessing food. People below the poverty line are often food insecure, while others may become insecure during calamities. The most affected groups in India include landless rural households and urban casual labourers in ill-paid, seasonal jobs. Food insecurity is higher in economically backward states, tribal/remote areas, and disaster-prone regions.

To ensure food availability for all, the Indian government implemented a food security system with two components: **buffer stock** and **public distribution system (PDS)**. Alongside PDS, various poverty alleviation programmes with a food security component were started, such as ICDS, FFW, Mid-Day Meals, and AAY.

Besides government efforts, various cooperatives and NGOs are actively working to improve food security, running fair price shops and establishing initiatives like grain banks.
