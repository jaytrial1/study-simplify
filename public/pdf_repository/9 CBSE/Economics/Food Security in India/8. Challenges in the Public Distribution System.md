# Challenges in PDS Functioning

The increased procurement at enhanced **MSP** is partly due to pressure from leading foodgrain producing states like Punjab, Haryana, and Andhra Pradesh. Procurement is concentrated in a few prosperous regions (Punjab, Haryana, Western Uttar Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, West Bengal) and mainly focuses on wheat and rice. The rise in MSP has encouraged farmers in surplus states to shift land from growing coarse grains (a staple food for the poor) to wheat and rice. Intensive water use for rice cultivation has also led to environmental degradation and falling water levels in these states, threatening sustainable agriculture.

According to NSSO report No. 558, per person per month consumption of rice declined in both rural and urban India between 2004-05 and 2011-12. However, per capita consumption of PDS rice doubled in rural India and increased by 66% in urban India since 2004-05. Per capita consumption of PDS wheat also doubled in both rural and urban India since 2004-05.

PDS dealers sometimes engage in malpractices:
*   Diverting grains to the open market for higher profits.
*   Selling poor quality grains at ration shops.
*   Irregular opening of shops.

It is common to find unsold stocks of poor quality grains at ration shops, which contributes to the large pile-up of foodgrains with the FCI.

Another factor in the decline of PDS in recent years is the shift from universal coverage to targeted schemes like **TPDS**. Previously, all families, poor and non-poor, had a ration card with a fixed quota of items at the same low price. Many low-income families just above the poverty line could benefit. With TPDS introducing different prices for different card types (BPL, APL), families above the poverty line receive very little discount, making them less incentivized to buy from ration shops as the price for APL families is almost as high as the open market price.
