This section describes the groups of people in India who are most vulnerable to food insecurity, using real-life examples to illustrate their situations.

# Who are Food-Insecure?

A large number of people in India suffer from **food and nutrition insecurity**. The most affected groups include:
*   **Landless people** with little or no land.
*   **Traditional artisans** and providers of traditional services.
*   **Petty self-employed workers**.
*   **Destitutes**, including beggars.

In urban areas, food-insecure families often have working members employed in **ill-paid occupations** and the **casual labour market**. These workers are frequently engaged in **seasonal activities** and receive very low wages that are barely enough for survival.

## Story of Ramu

Ramu is a casual agricultural labourer in Raipur village. His eldest son, Somu (10 years old), also works part-time looking after cattle for the Sarpanch, earning Rs 1,000 per year. Ramu has several younger children. His wife, Sunhari, works part-time cleaning livestock sheds and managing cow dung, receiving half a litre of milk and some cooked food and vegetables daily. She also works in the fields with Ramu during busy seasons to supplement his income.

Agriculture is seasonal, employing Ramu only during sowing, transplanting, and harvesting. He is unemployed for about 4 months a year during the plant consolidation and maturing period. During this time, he seeks work in other activities like brick laying or construction.

With all his efforts, Ramu usually earns enough (cash or kind) for two square meals for his family. However, when he cannot find work, he and his family face difficulties, and his young kids sometimes go to sleep without food. Milk and vegetables are not regular parts of their meals. Ramu is **food insecure** during the 4 months when he is unemployed due to the seasonal nature of agricultural work.

## Let's Discuss

*   Why is agriculture a seasonal activity?
    > **Answer:** Agriculture is seasonal because different crops require specific weather conditions and stages of growth (like sowing, growing, harvesting) that occur at particular times of the year.
*   Why is Ramu unemployed for about four months in a year?
    > **Answer:** Ramu is unemployed for about four months because agriculture, his main source of work, is a seasonal activity, and there is no work for him during the periods when the plants are consolidating and maturing.
*   What does Ramu do when he is unemployed?
    > **Answer:** When Ramu is unemployed in agriculture, he looks for work in other activities like brick laying or construction in the village.
*   Who are supplementing income in Ramu's family?
    > **Answer:** Ramu's wife, Sunhari (working part-time as a cleaner and in the fields), and his eldest son, Somu (looking after cattle), are supplementing the family's income.
*   Why does Ramu face difficulty when he is unable to have work?
    > **Answer:** When Ramu is unable to find work, his family faces difficulty because his income is essential for buying food and other necessities, and they sometimes have to go without meals.
*   When is Ramu food insecure?
    > **Answer:** Ramu is food insecure during the approximately four months of the year when he remains unemployed due to the seasonal nature of agricultural work.

## Story of Ahmad

Ahmad is a rickshaw puller in Bangalore who migrated from Jhumri Taliah with his large family (3 brothers, 2 sisters, old parents). They live in a *jhuggi* (slum dwelling). The survival of his entire family depends on his daily earnings from pulling a rickshaw.

His employment is not secured, and his earnings vary daily. Some days he earns enough to save a little after buying necessities. On other days, he barely earns enough for daily needs. Fortunately, Ahmad has a **yellow card**, which is a **PDS Card** for people **below the poverty line**. With this card, he can buy sufficient quantities of wheat, rice, sugar, and kerosene oil at half the market price from a ration shop on specific days. This helps Ahmad support his large family, even with his fluctuating and often insufficient earnings, as he is the only earning member.

## Let's Discuss

*   Does Ahmad have a regular income from rickshaw-pulling?
    > **Answer:** No, Ahmad does not have a regular income from rickshaw-pulling; his earnings fluctuate every day.
*   How does the yellow card help Ahmad run his family even with small earnings from rickshaw-pulling?
    > **Answer:** The yellow card allows Ahmad to buy essential food items (wheat, rice, sugar, kerosene) at a subsidized price (half the market price) from the ration shop. This significantly reduces his expenditure on food, enabling him to manage his household and feed his large family even with his limited and fluctuating earnings.

The social composition and inability to buy food also contribute to food insecurity. **SCs, STs, and some sections of OBCs** (lower castes) with poor land-base or low land productivity are prone to food insecurity. People affected by natural disasters who migrate for work are also highly vulnerable.

A high rate of **malnutrition** exists among women, which is serious as it puts unborn babies at risk of malnutrition. A significant portion of pregnant and nursing mothers and children under 5 years old are also food insecure. According to the National Health and Family Survey (NHFS) 1998-99, about 11 crore such women and children were food insecure.

Food-insecure people are concentrated in certain regions, such as economically backward states with high poverty, tribal and remote areas, and regions prone to natural disasters. States like Uttar Pradesh (eastern and south-eastern), Bihar, Jharkhand, Orissa, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, and parts of Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra have the largest numbers of food-insecure people.
