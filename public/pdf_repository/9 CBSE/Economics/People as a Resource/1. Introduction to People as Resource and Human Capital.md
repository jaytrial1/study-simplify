This section introduces the concept of population as an economic asset, explaining how investment in education, training, and health transforms people into human capital.

# People as Resource

The chapter 'People as Resource' explains population as an asset for the economy rather than a liability. Population becomes **human capital** when investment is made in the form of **education, training, and medical care**. Human capital is the stock of skill and productive knowledge embodied in people.

'People as Resource' refers to a country's working population in terms of their existing productive skills and abilities. This perspective highlights the population's ability to contribute to the creation of the **Gross National Product**. Like other resources, population is a resource – a 'human resource'. This is the positive side of a large population, often overlooked when considering only the problems of providing food, education, and health facilities.

When the existing 'human resource' is further developed through education and health, it is called **'human capital formation'**. This adds to the productive power of the country, similar to 'physical capital formation'.

Investment in human capital (education, training, medical care) yields a return, just like investment in physical capital. This return is seen as:
*   Higher incomes earned due to the higher productivity of educated or trained persons.
*   Higher productivity of healthier people.

Examples:
*   India's **Green Revolution** shows how knowledge (improved production technologies) increased the productivity of land.
*   India's **IT revolution** highlights the importance of human capital over material resources.

## Let's Discuss

Looking at the photograph can you explain how a doctor, teacher, engineer and a tailor are an asset to the economy?
> **Answer:** A doctor contributes by keeping the workforce healthy, reducing sick days and improving productivity. A teacher educates the population, increasing skills and knowledge, which leads to higher productivity and innovation. An engineer designs and builds infrastructure and technology, essential for economic growth. A tailor provides necessary goods (clothing), potentially runs a small business, and contributes to the local economy. All these professions represent skilled human capital that provides valuable services or goods, adding to the national income.

Not only do educated and healthier people gain higher incomes, but society also benefits indirectly. The advantages of a more educated or healthier population spread to those not directly educated or given health care. Human capital is superior to resources like land and physical capital because human resources can make use of land and capital, while land and capital cannot become useful on their own.

For many decades in India, a large population was considered a liability. However, it can be turned into a productive asset through investment in human capital (education, health, training, research).
