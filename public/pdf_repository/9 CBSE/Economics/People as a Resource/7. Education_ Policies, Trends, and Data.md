# Education

Sakal's education was crucial for his success, providing him with opportunities, aspirations, and values. Education benefits not just individuals but also society by:
*   Enhancing national income.
*   Enriching cultural heritage.
*   Increasing governance efficiency.

The government has policies aimed at improving education:
*   Providing universal access, retention, and quality in elementary education, especially for girls.
*   Establishing pace-setting schools like Navodaya Vidyalayas in each district.
*   Developing vocational streams to equip high school students with job-related knowledge and skills.

Government expenditure on education has significantly increased over the years. The plan outlay on education rose from Rs 151 crore in the first plan to Rs 99,300 crore in 2020-21. Expenditure on education as a percentage of GDP increased from 0.64% in 1951-52 to 3.1% in 2019-20 (B.E.), though it remained stagnant around 3% for several years and declined slightly to 2.8% in 2020-21 (B.E.).

Literacy rates have improved significantly, from 18% in 1951 to 85% in 2018. Literacy is essential for citizens to perform duties and enjoy rights. However, disparities exist:
*   Literacy among males is about 16.1% higher than females.
*   Literacy is about 14.2% higher in urban areas compared to rural areas.
*   As per the 2011 census, literacy rates varied widely across states (e.g., 94% in Kerala vs. 62% in Bihar).

The primary school system has expanded greatly (over 7,78,842 schools in 2019-20). However, challenges include poor quality of schooling and high dropout rates.

**Samagra Shiksha** is a scheme from pre-school to Class XII aiming for improved school effectiveness, equal opportunities, and equitable learning outcomes, aligned with Sustainable Development Goals. It views the school as a continuum from pre-school to secondary levels.

The **Mid-day meal scheme** aims to boost attendance, retention, and nutritional status of children.

These policies contribute to increasing India's literate population.

The Gross Enrolment Ratio (GER) in higher education (age 18-23) was 27% in 2020-21, close to the world average. Strategies focus on increasing access, quality, curriculum adaptation, vocationalisation, and using IT. There is also focus on distance education and integrating formal, non-formal, distance, and IT education.
