This section discusses the distinction between market and non-market activities, particularly focusing on the historical and cultural division of labor between men and women and its impact on recognition in national income.

# Market and Non-Market Activities

Due to historical and cultural reasons, there is often a division of labour between men and women in families. Women typically handle domestic chores, while men work outside the home in fields or other jobs.

Example from the story:
*   Sakal's mother, Sheela, cooks, cleans, washes clothes, and looks after children (domestic chores).
*   Sakal's father, Buta, cultivates the field, sells produce, and earns money (market activity).

Sheela is not paid for her domestic services, while Buta earns money. The household work done by women is generally *not* recognized or included in the National Income calculation.

However, women are paid for their work when they participate in the labour market, like Geeta (Vilas's mother) who earned income by selling fish. Women's earnings in the market, like men's, are determined by education and skill.

Education helps individuals utilize economic opportunities better. Education and skill are major factors determining earnings in the market. Many women have limited education and low skill levels, leading to lower pay compared to men. Most women in the labour market work in sectors with poor job security, meagre legal protection, irregular and low income, and absence of basic facilities like maternity leave and social security.

Women with high education and skill, however, are paid comparably to men. They are often found in organized sectors like teaching and medicine, and increasingly in administrative, scientific, and technological fields.
