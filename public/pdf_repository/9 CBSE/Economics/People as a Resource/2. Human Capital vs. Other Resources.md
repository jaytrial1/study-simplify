This section compares human capital with other traditional resources like land and physical capital, emphasizing the unique role of human resources.

# Human Capital's Unique Role

Human capital, formed through investment in education and health, is crucial for economic development. Unlike land and physical capital, which are passive resources, human capital is active and can utilize other resources effectively.

Investment in human resource (via education and medical care) can give high rates of return in the future. This investment on people is comparable to investment in land and capital.

A child, with investments made in her education and health, can yield a high return in the future in the form of higher earnings and greater contribution to society.

Educated parents tend to invest more heavily in their child's education and are more conscious of proper nutrition and hygiene, creating a **virtuous cycle**. In contrast, disadvantaged parents, lacking education and hygiene awareness, may perpetuate a **vicious cycle** for their children.

Countries like Japan, despite lacking natural resources, became developed and rich by investing heavily in human resources, particularly education and health. These skilled people made efficient use of other resources and developed technology, driving economic growth.
