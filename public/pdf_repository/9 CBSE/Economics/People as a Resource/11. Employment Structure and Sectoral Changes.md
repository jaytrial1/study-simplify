This section describes the distribution of employment across the primary, secondary, and tertiary sectors in India and discusses recent shifts in this structure.

# Employment Structure

The employment structure in India is characterized by **self-employment** in the primary sector, particularly agriculture. Entire families often work in the field, even if not all members are strictly needed, leading to disguised unemployment. However, the family shares the produce, which reduces the hardship of unemployment in rural areas, though it doesn't eliminate poverty. Gradually, surplus labour from households migrates from villages seeking jobs.

Let's discuss the employment scenario across the three sectors:
*   **Agriculture (Primary Sector):** Traditionally the most labour-absorbing sector. In recent years, dependence on agriculture has declined, partly due to disguised unemployment prompting migration.
*   **Secondary Sector:** Small-scale manufacturing is the most labour-absorbing part of this sector, attracting some surplus labour from agriculture.
*   **Tertiary Sector:** This sector is growing and absorbing labour, with new services like biotechnology and information technology emerging.
