This section presents two contrasting stories, illustrating the impact of investment in human capital (or lack thereof) on individual lives and potential economic contribution.

# Stories of Sakal and Vilas

The following cases illustrate how people can become more productive resources.

## Story of Sakal

Sakal was a twelve-year-old boy in Semapur village. His parents, Buta Chaudhary and Sheela, were eager for him to study. Despite helping his mother with chores and looking after siblings, they encouraged him to join the village school. Sakal was a bright student and completed his higher secondary education. His father took a loan for him to study a vocational computer course. Sakal excelled in the course and got a job in a private firm. He developed new software that increased the firm's sales, leading to a promotion and recognition from his boss. Sakal's education and skill made him a valuable asset.

## Story of Vilas

Vilas, eleven years old, lived in the same village. His father, a fisherman, died when Vilas was young. His mother, Geeta, sold fish from a landowner's pond in the nearby market, earning only Rs 150 a day. Vilas developed arthritis, but his mother could not afford medical care. He did not attend school and was not interested in studies. He helped his mother with cooking and looked after his younger brother. When his mother fell sick, Vilas was forced to sell fish like her, earning a meagre income with no family support. Vilas lacked the investment in health and education that could have improved his circumstances.

## Let's Discuss

Do you notice any difference between the two friends? What are those?
> **Answer:** Yes, there are significant differences. Sakal received education and vocational training, leading to a skilled job and higher income. He also had access to basic health, not requiring frequent doctor visits. Vilas lacked education and health care, suffered from illness, and was forced into low-income, unskilled work like his mother. Sakal's life improved due to investment in human capital, while Vilas remained in a cycle of poverty and poor health.

## Activity

Visit a nearby village or a slum area and write down a case study of a boy or girl of your age facing the same condition as Vilas or Sakal.

Comparing Sakal and Vilas:
*   Sakal went to school; Vilas did not.
*   Sakal was healthy; Vilas suffered from arthritis and lacked medical care.
*   Sakal acquired a skill (computer programming) and got a good job; Vilas continued low-income work like his mother.

Sakal's education improved his labour quality and productivity, contributing to economic growth and resulting in a good salary. Vilas, without education or healthcare, remained an unskilled labourer earning a meagre income.

Investing in human resources (education and medical care) yields high future returns, comparable to investing in land and capital.
