# Who Will Provide the Labour?

After land, labour is the next necessary factor for production. Farming is hard work. Small farmers and their families provide the labour for their own fields. Medium and large farmers hire farm labourers to work on their fields.

Farm labourers come from landless families or families with small land plots. They do not have rights over the crops grown but are paid wages by the farmer. Wages can be in cash or kind (like crop) and sometimes include meals. Wages and employment duration vary widely depending on the region, crop, specific activity (sowing, harvesting), and duration (daily wage, seasonal, or annual).

Dala, a landless farm labourer in Palampur, works on daily wages, meaning he constantly searches for work. The government minimum wage for farm labour was Rs 300/day (March 2019), but Dala gets only Rs 160 due to heavy competition for work. He complains to Ramkali, another farm labourer. Both Dala and Ramkali are among the poorest in the village.

# The Capital Needed in Farming

Modern farming methods require significant capital, meaning farmers need more money than before.

1.  **Small Farmers:** Most small farmers must borrow money to arrange for capital. They borrow from large farmers, village moneylenders, or traders, often at very high interest rates, making repayment difficult.
    Savita, a small farmer with 1 hectare, needs capital for seeds, fertilisers, pesticides, water, and repairs, estimating Rs 3,000 for working capital. Without savings, she borrows from Tejpal Singh, a large farmer, at a very high 24% interest for four months. She also agrees to work on his field during harvest for a low wage of Rs 100/day. Despite the difficult terms and her own harvesting work and household responsibilities, Savita accepts because getting a loan is hard for small farmers.
2.  **Medium and Large Farmers:** These farmers have savings from farming. They can arrange for the necessary capital from their own funds.

    We have read about the three factors of production - land, labour and capital - and how they are used in farming. Let us fill in the blanks given below.
    Among the three factors of production, we found that labour is the most abundant factor of production. There are many people who are willing to work as farm labourers in the villages, whereas the opportunities of work are limited. They belong to either landless families or **families cultivating small plots**. They are paid low wages, and lead a difficult life.
    In contrast to labour, **land** is a scarce factor of production. Cultivated land area is **fixed**. Moreover, even the existing land is distributed **unequally** among the people engaged in farming. There are a large number of small farmers who cultivate small plots of land and live in **difficult** conditions not much better than the landless farm labourer. To make the maximum use of the existing land, farmers use **multiple cropping** and **modern farming methods**. Both these have led to increase in production of crops.
    Modern farming methods require a great deal of **capital**. Small farmers usually need to borrow money to arrange for the capital, and are put to great distress to repay the loan. Therefore, capital too is a scarce factor of production, particularly for the small farmers.
    Though both land and capital are scarce, there is a basic difference between the two factors of production. **Land** is a natural resource, whereas **Capital** is man-made. It is possible to increase capital, whereas land is fixed. Therefore, it is very important that we take good care of land and other natural resources used in farming.
