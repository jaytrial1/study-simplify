This section explores methods used in Palampur to grow more from the fixed land, including multiple cropping and modern farming techniques.

# Ways to Grow More from the Same Land

Palampur resembles a village in western Uttar Pradesh in terms of crops grown and facilities. All land is cultivated, with none left idle. Farmers grow **jowar** and **bajra** during the rainy (kharif) season, used as cattle feed. This is followed by **potato** cultivation between October and December. In the winter (rabi) season, **wheat** is sown. Farmers keep enough wheat for their families and sell the surplus in Raiganj market. **Sugarcane** is also grown and harvested annually, sold as raw cane or jaggery to traders in Shahpur.

The ability to grow three different crops a year is due to Palampur's well-developed irrigation system. Electricity arrived early, transforming irrigation from traditional Persian wheels to electric-run tubewells, which could irrigate much larger areas effectively. Government-installed tubewells were followed by private ones, resulting in the entire 200 hectares of cultivated land being irrigated by the mid-1970s.

Not all Indian villages have such high irrigation levels. Riverine plains and coastal regions are well-irrigated, while plateau regions like the Deccan have low irrigation. Less than 40% of India's total cultivated area is irrigated today, with remaining areas dependent on rainfall.

**Multiple cropping** is growing more than one crop on the same piece of land during the year. This is the most common way to increase production on a given area. All Palampur farmers grow at least two main crops, and many have added potato as a third crop in recent decades.

Another way to increase production is using **modern farming methods** for higher yield. **Yield** is the crop quantity produced on a piece of land in a single season. Until the mid-1960s, traditional seeds with low yields were used, requiring less irrigation and relying on natural manure (cow-dung), which was readily available.

The **Green Revolution** in the late 1960s introduced **High Yielding Varieties (HYV)** of seeds for wheat and rice. HYV seeds promised much greater grain amounts per plant, leading to significantly larger foodgrain production from the same land. However, HYV seeds need ample water, chemical fertilizers, and pesticides for best results.

Higher yields were achieved through a combination of HYV seeds, irrigation, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and other inputs.

Farmers in Punjab, Haryana, and Western Uttar Pradesh were pioneers in adopting modern farming methods in India. They used tubewells, HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, and pesticides. Some invested in farm machinery like tractors and threshers to speed up ploughing and harvesting. This resulted in high wheat yields.

In Palampur, traditional seeds yielded 1300 kg of wheat per hectare. With HYV seeds, the yield increased to 3200 kg per hectare, leading to a large increase in total wheat production and more surplus for farmers to sell.

*   Is it important to increase the area under irrigation? Why?
> **Answer:** Yes, it is important to increase the area under irrigation because farming in many parts of India still depends on uncertain rainfall. Irrigation allows farmers to grow multiple crops in a year and use modern farming methods like HYV seeds, which require plenty of water, thereby increasing overall production and yield.

*   What is the difference between multiple cropping and modern farming method?
> **Answer:** **Multiple cropping** is growing more than one crop on the same piece of land within a year. **Modern farming method** involves using technologies like HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and machinery to increase the yield from a single crop or across multiple crops. Multiple cropping focuses on using the land for more growing cycles, while modern farming focuses on increasing the output per growing cycle and per unit of land.

*   What is the working capital required by the farmer using modern farming methods?
> **Answer:** The working capital required includes money for HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, water (for irrigation), and cash for making payments for inputs and daily needs during the production process.

*   Modern farming methods require the farmer to start with more cash than before. Why?
> **Answer:** Modern farming methods require farmers to purchase inputs like HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, and pesticides from the market, unlike traditional methods where inputs like traditional seeds and natural manure were often available on the farm. They also need money for increased irrigation and potentially for buying or renting machinery. This requires more upfront cash investment.
