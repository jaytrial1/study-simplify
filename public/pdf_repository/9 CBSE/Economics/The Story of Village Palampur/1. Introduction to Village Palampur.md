# Introduction to Palampur

The story of Palampur is used to introduce basic concepts related to production. Farming is the main activity, but other activities like small-scale manufacturing, dairy, and transport also exist on a limited scale. These activities require various resources including natural resources, man-made items, human effort, and money.

Palampur is well-connected to neighbouring villages and towns. Raiganj, a large village, is 3 km away, connected by an all-weather road. This road also connects to the nearest small town, Shahpur. Various modes of transport, from bullock carts to motor vehicles, are seen on this road.

The village has about 450 families from different castes. Eighty upper-caste families own most of the land and live in large brick houses. The SCs (Dalits) make up one-third of the population and live in smaller houses, often made of mud and straw, in one corner of the village.

Most houses in Palampur have electricity. Electricity powers the tubewells for irrigation and is used in small businesses. The village has two primary schools, one high school, a government-run primary health centre, and a private dispensary.

The description indicates Palampur has well-developed infrastructure for roads, transport, electricity, irrigation, schools, and health services.
