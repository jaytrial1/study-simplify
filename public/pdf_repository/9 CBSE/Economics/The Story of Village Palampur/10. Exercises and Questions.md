2.  Modern farming methods require more inputs which are manufactured in industry. Do you agree?
    > **Answer:** Yes, modern farming methods require inputs like HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and machinery (tractors, threshers) which are manufactured in industries.

3.  How did the spread of electricity help farmers in Palampur?
    > **Answer:** The spread of electricity helped farmers in Palampur by transforming the irrigation system. It powered electric-run tubewells, which could irrigate much larger areas of land more effectively than traditional Persian wheels, enabling farmers to grow multiple crops throughout the year.

4.  Is it important to increase the area under irrigation? Why?
    > **Answer:** Yes, it is important to increase the area under irrigation because farming in many parts of India still depends on uncertain rainfall. Irrigation allows farmers to grow multiple crops in a year and use modern farming methods like HYV seeds, which require plenty of water, thereby increasing overall production and yield and reducing dependence on rain.

6.  Why are the wages for farm labourers in Palampur less than minimum wages?
    > **Answer:** The wages for farm labourers in Palampur are less than minimum wages because there is heavy competition for work among the large number of landless labourers and those from small landholding families. This high competition allows farmers to hire labour at rates lower than the government-set minimum wage.

8.  What are the different ways of increasing production on the same piece of land? Use examples to explain.
    > **Answer:** There are two main ways to increase production on the same piece of land:
    > 1.  **Multiple Cropping:** Growing more than one crop on the same land during the year. *Example:* In Palampur, farmers grow jowar/bajra (kharif), followed by potato (between Oct-Dec), and then wheat (rabi) on the same land.
    > 2.  **Modern Farming Methods:** Using HYV seeds, chemical fertilisers, pesticides, and improved irrigation (like tubewells) to increase the yield per crop. *Example:* Using HYV seeds for wheat in Palampur increased yield from 1300 kg/hectare to 3200 kg/hectare.

9.  Describe the work of a farmer with 1 hectare of land.
    > **Answer:** A farmer with 1 hectare of land is typically a small farmer. They cultivate their land primarily using family labour. They might use a combination of traditional and modern farming methods depending on their resources. Production from such a small plot is often barely enough for the family's consumption, leaving little or no surplus to sell. They often struggle to arrange capital for modern inputs and may need to borrow money at high interest rates. They might also need to work as farm labourers on larger farms to supplement their income, like Savita in the chapter.

10. How do the medium and large farmers obtain capital for farming? How is it different from the small farmers?
    > **Answer:** Medium and large farmers obtain capital for farming primarily from their own savings earned from selling surplus farm products. They have enough surplus production to generate significant income, a portion of which is saved and reinvested as capital for the next season. This is different from small farmers who have little or no surplus production, making it difficult to save. Small farmers typically have to borrow money from moneylenders, traders, or large farmers, often on unfavorable terms with high interest rates, to arrange for capital.

11. On what terms did Savita get a loan from Tajpal Singh? Would Savita's condition be different if she could get a loan from the bank at a low rate of interest?
    > **Answer:** Savita got a loan from Tejpal Singh at a very high interest rate of 24% for four months. Additionally, she had to agree to work on Tejpal Singh's field during the busy harvest season for a very low wage of Rs 100 per day, significantly less than the minimum wage.
    > If Savita could get a loan from a bank at a low rate of interest, her condition would be significantly different. She would not be burdened by high interest payments, making it much easier to repay the loan. She would also likely not be forced to work for Tejpal Singh under unfavorable terms, freeing up her time and effort to focus on her own farm and potentially seek better-paying work if needed. Access to cheap credit would reduce her distress and improve her financial situation.
