# Non-Farm Activities in Palampur

While farming is the main activity, only 25% of the working people in Palampur are involved in non-farm activities.

## Dairy - A Common Activity

Dairy is common in many Palampur families. People feed their buffalos on various grasses and the jowar and bajra grown during the rainy season. The milk is sold in Raiganj. Traders from Shahpur have set up collection-cum-chilling centres in Raiganj, from where milk is transported to distant towns and cities.

## Small-Scale Manufacturing

Less than fifty people are engaged in manufacturing in Palampur. Unlike large factories, manufacturing here uses simple methods on a small scale, mostly done at home or in fields with family labour. Hired labour is rare.

Mishrilal bought an electric sugarcane crushing machine and set it up on his field. He also buys sugarcane from other farmers and processes it into jaggery, which he sells to traders in Shahpur, making a small profit.

*   What capital did Mishrilal need to set up his jaggery manufacturing unit?
> **Answer:** Mishrilal needed fixed capital (the mechanical sugarcane crushing machine, electricity connection) and working capital (money to buy sugarcane from other farmers, money for electricity bills, etc.).

*   Who provides the labour in this case?
> **Answer:** The text states that manufacturing in Palampur is mostly done with the help of family labour, implying Mishrilal likely uses his family for labour, though he might hire help occasionally.

*   Can you guess why Mishrilal is unable to increase his profit?
> **Answer:** Mishrilal's manufacturing is small-scale, likely limiting the volume of jaggery he can produce. He also sells to local traders in Shahpur, who might buy at lower prices than he could get by selling directly in a larger market or processing further. Competition from other jaggery producers might also limit his profit margin.

*   Could you think of any reasons when he might face a loss?
> **Answer:** Mishrilal could face a loss if: the price of sugarcane increases significantly, the price of jaggery falls in Shahpur, the machine breaks down requiring expensive repairs, electricity supply is unreliable or costly, or if there is a poor sugarcane crop reducing availability.

*   Why does Mishrilal sell his jaggery to traders in Shahpur and not in his village?
> **Answer:** Mishrilal sells to traders in Shahpur because Shahpur is a town, likely offering a larger market and potentially better prices or a more reliable demand compared to selling only within the small village of Palampur.

## The Shopkeepers

Few people in Palampur are involved in trade. The shopkeepers buy goods from wholesale markets in cities and sell them in the village. Small general stores sell items like rice, wheat, sugar, tea, oil, biscuits, soap, toothpaste, batteries, candles, stationery, and some cloth. Some families near the bus stand use part of their houses to run small shops selling eatables.

Kareem opened a computer class centre. Noticing students from the village attending college in Shahpur and taking computer classes there, he hired two village women with computer degrees. He bought computers and set up classes in the front room of his house. High school students attend in good numbers.

*   In what ways is Kareem's capital and labour different from Mishrilal's?
> **Answer:** Kareem's fixed capital includes computers and the space used for the centre, while Mishrilal's is a crushing machine. Kareem's working capital is likely money for salaries, electricity, and supplies, while Mishrilal's is mainly for raw materials (sugarcane) and electricity. Regarding labour, Kareem hires educated labour (women with degrees), whereas Mishrilal likely uses family labour for manual tasks.

*   Why didn't someone start a computer centre earlier? Discuss the possible reasons.
> **Answer:** Possible reasons include: students from the village might not have been attending college in Shahpur in large numbers earlier, there might not have been qualified people with computer degrees available in the village, or the demand for computer classes might not have been recognized or sufficient until recently. Kareem seems to have identified this emerging need and opportunity.

## Transport: A Fast Developing Sector

Transport is a growing sector connecting Palampur and Raiganj. Rickshaw pullers, tonga drivers, jeep, tractor, truck drivers, and traditional bullock cart/bogey drivers ferry people and goods, earning money for their services. The number of people in transport has increased over the years.

Kishora, a farm labourer struggling to meet his family's needs, took a cheap government loan for poor households. He bought a buffalo and now sells its milk. He also attached a wooden cart to the buffalo and uses it for transport, carrying clay from the river Ganga for potters or jaggery/commodities to Shahpur. This transport work provides him additional income, allowing him to earn more than before.

*   What is Kishora's fixed capital?
> **Answer:** Kishora's fixed capital includes his buffalo and the wooden cart attached to it.

*   What do you think would be his working capital?
> **Answer:** Kishora's working capital would likely include money for buffalo feed, maintenance/repair of the cart, and any other immediate expenses related to his transport or dairy activities.

*   In how many production activities is Kishora involved?
> **Answer:** Kishora is involved in three production activities: farm labour, dairy (selling milk), and transport.

*   Would you say that Kishora has benefitted from better roads in Palampur?
> **Answer:** Yes, Kishora has likely benefitted from better roads in Palampur. Good roads facilitate transport activities, making it easier and perhaps more frequent for him to ferry goods and people between Palampur, Raiganj, Shahpur, and the river Ganga, thereby increasing his earning opportunities in transport.
