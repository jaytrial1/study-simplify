This section explains what farmers do with their produce after harvesting and how medium and large farmers use their surplus earnings.

# Sale of Surplus Farm Products

After harvesting, farmers keep some produce for family consumption and sell the surplus. Small farmers like Savita and Gobind's sons have little surplus because their total production is small and a large share is kept for their families. Medium and large farmers are the main suppliers of surplus wheat to the market.

Picture 1.1 shows bullock carts carrying wheat to the market. Traders buy the wheat and sell it to shopkeepers in towns and cities.

Tejpal Singh, a large farmer, has a surplus of 350 quintals of wheat. He sells this in the Raiganj market and earns significantly. He uses his earnings in various ways. Last year, he saved most of the money in his bank account, using it later to lend to farmers like Savita and to arrange working capital for the next season. This year, he plans to buy another tractor, increasing his fixed capital.

Other large and medium farmers also sell surplus produce. They save part of their earnings to buy capital for the next season, allowing them to fund farming from their own savings. Some also use savings to buy cattle, trucks, or set up shops, which serve as capital for non-farm activities.

*   Compare the production of wheat by the three farmers over the years.
> **Answer:** Farmer 1's production increases over the years (100 -> 120 -> 160) because they have a surplus each year that can be used as capital to increase production the next year. Farmer 2's production remains constant at 80 units because their surplus (40 units) is just enough to maintain the same level of production (Capital = 40, Production = 2 * 40 = 80). Farmer 3's production decreases over the years (60 -> 40 -> 0) because their surplus decreases (20 -> 0), leading to less capital for the next year, eventually resulting in zero production.

*   What happens to Farmer 3 in Year 3? Can he continue production? What will he have to do to continue production?
> **Answer:** In Year 3, Farmer 3's production is 0 units. He cannot continue production using his own surplus as capital, because he had no surplus in Year 2. To continue production, he will have to arrange for capital from elsewhere, likely by borrowing money from large farmers, moneylenders, or banks, similar to how small farmers obtain capital.
