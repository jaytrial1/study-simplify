This section explores the historical and socio-economic factors that have contributed to widespread poverty in India.

# Causes of Poverty in India

Poverty in India has multiple causes:

*   **Historical Factors:** Low economic development under British colonial rule, which ruined traditional industries and discouraged modern industrial growth.
*   **Low Growth Rate:** A persistent low growth rate until the 1980s limited job opportunities and income growth, coupled with high population growth.
*   **Limited Impact of Green Revolution:** While irrigation and the Green Revolution created agricultural jobs, their impact was geographically limited.
*   **Insufficient Industrial Job Creation:** Industries in both public and private sectors did not create enough jobs to absorb all job seekers.
*   **Urbanization of Poverty:** Many unable to find well-paying jobs in cities became informal laborers (rickshaw pullers, vendors, domestic servants) with irregular, low incomes, leading to the growth of urban slums.
*   **Income Inequalities:** Huge income disparities, primarily due to unequal distribution of land and resources. Land reform policies aimed at redistribution have not been effectively implemented in many states.
*   **Socio-Cultural and Economic Factors:**
    *   Spending on social obligations and religious ceremonies, even by the poor, leading to wasted income.
    *   Indebtedness: Small farmers need loans for agricultural inputs. Poor people, lacking savings, borrow at high interest rates and become trapped in debt, which is both a cause and effect of poverty.

## Let's Discuss

Observe some of the poor families around you and try to find the following:

*   Which social and economic group do they belong to?
*   Who are the earning members in the family?
*   What is the condition of the old people in the family?
*   Are all the children (both boys and girls) attending schools?

> **Answer:** This is an activity prompt for observation. To approach this, one would identify families perceived as poor, then try to gather information (respectfully and ethically) about their caste/tribe/economic category (e.g., casual labour), who earns income, how elderly members are cared for, and if all children attend school, relating these observations back to the concepts discussed in the chapter like vulnerable groups, earning members, and access to education.
