This section presents two case studies, one urban and one rural, to highlight the various dimensions of poverty through real-life examples.

# Typical Cases Illustrating Poverty

## Urban Case: Ram Saran

Thirty-three year old Ram Saran works as a daily-wage laborer in a wheat flour mill near Ranchi in Jharkhand. His monthly earnings are about ₹3,500 when employed, which is inconsistent. This income is insufficient to support his family of six (wife and four children aged 6 months to 12 years). He also sends money to his elderly parents in a village near Ramgarh, who depend on him, as does his brother in Hazaribagh.

Ram Saran's family lives in a rented one-room temporary shack in a crowded slum on the city's outskirts. His wife, Santa Devi, earns an additional ₹1500 working as a part-time maid. They manage a meager diet of *dal* and rice twice a day, which is often not enough for everyone. Their elder son supplements the family income by working as a helper in a tea shop, earning ₹700. Their 10-year-old daughter attends school and cares for younger siblings. They own only two pairs of hand-me-down clothes each, buying new ones only when necessary. Shoes are a luxury. The youngest child enjoys the *dal*-rice provided at school.

## Rural Case: Lakha Singh

Lakha Singh lives in a small village near Meerut in Uttar Pradesh. His family is landless and relies on odd jobs for large farmers. Work and income are irregular. Sometimes they earn ₹200 for a hard day's work, but often receive payment in kind, such as a few kilograms of wheat, *dal*, or vegetables. His family of six struggles to have two meals a day.

Lakha resides in a *kuchha* hut on the village outskirts. The women of the family spend their days chopping fodder and collecting firewood. His father, who had tuberculosis, died two years ago due to lack of medication. His mother now suffers from the same disease. Although a primary school exists in the village, Lakha never attended. However, he is happy his children are going to school. New clothes are bought only once every few years. Basic items like soap and oil are considered luxuries.

These two cases demonstrate multiple aspects of poverty, including:

*   Hunger and lack of shelter.
*   Inability to provide nutritious food or medical treatment.
*   Lack of access to clean water and sanitation.
*   Absence of regular jobs with decent wages.
*   Living with a sense of helplessness and facing mistreatment.

Poverty is a state nobody desires. One of independent India's significant challenges has been lifting millions out of extreme poverty. Mahatma Gandhi emphasized that India's true independence would be realized only when its poorest citizens were free from human suffering.
