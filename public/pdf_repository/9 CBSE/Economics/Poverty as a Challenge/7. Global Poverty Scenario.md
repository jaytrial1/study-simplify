# Global Poverty Scenario

Poverty is a global issue. International organizations like the World Bank use a standard measure for comparing poverty across developing countries: the **international poverty line** of **\$2.15 per person per day** (based on 2017 Purchasing Power Parity). This international standard uses the US dollar for comparison purposes across different national currencies.

Global extreme economic poverty (living on less than \$2.15/day) has fallen significantly from 16.27% in 2010 to 9.05% in 2019. However, there are considerable regional differences. Poverty declined substantially in China and Southeast Asian countries due to rapid economic growth and investment in human resources. China's poverty rate was 0.1% in 2020. In South Asia (including India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, etc.), the proportion of poor decreased from 13% in 2017 to 11% in 2021, with the number of poor falling from 233 million to 207 million.

In Sub-Saharan Africa, poverty declined from 36.6% in 2017 to 35% in 2019 (Graph 3.3). However, in Latin America and the Caribbean, the poverty ratio increased from 4.4% in 2017 to 4.6% in 2021. Poverty also reappeared in some former socialist countries like Russia, reaching 3% in 2000 after being officially non-existent.
