# Poverty Estimates and Trends in India

Poverty estimates in India are often presented as the **Head Count Ratio (HCR)**, the proportion of poor people. Table 3.1 shows that the consumption-based poverty proportion decreased from 45% in 1993-94 to 37% in 2004-05, though the number of poor remained around 404-407 million. Between 2009-10 and 2011-12, both the proportion and number of poor declined significantly. The multidimensional poverty proportion decreased from 25% in 2015-16 to 15% in 2019-21, with 13.5 crore people escaping multidimensional poverty during this period. Deprivations decreased more sharply in rural areas.

## Twelve Indicators of National Multidimensional Poverty Index

The NITI Aayog's NMPI uses the following 12 indicators. A household is considered multidimensionally poor if deprived in these areas:

1.  **Nutrition:** Undernourishment in children (0-59 months), women (15-49 years), or men (15-54 years).
2.  **Child-Adolescent Mortality:** Death of a child or adolescent under 18 in the last five years.
3.  **Maternal Health:** Woman giving birth in the last 5 years without skilled medical assistance.
4.  **Years of Schooling:** No household member aged 10+ has completed six years of schooling.
5.  **School attendance:** Any school-aged child is not attending school.
6.  **Cooking Fuel:** Using dung, crops, shrubs, wood, charcoal, or coal for cooking.
7.  **Sanitation:** Unimproved or no sanitation facility, or improved but shared.
8.  **Drinking Water:** No access to improved drinking water, or source is 30+ minutes walk away.
9.  **Housing:** Inadequate housing (natural floor, rudimentary roof/walls).
10. **Electricity:** No electricity.
11. **Assets:** Owning only one or none of specific assets (radio, TV, phone, computer, cart, bicycle, motorbike, refrigerator) and no car/truck.
12. **Bank Account:** No household member has a bank or post office account.

Source: https://www.undp.org/india/national-multidimensional-poverty-index-progress-review-2023

## Let's Discuss

Study Table 3.1 and answer the following questions:

*   Even if poverty ratio declined between 1993-94 and 2004-05, why did the number of poor remain at about 407 million?
*   Are the dynamics of poverty reduction the same in rural and urban India?

> **Answer:**
> *   While the poverty ratio (percentage of poor) declined between 1993-94 and 2004-05, the total number of poor remained high (around 407 million) because the total population of India was growing during this period. A smaller percentage of a larger population can still result in a similar or even higher absolute number.
> *   Based on Table 3.1, the dynamics of poverty reduction differ between rural and urban India. The percentage decline in HCR between 1993-94 and 2011-12 was greater in rural areas (50% to 26%) compared to urban areas (32% to 14%). The multidimensional poverty reduction (2015-16 to 2019-21) also shows a larger percentage point decline in rural HCR (32.6% to 19.3%) than urban HCR (8.7% to 5.3%), indicating potentially different factors or rates of impact in the two regions.
