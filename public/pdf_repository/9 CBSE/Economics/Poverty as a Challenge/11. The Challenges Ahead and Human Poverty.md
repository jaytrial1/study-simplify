This section summarizes the progress made in poverty reduction, acknowledges the remaining challenges, and broadens the definition of poverty to include non-economic aspects, introducing the concept of human poverty.

# The Challenges Ahead and Human Poverty

While poverty has decreased in India, its reduction remains a significant challenge. There are still considerable disparities between rural and urban areas and across states. Certain social and economic groups remain more vulnerable. Continued poverty reduction is crucial for India's development, facilitated by higher economic growth, universal education, declining population growth, and empowerment of women and weaker sections.

The income-based poverty definition provides only a limited view. It focuses on a 'minimum' subsistence level, not a 'reasonable' standard of living. Many scholars argue for broadening the concept to **human poverty**. This includes not just lack of income, but also lack of education, shelter, healthcare, job security, self-confidence, and freedom from discrimination (caste, gender), as well as issues like child labor. As countries develop, the definition of poverty evolves. The National Multidimensional Poverty Index (NMPI) acknowledges this broader perspective, and its findings indicate a decline in multidimensional poverty in India, possibly due to recent government interventions.
