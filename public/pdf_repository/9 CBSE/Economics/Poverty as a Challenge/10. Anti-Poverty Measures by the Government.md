This section details the strategies and specific programs implemented by the Indian government to reduce poverty.

# Anti-Poverty Measures by the Government

Poverty removal has been a key goal of India's development strategy, focusing on two main approaches:

1.  **Promotion of Economic Growth:**
    *   For the first thirty years post-independence (until the early eighties), per capita income growth was slow, and poverty reduction was minimal.
    *   Since the 1980s, India's economic growth accelerated significantly (from ~3.5% to ~6%). The 21st century has also seen rapid growth.
    *   Higher economic growth is strongly linked to poverty reduction as it creates opportunities and resources for human development (e.g., encouraging education).
    *   However, agricultural growth has lagged, directly impacting the large number of poor people dependent on it in rural areas.

2.  **Targeted Anti-Poverty Programmes:** Specific schemes designed to directly or indirectly alleviate poverty.

### Key Anti-Poverty Programmes

*   **Mahatma Gandhi National Rural Employment Guarantee Act (MGNREGA), 2005:** Aims to provide 100 days of wage employment per household per year in rural areas to ensure livelihood security. It also promotes sustainable development (addressing drought, deforestation, soil erosion). One-third of jobs are reserved for women, and wage rates are regularly revised.
*   **Pradhan Mantri Poshan Shakti Abhiyan (PM Poshan):** Formerly the Midday Meal Scheme, this program aims to improve nutritional intake (calories, protein) and boost school enrollment and attendance for students in Classes I-VIII in government and government-aided schools. It helps poor children, especially from disadvantaged sections, attend school regularly and concentrate better.
*   **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA), 2016:** Aims to reduce maternal and infant mortality by providing quality antenatal care to pregnant women monthly on the 9th day. It addresses gaps in maternal care and encourages private practitioners to volunteer services at government health facilities.
*   **Pradhan Mantri Ujjwala Yojana (PMUY), 2016:** Aims to empower rural women by promoting the use of clean cooking fuel (LPG), reducing indoor air pollution and related health issues (respiratory disorders, poor eyesight). It provides free LPG connections, including the first refill and stove, to economically poor households (BPL, SC, ST, forest dwellers, marginalized groups). Connections are issued in the woman's name, promoting environmental sustainability and allowing women more time for other activities.
