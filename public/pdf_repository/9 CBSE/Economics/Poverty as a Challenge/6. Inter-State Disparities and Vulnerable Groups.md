This section discusses the variation in poverty levels across different Indian states and identifies specific social and economic groups that are more susceptible to poverty.

# Inter-State Disparities and Vulnerable Groups

## Inter-State Disparities

Poverty levels vary significantly across Indian states (see Graph 3.1). While poverty has generally declined since the 1970s, the rate of reduction differs. States like Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Punjab, Himachal Pradesh, Andhra Pradesh, Haryana, and Maharashtra had an HCR below 10% in 2019-21. States such as Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, and Rajasthan have made notable progress recently. Different states have adopted varied strategies; for example, Kerala focused on human resource development, West Bengal on land reforms, and Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu on public distribution of food grains.

## Vulnerable Groups

Not all social groups and economic categories in India face the same proportion of poverty. The most vulnerable social groups are **Scheduled Caste (SC)** and **Scheduled Tribe (ST)** households. Among economic groups, **rural agricultural-labour households** and **urban casual-labour households** are most vulnerable.

Graph 3.2 shows that while the average poverty rate for all groups in India was 22% (in 2011-12 data), 43% of Scheduled Tribes and 34% of urban casual laborers were poor. Approximately 34% of rural casual farm laborers and 29% of Scheduled Castes were also poor. The combined disadvantage of being a landless casual laborer from an SC or ST group highlights the severity of the problem. Recent studies indicate a decline in poverty among SC, rural agricultural laborers, and urban casual laborers since the 1990s, though ST households still face high poverty rates.
