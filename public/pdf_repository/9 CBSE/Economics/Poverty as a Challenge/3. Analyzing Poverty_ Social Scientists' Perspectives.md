This section explains how social scientists view and measure poverty, moving beyond just income and consumption to include concepts like social exclusion and vulnerability.

# Analyzing Poverty: Social Scientists' Perspectives

Given its multifaceted nature, social scientists analyze poverty using various indicators, traditionally linked to income and consumption levels. More recently, additional indicators are used to identify poverty. The **National Multidimensional Poverty Index (NMPI)**, for instance, is calculated based on three main dimensions:

*   **Health:** Indicators include nutrition, child and adolescent mortality, and maternal health.
*   **Education:** Indicators include years of schooling and school attendance.
*   **Standard of Living:** Indicators cover cooking fuel, sanitation, drinking water, housing, electricity, assets, and bank accounts.

Indicators such as **social exclusion** and **vulnerability** are also crucial for understanding poverty.

## Social Exclusion

This concept views poverty as the poor being confined to impoverished surroundings, excluded from the social equality, facilities, benefits, and opportunities enjoyed by the better-off. Social exclusion can be both a cause and a consequence of low income. It's a process where individuals or groups are marginalized. The caste system in India is a typical example, where certain castes face exclusion from equal opportunities. Social exclusion can potentially cause more harm than merely having a low income.

## Vulnerability

**Vulnerability to poverty** measures the increased likelihood of certain groups (e.g., backward castes) or individuals (e.g., widows, physically handicapped) becoming or remaining poor. Vulnerability depends on the options available for alternative livelihoods (assets, education, health, job opportunities). It also considers the higher risks these groups face during natural disasters (earthquakes, tsunami) and their capacity to cope with such risks. Vulnerability essentially describes the greater probability of being negatively affected during difficult times, whether a disaster or job loss.
