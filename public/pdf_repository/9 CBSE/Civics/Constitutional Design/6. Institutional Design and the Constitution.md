# Institutional Design

A constitution is more than just a statement of values; it embodies these values in concrete institutional arrangements. A large part of the Indian Constitution details these arrangements. It is a lengthy and comprehensive document designed to be updated regularly through **constitutional amendments** to reflect people's aspirations and societal changes. The framers did not intend it to be static or unchangeable.

The Constitution describes institutional arrangements in legal language, which can initially seem complex. However, the basic structure is understandable. Like other constitutions, it outlines the process for selecting those who will govern, defines the powers held by different authorities, and limits government power by guaranteeing citizens' rights that cannot be violated. The subsequent chapters of this book will explore these aspects of the Indian Constitution's operation in a democracy, examining key provisions and their practical application. This textbook covers some, but not all, major features of the institutional design; other aspects will be studied in later courses.
