# The Role of a Constitution

In a democracy, rulers are bound by basic rules that citizens and the government must follow. Collectively, these rules form the **constitution**, which is the supreme law of a country. The constitution defines citizens' rights, outlines the government's powers, and dictates how the government should operate.

This chapter explores the fundamental questions surrounding constitutional design: Why is a constitution necessary? How are constitutions created? Who is involved in their design, and what process do they follow? What core values underpin constitutions in democratic states? Can a constitution be modified over time to adapt to changing circumstances?

The chapter will first examine the recent process of designing a democratic constitution in **South Africa**, exploring the events that led to it and how South Africans approached this task. Following this, it will shift focus to the making of the **Indian Constitution**, its foundational values, and how it provides a framework for the lives of citizens and the functioning of the government.
