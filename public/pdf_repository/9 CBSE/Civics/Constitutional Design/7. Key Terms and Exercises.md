# Glossary

*   **Apartheid:** The official policy of racial separation and ill-treatment of blacks followed by the government of South Africa between 1948 and 1989.
*   **Clause:** A distinct section of a document.
*   **Constituent Assembly:** An assembly of people's representatives that writes a constitution for a country.
*   **Constitution:** Supreme law of a country, containing fundamental rules governing the politics and society in a country.
*   **Constitutional amendment:** A change in the constitution made by the supreme legislative body in a country.
*   **Draft:** A preliminary version of a legal document.
*   **Philosophy:** The most fundamental principles underlying one's thoughts and actions.
*   **Preamble:** An introductory statement in a constitution which states the reasons and guiding values of the constitution.
*   **Treason:** The offence of attempting to overthrow the government of the state to which the offender owes allegiance.
*   **Tryst:** A meeting or meeting place that has been agreed upon.

# Exercises

1 Here are some false statements. Identify the mistake in each case and rewrite these correctly based on what you have read in this chapter.
a Leaders of the freedom movement had an open mind about whether the country should be democratic or not after independence.
b Members of the Constituent Assembly of India held the same views on all provisions of the Constitution.
c A country that has a constitution must be a democracy.
d Constitution cannot be amended because it is the supreme law of a country.

> **Answer:**
> a. **Mistake:** Leaders had an open mind about whether the country should be democratic.
>    **Corrected:** Leaders of the freedom movement had largely reached a consensus that independent India should be democratic, based on discussions and documents drafted well before independence.
> b. **Mistake:** Members of the Constituent Assembly held the same views on all provisions.
>    **Corrected:** Members of the Constituent Assembly of India held diverse views and ideologies, and provisions of the Constitution were finalized after extensive debates and consideration of thousands of amendments.
> c. **Mistake:** A country that has a constitution must be a democracy.
>    **Corrected:** All democratic countries have constitutions, but not all countries that have constitutions are necessarily democratic.
> d. **Mistake:** Constitution cannot be amended because it is the supreme law.
>    **Corrected:** A constitution can be amended to keep it updated and in accordance with people's aspirations and societal changes, even though it is the supreme law.

8 Here are different opinions about what made India a democracy. How much importance would you give to each of these factors?
a Democracy in India is a gift of the British rulers. We received training to work with representative legislative institutions under the British rule.
b Freedom Struggle challenged the colonial exploitation and denial of different freedoms to Indians. Free India could not be anything but democratic.
c We were lucky to have leaders who had democratic convictions. The denial of democracy in several other newly independent countries shows the important role of these leaders.

> **Answer:** This is an open-ended question asking for evaluation. A possible response drawing from the chapter could be:
> a. This factor has *some* importance. The chapter mentions that experience gained from working in the limited provincial legislatures under the Government of India Act, 1935, was useful for setting up India's own institutions. However, calling it a "gift" might be misleading, as the British system was not fully democratic and restricted rights. So, it provided *training* but wasn't the *source* of democracy.
> b. This factor is *very important*. The freedom struggle itself was a movement for freedom and equality, challenging the undemocratic nature of colonial rule. The consensus for a democratic India, including universal adult franchise and minority rights, emerged *during* the struggle. The desire for freedom from exploitation naturally led to the demand for a democratic setup where people could govern themselves.
> c. This factor is also *very important*. The chapter highlights the vision of leaders like Gandhi, Nehru, and Ambedkar, who were committed to democratic values, equality, and social justice despite their differences. Their leadership, ability to build consensus in the Constituent Assembly, and commitment to these values were crucial in drafting and implementing a successful democratic constitution, unlike in some other nations where such leadership might have been lacking or where democratic efforts failed. All three factors contributed, but the strength of the freedom struggle's democratic ideals and the conviction of the leaders were arguably the most decisive.

9 Read the following extract from a conduct book for 'married women', published in 1912. 'God has made the female species delicate and fragile both physically and emotionally, pitiably incapable of self-defence. They are destined thus by God to remain in male protection - of father, husband and son - all their lives. Women should, therefore, not despair, but feel obliged that they can dedicate themselves to the service of men'. Do you think the values expressed in this para reflected the values underlying our constitution? Or does this go against the constitutional values?

> **Answer:** The values expressed in this extract from 1912 *strongly go against* the values underlying our constitution. The constitution is based on the principle of **Equality**, stating that all are equal before the law and traditional social inequalities must end. It also guarantees **Liberty** and seeks to promote **Fraternity**, assuring the dignity of the individual. The extract's view that women are inherently weak, incapable of self-defence, destined for male protection, and should dedicate themselves to serving men directly contradicts the constitutional guarantees of equality of status and opportunity for all citizens, including men and women, and the fundamental belief in the equal dignity of every individual.

10 Read the following statements about a constitution. Give reasons why each of these is true or not true.
a The authority of the rules of the constitution is the same as that of any other law.
b Constitution lays down how different organs of the government will be formed.
c Rights of citizens and limits on the power of the government are laid down in the constitution.
d A constitution is about institutions, not about values

> **Answer:**
> a. **Not True:** The authority of the rules of the constitution is *higher* than that of any other law. The constitution is the supreme law of the country, and all other laws must conform to it.
> b. **True:** The constitution does lay down how different organs of the government, such as the legislature, executive, and judiciary, will be formed and their composition.
> c. **True:** The constitution defines the rights of the citizens and also puts limits on the powers of the government to protect those rights.
> d. **Not True:** A constitution is about both institutions *and* values. While it establishes the framework and powers of governmental institutions, it is fundamentally built upon and guided by a set of core values and philosophy, often articulated in its preamble.
