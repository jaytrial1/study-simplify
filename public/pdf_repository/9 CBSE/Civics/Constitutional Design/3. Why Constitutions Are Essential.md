This section uses the South African experience to explain the fundamental reasons why any country, democratic or not, needs a constitution. It outlines the key functions a constitution performs for a society.

# Why Do We Need a Constitution?

The South African case illustrates the necessity and function of a constitution. In their new democracy, former oppressors and the oppressed had to coexist as equals. Building trust was challenging due to past fears and the need to protect respective interests. The black majority sought to secure majority rule and substantial social and economic rights, while the white minority aimed to safeguard their privileges and property.

A compromise was reached after extensive negotiations. Whites accepted majority rule and the principle of one person, one vote, along with basic rights for the poor and workers. Blacks agreed that majority rule would not be absolute and that the majority would not seize the white minority's property.

Implementing and maintaining this difficult compromise required establishing universally accepted rules. These rules define how future rulers are chosen, specify the powers and limitations of elected governments, and determine citizens' rights. For these rules to be effective, they must be difficult for any single winning group to change easily. This is precisely what the South Africans did – they agreed on fundamental rules and declared them supreme, binding upon all governments. This collection of basic rules constitutes a **constitution**.

Constitution-making is common to most countries, regardless of their democratic status. While relationships between diverse groups may not always be as strained as in South Africa, differences in opinion and interest exist globally. Even associations like clubs, cooperative societies, or political parties require a form of constitution or rule book.

A country's constitution is a set of written rules accepted by everyone living within its territory. As the supreme law, it governs the relationship among citizens and between citizens and the government. A constitution serves multiple crucial purposes:

*   First, it fosters the necessary trust and coordination among diverse people living together.
*   Second, it outlines how the government is formed and who holds the authority to make which decisions.
*   Third, it sets limits on government powers and defines the rights of citizens.
*   Fourth, it reflects the aspirations of the people for creating a just and good society.

While not all countries with constitutions are democratic, all democratic countries have constitutions. The United States adopted a constitution after its War of Independence, and France approved a democratic constitution after its Revolution. Since then, having a written constitution has become standard practice in democracies.
