# Democratic Constitution in South Africa

Nelson Mandela, a key figure in the fight against apartheid, stated during his trial for treason by the white South African government: "I have fought against white domination and I have fought against black domination. I have cherished the ideal of a democratic and free society in which all persons live together in harmony and with equal opportunities. It is an ideal which I hope to live for and to achieve. But if needs be, it is an ideal for which I am prepared to die."

Mandela and seven other leaders received life sentences in 1964 for opposing the **apartheid** regime. He endured 28 years of imprisonment on Robben Island.

## Struggle Against Apartheid

**Apartheid** was a unique system of racial discrimination imposed by white Europeans in South Africa. European trading companies occupied South Africa, similar to India, but a large number of whites settled there and became local rulers. The apartheid system categorized people based on skin colour. Native South Africans, making up about three-fourths of the population, were called '**blacks**'. Other groups included people of mixed races (**coloured**) and those who migrated from India. White rulers considered all non-whites inferior and denied them voting rights.

Apartheid was particularly harsh on blacks. They were prohibited from living in white areas and could only work there with a permit. Public facilities like trains, buses, taxis, hotels, hospitals, schools, colleges, libraries, cinema halls, theatres, beaches, swimming pools, and public toilets were segregated for whites and blacks. This separation was known as **segregation**. Blacks were even forbidden from attending churches where whites worshipped and could not form associations or protest the discriminatory treatment.

Since 1950, blacks, coloured people, and Indians united against apartheid through protests and strikes. The **African National Congress (ANC)** served as the main organization leading the struggle against segregation policies, including many workers' unions and the Communist Party. Numerous sensitive white individuals also joined the ANC to oppose apartheid. While many countries condemned apartheid as unjust and racist, the white government maintained control through detention, torture, and killing thousands of black and coloured people.

## Towards a New Constitution

As resistance against apartheid intensified, the government recognized its inability to suppress blacks through repression alone. The white regime began changing its policies, repealing discriminatory laws and lifting bans on political parties and media restrictions. After 28 years, Nelson Mandela was released from prison.

On 26 April 1994, at midnight, the new national flag of the Republic of South Africa was raised, signifying the birth of a new democracy. The apartheid government ended, paving the way for a multi-racial government.

Recalling this transition, Nelson Mandela, the first president of the new South Africa, noted: "Historical enemies succeeded in negotiating a peaceful transition from apartheid to democracy exactly because we were prepared to accept the inherent capacity for goodness in the other. My wish is that South Africans never give up on the belief in goodness, that they cherish that faith in human beings is the cornerstone of our democracy."

Following the establishment of democratic South Africa, black leaders urged their fellow blacks to forgive the whites for past atrocities. They advocated building a new South Africa founded on the equality of all races, genders, democratic values, social justice, and human rights. The party that had enforced oppression and the party that led the freedom struggle came together to draft a common constitution.

After two years of deliberation, they produced one of the most progressive constitutions globally, granting citizens extensive rights. They agreed that, in seeking solutions, no one should be excluded or demonized, regardless of their past actions or affiliations. Everyone was to be part of the solution. The spirit of this agreement is captured in the preamble to the South African Constitution.

The South African constitution is a source of inspiration worldwide. A state previously condemned globally as undemocratic is now seen as a democratic model. This transformation was possible due to the determination of South Africans to collaborate, turning painful experiences into a bond for a **rainbow nation**. Mandela commented on the South African Constitution:

"The Constitution of South Africa speaks of both the past and the future. On the one hand, it is a solemn pact in which we, as South Africans, declare to one another that we shall never permit a repetition of our racist, brutal and repressive past. But it is more than that. It is also a charter for the transformation of our country into one which is truly shared by all its people - a country which in the fullest sense belongs to all of us, black and white, women and men."

> **Answer:** South Africans call themselves a 'rainbow nation' because the country is made up of people of many different races, cultures, languages, and backgrounds, who are now united and living together in harmony after the end of apartheid. The term symbolizes the diversity and unity of the country.
