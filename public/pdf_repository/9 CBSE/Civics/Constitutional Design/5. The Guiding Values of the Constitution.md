# Guiding Values of the Indian Constitution

The values that inspired and were shaped by the freedom struggle formed the bedrock of India's democracy. These values are enshrined in the **Preamble** of the Indian Constitution and guide all its articles. The Constitution begins with a concise statement of its fundamental values, known as the **Preamble**. Following the example of the American model, most modern countries include a preamble in their constitutions.

## The Dream and the Promise

Mahatma Gandhi, though not a member of the Constituent Assembly, deeply influenced many members who followed his vision. In his magazine *Young India* in 1931, he articulated his aspirations for the constitution:

"I shall strive for a constitution which will release India from all thralldom and patronage ... I shall work for an India in which the poorest shall feel that it is their country in whose making they have an effective voice; an India in which there shall be no high class and low class of people; an India in which all communities shall live in perfect harmony. There can be no room in such an India for the curse of untouchability or the curse of the intoxicating drinks and drugs. Women will enjoy the same rights as men ... I shall be satisfied with nothing else."

Dr. Ambedkar shared this dream of an India free from inequality but held different views on how to achieve it, often criticizing Gandhi. In his final speech to the Constituent Assembly, he expressed concern about the contradictions India would face:

"On the 26th of January 1950 we are going to enter a life of contradictions. In politics we will have equality and in social and economic life we will have inequality. In politics we will be recognising the principle of one man one vote and one vote one value. In our social and economic life, we shall, by reason of our social and economic structure, continue to deny the principle of one man one value. How long shall we continue to live this life of contradictions? How long shall we continue to deny equality in our social and economic life? If we continue to deny it for long, we will do so only by putting our political democracy in peril."

Finally, Jawaharlal Nehru's famous speech to the Constituent Assembly at the stroke of midnight on August 15, 1947 (*Tryst with Destiny*):

"Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny, and now the time comes when we shall redeem our pledge, not wholly or in full measure, but very substantially. At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom. A moment comes, which comes but rarely in history, when we step out from the old to the new, when an age ends, and when the soul of a nation, long suppressed, finds utterance. It is fitting that at this solemn moment we take the pledge of dedication to the service of India and her people and to the still larger cause of humanity ...

Freedom and power bring responsibility. The responsibility rests upon this Assembly, a sovereign body representing the sovereign people of India. Before the birth of freedom we have endured all the pains of labour and our hearts are heavy with the memory of this sorrow. Some of those pains continue even now. Nevertheless, the past is over and it is the future that beckons to us now.

That future is not one of ease or resting but of incessant striving so that we may fulfil the pledges we have so often taken and the one we shall take today. The service of India means the service of the millions who suffer. It means the ending of poverty and ignorance and disease and inequality of opportunity. The ambition of the greatest man of our generation has been to wipe every tear from every eye. That may be beyond us, but as long as there are tears and suffering, so long our work will not be over."

Read the three quotations above carefully.
- Can you identify one idea that is common to all these three?
- What are the differences in their ways of expressing that common idea?

> **Answer:**
> - **Common Idea:** A common idea is the commitment to building a better India free from suffering and inequality, where all people have dignity and opportunity.
> - **Differences in Expression:**
>     *   Gandhi expresses it as striving for a constitution that ensures freedom, harmony among communities, equal rights for women, and the upliftment of the poorest and marginalized (like ending untouchability). His focus is on social and economic equality and harmony.
>     *   Ambedkar highlights the potential contradiction between political equality (one person, one vote) and existing social and economic inequality. He expresses anxiety that this contradiction could endanger political democracy if not addressed. His focus is specifically on the threat posed by existing inequalities.
>     *   Nehru speaks of India awakening to freedom after a long struggle, taking a pledge to serve India and humanity. He defines service as ending the suffering of millions, specifically poverty, ignorance, disease, and inequality of opportunity. His language is more poetic and focuses on the historical moment and the large-scale task ahead.

## Philosophy of the Constitution

The Preamble to the Indian Constitution embodies the core philosophy upon which the entire document is built. It serves as a standard for evaluating laws and government actions and is considered the soul of the Indian Constitution.

Let us understand the meaning of the key words in the Preamble:

**WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA**
The constitution was created and enacted by the people through their elected representatives, not imposed by a ruler or external authority.

**SOVEREIGN**
India is an independent state with the supreme right to make decisions on both internal and external matters, free from any external power's dictation.

**SOCIALIST**
Wealth is generated collectively by society and should be shared equitably. The government should regulate land and industry ownership to reduce socio-economic disparities. (Note: The term Socialist was added later by the 42nd Amendment in 1976).

**SECULAR**
Citizens have complete freedom to practice any religion. The state does not have an official religion and treats all religious beliefs and practices with equal respect. (Note: The term Secular was added later by the 42nd Amendment in 1976).

**DEMOCRATIC**
A system of government where people enjoy equal political rights, elect their leaders, and hold them accountable. The government operates according to established basic rules.

**REPUBLIC**
The head of the state (the President) is an elected person, not someone who inherits the position.

**JUSTICE**
Citizens are protected from discrimination based on caste, religion, or gender. Social inequalities should be reduced, and the government should work for the welfare of all, especially disadvantaged groups.

**LIBERTY**
Citizens have freedom from unreasonable restrictions on their thoughts, expression of thoughts, and actions based on their thoughts.

**EQUALITY**
All individuals are equal before the law. Traditional social inequalities must be abolished, and the government must ensure equal opportunity for everyone.

**FRATERNITY**
All citizens should behave as members of the same family, treating fellow citizens with respect and not as inferiors, assuring the dignity of the individual and the unity and integrity of the Nation.

Compare the Preambles to the constitutions of the United States of America, India and South Africa.
- Make a list of ideas that are common to all these three.
- Note down at least one of the major differences among these.
- Which of the three makes a reference to the past?
- Which of these does not invoke God?

> **Answer:**
> - **Common Ideas:** All three preambles emphasize the concept of "We, the People" as the source of authority, the goals of justice, liberty (or freedom), and establishing a system for the welfare and unity of the nation.
> - **Major Differences:**
>     *   The Preamble to the South African Constitution explicitly acknowledges the injustices of the past and honors those who suffered.
>     *   The Indian Preamble includes specific terms like "Socialist" and "Secular" (added later), which are not present in the US or original South African preambles.
>     *   The US Preamble focuses on forming a "more perfect union" and providing for "common defence."
> - **Reference to the Past:** The Preamble to the South African Constitution explicitly refers to the past ("Recognise the injustices of our past; Honour those who suffered... Heal the divisions of the past...").
> - **Does Not Invoke God:** The Preamble to the Indian Constitution does not invoke God. The US Preamble does not explicitly mention God, but the South African Preamble includes a prayer ("May God protect our people...").
