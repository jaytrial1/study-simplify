This section details the Right to Equality as defined in the Indian Constitution, covering equality before law, prohibition of discrimination, equal opportunity in public employment, and the abolition of untouchability.

# Right to Equality

The Constitution states that the government cannot deny any person in India **equality before the law** or the **equal protection of the laws**. This means laws apply uniformly to everyone, regardless of status, establishing the **rule of law**. Rule of law is the bedrock of democracy, signifying that no one, including political leaders or officials, is above the law.

Every citizen, from the Prime Minister to a village farmer, is subject to the same laws. No person can claim special treatment or privilege based on importance. For instance, a former Prime Minister faced a court case for cheating charges and had to attend court and file papers like any other citizen, even though he was eventually declared not guilty.

The Constitution further clarifies the Right to Equality:

*   **Prohibition of Discrimination:** The government cannot discriminate against any citizen based *only* on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth.
*   **Equal Access to Public Places:** All citizens have equal access to public places like shops, restaurants, hotels, cinemas, wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, playgrounds, and public resorts maintained by the government or dedicated to public use. This was crucial in India due to the traditional caste system that restricted access for some communities.
*   **Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment:** All citizens have equal opportunity in matters of employment or appointment to government positions. No discrimination based on religion, race, caste, sex, or place of birth is allowed in eligibility for jobs.

### Reservations and Equality

The Constitution allows for **reservations** for Scheduled Castes, Scheduled Tribes, and Other Backward Classes, as well as preferences for women, the poor, or physically handicapped in certain jobs. These are **not** against the Right to Equality. Equality does not mean identical treatment for everyone regardless of need. It means providing **equal opportunity**. Sometimes, special treatment is needed to ensure equal opportunity. Reservations aim to provide a level playing field, and the Constitution explicitly states that such reservations do not violate the Right to Equality.

### Abolition of Untouchability

The principle of non-discrimination extends to social life. The Constitution addresses the extreme social discrimination of **untouchability**, directing the government to end it. The practice is forbidden in *any* form and is a punishable offense. Untouchability is not just physical touch but any belief or social practice that demeans people based on their caste at birth, denying them interaction or access to public places as equal citizens.

### Many Forms of Untouchability

In 1999, P. Sainath's news reports in The Hindu documented the continued practice of untouchability and caste discrimination against Dalits (Scheduled Castes) across India. Examples included:

*   Separate cups for Dalits at tea stalls.
*   Barbers refusing to serve Dalit clients.
*   Dalit students seated separately or using separate water pitchers in classrooms.
*   Dalit grooms barred from riding a horse in wedding processions.
*   Dalits denied use of common handpumps, or handpumps being 'purified' after their use.

All these practices fall under the definition of untouchability. Can you think of some examples from your own area?
