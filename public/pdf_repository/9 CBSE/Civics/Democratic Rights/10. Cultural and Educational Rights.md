# Cultural and Educational Rights

The Constitution makers specifically guaranteed the rights of **minorities**. This is because in a democracy, the majority holds power, and the language, culture, and religion of minorities could be neglected or undermined by the majority's influence.

To protect minority identities, the Constitution provides **Cultural and Educational Rights**:

*   Any section of citizens with a distinct **language or culture** has the right to **conserve** it.
*   Admission to any educational institution maintained or aided by the government cannot be denied to any citizen based on **religion or language**.
*   All minorities have the right to **establish and administer educational institutions** of their choice.

The Constitution does not give people their religion. Then how can it give people the right to practise their religion?
> **Answer:** The Constitution does not *give* people their religion; religion is a matter of personal belief. The Constitution *guarantees the right* to practice one's religion freely, meaning it protects the existing freedom of individuals to follow their chosen faith without state interference or discrimination, provided it does not harm public order, morality, or health.

Here, "minority" includes not just religious minorities nationally, but also linguistic minorities in specific regions. For example, Telugu speakers are a majority in Andhra Pradesh but a minority in Karnataka. Sikhs are a majority in Punjab but a minority in Rajasthan, Haryana, and Delhi.
