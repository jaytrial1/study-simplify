This section discusses the Right to Freedom, outlining the various freedoms guaranteed to Indian citizens, including speech, assembly, association, movement, residence, and occupation, and explaining the concept of reasonable restrictions.

# Right to Freedom

**Freedom** implies the absence of constraints or interference in our affairs by others or the government. While living in society, we desire freedom to act as we wish, without others dictating our choices. Under the Indian Constitution, all citizens are guaranteed the following freedoms:

*   **Freedom of speech and expression**
*   **Freedom to assemble peacefully and without arms**
*   **Freedom to form associations and unions**
*   **Freedom to move freely throughout the territory of India**
*   **Freedom to reside and settle in any part of the territory of India**
*   **Freedom to practice any profession, or to carry on any occupation, trade or business**

It's crucial to remember that these freedoms belong to *every* citizen. Therefore, you cannot exercise your freedom in a way that violates the freedom of others. Your freedoms should not cause public nuisance or disorder. Freedom is not an unlimited license; the government can impose **reasonable restrictions** on these freedoms in the larger interests of society.

### Freedom of Speech and Expression

This is a vital feature of democracy. Our ideas and personalities develop through free communication. You have the freedom to think differently from others and express your views, even if they differ from the majority or government policies. You can criticize the government or organizations through conversations, pamphlets, magazines, newspapers, paintings, poetry, or songs. However, this freedom cannot be used to:

*   Instigate violence against others.
*   Incite rebellion against the government.
*   Defame others by spreading false information that damages their reputation.

### Freedom of Assembly and Association

Citizens have the freedom to hold peaceful meetings, processions, rallies, and demonstrations to discuss issues, exchange ideas, mobilize support, or campaign for elections. These gatherings must be peaceful and not lead to public disorder or breach of peace. Participants should not carry weapons. Citizens can also form associations, such as workers' unions or groups campaigning against corruption or pollution.

### Freedom of Movement and Residence

As citizens, we can travel freely anywhere in India and reside or settle in any part of the country. This right allows people to migrate for work or settlement, contributing to internal migration from villages to towns or poorer regions to more prosperous ones.

### Freedom of Occupation

This freedom extends to choosing any occupation. No one can be forced into or prevented from doing a particular job. Women cannot be restricted from certain occupations, nor can people from deprived castes be confined to traditional jobs.

### Protection of Life and Personal Liberty

The Constitution guarantees that no person can be deprived of their life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**. This means life can only be taken through a court-ordered death sentence, and arbitrary arrest or detention by government or police is illegal. Even with legal justification, specific procedures must be followed upon arrest:

*   The arrested person must be informed of the reasons for arrest and detention.
*   The arrested person must be produced before the nearest magistrate within 24 hours.
*   The arrested person has the right to consult and be defended by a lawyer.

Let us recall the cases of Guantanamo Bay and Kosovo. The victims in both these cases faced a threat to the most basic of all liberties, the protection of individual life and personal liberty.

Are these cases instances of violation of right to freedom? If yes, which constitutional provision does each of these violate?
> **Answer:** Yes, both cases involve severe violations of the right to freedom, particularly the protection of life and personal liberty. In Guantanamo Bay, individuals were detained without legal justification, denied information about their arrest reasons, not produced before a magistrate, and denied access to lawyers, violating the procedures established by law for arrest and detention. In Kosovo, the ethnic massacre involved the arbitrary taking of life by state forces, a direct violation of the right to life.

### Consider these situations. Which freedoms are being debated?

*   The government of India banned Salman Rushdie's book Satanic Verses on the ground that it was disrespectful to Prophet Mohammed and was likely to hurt the feelings of Muslim community.
    > **Answer:** Freedom of speech and expression.
*   Every film has to be approved by the Censor Board of the government before it can be shown to the public. But there is no such restriction if the same story is published in a book or a magazine.
    > **Answer:** Freedom of speech and expression (specifically regarding artistic and creative expression).
*   The government is considering a proposal that there will be industrial zones or sectors of economy where workers will not be allowed to form unions or go on strike.
    > **Answer:** Freedom to form associations and unions, and potentially the freedom to practice any profession (if the ban on strikes is seen as unduly restricting the ability to negotiate working conditions).
*   City administration has imposed a ban on use of public microphones after 10 p.m. in view of the approaching secondary school examinations.
    > **Answer:** Freedom of speech and expression, and freedom to assemble (as microphones are often used in public meetings). This is an example of a reasonable restriction imposed in the interest of public order and peace.
