# Life Without Rights: Case Studies

We begin by examining three examples that demonstrate the reality of living without guaranteed rights.

## Prison in Guantanamo Bay

Around 600 individuals were secretly apprehended by US forces globally and imprisoned in **Guantanamo Bay**, an area near Cuba under American Navy control. Jamil El-Banna, Anas's father, was one of them. The US government claimed these individuals were enemies linked to the 9/11 attacks. In most instances, the governments of their home countries were neither consulted nor informed. Families, media, and even UN representatives were denied access. The US army conducted arrests, interrogations, and decided detention status without trial before any US magistrate. Prisoners could not access courts in their own countries.

**Amnesty International**, a human rights organization, reported that prisoners were subjected to torture violating US laws and were denied treatment due to prisoners of war under international treaties. Many protested through hunger strikes. Some prisoners were not released even after being officially cleared. An independent UN inquiry confirmed these findings, and the UN Secretary General called for the prison's closure, but the US government refused.

## Citizens' Rights in Saudi Arabia

While Guantanamo Bay involved non-citizens, the case of Saudi Arabia highlights the position of citizens relative to their government. Consider these facts:

*   The country is governed by a **hereditary king**; citizens have no role in electing or changing rulers.
*   The king selects the legislature, executive, and appoints judges, with the power to alter judicial decisions.
*   Citizens cannot form political parties or organizations. Media reporting is restricted to what the monarch approves.
*   There is **no freedom of religion**; all citizens must be Muslim. Non-Muslim residents can practice privately but not publicly.
*   Women face significant public restrictions, and the testimony of one man is valued equally to that of two women.

These conditions exist in many countries, not just Saudi Arabia.

## Ethnic Massacre in Kosovo

One might assume such denial of rights is limited to absolute monarchies, but the story of Kosovo, a province of former Yugoslavia, shows otherwise. Kosovo had an overwhelming ethnic Albanian population, but Serbs were the majority in the country. A Serb nationalist, **Milosevic**, won elections and his government was hostile to Kosovo Albanians, aiming for Serb dominance. Many Serb leaders believed ethnic minorities should leave or accept Serb dominance.

In April 1999, an Albanian family in Kosovo experienced this firsthand. Batisha Hoxha and her husband Izet were in their kitchen when Serbian troops burst in, demanding to know where their children were. Batisha recounts, "...they shot Izet three times in the chest". Soldiers took her wedding ring and forced her out. Her house was burnt before she reached the gate, leaving her with nothing but her clothes.
