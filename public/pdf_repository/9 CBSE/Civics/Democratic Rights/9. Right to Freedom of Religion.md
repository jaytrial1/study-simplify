This section explains the Right to Freedom of Religion, defining India as a secular state and outlining the rights of individuals and religious groups to profess, practice, and propagate their religion while maintaining state neutrality.

# Right to Freedom of Religion

The right to freedom also includes the **Right to Freedom of Religion**. The Constitution makers explicitly included this, reinforcing India's identity as a **secular state**. Secularism is based on the principle that the state deals with human relations, remaining neutral regarding religious beliefs. A secular state does not establish any one religion as official and maintains equal distance from all religions. The state must be impartial towards all faiths.

Every person has the right to **profess, practice, and propagate** their religion. Religious groups can manage their own affairs. However, the right to propagate does not permit compelling conversion through force, fraud, or inducement. Individuals are free to change religion voluntarily. Freedom of practice does not allow actions harmful to others or society in the name of religion, such as animal or human sacrifice. Religious practices that treat women as inferior or infringe upon their freedom, like forcing a widow to shave her head or wear white, are not permitted.

A secular state avoids giving privileges or favors to any religion. It does not discriminate based on religion. The government cannot force citizens to pay taxes for the promotion of any specific religion or institution. Government educational institutions cannot provide religious instruction. Private educational institutions cannot compel anyone to participate in religious instruction or worship.
