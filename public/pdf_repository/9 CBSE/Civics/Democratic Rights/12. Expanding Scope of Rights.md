This section discusses how the scope of rights is not limited to the Fundamental Rights listed in the Constitution but has expanded over time through legal interpretations, new laws, and the influence of international human rights standards.

# Expanding Scope of Rights

While Fundamental Rights are the source of many rights, the Constitution and law offer a broader range, and the scope of rights has expanded over the years.

This expansion occurs in several ways:

*   **Judicial Interpretation:** Courts have expanded the scope of existing Fundamental Rights through judgments. Rights like freedom of the press, right to information, and right to education are derived from Fundamental Rights (e.g., freedom of speech and expression, right to life).
*   **New Laws:** Parliament has enacted laws recognizing new rights, such as the Right to Education (making free and compulsory education for children up to 14 a right) and the Right to Information (allowing citizens to seek information from government offices, based on the right to freedom of thought and expression).
*   **Judicial Expansion of Existing Rights:** The Supreme Court has expanded the meaning of the Right to Life to include the right to food.
*   **Constitutional Rights (beyond Fundamental):** Some rights are constitutional but not Fundamental, like the right to property (though its status has changed) and the right to vote in elections.

### Human Rights and International Influence

Expansion also occurs in the realm of **human rights**. These are universal moral claims that may or may not yet be legally recognized. While not strictly "rights" by the earlier definition (sanctioned by law), growing democracy globally and international covenants put pressure on governments to recognize these claims.

#### International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights

This covenant recognizes rights not directly in the Indian Constitution's Fundamental Rights, seen as a standard for human rights globally:

*   Right to work (opportunity to earn livelihood)
*   Right to safe and healthy working conditions, fair wages for a decent living standard
*   Right to adequate standard of living (food, clothing, housing)
*   Right to social security and insurance
*   Right to health (medical care, maternal care, disease prevention)
*   Right to education (free primary, equal access to higher education)

New rights evolve over time through people's struggles and societal development. The Constitution of South Africa, for instance, guarantees new rights like:

*   Right to privacy (protection from search, phone tapping, communication interception)
*   Right to an environment safe for health and well-being
*   Right to access adequate housing
*   Right to access healthcare, food, and water; no denial of emergency medical treatment
