# Introduction to Democratic Rights

In previous chapters, we explored two key aspects of democratic government: periodic free and fair elections and governance through established institutions following rules and procedures. While essential, these elements alone are not sufficient for a democracy. They must be combined with the **enjoyment of rights** by citizens.

Democratic rights serve as **limits** that even properly elected rulers, working through institutional processes, must not cross.

This chapter will begin by examining real-life situations to understand what it means to live without rights. This leads to a discussion on the meaning and necessity of rights. The chapter will then focus on India, detailing the **Fundamental Rights** enshrined in the Indian Constitution, how ordinary citizens can use and enforce these rights, and how the scope of rights has been expanding.
