# Parliament: The National Assembly

In democracies, an assembly of elected representatives holds supreme political authority on behalf of the people. In India, this national assembly is called **Parliament**. At the state level, it's called the Legislature or Legislative Assembly. While names vary, such an assembly exists in every democracy and exercises political authority in several ways:

1.  **Law Making:** Parliament is the final authority for making laws (**legislation**). It can make new laws, change existing ones, or abolish old ones. This crucial task gives these assemblies the name "legislatures."
2.  **Control over Government:** Parliaments control those who run the government. In India, this control is direct; the government can only make decisions as long as it has Parliament's support.
3.  **Financial Control:** Parliaments control public money. Government spending requires Parliament's sanction.
4.  **Forum for Debate:** Parliament is the highest forum for discussion and debate on public issues and national policy. It can seek information on any matter.

In the Mandal case, although Parliament didn't directly issue the order, its discussions on the report influenced and shaped the government's decision and put pressure for implementation. If Parliament had opposed the decision, the government likely couldn't have proceeded, as the Prime Minister and Cabinet need Parliament's support.
