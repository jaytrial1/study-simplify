This section explains the concept of judicial independence and the significant powers of the Indian judiciary, including judicial review and public interest litigation.

# Independence and Powers of the Judiciary

**Independence of the judiciary** means it is not under the control of the legislature or the executive. Judges do not act on government direction or ruling party wishes. Modern democracies require courts independent of other branches. India has achieved this.

Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts are appointed by the President. In practice, this process involves the senior judges of the Supreme Court selecting new judges (the collegium system), with very little political executive interference. The senior-most Supreme Court judge is usually appointed Chief Justice.

Removing a Supreme Court or High Court judge is extremely difficult, requiring an **impeachment motion** passed separately by two-thirds majorities in both Houses of Parliament. This has never happened in Indian democracy.

The Indian judiciary is one of the most powerful globally. The Supreme Court and High Courts can interpret the Constitution. They can declare any law passed by the legislature or action taken by the executive (at Union or state level) invalid if it violates the Constitution. This power is called **judicial review**. The Supreme Court has also ruled that the basic principles of the Constitution cannot be changed by Parliament.

The judiciary's powers and independence make it the guardian of **Fundamental Rights**. Citizens can approach courts if their rights are violated. In recent years, courts have issued judgments and directives to protect public interest and human rights through **Public Interest Litigation (PIL)**. Anyone can approach the courts if public interest is harmed by government actions. Courts intervene to prevent misuse of government power and check malpractices by officials, which is why the judiciary enjoys high public confidence.

Give one reason each to argue that Indian judiciary is independent with respect to:
Appointment of judges:
> **Answer:** The appointment process, in practice, involves senior judges selecting new judges, limiting the scope for political executive interference.
Removal of judges:
> **Answer:** Judges can only be removed through a difficult impeachment process requiring a two-thirds majority in both Houses of Parliament, which has never occurred.
Powers of the judiciary:
> **Answer:** The power of judicial review allows the judiciary to strike down laws or executive actions that violate the Constitution, acting as a check on the other branches.

Why are people allowed to go to courts against the government's decisions?
> **Answer:** People are allowed to go to courts against government decisions because the judiciary acts as a guardian of the Constitution and the Fundamental Rights of citizens. If a government decision is seen as unconstitutional, violates rights, or harms public interest, citizens have the right to seek remedy from the courts. This holds the government accountable and ensures rule of law.
