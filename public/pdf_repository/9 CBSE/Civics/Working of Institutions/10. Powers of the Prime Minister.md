# Powers of the Prime Minister

Although the Constitution doesn't explicitly detail the Prime Minister's powers, as head of the government, the Prime Minister holds wide-ranging authority:

*   Chairs Cabinet meetings.
*   Coordinates the work of different Departments.
*   Resolves disagreements between Departments.
*   Exercises general supervision over ministries.
*   Ministers work under the Prime Minister's leadership.
*   Distributes and redistributes work among ministers.
*   Has the power to dismiss ministers.
*   When the Prime Minister quits, the entire ministry resigns.

The Prime Minister is the most powerful figure within the Cabinet, which is itself the most powerful institution in India. The Prime Minister's powers have grown significantly in recent decades, leading some to describe parliamentary democracies as **Prime Ministerial forms of government**. The Prime Minister controls the Cabinet and Parliament largely through their party leadership. Media focus on top leaders during elections also contributes to this trend.

In India, Prime Ministers like Jawaharlal Nehru and Indira Gandhi wielded enormous authority due to their influence and personality. The extent of a Prime Minister's power often depends on the individual's personality and political circumstances.

However, the rise of **coalition politics** in recent years has constrained the Prime Minister's power. A Prime Minister in a coalition government must accommodate different groups and factions within their own party and among alliance partners. They must also consider the views of coalition partners, whose support is essential for the government's survival.
