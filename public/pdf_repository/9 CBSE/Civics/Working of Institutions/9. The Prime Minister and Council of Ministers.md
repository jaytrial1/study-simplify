This section describes the position of the Prime Minister as the most important political institution and the structure of the Council of Ministers.

# Prime Minister and Council of Ministers

The **Prime Minister** is the most important political institution in India. While there is no direct election for this post, the President appoints the leader of the party or coalition that holds a majority in the Lok Sabha as Prime Minister. If no single party has a majority, the President appoints the person most likely to secure majority support and asks them to prove it within a specified time. The Prime Minister does not have a fixed term and stays in power as long as they retain the support of the majority.

After appointing the Prime Minister, the President appoints other ministers based on the Prime Minister's advice. Ministers are usually members of Parliament from the majority party or coalition. A non-MP can become a minister but must get elected to Parliament within six months.

The **Council of Ministers** is the official body comprising all ministers, typically 60 to 80 members of different ranks:

*   **Cabinet Ministers:** Top-level leaders in charge of major ministries. They meet to take decisions in the name of the Council of Ministers. The Cabinet is the inner core, usually around 20 ministers.
*   **Ministers of State with independent charge:** In charge of smaller ministries, attending Cabinet meetings only when invited.
*   **Ministers of State:** Attached to and assist Cabinet Ministers.

Decisions are primarily taken in Cabinet meetings, making parliamentary democracy often known as the **Cabinet form of government**. The Cabinet works as a team, and all ministers must support Cabinet decisions publicly, even if they disagreed privately. Each ministry is assisted by civil servant secretaries who provide background information. The **Cabinet Secretariat**, composed of senior civil servants, assists the Cabinet and coordinates ministries.
