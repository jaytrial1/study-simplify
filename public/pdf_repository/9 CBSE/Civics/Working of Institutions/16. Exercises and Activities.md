# Exercises and Activities

1.  If you are elected as the President of India which of the following decision can you take on your own?
    a Select the person you like as Prime Minister.
    b Dismiss a Prime Minister who has a majority in Lok Sabha.
    c Ask for reconsideration of a bill passed by both the Houses.
    d Nominate the leaders of your choice to the Council of Ministers.
    > **Answer:** c Ask for reconsideration of a bill passed by both the Houses. (Explanation: The President has limited discretion, mainly in appointing a PM when there's no clear majority. Asking for reconsideration of a bill is one of the few actions she can take without binding advice.)

2.  Who among the following is a part of the political executive?
    a District Collector
    b Secretary of the Ministry of Home Affairs
    c Home Minister
    d Director General of Police
    > **Answer:** c Home Minister (Explanation: Political executive consists of elected leaders who make major decisions. District Collectors, Secretaries, and DGPs are part of the permanent executive or civil services.)

3.  Which of the following statements about the judiciary is false?
    a Every law passed by the Parliament needs approval of the Supreme Court
    b Judiciary can strike down a law if it goes against the spirit of the Constitution
    c Judiciary is independent of the Executive
    d Any citizen can approach the courts if her rights are violated
    > **Answer:** a Every law passed by the Parliament needs approval of the Supreme Court (Explanation: Laws passed by Parliament do not need prior approval from the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court can only review a law *after* it is passed, if its constitutionality is challenged.)

4.  Which of the following institutions can make changes to an existing law of the country?
    a The Supreme Court
    b The President
    c The Prime Minister
    d The Parliament
    > **Answer:** d The Parliament (Explanation: Parliament is the legislature, which has the power to make, change, or abolish laws.)

6.  Of all the institutions that we have studied in this chapter, name the one that exercises the powers on each of the following matters.
    a Decision on allocation of money for developing infrastructure like roads, irrigation etc. and different welfare activities for the citizens
    > **Answer:** Executive (specifically the Cabinet/Council of Ministers, guided by Parliament's budget approval)
    b Considers the recommendation of a Committee on a law to regulate the stock exchange
    > **Answer:** Parliament (as it makes laws) or Executive (Cabinet, which initiates legislation and considers committee reports) - Parliament is the final authority for making the law.
    c Decides on a legal dispute between two state governments
    > **Answer:** Judiciary (Supreme Court)
    d Implements the decision to provide relief for the victims of an earthquake.
    > **Answer:** Executive (Civil Services/Government officials under the direction of the political executive)

7.  Why is the Prime Minister in India not directly elected by the people? Choose the most appropriate answer and give reasons for your choice.
    a In a Parliamentary democracy only the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha can become the Prime Minister.
    b Lok Sabha can remove the Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers even before the expiry of their term.
    c Since the Prime Minister is appointed by the President there is no need for it.
    d Direct election of the Prime Minister will involve lot of expenditure on election.
    > **Answer:** a In a Parliamentary democracy only the leader of the majority party in the Lok Sabha can become the Prime Minister. (Explanation: This is the most fundamental reason. The system is designed so the head of government has the direct confidence of the legislature, which is directly elected by the people. Option b is a consequence of the parliamentary system, not the primary reason for indirect election. Option c is incorrect; appointment by President is part of the process, not the reason for indirect election. Option d might be true but is not the constitutional or primary political reason.)

8.  Three friends went to watch a film that showed the hero becoming Chief Minister for a day and making big changes in the state. Imran said this is what the country needs. Rizwan said this kind of a personal rule without institutions is dangerous. Shankar said all this is a fantasy. No minister can do anything in one day. What would be your reaction to such a film?
    > **Answer:** This question invites personal reflection based on the chapter. A reaction drawing on the chapter's themes might agree with Rizwan and Shankar. Rizwan's point aligns with the chapter's emphasis on institutions providing necessary checks and balances and preventing arbitrary rule. Shankar's point about a minister's limitations highlights the reality of institutional processes, rules, and the need for consultation and implementation by the permanent executive, which makes rapid, sweeping changes in one day unrealistic in a democratic system.

9.  A teacher was making preparations for a mock parliament. She called two students to act as leaders of two political parties. She gave them an option: Each one could choose to have a majority either in the mock Lok Sabha or in the mock Rajya Sabha. If this choice was given to you, which one would you choose and why?
    > **Answer:** Based on the chapter's comparison of the powers of the two Houses, you would likely choose to have a majority in the mock Lok Sabha. Reason: The Lok Sabha is constitutionally more powerful in key areas. It controls the Council of Ministers (can pass a no-confidence motion), has more power in money matters, and its view is likely to prevail in a joint session for ordinary laws due to its larger size. Having a majority in the Lok Sabha allows you to form and sustain the government and largely control the legislative agenda and finances.

10. After reading the example of the reservation order, three students had different reactions about the role of the judiciary. Which view, according to you, is a correct reading of the role of judiciary?
    a Srinivas argues that since the Supreme Court agreed with the government, it is not independent.
    b Anjaiah says that judiciary is independent because it could have given a verdict against the government order. The Supreme Court did direct the government to modify it.
    c Vijaya thinks that the judiciary is neither independent nor conformist, but acts as a mediator between opposing parties. The court struck a good balance between those who supported and those who opposed the order.
    > **Answer:** Anjaiah's view is the most accurate reading based on the chapter. The judiciary's independence is demonstrated by its ability to review government actions and laws. While the Supreme Court upheld the *principle* of reservation (agreeing with the government on that point), it also exercised its independence by directing the government to *modify* the order to exclude the creamy layer. This shows the court was not simply conforming but was independently interpreting the Constitution and ensuring the policy was implemented fairly according to its interpretation. Vijaya's view is partially correct in that courts resolve disputes, but describing the judiciary purely as a "mediator" doesn't fully capture its power to interpret the Constitution and strike down actions/laws. Srinivas is incorrect; agreeing with the government on one aspect does not negate the court's independence, especially when it also orders modifications.
