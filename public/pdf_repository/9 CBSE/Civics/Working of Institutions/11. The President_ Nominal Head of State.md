This section explains the role and powers of the President of India as the formal head of state, distinguishing this from the Prime Minister's role as head of government.

# The President

While the Prime Minister heads the government, the **President** is the **head of the State**. In India's political system, the President exercises mostly **nominal powers**. The President's role is largely ceremonial, similar to the Queen of Britain. The President supervises the overall functioning of political institutions to ensure they work harmoniously.

The President is not directly elected by the people but by the elected Members of Parliament (MPs) and Members of the Legislative Assemblies (MLAs). A candidate needs a majority of votes, ensuring they represent the entire nation. However, lacking a direct popular mandate like the Prime Minister, the President remains a nominal executive.

Constitutionally, the President appears very powerful. All government activities, laws, major policy decisions, and appointments (Chief Justice, Supreme/High Court judges, Governors, Election Commissioners, ambassadors) are made in the President's name. The President also makes international treaties and commands the defence forces.

However, the President exercises these powers only on the **advice of the Council of Ministers**. The President can ask the Council to reconsider advice, but is bound to act on it if given again. Similarly, the President's assent is needed for a bill to become law. The President can delay assent and send a bill back for reconsideration, but must sign it if Parliament passes it again.

So, what does the President truly do? The most significant independent action is appointing the Prime Minister. If a party or coalition has a clear majority, the President must appoint their leader. When no party has a majority, the President uses discretion to appoint a leader believed capable of mustering majority support, requiring them to prove it in the Lok Sabha within a set time.
