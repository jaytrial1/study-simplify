This section explains the two-house structure of the Indian Parliament and the purpose behind having two chambers.

# Two Houses of Parliament

Most large democracies divide the work and powers of Parliament into two parts, called **Chambers** or **Houses**.

*   One House is usually directly elected by the people and exercises real power.
*   The second House is usually elected indirectly and often represents the interests of states, regions, or federal units.

In India, Parliament consists of two Houses:

*   **Council of States (Rajya Sabha)**
*   **House of the People (Lok Sabha)**

The **President of India** is part of Parliament, although not a member of either House. Laws passed by the Houses come into force only after the President's assent.

Let's recall key differences between the composition of Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha:

*   **What is the total number of members?**
    > **Answer:** Lok Sabha: Maximum 550 (currently 543 elected + 2 nominated, though nomination provision is suspended). Rajya Sabha: Maximum 250 (238 elected + 12 nominated).
*   **Who elects the members?**
    > **Answer:** Lok Sabha: Directly elected by the people through universal adult suffrage. Rajya Sabha: Elected by the elected Members of the Legislative Assemblies of the states and Union Territories; 12 members are nominated by the President.
*   **What is the length of the term (in years)?**
    > **Answer:** Lok Sabha: 5 years (unless dissolved earlier). Rajya Sabha: 6 years; members retire every two years.
*   **Can the House be dissolved or is it permanent?**
    > **Answer:** Lok Sabha: Can be dissolved before the completion of its term. Rajya Sabha: Is a permanent House and cannot be dissolved.
