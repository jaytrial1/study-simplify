# How the Decision Was Made

A major decision like the 1990 reservation order involved several key functionaries, not just the signing officer who was merely implementing instructions. The process leading to the Office Memorandum was a long chain of events:

1.  **Commission Appointment:** The Government of India appointed the Mandal Commission in 1979 to identify SEBCs and recommend measures.
2.  **Report Submission:** The Commission submitted its report in 1980, recommending 27% job reservation among other things.
3.  **Parliamentary Discussion:** The report and recommendations were discussed in Parliament over several years, with many parliamentarians and parties demanding implementation.
4.  **Election Promise:** The Janata Dal included the implementation of the Mandal Commission report in its election manifesto for the 1989 Lok Sabha election.
5.  **Government Formation:** The Janata Dal formed the government after the 1989 election, with V. P. Singh becoming Prime Minister.
6.  **Presidential Address:** The President of India announced the government's intention to implement the recommendations in his address to Parliament.
7.  **Cabinet Decision:** On August 6, 1990, the Union Cabinet formally decided to implement the recommendations.
8.  **Prime Minister's Announcement:** The next day, Prime Minister V.P. Singh informed Parliament about the Cabinet's decision.
9.  **Drafting and Issuance:** The Cabinet decision was sent to the Department of Personnel and Training. Senior officers drafted the order, obtained the minister's approval, and an officer signed it on behalf of the Union Government on August 13, 1990.

This sequence shows how a decision originating from a commission's report, influenced by political parties and parliamentary debate, was finally formalised by the executive branch.

For the next few months after the order, this issue was hotly debated in the country, leading to widespread protests and counter-protests, some violent.

*   Some people supported the decision, arguing that job reservations were necessary to address historical inequalities and provide fair opportunities for communities not adequately represented in government jobs.
*   Others opposed it, feeling it was unfair to those not belonging to backward communities and would deny them jobs even if more qualified. Some also felt it would perpetuate caste divisions and harm national unity.

The chapter focuses on understanding the process of decision-making and implementation, not on whether the decision itself was right or wrong.

Who resolved the dispute arising from this order? The **Supreme Court** and the **High Courts** in India settle disputes regarding governmental decisions. Several cases were filed against the order. The Supreme Court of India heard these cases together, known as the **'Indira Sawhney and others Vs Union of India case'**. In 1992, by a majority, the Supreme Court declared the order valid but asked the government to exclude well-to-do persons among the backward classes (the 'creamy layer') from the reservation benefits. Following this, the Department of Personnel and Training issued another Office Memorandum on September 8, 1993, modifying the original order. This resolved the dispute, and the modified policy has been followed since.
