# What is Democracy?

In the previous chapter, we examined various governments around the world and identified some as **democracies** and others as **non-democracies**. We saw transitions between these forms. Now, we move to the fundamental question: What exactly is democracy? What are its defining characteristics?

This chapter will develop a simple definition of democracy step-by-step, clarifying the terms involved. The goal is to understand the essential minimum features of a democratic government so we can distinguish it from non-democratic forms. Towards the end, we will explore a broader concept of democracy.

Democracy is the most widespread form of government globally today and continues to expand. We will also explore *why* this is the case and what makes it potentially better than other systems.

In Chapter One, we discussed stories from countries like Chile (before and after Pinochet), Poland (after communism), and Ghana (early Nkrumah period), describing their governments.

Think about:
*   What was common to the governments described as democracies in these countries?
*   Why are they all grouped under the label of **democracy**?
*   What distinguishes these governments from Pinochet's rule in Chile, communist rule in Poland, or the later period of Nkrumah's rule in Ghana?
*   What do the non-democratic governments (Pinochet, communist Poland, later Nkrumah) have in common with the military rule in Myanmar? Why are they considered non-democratic?

> **Answer:** Based on the examples from Chapter One, common features of democratic governments likely include: governments formed through elections, some degree of political freedom, and rulers being accountable to the people. Non-democratic governments, in contrast, often involve military control, dictatorships, or one-party rule where rulers are not elected by the people and there is limited or no political freedom or accountability.

## A simple definition

Returning to the similarities and differences among governments called democracies, we identified one common factor in the previous chapter: the government is chosen by the people.

A simple starting definition: **democracy is a form of government in which the rulers are elected by the people.**

This definition helps distinguish democracy from governments that are clearly not democratic:
*   Army rulers in Myanmar are not elected.
*   Dictators like Pinochet were not elected.
*   Monarchs like the kings of Nepal and Saudi Arabia rule by birthright, not election.

This simple definition, however, is not enough. If applied without thinking, it might label almost any government holding an election as a democracy, which can be misleading. Many governments today claim to be democracies, even when they are not. We need to carefully distinguish a true democracy from one that only pretends. We can do this by examining each part of the definition and spelling out the features of a democratic government.
