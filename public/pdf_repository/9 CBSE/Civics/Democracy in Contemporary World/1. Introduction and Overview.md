# Introduction and Overview

This book is about democracy. This first chapter explores how democracy has spread to many countries worldwide over the last hundred years. Today, more than half of independent countries are democracies. However, this expansion has not been smooth, experiencing many ups and downs. Democracy remains an unstable and uncertain achievement in many places.

The chapter begins with stories from different parts of the world about the rise and fall of democracy, aiming to convey the experience of living under democratic rule and its absence. It then presents the pattern of democracy's spread using maps and a brief history. The focus is primarily on democracy within countries. Towards the end, the chapter examines democracy, or its lack thereof, in relations *among* countries and within international organizations, prompting a discussion on whether the world is moving towards democracy at a global level.

Why did President Allende address himself mainly to 'workers'? Why were the rich unhappy with him?
> **Answer:** President Allende primarily addressed 'workers' because he was the founder and leader of the Socialist Party of Chile and his Popular Unity coalition won the election with significant support from the working class and poor. His policies, such as land redistribution, free milk for children, and opposition to foreign companies exploiting natural resources, were designed to help the poor and workers. The rich, landlords, and foreign companies were unhappy with him because these policies challenged their wealth, land ownership, and economic interests.
