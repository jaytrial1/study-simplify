This section details the political history of Chile, focusing on the democratic government of Salvador Allende, the military coup led by Augusto Pinochet, and the eventual restoration of democracy.

# Stories of Democratic Change: Chile

## Allende's Government and Policies

The chapter begins with extracts from the last speech of Salvador Allende, the President of Chile, given on 11 September 1973, the day his government was overthrown by the military. Allende was the founder of the **Socialist Party of Chile** and led the Popular Unity coalition to victory in the 1970 presidential election. After being elected, Allende implemented several policies aimed at helping the poor and workers, including educational reform, free milk for children, and land redistribution. He also opposed foreign companies exploiting Chile's natural resources like copper. His policies were opposed by landlords, the rich, the Church, and some other political parties.

## The 1973 Military Coup

On the morning of 11 September 1973, the military took control of the seaport and arrested the Defence Minister. Military commanders demanded Allende's resignation, but he refused. He addressed the people on the radio before the military surrounded and bombed his house. President Allende died in the attack, a sacrifice he referenced in his last speech. This event was a **military coup**, an illegal and sudden overthrow of the government through conspiracy and violence.

General Augusto Pinochet led the coup. The government of the United States of America, unhappy with Allende's rule, is known to have supported and funded activities leading to the coup. Pinochet became the country's President and ruled for 17 years, establishing a **military dictatorship**. Power shifted from elected officials to military officers who ruled without being questioned. Pinochet's government was brutal, torturing and killing supporters of Allende and those who sought the return of democracy. This included General Alberto Bachelet and other officers who refused to join the coup. General Bachelet's wife and daughter were imprisoned and tortured. Over 3,000 people were killed, and many more were reported 'missing'.

## Restoration of Democracy in Chile

Pinochet's dictatorship ended after he held a **referendum** in 1988, confident people would vote for him to stay in power. However, the people voted a decisive 'no'. This led to Pinochet losing his political and military authority. Political freedom was restored, fulfilling the hope Allende expressed in his last speech. Since then, Chile has held four presidential elections with multiple parties participating. The army's role in government has been gradually eliminated. Inquiries into Pinochet's rule revealed its brutality and corruption.

Michelle Bachelet, the daughter of General Bachelet who was imprisoned and tortured, was elected President of Chile in January 2006. A medical doctor and moderate socialist, she became the first woman Defence Minister in Latin America before winning the presidency. Her victory demonstrated the country's return to democracy and a rejection of its dictatorial past.
