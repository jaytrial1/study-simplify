This section discusses the controversial practice of powerful countries intervening in other nations to establish democracy, using the US-led invasion of Iraq as a primary example.

# The Debate on External Democracy Promotion

Many powerful countries, particularly the United States, advocate for and sometimes actively intervene to promote democracy in non-democratic countries. They argue that merely promoting democratic values is insufficient; existing democracies should directly intervene to establish democracy elsewhere. In some cases, this has involved armed intervention.

## The Case of Iraq

Iraq, a country in Western Asia, gained independence in 1932. After a series of coups, it was ruled by the Arab Socialist Ba'th Party from 1968. **Saddam Hussein**, a key Ba'th leader, became President in 1979 and established a dictatorial government. His regime suppressed dissent, killed political opponents, and massacred ethnic minorities. While his government did abolish traditional Islamic law and granted some freedoms to women not common in other West Asian countries, it was fundamentally authoritarian.

The US and its allies, such as Britain, alleged that Iraq possessed secret nuclear weapons and 'weapons of mass destruction' (WMD) that threatened global security. However, a UN inspection team found no such weapons. Despite this, the US and its allies invaded Iraq in 2003, overthrew Saddam Hussein, and installed an interim government of their choice. The UN Security Council did not authorize this war, and the UN Secretary General, Kofi Annan, called it illegal.

## Questions on Intervention

The example of Iraq raises fundamental questions about promoting democracy:

- Is invading other countries the right way to promote democracy?
- Does external help work, or is it only effective when people within a nation are actively struggling for democracy?
- Would externally imposed democracy last and have citizen support?
- Is using external force to "gift" democracy consistent with the spirit of democracy itself?

These questions require careful consideration based on the principles of democracy and the historical examples discussed.
