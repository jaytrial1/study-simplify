# The Changing Map of Democracy

The twentieth century saw many transitions to democracy, challenges, coups, and struggles to restore democratic rule. To see if there's a pattern, we can identify democracies using the basic features noted earlier and look at maps showing democratic countries in 1900-1950, 1975, and 2000.

These maps help visualize the evolution of democracy globally. We can ask: How did democracy spread? Was the expansion consistent? When and where did it expand most?

MAP 1.1: DEMOCRATIC GOVERNMENTS IN 1900-1950
![img-7.jpeg](Democracy in Contemporary World/5. Global Spread of Democracy (1900-2000)/img_img-7.png)
**Image Description:** World map showing countries with democratic governments in 1900 (darker shade) and countries that became democratic by 1950 (lighter shade). It shows democracy concentrated in North America, Western Europe, parts of South America, and Oceania by 1900, with significant expansion by 1950 into other parts of Europe, the Middle East, South Asia (like India and Pakistan), and Oceania.

Let us summarise the main points that emerge from a reading of these maps. You need to go back to the maps to answer the question that comes after each point.

- Democracy has expanded throughout the twentieth century. Is it correct to say that at each point in these maps, the number of democratic countries is larger than at the previous point in time?
> **Answer:** Yes, looking at the maps, the number of countries shaded as democratic generally increases from 1900/1950 to 1975 and then significantly by 2000.
- Democracy did not spread evenly in all parts of the world. It was established first in some regions and then spread to other regions. Which continents in the world had a large number of democracies in 1900 and 1950? And which continents did not have almost any?
> **Answer:** In 1900 and 1950, Europe and North America had a large number of democracies. Africa and Asia had almost no democracies in 1900, though this began to change by 1950, especially in Asia.
While a majority of countries are democratic today, there are still large parts of the world that are not democratic. Which regions in the world account for most of the countries that were not democracies in 2000?
> **Answer:** In 2000, regions like parts of Africa, the Middle East, and Asia accounted for most of the countries that were not democracies.
