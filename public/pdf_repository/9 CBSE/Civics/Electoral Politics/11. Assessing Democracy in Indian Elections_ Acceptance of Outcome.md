This section evaluates the democratic nature of Indian elections based on whether the results are accepted, particularly by losing parties, and presents evidence from India's electoral history.

# Acceptance of election outcome

A final measure of free and fair elections is the outcome itself. If elections are rigged, results favour the powerful, and ruling parties rarely lose. Losing parties usually wouldn't accept such results.

The outcomes of Indian elections demonstrate their fairness:
-   **Ruling parties frequently lose** elections in India at both national and state levels. In the last fifteen years, the ruling party lost in about two out of every three elections.
-   Unlike the US, where incumbents rarely lose, about **half of sitting MPs or MLAs in India lose** elections.
-   Candidates known for spending heavily to 'buy votes' or those with criminal connections **often lose**.
-   Apart from very few disputed cases, election outcomes are generally **accepted as the 'people's verdict'** by defeated parties.
