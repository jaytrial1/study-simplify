# What Is Our System of Elections?

We examine whether Indian elections are democratic by looking at how they are conducted. Lok Sabha and Vidhan Sabha (Assembly) elections are held every five years. After five years, the term of representatives ends, and the Lok Sabha or Vidhan Sabha is dissolved. Elections are held simultaneously across all constituencies, either on the same day or within a few days. This is called a **general election**. Sometimes, an election is held for just one constituency due to a vacancy (death or resignation); this is a **by-election**. This chapter focuses on general elections.

## Electoral constituencies

To elect 90 MLAs in Haryana, voters don't vote for all 90. India uses an **area-based system of representation**. The country is divided into areas called **electoral constituencies** for elections. Voters in each area elect one representative.

For Lok Sabha elections, India is divided into 543 constituencies. The elected representative from each is a **Member of Parliament (MP)**. A key feature of democratic elections is equal vote value, so the Constitution requires each constituency to have a roughly equal population.

Similarly, each state is divided into Assembly constituencies, electing a **Member of Legislative Assembly (MLA)**. Each Parliamentary constituency contains several Assembly constituencies. The same applies to Panchayat and Municipal elections, where villages/towns are divided into 'wards', each electing one member. Constituencies are sometimes called 'seats'. Saying 'Lok Dal won 60 seats' in Haryana means their candidates won in 60 Assembly constituencies, giving them 60 MLAs.

## Voters' list

After constituencies are defined, the next step is identifying eligible voters. This list, called the **Electoral Roll** or **Voters' List**, is prepared well before elections and distributed. This is crucial for the first condition of democratic elections: equal opportunity to choose representatives.

The principle of **universal adult franchise** means everyone aged 18 and above can vote, regardless of caste, religion, gender, wealth, or education. Every citizen gets one vote of equal value. Only in rare cases (e.g., criminals, persons with unsound mind) is the right to vote denied. The government is responsible for updating the list, adding new eligible voters and removing those who have moved or died. A complete revision happens every five years.

Recently, the **Election Photo Identity Card (EPIC)** system was introduced, aiming to provide a card to every voter to prevent impersonation. While voters are encouraged to carry EPIC, other identity proofs like ration cards or driving licenses can also be used for voting, as EPIC is not yet compulsory.
