This section uses the example of the Haryana Assembly election in 1987 to illustrate the impact of elections and discusses why elections are essential for representative democracy.

# The Need for Elections

## Assembly Election in Haryana

A newspaper report describes the anticipation before a meeting addressed by Mr. Devi Lal during the 1987 Haryana State Assembly election campaign. The incumbent government was led by the Congress party since 1982. Chaudhary Devi Lal, an opposition leader, led the 'Nyaya Yudh' (Struggle for Justice) movement and formed the Lok Dal party, which allied with other opposition parties. Devi Lal promised that if his party won, his government would waive the loans of farmers and small businessmen, making it the first action of his government.

People were dissatisfied with the existing government and attracted by Devi Lal's promise. In the election, Lok Dal and its allies won overwhelmingly, securing 76 out of 90 seats, with Lok Dal winning 60 seats alone. The Congress won only 5 seats. Following the results, the sitting Chief Minister resigned. The Lok Dal MLAs chose Devi Lal as their leader, and the Governor invited him to become the new Chief Minister. Three days later, he took office and his government issued an order waiving loans for small farmers, agricultural labourers, and small businessmen. His party governed for four years. In the 1991 elections, his party lost popular support, and the Congress won and formed the government.

Jagdeep and Navpreet read this story and drew the following conclusions. Can you say which of these are right or wrong (or if the information given in the story is inadequate to call them right or wrong):

-   Elections can lead to changes in the policy of the government.
-   The Governor invited Devi Lal to become the Chief Minister because he was impressed with his speeches.
-   People are unhappy with every ruling party and vote against it in the next election.
-   The party that wins the election forms the government.
-   This election led to a lot of economic development in Haryana.
-   The Congress Chief Minister need not have resigned after his party lost elections.

> **Answer:**
> -   Elections can lead to changes in the policy of the government. **Right.** The story shows Devi Lal's government waiving loans as promised, a direct policy change resulting from the election outcome.
> -   The Governor invited Devi Lal to become the Chief Minister because he was impressed with his speeches. **Wrong.** The Governor invited Devi Lal because he was chosen as the leader by the newly elected Members of Legislative Assembly (MLAs) of the Lok Dal party, which had won a clear majority.
> -   People are unhappy with every ruling party and vote against it in the next election. **Wrong.** The story shows people were unhappy with the Congress government in 1987 and voted against it. However, it doesn't provide enough information to conclude that people are *always* unhappy with *every* ruling party and vote against them in *every* subsequent election. Devi Lal's party also lost the next election, suggesting people were unhappy with them too after four years.
> -   The party that wins the election forms the government. **Right.** The story explicitly states that Lok Dal and its allies won, Lok Dal had a clear majority, and Devi Lal (their leader) became the Chief Minister and formed the government.
> -   This election led to a lot of economic development in Haryana. **Inadequate Information.** The story only mentions the waiving of loans. It does not provide information or data to assess the overall economic development in Haryana during Lok Dal's rule.
> -   The Congress Chief Minister need not have resigned after his party lost elections. **Wrong.** In a parliamentary democracy like India, the Chief Minister and their government are collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly. If the ruling party loses the election and no longer has a majority, the Chief Minister must resign.

## Why do we need elections?

Elections are a regular feature of any democracy. While over a hundred countries worldwide hold elections, many non-democratic countries also conduct some form of elections.

Imagine a democracy without elections. If everyone could gather daily to make all decisions, representatives wouldn't be needed. However, this is impossible in large communities where not everyone has the time or knowledge for all decisions. Therefore, most democracies rely on representatives.

Could representatives be selected democratically without elections? Methods based on age, experience, education, or knowledge might be considered. While deciding who is most qualified might be difficult, let's assume it's possible. Such a system wouldn't require elections.

But would this be a democracy? How would we know if people approve of their representatives? How would we ensure representatives act according to people's wishes? How could we remove representatives people dislike? This requires a mechanism for people to choose representatives periodically and change them if desired. This mechanism is called **election**. Thus, elections are considered *essential* for representative democracies today.

In an election, voters make several key choices:
-   They choose who will **make laws** for them.
-   They choose who will **form the government** and make major decisions.
-   They choose the **party whose policies** will guide the government and lawmaking.
