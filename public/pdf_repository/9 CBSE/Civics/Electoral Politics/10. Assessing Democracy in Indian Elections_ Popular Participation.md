This section assesses the democratic nature of Indian elections based on the level of public engagement and enthusiasm in the electoral process.

# Popular participation

Another measure of election quality is enthusiastic public participation. If elections aren't free or fair, people might stop participating. Let's examine participation in India:

1.  **Voter turnout** measures the percentage of eligible voters who vote. Turnout has declined in Europe and North America over the last fifty years, but in India, it has remained stable or increased.

2.  In India, **poor, illiterate, and underprivileged people vote in larger proportions** than the rich and privileged. This contrasts with Western democracies like the US, where poor people, African Americans, and Hispanics vote significantly less than white people.

**Image:**

3.  **Common people in India value elections highly**. They believe elections allow them to pressure parties to adopt favourable policies and that their vote influences the country's governance.

4.  **Voter interest in election activities has grown**. In 2004, over one-third of voters participated in campaign activities. More than half identified closely with a political party, and one in seven voters was a party member.

## Activity: Participation in Your Family

Ask the eligible voters in your family whether they voted in the last election to the Lok Sabha or to the state assembly. If they did not, ask them why did they not vote. If they did, ask them which party and candidate they voted for and why. Also ask them whether they had participated in any other election-related activity like attending an election meeting or rally etc.
