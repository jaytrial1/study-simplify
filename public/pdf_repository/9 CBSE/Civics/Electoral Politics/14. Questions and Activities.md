# Questions and Activities

**1** Which of the following statements about the reasons for conducting elections are false?

-   **a** Elections enable people to judge the performance of the government.
-   **b** People select the representative of their choice in an election.
-   **c** Elections enable people to evaluate the performance of the judiciary.
-   **d** People can indicate which policies they prefer.

> **Answer:** **c** Elections primarily enable people to choose representatives, form a government, judge the *government's* performance, and influence policies. They do not directly enable people to evaluate the performance of the judiciary, which is an independent branch.

**2** Which of these is *not* a good reason to say that Indian elections are democratic?

-   **a** India has the largest number of voters in the world.
-   **b** India's Election Commission is very powerful.
-   **c** In India, everyone above the age of 18 has a right to vote.
-   **d** In India, the losing parties accept the electoral verdict.

> **Answer:** **a** Having a large number of voters is a characteristic of India but doesn't inherently make the *elections* democratic. Options b, c, and d directly relate to the conditions and tests for democratic elections discussed in the chapter (independent EC, universal adult franchise, acceptance of outcome).

5 Surekha is an officer in-charge of ensuring free and fair elections in an assembly constituency in a state. Describe what should she focus on for each of the following stages of election:
a Election campaign
b Polling day
c Counting day

> **Answer:**
> a **Election campaign:** Surekha should focus on ensuring that parties and candidates adhere to the Model Code of Conduct and election laws. This includes monitoring campaign spending, preventing the use of religious places or government resources for campaigning, stopping bribery or threats to voters, and ensuring a level playing field for all candidates to hold meetings and rallies.
> b **Polling day:** Surekha should focus on ensuring the security of polling booths, proper identification of voters (using EPIC or other valid IDs), preventing impersonation, ensuring peaceful voting without intimidation or booth capturing, and ensuring that election officials at the booths follow all procedures correctly.
> c **Counting day:** Surekha should focus on ensuring the secure transport and storage of EVMs, supervising the counting process to ensure accuracy and transparency, allowing candidate agents to observe the counting, and preventing any attempts at tampering or rigging during the counting process.

8 Chinappa was convicted for torturing his wife for dowry. Satbir was held guilty of practicing untouchability. The court did not allow either of them to contest elections. Does this decision go against the principles of democratic elections?

> **Answer:** No, this decision does *not* go against the principles of democratic elections. While democratic elections emphasize minimal restrictions on contesting, the chapter mentions that there are "some other restrictions on criminals etc. but these apply in very extreme cases." Convictions for serious crimes like torturing for dowry or practicing untouchability (which is outlawed and a severe social discrimination) fall under such extreme cases where denying the right to contest is considered a reasonable restriction to maintain the integrity and ethical standards expected of public representatives. It aligns with the idea that representatives should act in the best interest of the people and uphold fundamental rights and social justice principles.

9 Here are some reports of electoral malpractices from different parts of the world. Is there anything that these countries can learn from India to improve their elections? What would you suggest in each case?
a During an election in Nigeria, the officer in charge of counting votes deliberately increased the votes of one candidate and declared him elected. The court later found out that more than five lakh votes cast for one candidate were counted in favour of another.
b Just before elections in Fiji, a pamphlet was distributed warning voters that a vote for former Prime Minister, Mahendra Chaudhry will lead to bloodshed. This was a threat to voters of Indian origin.
c In the US, each state has its own method of voting, its own procedure of counting and its own authority for conducting elections. Authorities in the state of Florida took many controversial decisions that favoured Mr. Bush in the presidential elections in 2000. But no one could change those decisions.

> **Answer:** These countries could potentially learn from India's system, particularly regarding the powers and independence of the Election Commission and specific electoral laws/codes of conduct.
> a **Nigeria:** This is a case of rigging during counting. India's system of having **party agents present** during counting to observe and verify the process, combined with the EC's power to order a **re-poll** if fraud is suspected, could help prevent or detect such deliberate manipulation by officials. Suggestion: Implement strict monitoring of counting with party/candidate agent presence and empower an independent body (like India's EC) to investigate and order repolls or recount if irregularities are found.
> b **Fiji:** This is voter intimidation and potentially appealing on ethnic lines. India's **election law prohibiting threatening voters** and **appealing in the name of caste or religion** could be adopted. The EC's power to enforce the **Code of Conduct** and punish violations is also relevant. Suggestion: Enact and strictly enforce laws against voter intimidation and appeals based on ethnicity/religion, with penalties for violators. Empower an independent body to investigate and take action against such practices during the campaign.
> c **US (Florida 2000):** This highlights issues with decentralized election authority and controversial decisions that couldn't be overturned by an independent body. India's system relies on a single, **powerful, and independent Election Commission** overseeing elections nationwide. This central authority ensures consistency and has the power to intervene and correct procedural lapses or unfair decisions made by local officials. Suggestion: Centralize oversight of federal elections under a single, independent national commission with clear powers to set nationwide standards, investigate complaints, and overrule state-level decisions that compromise fairness.

10 Here are some reports of malpractices in Indian elections. Identify what the problem in each case is. What should be done to correct the situation?
a Following the announcement of elections, the minister promised to provide financial aid to reopen the closed sugar mill.
b Opposition parties alleged that their statements and campaign was not given due attention in Doordarshan and All India Radio.
c An inquiry by the Election Commission showed that electoral rolls of a state contain name of 20 lakh fake voters.
d The hoodlums of a political party were moving with guns, physically preventing supporters of other political parties to meet the voters and attacking meetings of other parties.

> **Answer:**
> a **Problem:** Violation of the Model Code of Conduct. Ministers are prohibited from making such promises of public facilities or taking major policy decisions after the election schedule is announced, as it can influence voters using the power of their office.
> **Correction:** The Election Commission should take strict action against the minister and the party for violating the Code of Conduct. This could involve reprimands, warnings, or even disqualification if the violation is severe.
> b **Problem:** Misuse of government-controlled media (Doordarshan, All India Radio) by the ruling party, leading to unfair campaign coverage and denying a level playing field to opposition parties.
> **Correction:** The Election Commission should ensure fair and equitable access to state-controlled media for all recognized political parties during the election period. Guidelines should be enforced to prevent biased reporting or coverage that favours the ruling party.
> c **Problem:** Inclusion of false names and exclusion of genuine names in the voters' list, which is a fundamental flaw affecting the first condition of democratic elections (everyone able to choose, equal vote value).
> **Correction:** The Election Commission must conduct a thorough and accurate revision of the electoral rolls, using methods like door-to-door verification and public checks, to remove fake entries and include all eligible genuine voters. Officials responsible for preparing faulty lists should be held accountable.
> d **Problem:** Intimidation of voters and rigging (preventing voters, attacking meetings) through the use of force and criminal elements. This violates the condition of free and fair elections where people can choose as they wish without fear.
> **Correction:** The Election Commission, in coordination with law enforcement agencies, must ensure adequate security presence in affected areas. Strict action should be taken against the individuals using violence and intimidation, as well as the political party or candidate employing them. This could involve arrests, increased police patrolling, and ordering repolls if voting was affected.

11 Ramesh was not in class when this chapter was being taught. He came the next day and repeated what he had heard from his father. Can you tell Ramesh what is wrong with these statements?
a Women always vote the way men tell them to. So what is the point of giving them the right to vote?
b Party politics creates tension in society. Elections should be decided by consensus not by competition.
c Only graduates should be allowed to stand as candidates for elections.

> **Answer:**
> a **What is wrong:** This statement is based on a stereotype and is incorrect. The chapter explains that in a democracy, *everyone* aged 18 and above has the right to vote, regardless of gender. The principle of universal adult franchise and 'one person, one vote' means each individual voter makes their own choice. Assuming women vote based on instructions from men denies their individual agency and equal standing as citizens with independent views and needs.
> b **What is wrong:** While party politics can sometimes create tension, the chapter argues that political competition is essential for a representative democracy. Competition forces parties and leaders to serve the people to win elections. Deciding elections by 'consensus' sounds peaceful but could lead to a situation where people have no real choice, and leaders are not accountable, which would not be democratic according to the conditions discussed in the chapter.
> c **What is wrong:** This statement contradicts the principles of democratic elections discussed in the chapter. The chapter explicitly argues against imposing educational qualifications for candidates. It states that the relevant qualification for representatives is the ability to understand and represent people, which voters assess. Imposing a graduate degree would disqualify the vast majority of citizens from contesting, going against the spirit of democracy and equal opportunity.
