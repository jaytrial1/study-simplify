# What Makes Elections in India DEMOCRATIC?

We often hear about unfair practices in elections, such as:
-   False names in voters' lists or exclusion of genuine voters.
-   Misuse of government resources by the ruling party.
-   Excessive spending by wealthy candidates and parties.
-   Intimidation of voters and rigging on polling day.

While these reports exist and are sometimes true, they are generally not on a scale that invalidates the entire election process. The critical question is: Can a party win purely through malpractices, without popular support?

## Independent Election Commission

A key factor in fair elections is who conducts them. Are they independent of the government and ruling party? Do they have sufficient power to ensure fairness and do they use it?

For India, the answer is largely positive. Elections are conducted by an independent and powerful **Election Commission (EC)**. It has independence similar to the judiciary. The **Chief Election Commissioner (CEC)** is appointed by the President but cannot be easily removed by the government or ruling party, even if they disagree with the EC's actions.

The Election Commission of India has wide-ranging powers:
-   It makes decisions on *every* aspect of election conduct and control, from announcement to result declaration.
-   It enforces the **Code of Conduct** and punishes violations.
-   During elections, it can issue guidelines to the government to prevent misuse of power and can order the transfer of officials.
-   Government officers on election duty work under the EC's control, not the government's.

In recent decades, the EC has increasingly used and expanded its powers. It frequently reprimands the government and administration for lapses. If polling is deemed unfair in a booth or constituency, the EC can order a **re-poll**. Ruling parties often dislike the EC's actions but must comply, demonstrating the EC's independence and power.
