# Election Campaign

The primary goal of elections is to let people choose their preferred representatives, government, and policies. This requires free and open discussion about who is the best representative, which party would form the best government, or what policies are best. This discussion takes place during **election campaigns**.

In India, the official campaign period lasts two weeks, between the announcement of the final list of candidates and the polling date. During this time, candidates contact voters, leaders hold meetings, and parties mobilize supporters. Newspapers and television are filled with election news and debates. However, campaign preparations often begin months earlier.

In campaigns, parties focus on major issues to attract public attention and votes. Here are some historical slogans:
-   *Garibi Hatao* (Remove poverty) - Congress (Indira Gandhi) in 1971 Lok Sabha elections.
-   *Save Democracy* - Janata Party in 1977 Lok Sabha elections (promised to reverse Emergency excesses).
-   *Land to the Tiller* - Left Front in 1977 West Bengal Assembly elections.
-   *Protect the Self-Respect of the Telugus* - N. T. Rama Rao (Telugu Desam Party) in 1983 Andhra Pradesh Assembly elections.

While freedom in campaigning is ideal in a democracy, regulation is sometimes needed to ensure a fair chance for all. According to India's election law, no party or candidate can:
-   **Bribe or threaten voters.**
-   **Appeal to voters based on caste or religion.**
-   **Use government resources** for campaigning.
-   Spend over Rs. 25 lakh in a Lok Sabha constituency or Rs. 10 lakh in an Assembly constituency.

Violating these rules can lead to the election being rejected by the court.

Additionally, political parties have agreed to a **Model Code of Conduct** for campaigns:
-   No party or candidate can use any **place of worship** for election propaganda.
-   No party or candidate can use **government vehicles, aircraft, or officials** for elections.
-   Once elections are announced, Ministers cannot lay foundation stones, make big policy decisions, or promise public facilities.
