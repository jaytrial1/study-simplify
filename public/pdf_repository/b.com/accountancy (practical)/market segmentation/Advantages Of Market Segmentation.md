3\. ADVANTAGES OF MARKET SEGMENTATION:\*

The basic object of market segmentation is to identify the groups of homogenous buyers and to devise a marketing plan suitable to each of these groups. This way sales and profits can be raised. This is the most important benefit of market segmentation. In detail, the main advantages of market segmentation can be described as follows:

(1) Significant Increase in Sales: Naturally, there will be a significant increase in sales, if needs and preferences of a particular group of buyers are studied systematically and a sales promotion campaign appealing to them is forcefully launched. A producer who sells in a general market to all groups of buyers cannot influence them so successfully and therefore his market is bound to be limited.

(2) Proper Market Strategy: If a limited group of buyers is to be

served, it is possible to frame a suitable advertisement and publicity to appeal to the group in question most effectively. For example, the companies making jeans pants can attract the youth and the adolescent through impressing upon them the charm of current fashions. In the field of personal selling also, salesmen can focus on a limited group of buyers. The gift scheme, competition etc. can be arranged to appeal to a particular group of buyers. For instance, the jeans pant manufacturers can arrange a competition of identifying the film actors on the basis of the given photographs. The expenditure on such marketing strategies prove to be profitable.

(3) Production According to Needs: The needs of the distinct group buyers can be determined and only these products can be produced which can meet those needs effectively. Thus, the modern concept of marketing can materialise.

(4) Specialisation in Management: The management of different market segments can be entrusted to different sales managers and advantages of specialisation can be obtained. Each sales manager, being familiar with the peculiarities and preferences of the group of customers assigned to him, can satisfy their needs most efficiently.

(5) Transformation on Market Opportunities: On the basis of the knowledge of distinct characteristics of each group of customers, it can be easily judged whether there is any possibility of introducing a new product and risks of business can be reduced. Moreover, through a careful study of different market segments, information can be obtained about market segments in which there are profitable opportunities of sales of the existing product. This information can help the producers in extending the market of the existing products.

(6) Adjustments to Changing Circumstances: When a producer produces for one or two market segments only, he can be in close contact with customers therein. He can be well-informed of the change in their needs and preferences. Therefore, he can immediately change production and sales policies in accordance with the change in market conditions. The result is that he can successfully stand in competition against the rival firms.

(7) Beneficial to Customers: Each group of customer gets a product according to its needs. In addition, the average cost of production declines due to the increase in efficiency and also due to the economies of large scale production. The customers also get the benefit in the form of lower prices For example, if a textile mill, instead of producing so many different varieties of cloth each on a small scale, concentrates on the production of shirting and suiting only, average cost will decline to a significant extent. Moreover producers, being aware of the peculiarities of each group of buyers can render better service and add to their satisfaction.

(8) Beneficial to Society: The producers are in constant search for new products which can better satisfy the needs of a limited group of buyers. Thus, new products are continuously produced and put in the markets, so that buyers can enjoy a high and varied standard of living. And with the setting up of new industries, the industrial and economic development of the country is also accelerated.

(9) Better Use of Resources: In underdeveloped countries, there is a shortage of capital and natural resources, Hence it is in the interest of the nation that they are efficiently utilised. Market segmentation helps in the efficient utilisation of available resources because production takes place in conformity with the needs of the people in each market segment. There is less possibility of waste in marketing also as the strategy of marketing conforms to each market segment. For example, an advertisement meant for the whole market consisting of various groups of buyers with different needs and preferences can be less effective as compared to an advertisement that is specially prepared and conducted for a distinct group of customers only.

(10) Beneficial to Small Firms: Small firms can concentrate on the production and sale of goods meant for only a distinct small group of buyers and stand in competition against the big firms. This cannot be possible in the absence of market segmentation because small firms with limited resources cannot be expected to satisfy the whole multitude of buyers in a large market.

Limitations of Market Segmentation Due to the above mentioned advantages, market segmentation is becoming increasingly popular. But its limitations must not be overlooked.

(1) Since each market segment contains a limited group of buyers, it is difficult to reap full advantages of large scale production and sale.

(2) Different varieties of a product are produced to satisfy the needs of different market segments. This means special-purpose machines are purchased for the production of each variety. Thus market segmentation requires huge investment: in different kinds of specific machines. But if due to smallness of each market segment, sales revenues do not increase at the same rate as investment, rate of return declines.

(3) Different media of publicity are used to influence the decisions of buyers in different market segments. Hence, the advantages of large investment in a single medium of publicity are lost.

(4) Marketing programmes devised for different market segments become complicated, because it becomes necessary to make different arrangements of distribution and to keep stocks of different varieties of a product.

Of course, the above limitations will be less serious, if each market segment consists of a large number of buyers.
