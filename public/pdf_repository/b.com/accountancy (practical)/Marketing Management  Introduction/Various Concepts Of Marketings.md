8\. VARIOUS CONCEPTS OF MARKETINGS: \*\*

Marketing activity can be conducted under different concepts, If a producer produces with the belief that what he produces will all be bought by the customers, it is production-oriented concept of marketing. Some producers hold that their products are superior in quality and can be put into the the market and sold easily, it is product-oriented concept of marketing in Some producers believe that anything can be sold if sales promotion activity pe is effective. They are operating under sales-oriented concept. In all these u concepts consumers' needs are neglected, hence in recent times a new ca concept called market-oriented concept has been advocated. The central idea of this concept is that consumers' needs should be identified and those goods should be produced which can satisfy their needs effectively. This new concept has received wide popularity in the industrially developed countries of the world. To this has been added recently, a latest concept p known as the societal concept of marketing in which not only consumers on needs, but the welfare of the whole society is stressed. This goes beyond marketing concept and is developed out of the belief that those products tl- which satisfy the needs and wants of the consumers may not be in the interest of the society e.g, cigarettes, liquor etc.

Let us discuss these concepts of marketing in detail:

(A) Production Concept: This concept holds that buyers would purchase whatever is produced and, hence it is not necessary to identify their needs and preferences. In the words of Philip Kotler "The production concept holds that consumers will favour those products that are available and highly affordable and therefore, management should concentrate on improving production and distribution efficiency". \*

This concept is based on the following assumptions:

(i) Consumers are interested in the goods that are available to them and are of low prices;

(ii) Consumers have knowledge of the prices charged by the rival firms

(iii) Except price, consumers do not take into account other differentials in the products of the same class.

(iv) Hence, management should concentrate only on production efficiency and low prices.

A Production-oriented concept of marketing may be proper in underdeveloped countries, like India because goods are in short supply in these countries and hence, buyers are in a hurry to buy whatever is available in the market. If groundnut oil is not available, they are ready to purchase palmoline oil, or cotton seeds oil or sunflower oil etc. Hence, in underdeveloped countries, firms concentrate only on production and take care to see that goods are made available to the people. This concept can be applied also to a situation in which production costs are high and markets can be expanded only by reducing costs and prices. For example, Ford wanted to make available a motor-car to each of the American families at the beginning of this century and planned to reduce costs and price by producing motor cars on a large scale. And the result is that there is at least one motor-car per family in the U.S.A. now-a-days.

(B) Product Concept: This concept holds that consumers will favour those products which are best in quality. Management in these product- oriented organisations focus their energy on making good products and improving them over time. As Kotler said, many of these managers are caught up in a love affair with their product. For example, the Indian railways hold that they provide best services and people cannot do without these services. On the other hand, the bus and air transport companies can override the railways by providing services better than those of railways.

The product concept is based on the following assumptions:

(i) Consumers purchase quality products and not a commodity that satisfies their needs.

(ii) Consumers are interested in high quality only.

(iii) Consumers have knowledge of differences in quality of different products and choose only that product which is superior in quality.

(iv) Hence, producers concentrate only on the improvement of the quality of their products.

This is a narrow concept of marketing. It is relevant when consumers' needs can be satisfied with existing products. But in the long run, it may be necessary to change the quality of the product, if better products are introduced in the market to meet the needs of the consumers more effectively. If product quality is not changed under these circumstances, it will be discarded by the buyers. It is only in such fields as railways, post offices and some non-commercial fields such as music parties which adhere to the product-concept, believing that their services are superior. In the field of business, for example, it may be stated that tape-recorded cassettes may be top most in quality yet they can never replace the video cassettes.

(C) Selling Concept: According to this concept, sales can be raised if sufficient selling and promotion efforts are made by the producers. The consumers will not buy enough of their products not because the product fo is not good, but because selling efforts are not made. Thus this concept can lay stress on the sales promotion efforts and not on the needs of the consumers. It represents a sales oriented approach. As Philip Kotler has on stated, 'The selling concept holds that consumers, if left alone, will ordinarily fa not buy enough of the organisation's products. The organisation must therefore undertake an aggressive selling and promotion effort'.

The selling concept is based on the following assumptions:

(i) The consumers generally have a tendency not to buy goods, which they do not need.

(ii) The consumers can be induced to buy more through various sales promotion efforts.

(iii) The main function of a firın is to strengthen its sales organisation so that consumers can be induced to buy its product.

The concept was popular among businessmen before 1950\. The sectors well-known for relying too much on this concept of marketing are insurance companies, automobile industry and political parties fighting elections. Generally, people are not aware of benefits of insurance, and hence they do not come forward on their own to have insurance. Hence, insurance agents have to go to their doors to convince them of the importance of insurance. The same approach is adopted by the automobile industry in the U. S. A and also by the manufacturers of Maruti cars and scooters in India. Similarly, each political party has to undertake a propaganda campaign v in times of election to impress upon the voters that its candidates are the best. Once they win the election, they hardly care for the needs and problems of the voters in their respective constituencies.

This concept of marketing is based on a wrong notion. Its basic features are to promote sales by any means and not to care for the satisfaction of the consumers.

It is clear that in all the three concepts of marketing discussed above, consumers' needs are ignored, consumers' satisfaction is not taken into account. Such concepts are bound to fail in modern times.,

Let us illustrate this point. A wrist watch company was well-known for its fine quality wrist watches. Its sales rose continuously. But a time came when its sales began to decline. Because this company was proud only of the quality and precision of its products. Its approach was product- oriented. On the other hand, the buyers' attitudes had changed now in favour of attractive shape, automatic working and low prices of these products. The company ignored these changes. To maintain sales, the company increased its expenditure on salesmen and advertisement etc. but its efforts did not yield fruits. There was no favourable change in sales. The fact was that those rival companies which made wrist watches in accordance with buyers' preferences succeeded in capturing its market. The rival companies' approach was consumers oriented or market oriented. They practised the marketing concept and reaped the fruits.

The example is a testimony to the fact that only those firms can succeed in the market, which respect the preferences of the buyers. While those firms which ignore buyers' preferences are bound to fail.

The other interesting example is from the autobiography of the once powerful ruler of the U.S.S.R. Nikita Khrushchev. He has stated. "I also asked comrade Tito (late president of Yugoslavia) if they had much of a problem with fashion conscious young men and women, chasing after tourists, trying to buy all sorts of trinkets off then, especially around the hotels. In our country" I said, "We are ashamed to see our people buying and bartering and begging from foreigners. How do you deal with this problem?"

"We don't really have that problem here." said Tito, "and I'll tell you why. When some item becomes fashionable among our young people, we buy the necessary equipment for a factory and start manufacturing the item ourselves. Of course, consumer tastes are always changing, but all you have to do is use your head and make sure your industries keep up with fashions and adapt to changing consumer demands."\*

This is why a new concept has come into vogue in which consumer's P preferences are respected. This is known as marketing concept.\*\*

(D) Marketing Concept: (i) According to Stanton, "In its fullest sense, marketing concept is a philosophy of business that states that the customers' want satisfaction is the economic and social justification for a firm's existence. Consequently, all company activities must be devoted to finding out what the customers want and then satisfying those wants, while still making a profit over the long run."

(ii) According to Philip Kotler, "The marketing concept is a management orientation that holds that the key task of the organisation is to determine the needs and wants of target markets and to adapt the organisation to delivering the desired satisfaction more effectively and efficiently than its competitors."

(iii) According to Jefkins, 'Marketing concept is business philosophy that bases decisions on objective appreciation of consumer needs and preferences." (from Dictionary of Marketing and Communication).

On the basis of these definitions, it is possible to describe some important characteristics of marketing concepts. These are as follows:

(i) Consumer-oriented Philosophy:\* It is a philosophy, an approach to management, a mental state. Its main feature is its consumer orientation, which prevails over the company's policy, procedures, thinking and work guide etc. All business activities are directed to this goal. The focus is on what consumers want and how to satisfy their needs.

(ii) Emphasis on Research: The marketing policy of the business should be based on market research on consumers' preferences and needs etc. In addition, emphasis is placed on the product planning and product development so that consumers' need may satisfy more effectively

(iii) Goal of Profit to Both Parties Marketing concept is consistent with the goal of profit maximisation. But, it implies profits to be enjoyed by both parties of exchange. The basic goal of each business unit is profit, but it cannot survive for long if it does not work in a way which is beneficial to customers also. Thus, this concept's message is that business should be so managed that it "proves profitable to the producer and customers both."

(iv) Market-oriented Organisation A company that practises marketing concept devises its organisation in such a manner that marketing is given importance over other issues. As Peter Drucker has remarked, "If we want to know what a business is, we have to start with its purpose. And its purpose must lie outside of the business itself...... There is only one valid definition of business purpose, to create a customer." Philip Kotler rightly says that, "The marketing concept says find wants and fill them rather than create products and sell them." In the modern time, due to keen competition and worldwide markets there is no other alternative for a business unit without accepting the marketing concept approach.

If a business is organised in view of this objective, the marketing manager will rank higher in the organisation. If a company is too large and is divided into various departments, this division will also be based on market considerations and not on the basis of production,

(v) Change according to Market Environment: The factors affecting the trends of the market environment are also given due importance in business policy. In other words, changes are made according to the changes in the market environment.

(vi) Integrated Marketing Policy Marketing is viewed as a system. In other words, all activities associated with marketing are integrated into a definite system. All activities are undertaken with the same goal in mind-the goal of consumers' satisfaction.

(E) Societal Marketing Concept: Even marketing concepts are not free from criticism in recent times. The concept recommends that consumers' needs be identified and satisfied and satisfied effectively. Now, many people have begun to raise the question whether this is really in the long run in the interests of the consumers and society. They point out that (i) the fast food industry does offer tasty food to the people but it is not nutritious, and hence, it is detrimental to their health in the long run. (ii) These foods have high fat content and increase the incidence of heart failure. (iii) The chemical industry caters to the needs of many other industries, but they pollute rivers and streams, emit smoke and pollute the atmosphere and spoil the health of the people around.

(iv) The detergent industry caters to the peoples' desire for whiter clothes, but it also pollutes rivers, kills fish and injures the skin also.

(v) The auto industry caters to the demand for large automobiles like trucks, but the diesel smoke pollutes the air and leads to more fatal accidents.

These situations led to an improvement in marketing concepts. The new concept known as 'societal marketing concept' suggests that marketing policies should be framed so that consumers' needs are satisfied, the company makes profit and the interests of the society are protected. Thus, marketing should strike a balance between these three considerations.

According to Philip Kotler, "The societal marketing concept is a management orientation that holds that the key task of the organisation is to determine the needs and wants of target markets and to accept the organisation to deliver the desired satisfaction more effectively and efficiently than its competitors in a way that preserves or enhances the consumer's and the society's well being"

This concept is based on the following assumptions:

(i) Consumers' needs are not always consistent with long run interests of themselves or of the society.

(ii) Now, consumers favour those firms which give consideration to consumers' satisfaction and also to the long run consumer and societal well-being.

(iii) The main function of business management is to satisfy consumers' needs in a manner which is conducive to long term interests of consumers and society both.

To practise societal marketing concept, following measures may be suggested:

(i) The business units should place those goods in the market which can satisfy consumers' needs. The customers know their needs, but they do not know how or through which goods can these needs be satisfied. Under such circumstances, firms should discover proper goods to satisfy consumers' needs.

(ii) Those of the existing goods should be improved which are popular in the market and can satisfy consumers' needs, but are injurious to society. For example, vehicles using petrol or diesel should be replaced by vehicles run by electricity. The cigarette industry should invent a type of cigarette which does not harm smokers' lungs.

But, the question is whether the firms wedded to the societal marketing concept can earn enough profit. It is needless to say that if a business practising societal marketing is not profitable or is less profitable, no one will come forward to run it. Of course, there are some companies which serve the long term interests of the society and earn enough profits also.

SOCIAL MARKETING CONCEPT:

According to Kotler and al. (2005) "The societal \[social\] marketing concept holds that the organization's task is to determine the needs, wants, and interests of target markets and to deliver the desired satisfactions more effectively and efficiently than competitors, in a way that preserves or enhances the consumer's and the society's well-being." Ethical and social considerations are built in marketing concepts in a way that penetrates consumer's attention and motivates them to make the change.

The social marketing that is practiced in these cases could be characterized as cause-related marketing. This means that the general purpose of these programmes is to deliver mutual benefit and well-being for both consumers and the partners of the programme (in publicly financed programmes the society). In cause-related marketing it must be taken into account that some consumers look for signs of good corporate citizenship. This applies to such programmes in which both social marketing and product sales are combined. In other words, consumers when making purchase decisions regarding, for example, energy saving domestic appliances, may have expectations for companies' public interest to environmental issues and not only for profits.

In social and cause-related marketing it is also a question about companies' reputation as a good corporate citizenship. This sets criteria for planning of the whole project. Several examples of companies implementing or participating energy-related behavioral change programmes were presented among the case studies. However, over half of the programmes were implemented by national or regional organizations concentrating mainly on promoting energy efficiency and/or renewable energy. It may be possible that these organizations are considered more neutral by the consumers.

For Example:

(a) Johnson & Johnson has stopped the production of powdered milk, just to promote breast feeding for the healthy baby.

(b) Raymond's has stopped giving plastic bags and promoted paper bags for saving the globe from global warming.
