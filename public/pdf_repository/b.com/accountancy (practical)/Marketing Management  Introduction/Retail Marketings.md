9\. RETAIL MARKETINGS:

"Marketing is the activity, set of institutions, and processes for creating, communicating, delivering, and exchanging offerings that have value for customers, clients, partners and society at large. It generates the strategy that underlies sales techniques, business communication, and business developments. It is an integrated process through which companies build strong customer relationships and create value for their customers and for themselves."

Concepts:

In the growing market, retail marketing has become one of the major emerging trends in the entire economical cycle. It is the retail market only which provides the consumer a basic platform to encounter with goods and a shop keeper for the first time. A retail market consists of a fixed location like a boutique, store, departmental store etc., where in these locations consumers meet the shop keeper and purchase goods in return of certain value. Maintaining a certain profit margin, these shop keepers sell goods to their consumers. The basic motive of these shopkeepers is to satisfy the consumers and fulfill their needs and demands.

Customer needs are the basic key factors of retail. Retail marketing consists of 5 basic pillars, first is saving the precious time of the customers. Second is setting the right prices of the goods, third is creating a proper connection with the emotions of the customers, fourth pillar is paying the right respect to the customers and lastly solving the problems of the customer is another pillar of retail.

Creating customer loyalty is the basic function of retail, as once you create customer loyalty towards your brand it will be easier for you to stay in the market for a longer period of time. Creating customer loyalty is not a very easy task, as it takes years for a brand to create customer loyalty.

Previously customers used to shift to other brands very easily as there did not exist any brand loyalty. But now with the extensive features of retail marketing, it has become easier for the company not only to capture a huge market but at the same time create a strong bonding with the customers. Thus, this sort of marketing strategy did not only ignite the sales target and profits but at the same time increased the brand loyalty.

9.1 DEFINITION OF RETAIL MARKETING:

According to Philip Kotler, "Retailing includes all activities involved in selling goods or services directly to the final consumers for their personal, non-business use. Any organisation that does this selling whether a manufacturer, wholesaler or retailer is doing retailing. It does not matter how the goods or services are sold or where they are sold."

9.2 STORES RETAILING/TRADITIONAL RETAIL MARKETING WITH STORES:

Most of the retailing takes place through stores. But there are different types of stores. Important among them are as follows:

(1) Convenience Stores

(2) Speciality Stores

(3) Departmental Stores

(4) Super Markets

(5) Discount Stores

(6) Off Price Stores

(7) Super Stores

(8) Catalogue Show rooms.

(It should be noted that some of these types of stores are not found in India, but they are found in developed countries.)

(1) Convenience Stores: Such stores are found in every nook and corner in India. These stores are situated near the residence of the customers and kept open for a long period of time. Often these stores work throughout the week, observe no holiday and provide all kinds of goods of many manufacturers. Most glaring examples of this type of stores are medical stores and provision stores. These stores provide such facilities that customers can go to purchase any product they have at any time convenient to them. Also they give credit facilities also to customers.

(2) Speciality Stores: These stores sell special types of products made or manufactured by different companies. For example, clothing stores, furniture shops, book stores etc. Many of these stores adopt specialisation to a greater extent. For instance, the garments stores are subdivided into gents' wear, ladies wear and children's wear. Nowadays, some super speciality stores are gaining popularity, for example stores selling only men's shirts. Many experts are of the opinion that super speciality stores will grow the fastest to take advantage of increasing opportunities for market segmentation and specialisation.

(3) Departmental Stores: Though the number of departmental stores has been increasing in India, they have not so developed as in other countries. Generally, departmental stores provide goods to meet the requirements of the families such as garments, toys, crockery etc. Different goods are sold through separate departments in the store and each department is under the supervision of the expert with specialised knowledge relating to that product. Some departmental stores give contracts to run at certain sections of the store to the traders dealing in particular goods.

The departmental stores have developed rapidly after the pr Second world war. But now it seems that these stores are in the declining stage of their life cycle. This is because in most of them there are the problems of heavy traffic and of the lack of parking at facilities for the vehicles. Also, other types of retail stores have been set up in far off places and these stores sell goods at a relatively low price. To avoid these problems, departmental stores have now started opening their branches in suburban areas where there are better parking facilities. Moreover, these stores also organise "sales' offering goods at concessional rates with a view SC to attract customers.

(4) Super Markets: Such stores have been established to supply food, laundry and other household maintenance products W on a large scale. Generally, customers themselves select the goods they want to buy. Of course, the profitability of supermarkets is quite low because they have to face competition from various types of stores such as super stores and shops at the corners of the streets etc. For this reason super markets have begun selling various other goods in addition to food items. The largest increase has been in non-food items, which now constitute 25 per cent of their h total sales. The trend is towards keeping those goods for sale m which offer a greater margin of profit, e.g., medicines, sports goods, hardware and camera etc. Moreover, departmental stores are now striving to provide more facilities to the customers. They are upgrading their facilities through more expensive locations, larger parking plots, carefully planned architecture and decor.

(5) Discount Stores: These are the stores where standard quality goods are always sold at prices lower than those charged at other stores. These stores sell standard goods at lower prices by accepting lower margin and selling higher volume.

The stores which sell goods on discount occasionally, say, once a year cannot be called discount stores. Only those stores are regarded as discount stores which regularly sell goods at lower prices throughout the year. In Ahmedabad, for example, there are some stores which sell hosiery products at a price lower than 2 to 10 rupees per piece as compared to other stores. In the Revadi Bazar in Ahmedabad, cloth is always available at a low price as compared to other stores if it is purchased in bulk. Since discount stores usually do business in shops of reasonable size without attractive showcases and furniture, they can sell goods at low prices. Of course, discount stores in India are not comparable to those in the U.S.A. Even in the western countries, discount stores have lost their peculiarity as many of the department stores also have now started selling goods on discount. Some of the departmental stores have adopted specialisation and again sell such goods as radio sets, shoes and electronics on discount and so the discount stores are losing their importance.

(6) Off-price Retailers Off-price retailers are different from discount stores. These retailers buy goods at less than regular wholesale prices and hence they can sell these goods to the customers at less than retail prices. They generally purchase at low prices the second quality goods from the factory, or unsold goods lying in bulk at the factory or entire stock of goods from the shop or company on the verge of closure. This is why they can afford to sell at very low prices. Such stores are found generally in the shoes and cloth industries. There are three types of off-price retailers: Factory outlets, independents and warehouse clubs. First type of stores are owned and managed by the manufacturers themselves, where irregular goods or discontinued goods are sold. These stores offer discounts at the rate of 30 to 50 per cent of the retail price.

Second, small independent retailers do this business or they work as a constituent of a large store. These may be described as independent low price offering retailers. Thirdly, there are warehouse clubs which sell goods at low prices to their members only. They do not offer the facility of home delivery or credit card or credit facilities.

(7) Super Store: The retail shops selling goods on a scale larger than that of a super market are known as super stores. These stores sell goods meeting the consumers total needs for daily requirements of food and non-food items. These stores offer othe services also e.g., laundry, shoe repairing, discounting of cheque and bargain lunch counters etc. The combination stores among them are larger in size, while hyper markets are so large as to occupy 80,000 to 2,20,000 square feet area. In these stores, some special items, in addition to routine ones, are also provided. The customers themselves select goods for purchase. Every community carries a card giving information about its price and use to the customers.

(8) Catalogue Showrooms: This type of stores are found rarely in India. These stores sell most saleable branded goods at prices lower than those stated in the catalogue. The catalogue, showrooms are found in such business fields as jewellery, luggage toys, sports goods etc. They prefer to supply goods on a large scale at low prices by reducing their costs. The catalogue showrooms are also facing tough competition from the departmental stores and discount stores.

Some of the characteristics of retailers are as follows:

(1) Just as every product has a life cycle comprising, force stages through which the product must pass, various types of retailers also have a life cycle of their own. A new type of retail store is formed, it grows, moves to its climax and finally it declines. Of course, different types take different time to reach their peak level.. Some of them take 50 to 80 years, e.g., departmental stores Some would reach their climax within a period of 8 to 10 years only. Moreover, old types decline in the sense that the number of retail shops wound up exceeds the number of retail shops set up during a year. Of course, some of the declining stores strive to sustain by adopting new methods of management or marketing.

The life cycle is caused by the fact that the old stores provide different kinds of services for which they charge high prices to the customers. As against this, some new stores get established providing less services but selling goods at lower prices. For instance, many buyers visit the departmental stores, take a glance at the goods available there, make decisions about the goods to be purchased and then go to discount stores to purchase those goods. Gradually, the discount stores also begin to offer additional services to the customers and raise prices along with it.

For this reason, the stores selling at low prices get a boost. (This does not apply to India, because the retailers in India do not offer services to the customers. They seem to be ignorant of the kind of services the customers need.)

(2) There are so many varieties of stores providing various services to the customers. For example, self service shops where customers themselves select goods they purchase and pay money for them. Since stores do not provide any service, they charge Jow prices. Secondly, in the stores where customers themselves choose goods, services of the salesmen are also made available to the customers. Since in these stores, some salaried salesmen are appointed, the cost goes up and to recover a higher cost, they charge high prices for the goods. Third, there are retail shops which provide additional services such as the facility to buy on credit and therefore they have to bear a little more cost. Lastly, there are stores which extend full services to the customers such as sale on credit, free delivery, acceptance of returned goods, waiting rooms and restaurants etc.

They have to bear a very high cost of these services and consequently they charge very high prices to the customers.

9.3 NON-STORE RETAILING / TRADITIONAL RETAIL MARKETING WITHOUT STORES:

Though most of the sales occur through stores and shops, recently non-store retailing is gathering momentum. It is estimated that about 15 to 20 per cent of the retail sale to the final consumers is channelled through this route. Some experts believe that this will increase to 30 to 35 per cent in the near future. The main methods of non-store retailing are as follows:

(1) Direct Selling

(2) Automatic Vending

(3) Buying Service Selling

(4) Direct Marketing

(1) Direct Selling Under this method of retailing, retailer himself or his salesmen go to the residence or to the office of the customers to sell their goods. The company imparts training to its salesmen, fixes quota for each salesman and pays them commission on their sales. Sometimes the company arranges a small gathering at the residence of some individual and it attempts there to sell its product. In a way, direct selling is a costly method as it requires paying a high rate of commission to all salesmen. In addition, it involves the cost of selection of salesmen, their training and their supervision also. Besides, to motivate the salesmen to expand sales as much as possible, a scheme of awarding them prizes for good performance is also necessary.

(2) Automatic Vending: The system of obtaining goods by inserting coins in the machines has developed after the second world war. According to one estimate sales under this system in the U.S.A. have gone up to 22 billion dollars by the year 1988\. This system has been most suitable for such items as cigarette newspapers, tea, coffee, cold drinks, hosiery and cosmetics etc.For this purpose, machines have been installed at such places as base workshops, offices, large retail shops, petrol pumps and railway stations etc. In the U.S.A. about 7000 traders have installed 10 lakhs such machines at different places.

The main advantage of this system is that sales continue for 24 hours and customers themselves get the goods they need. Despite this, this method is expensive and the goods this system t provides are costlier by 15 to 20 per cent according to one estimate. The reason is that maintenance and repair of the machines involve costs and the cases of theft have also been reported at some places. To the customers, the main problem is that machines often go out of order. The stocks of goods may not be adequate and if the goods are damaged or spoilt, they cannot be returned back. Yet, in some fields, the system is well received, especially in the case of video games and the ATM facility in the banks which makes money available all the time during 24 hours.

(3) Buying Service Selling Under this method some buying service institutions enrol employees of the large organisations as their members, e.g., school, college, hospital and government offices etc. The members are given a list of retailers from whom they can get goods they need on discount. The retailer offers commission to such buying service institutions. Many institutions distributing credit cards provide similar service in India.

(4) Direct Marketing: When a producer himself directly sells these goods to the final consumers, it is called direct marketing. There are two types of direct marketing: one, the producer opens a store for a chain of stores and second the producer sells through his salesmen as is the practice adopted by the small local producers most often. In addition, there are some other forms of direct marketing. For instance, direct mail marketing in which the producer sends catalogue and other literature to the potential buyers. The Ford Company in the U.S.A. sends video tapes to the persons making inquiries in the context of its advertisement. The video tape contains technical information about the car as also some answers to the questions which the customers would most probably like to ask about the car. Either the company itself collects the names of the potential buyers or obtains them from the intermediaries whose business is to provide the list of the names of potential buyers. It is estimated that in the U.S.A. about 30 per cent of the customers make purchases through postal correspondence. The fourth instrument of sales is the telephone. Looking at the rapidity with which the number of telephone holders is increasing, it is safe to say that this method will spread rapidly in future. According to one estimate, the retailers selling goods through this method paid as much as 142 billion dollars to the telephone company in 1987 by way of their telephone bills. The fifth method is selling through television. In India also, special programmes are sponsored by the companies on different T. V. channels for promoting sales of their goods, e.g. at Asian Skyshop. In addition, some private channels also display advertisements on T.V. for the purpose. In the U.S.A. electronic shopping and Kiosk shopping methods are also prevalent nowadays. 

9.4 RETAIL ORGANISATIONS:

Generally, retailing is of the nature of sole proprietorship. Sometimes however, company and cooperative form of organisations are also observed wherein company chain stores, voluntary chains, consumer cooperatives, franchise organisations and merchandising conglomerate all most prevalent.

(1) Company Chain Stores: The company chain stores run by the Bata Company are well-known in India. A company under this organisation set up its stores in the same city (e.g. The Edward washing company in Ahmedabad) or in a number of cities. The purchase and sales system in all these stores is managed by the central office. Generally, a single product is sold through these stores. The company organisation is more suitable for it because it can purchase a commodity in bulk and sell it at a low price. I can avail of services of the experts in different fields. The advertisement expenditure can also be minimised because a single piece of advertisement is enough for the publicity of so many stores. Some chain stores give autonomy to their local managers to enable them to adjust to the local conditions.

(2) Voluntary Chain In an effort to meet the competition from the chain stores run by the companies, the wholesaler forms a union of the retailers through a voluntary chain store and purchases a product on a large scale on behalf of all of them Sometimes, retailers form a retail co-operative of their own and make purchases on behalf of it and jointly attempt to sell the goods. This is how they can successfully withstand the competition from the chain stores.

(3) Consumer Cooperatives: This is a retail store run by the consumer co-operatives. When a group of people feels that they have to pay a higher price in purchasing them from the retailers, they set up a co-operative society which purchases goods directly from the producers or wholesalers and sells these goods to its members at prices lower than the current ones or sells these goods at regular retail prices and distributes profit among the members on the basis of their individual purchases. The cooperative society is managed by the members themselves. The policy decisions are taken in the meetings of the members.

(4) Franchise Organisation Franchise means a right to sell. When a well reputed company intends to increase its sales, it confers on the retailers in different localities and cities a right to sell its goods. The goods are sold based on the company's prestige and retailers take advantage of the goodwill of the company. In return, the retailers pay royalty to the company. Many companies charge a fee before granting selling rights to the retailers, and then they take royalty from them on the basis of the sales revenue. If the companies provide some resources to the retailers, they charge a rent also. The world famous American company McDonald Fast Foods grants selling rights to the retailers all over the world in the same manner.(5) Merchandising Conglomerate: Some companies set up Sestores to sell a number of different products. These stores are managed by the central office of the company. The company does not restrict sales to a single product, but goes on adding whichever product it finds to be profitable.
