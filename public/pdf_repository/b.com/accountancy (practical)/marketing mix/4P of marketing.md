
## **2\. 'FOUR Ps' IN MARKETING / '4P' OF MARKETING MIX :**

Product is the point of origin of marketing in any business unit. It is only when a product is produced by a producer or purchased for resale by a trader, that the problem of marketing arises. Hence, 'product' is the most significant element of marketing. The product may be a physical commodity or it may be a service. The next question is what price to charge for this product. Obviously that price is considered as reasonable which is profitable to the producer and reasonable to the buyers. Hence, the other important element in marketing is price. When a product is ready for sale and price is also fixed, the problem is how to send it to the innumerable buyers scattered over vast areas. To solve this problem, a proper system of physical distribution is necessary. The marketing manager has to decide whether the company itself should sell the product through its retail stores, or sell it through wholesalers, or through retailers or through some other means. Once a product is placed in the market, it is necessary to take some steps for sales promotion also. In other words, sales promotion is also an important element in marketing. There are four well-known methods to promote sales; advertisement, personal selling, sales promotion and publicity.

Philip Kolter writes. "Marketing mix is the set of marketing tools that the firm uses to pursue its marketing objectives in the target market."

The other definition given by him is, "Marketing mix is the set of controllable variables that the firm can use to influence the buyers' responses." According to R. S. Davar, "The policies adopted by the manufacturers to attain success in the market constitute the Marketing Mix."

In short, there are four P's which constitute a marketing mix. They are as follows:\*

(1) Product

(2) Price

(3) Physical Distribution

(4) Promotion

The group of these four P's marketing-mix. Prof. Neil Borden of Harvard University propounded the idea of marketing mix in an article entitled 'The Concept of Marketing Mix' published in the Journal of Advertising Research, in June, 1964\. A marketing mix is a combination of product, distribution, sales promotion and price in an optimum proportion so that the company can re	 ach effectively that segment of the market to which it wants to sell goods.

(1) Product Product is a basic element of marketing. The marketing organisation has to take some important decisions concerning the product, which together form the product strategy of the company. Two important aspects of this strategy are once to place new product in the market ie. product innovation and, two, to make changes in the existing product i.e. product planning and development.

The producer has to decide what should be the nature of the new product and whether it will be acceptable to the market. He has to inquire whether the new product will satisfy the needs and wants of the customers and whether customers will be able to pay the price. If proper investigation is not made and the problem has not been given due consideration, the new product is likely to fail.

The other decision is with regard to product mix. Generally, a company prefers to produce a product related to its main product. For example, the Bush Company makes T.V. sets along with its radio sets. The D.C.M. The company makes cloth, but along with it, it also produces cement and chemicals. Thus, the company has to examine the nature and profitability of its product mix.

The third decision is concerning the policy to be adopted at different stages of the life cycle of the product. In the initial stage, the price of a newly introduced product is high generally, and yet its demand tends also to remain high due to its novelty. For instance, when T.V. sets were introduced in the market in 1982, the demand for colour T.V. The set was high, and its market price was also quite high. Then follows the stage of its growth in the sense that its demand increases as the number of buyers increases gradually. The competition among the producers also increases and consequently its price goes down. The third stage is that of maturity, when demand for the product gets stabilised at a definite level. This stage continues for a long period. The television market in India is in the third stage of its life cycle at present. In the final stage, when the buyers get fed up with the product and turn to some other product, it enters into its last stage of life cycle. i.e. its declining stage.

(2) Physical Distribution: This is the second important element of the marketing-mix. A proper channel of distribution should be selected so that the product of the company can easily reach into the hands of its final consumers. The mode of transportation and storing facilities are to be selected carefully.  
Of course, the choice of the channel of distribution depends on the nature of the product, financial resources and the business traditions etc. But it must be so selected that in combination with other elements of marketing mix it helps in the realisation of goals of the company such as maximisation of profit, economic stability and long term growth of the business etc. The decision about physical distribution means decisions about whether the product is to be marketed through wholesalers or retailers, through company's own stores or through sole selling agents etc.

(3) Price The third element of the marketing mix is about pricing of the product. The price should be fixed at such a level that it will create an optimum marketing-mix along with other elements. The price should be fixed keeping in view the channels of distribution selected so that the company can realise its long run objective of profit maximisation, even after allowing a satisfactory return to the intermediaries. Along with prices, discounts, freight charges and other problems related to price must also be considered carefully.

To fix the price of the product, three criteria are available cost, competition and demand. Sometimes, a fixed percentage of profit is just added to the average cost of production to determine the price of the product. Sometimes, the price charged by the rivals is fixed, because a price which is even slightly higher than that of the rivals may be harmful. Most of the time, price is fixed according to the demand for the product. Sometimes it is possible to follow the policy of price discrimination, i.e. charging different prices to different customers for the same product.

(4) Promotion: Mere production of a commodity is not enough. To sell it, consumers must be informed of its existence and advantages, they must be induced to purchase it and if necessary, special incentives must be offered to them. Thus, sales promotion efforts are also necessary. There are four ways by which sales can be promoted: advertisement, personal selling, sales promotion and publicity. The decision to combine these in the best possible manner determines the success of the business. The best combination can be decided on the basis of the nature of the product, financial position of the company, the number of customers and the group to which they belong etc. The other equally important decisions involved at this stage are with regard to the size of advertisement budget, timing of the sales promotion efforts, training of the salesmen and planning and controlling of their activities and use of such media as radio, television and newspapers etc.

Thus, the four P's stated above are the cornerstone of marketing activity. They must be well coordinated so that an integrated system of marketing can be developed.
