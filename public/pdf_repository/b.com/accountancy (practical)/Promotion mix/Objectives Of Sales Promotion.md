10\. OBJECTIVES OF SALES PROMOTION:\*

The ultimate objective of sales promotion is to increase sales above its normal level. But if its objectives are to be spelt out in detail, they would be as follows:

(1) To Attract New Customers: The main object of sales promotion is to increase sales by attracting new customers. It always thinks of devices to attract new customers. That is, its attempt is to divert customers from the products of competitors to the firm it belongs to. The devices it uses for this purpose are price reduction, offering samples, organisation of sales contests, award of prizes, conducting demonstration programmes etc. These devices induce the consumer to purchase and use a product unknown to him. If he is satisfied with it, he becomes a regular customer.

(2) To Increase Off-season Sales: Some products like ceiling fans are in demand only during a definite season. Sales promotion is undertaken to increase sales of such products during the off-season when their demand is at the lowest level. For instance, ceiling fans are sold at special off-season prices during some months Similarly, at tourist places, the accommodation at the hotels are offered at reduceć rates during off-seasons.

(3) Introducing New Products: Sales promotion is resorted to when an existing company intends to introduce a new product in the market. For instance the Godrej Company recently declared a scheme under which its Cinthol toilet soap was offered free of charge to anyone who bought its Megha detergent cake. The Colgate company, while introducing new small packs in the market, distributed through its salesmen discount coupons to the consumers who purchased small packs of Colgate toothpaste. These coupons entitled them to buy large packs at a lower price. Sometimes, samples are offered to the consumers free of cost. Dealers are offered higher commission or bonus to encourage them to keep larger stock of the product.

(4) To Meet the Competition: When competitors take measures to increase the sales of their product, other firms are also compelled to take sales promotion measures to protect themselves against the aggressive policies of the rival firms. If for example, a baby food manufacturing company starts (4 distributing discount coupons to those who buy its products, other companies will have to follow it in the same direction. This is the case with all sorts of firms, whether they produce consumer goods or industrial goods. Clearly, the object of these measures is to face competition from the rivals successfully.

(5) To Encourage Present Customers Buy More: In some cases sales promotion activities are undertaken to motivate the present customers to purchase more of the product. For this purpose, the benefits of larger consumption, or of frequent consumption are stressed before the consumers. For instance, the tooth paste companies stress the point that it is necessary and advantageous to brush teeth not only in the morning, but also before ( going to bed at night.

(6) To Induce Dealers to Stock More: Sales promotion is undertaken not only through consumers but through dealers also. Hence, an attempt is always made to induce the dealers and retailers to keep a larger stock of the product. Because, the larger the stock they have, the more eager they will be to sell it. If they succeed in increasing sales, it will be possible to produce it on a large scale. Hence, costs and prices will decline due to the economies of large scale production. Sales will expand further as a result of lower prices.

(7) To Raise Company's Prestige Sometimes, the glamorous and attractive programmes are arranged as a part of sales promotion efforts so that it will enhance the prestige of the company in the society and corporate world. e.g. Reliance Industries Ltd. organized Cricket World Cup matches and attracted the attention of people. This enhanced the company's prestige not only in India, but also in foreign countries.
