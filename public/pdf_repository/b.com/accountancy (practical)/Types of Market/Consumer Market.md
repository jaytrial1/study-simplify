2\. CONSUMER MARKET:\*

"The Consumer Market consists of all the individuals and households that buy or acquire goods and services for personal consumption." Philifa Kotler

The consumer market is a very large market. e.g. The Indian population is more than 100 crore. We can consider the customer numbers to be 100 crore for certain products which are used by all the persons t Thus India is a very large consumer market for the entire world. The consumer market can be divided into many groups based on age, income, education level etc. If a certain group is the large buyer for the company's product, then the company concentrates on that group by keeping a separate C marketing department for that.

The characteristics of the Consumer Market can be described as under

(1) Large Number: In the consumer market the number of consumers is very large. e.g. for daily necessities of life in India the number of consumers can be considered to be 95 to 100 crore. That is to say that a marketing manager has to develop a large marketing network. The personal contact of all these b consumers is impossible. Still however' efforts must be made to remain in their contacts through wholesalers, retailers, brokers etc.

(2) Purchases on Small Scale Each consumer of this market purchases only for his personal needs or for his family needs, resulting in lower purchase volume. Hence, in this type of market for each type of product, it is not possible to meet each consumer personally.

(3) Direct Demand: The consumer is purchasing the product for his own consumption; hence the demand is a direct demand. The consumer directly uses or consumes the same. He is not purchasing for producing the other product or for reselling to others.

(4) Scattered Buyers: The consumers purchasing goods or services are scattered all around the country. As a result, the transportation expenditure of the goods to be dispatched to the place of consumption is very high. Hence many times national companies establish their factories into different locations of the country. e.g. The factory of H.M.T. is at Bangalore and at Shrinagar Thus, marketing manager reduces the transportation cost.

(5) Elasticity of Demand: The demand of most of the consumer products is elastic about its price. i.e. if the price of the product increases, its demand decreases without fail. But the same rule does not apply to the basic necessities of life. Still, if a cheaper option is available, the consumer may diver towards that option.

6\) Many Forces Affect Buying Decision: The decision of the consumer to purchase a certain product is influenced by a number of factors, which are discussed at length below. They are like economic factors, psychological factors, cultural factors etc.

(7) Immature Buying Decision: Sometimes, if the consumer is to purchase a daily utility technical product, his decision is not based on enough technical information about the product. As a result, many times he tends to make wrong decisions. Hence, marketing manager should try to give correct technical information to the consumers.

(8) Lack of Personal Contact between Consumer and Trader: In the consumer market barring purchase-sale of essential commodities, there is no personal contact between consumer and businessman. Hence, confidence level is not boosted. So marketing manager should try to increase confidence level with the help of T.V. advertisement or personal demonstration.

Factors Influencing Consumer Behaviour:

Whenever consumers think of buying, their buying behaviour is affected by many factors, some of which are as under:

(1) Economic Factors: Before buying any product, the consumer thinks about the income which can be spent. He also decides about total family needs and income. Then he thinks about future flow of income and also whether the credit is available or not.

(2) Psychological Factors: The psychological characteristics of the consumers influence his buying. e.g. He is buying to satisfy his primary needs. But his buying is based on his attitude. His decision is based on his past experience whether to buy a certain item or not. His perception about the product also affects his buying decision. His personality, profession, lifestyle etc. all influence his buying decision.

(3) Social Factors: On the buying of the consumer, the influence of his social factors is always there. e.g. the effect of neighbour's or friend's opinion. He thinks about family members' needs and their feelings at what level he is placed in the society. Based on all these his buying decision is taken. His position and status in the family and his position and status in the society also influence his buying decision.

(4) Cultural Factors: The culture is an important factor influencing human needs, his behaviour and his buying decision. The approach and view- point of any person is developed on the basis of his culture. Among such factors, desire to live a comfortable life, change in family life, change in female role, change of fashion in the society, traditions and customs based on geographical locations etc. all influence his buying decision.

5\) Personal Factors: The consumer buying is influenced by his personal characteristics. Especially his age, profession, and his economic circumstances. lifestyle, his personality etc. influence his buying decision.
