5\. BRANDING:\*

(1) Introduction: A manufacturer generally gives a distinct name to his product which is ordinarily called 'brand'. For instance, a manufacturer of a toothpaste may give the name 'Colgate' to his product, which is its brand. The producer wishes to differentiate his product from those of his competitors and build up its image by giving it a brand. Each product acquires an independent image of its own due to branding, e.g. a toilet soap with pleasing fragrance is 'Cinthol' soap, a high quality television set means Sony T.V., suits for charming appearance is 'Raymonds' etc. Thus there is a definite image of the product which influences the customer's decisions to buy it. If a manufacturer produces a large number of products, their brands help him to identify each of them. The dealers also can easily identify the product of each manufacturer and purchasing becomes easy for the customer... The producers must take such decisions in connection with branding as weather. to use a brand name or not, whether branding is necessary, whether a separate brand name should be given to each one of the products or to sell all products under the banner of the company. Let us consider these issues in detail.

C \[5.1\] Definition of Branding:

Branding is defined by different authors as follows:

(1) In ordinary life, a brand is defined as a name or symbol or design of a product whereby it gets differentiated from other products in the market.

(2) 'A producer gives a special name or a symbol or applies a trade-mark to his products to differentiate them from other products, so that buyers can easily identify them and save themselves from fraud or cheating and also to ensure that his products are not imitated by others. This is known as branding.'

(3) According to the American Marketing Association, "a brand is a name, term, symbol or design or a combination of them which is intended to identify the goods or services of one seller or group of sellers and differentiate them from those of competitors."

(4) 'A brand of a product is a device which helps in identifying and describing it clearly.'

(3) Some examples of brands are as follows:

(1) Vadilal Icecream (2) Usha Fans (3) Bournvita (4) Coca-Cola (5) 555 Cigarette (6) Robin Blue (7) No. 30 Biri (8) Vimal Cloth (9) Nirma Detergent (10) Bajaj Scooter (11) Rasna Soft Drink.

\[5.2\] Characteristics of Brands:

Following characteristics of brands can be derived on the basis of above definitions:

(1) Brand is a comprehensive term which is employed to identify a product.

(2) Brand takes the form of a name of the product, mark, design, number or word.

(3) In branding, all devices, except packing, are used to identify product.

(4) The objective of branding is to recognise visually and orally a distinct product of a business firm and to distinguish it from the rival products.

(5) Even a trade-mark can serve as a brand. A trade-mark is also regarded as a brand but it is a brand which must be registered under the Trade-Mark Act of the country. Hence it is a legal concept. No other person or institution is allowed to use it. For example, "Thums-Up' is a trade-mark which no other firm can use.

Trade-Mark: In this age of cut-throat competition among manufacturers, trade-mark is considered necessary to advertise the product and to attract buyers. This trade-mark has to be registered under the related Act, so that no other person can use it or imitate it. An attempt by other persons to use or imitate it is a legal offence.

The American Marketing Association defines it as a brand that is given legal protection because, under the law, it has been appropriated by one seller.

Trade-mark is a symbol, design, word or group of words, which indicates the ownership of the product and differentiates it from other products on the basis of its quality and no other person can use it.

In short, trade-mark is a legal word, registered under law and protected by law. Each trade-mark is a brand, but each brand is not a trade-mark.

\[5.3\] Advantages of Branding :\*

The advantages of branding may be considered from two different angles: (a) from the producers' point of view and (b) from the customers point of view.

(a) Advantages to Producers :\*

(1) Fixed Price Producer can establish a fixed price for his branded product. A retailer has not to waste his time and energy in higgling over price with every customer.

(2) Image or Goodwill: A brand builds a distinct image of the product in the minds of the buyers. It enjoys popularity among the people. The producer or seller earns prestige.

(3) Definite Group of Buyers: When the brand becomes popular, a definite group of customers get attached to it. These buyers insist on buying and using this product only. Thus, a definite patronage is created.

(4) Facilitates Advertisement: It is easier and less costly to advertise

a branded product. It can be advertised only by showing a special mark or label, e.g. VIP underwear, Thums up

(5) Sales Promotion: An appealing and meaningful trade-mark or symbol can easily attract the attention of the customers. Thus, sales can be easily increased through the use of brands.

(6) Protection from being Imitated: A registered trade-mark cannot

be used by any other individual or firm. It is a cognisable offence to use or imitate a registered trade-mark of a firm. Thus, branded products are less likely to be imitated.

(7) Direct Sales Branding helps manufacturers to maintain direct contact with the consumers. He does not have to depend on the wholesalers or retailers to create demand for his products.

(8) Market Control: The main object of branding is to differentiate a product from other products in the market. Thereby, a manufacturer can monopolise a part of the market. A definite group of buyers always prefers to purchase his product. To that extent the manufacturer is protected from the adverse effects of competition.

(9) Helpful in Sales Promotion: When the manufacturer undertakes a programme of sales promotion, the brand name becomes helpful, as buyers are already familiar with their products due to branding.

(10) Legal Protection Branding provides legal protection to the manufacturer and dealers.

(b) Advantages to the Consumers :\*

(1) Identification: In spite of the existence of a large number of different products in the market, consumers can easily identify the product that they wish to purchase. This is because a trade-mark or brand differentiates a product from others.

(2) Standard Quality: A branded product has a standard quality. It is the same quality everywhere, in all parts of the market. Hence, buyers can rest assure for the quality of branded product.

(3) Protection Against Cheating Customers ask for a particular brand of a product. The sellers cannot cheat them by supplying some other brand. For example, if a buyer asks for Lux soap, the retailer cannot give him. Hamam soap.

(4) Fixed Price: Retail price of a branded product is generally known and fixed. The buyers need not waste time in inquiring about price and are not cheated as regards price.

(5) Uniform Quality: Buyers can purchase a branded product from any part of the market without hesitation, because it is of uniform quality everywhere. For instance, Brook Bond Red label tea is sold throughout India and every where its quality and taste are the same.

(6) Facilitates Buying: The customers can order the product without detailed description or without personally contacting the sellers. They have only to state the trade-mark or brand, which they can do through a written message or telephone.

(7) Protection: Generally, branded products are sold in attractive packings. Hence, they are protected from the effect of heat, dust and moisture.

(8) Social Status: A highly popular brand imparts a social status to the users. For instance, the Sony Television has become a symbol of high social rank and hence its users can be proud of having this television set.

\[5.4\] Limitations of Branding:

(1) Responsibility for the Manufacturers: Once an image of a product is built through branding, manufacturers have a responsibility to maintain its quality and its standard for ever.

(2) Heavy Costs: Branding of a product entails heavy costs. If a product is sold to the wholesalers, they may apply their own mark on it.

(3) Indifference of Wholesalers and Retailers: So long as a product does not become popular, the wholesalers and retailers are not ready to stock it and sell it.

(4) Inferior Quality: For a branded product, a definite quality of raw material has to be used. But if that raw material is not available in the market, the producers use inferior raw materials. This affects the quality of the final product.

(5) Difficulty of Choice: (i) When a product is available in a large number of varieties carrying different brands or trade-marks, buyers get confused and find it difficult to make a correct choice. (ii) High Price: Buyers have to pay a high price for the branded products. (iii) Lack of Quality: The branded products are not of standard quality always. With the passage of time, its quality deteriorates. Hence, the buyers loyal to the brand suffer in that, though they pay high price, they receive a sub-standard product. (iv) Imitation: Dishonest manufacturers do not hesitate to copy a popular brand. Buyers can be easily deceived by an inferior product under the name of the same brand.
