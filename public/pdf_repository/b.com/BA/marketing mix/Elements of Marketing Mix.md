

## **3\. Elements of Marketing Mix :**

Dr. R. S. Davar in his book 'Modern Marketing Management' has given following eight elements of marketing mix:

(1) General Planning: The enterprise has to clarify the acceptable and possible rate of growth in general planning. The environment in which the business has to function is studied in an organised manner for this purpose. Then it has to determine the size and extent of the market where business may function profitably. Besides, it has also to determine the costs and expenses needed for the business operations and also estimate the finances needed for the purpose. Of course, an eye has to be constantly kept on minimum size and area, as any reduction in it would result in reduced profitability. Such general planning is essential to make business successful. It helps in policy formation to achieve the objectives.

(2) Product Planning Product planning signifies the development of a product in such a way that it will give maximum satisfaction to the consumers in the context of their needs and wants. It includes all aspects relating to the product like design, colour, alternative uses, quality, packing etc. The marketing research makes product planning more meaningful. The product planning includes (1) Determining the product or service offered (2) The possible improvement and innovation in the product. (3) The brand and packaging policies (4) The importance of servicing and after-sale services.

(3) Pricing: The work of determining the price of a product is a very delicate task. Generally, this is done by the sales manager in consultation with top management. While determining price the factors which are considered are cost of production, competitors' prices, condition of demand, appropriate margin, resale price maintenance, government control, if any etc.

(4) Channel of Distribution: There are mainly two methods of distribution direct and indirect. Under direct method, the producer sells directly to consumers for which he opens his own retail shop. It also includes selling through mail. Under the indirect system, the producer sells through wholesalers or through some other intermediaries.

(5) Sales Force This includes recruitment, training, remuneration, control and incentive to salesmen. Sales quotas are fixed for each salesman taking into consideration the potential sales of each territory. The total requirement of sales force depends upon the extent of personal selling, the extent of approach to wholesalers and retailers and extent of approach to consumers directly.

(6) Advertising and Sales Promotion: In the modern era of cut-throat competition, the effective advertising and efforts of sales promotion assumes special significance. In determining the importance of these aspects, the management has to take into account. (1) The advertisement programme (2) Importance given to display and point-of-sale material (3) Extent of sales promotion aimed at consumers, and (4) Extent of sales promotion to dealers.

(7) Physical Handling: This factor includes such aspects as transportation, warehousing, inventory prices, and possible reduction in costs. To prevent unnecessary increase in cost, it is essential that transportation and warehousing costs should be properly controlled.

(8) Marketing Research: This includes research about products, customers, efficiency of marketing set-up, distribution channels, effectiveness of advertisement, extent and nature of competition etc. This includes sales analysis field surveys and use of outside agencies.

As one author of Gujarat has stated, following are some of the practical examples of eight P's of marketing \['Eight P' presented by Prof. Dhaval Mehta\]:\*

(1) Product: Constant change must be made in the product so as to make it suitable for customer's requirements. Two-wheeler Bajaj Company is a completely Indian company. It makes constant changes in its scooters and motor bikes to suit customers. Famous icecream manufacturing company of Gujarat goes on constantly new flavours of icecream which shows its dynamism. Well-known pharma co. Zydus Cadila goes on adding new products and improving its existing drugs in the light of the experience of customers. It has introduced a new drug Zinicore which contains potassium channel activator, which dilates the blood carrying arteries. In the heart pain called angina pain, this drug is considered as very effective. Zydus Cadila introduced some 30 new drugs in the market in 1996\. It is an outstanding example of dynamism in business. In the U.S.A., Black and Decker Co. was established in 1910, which makes drills for cutting holes in wood and metals. When it put its first electric drill in the market in 1910\. it weighed 50 lbs (pounds 454 gms.) Two persons were required to hold it and a third person was required to control the electric switch. This instrument was costly and very inconvenient. The company thought that the market requires a convenient electric drill, which can be handled by one person only. Hence, it prepared a design of light weight portable drill and put a small motor and electric switch in the same small machine and it became an uncrowned king in the field of portable power tools. Why? Because it concentrated on customers' needs and not on sales or advertisement.

(2) Price: Another important element of four P is the price. While pricing a product, it should not be assumed that the customer always wants a cheap item. He wants value for money spent by him. What is proper value for money is a subjective concept and is in the mind of the customer. A manufacturer who charges arbitrarily high prices will ultimately ruin himself. The multi-national companies have come to India to capture the Indian market but they charge very high prices and so their sales estimates have gone wrong.

Their sales forecasts have been proved wrong as they had not been able to understand Indian customers' psychology. There may be different motives of pricing like maximising profits, obtaining market leadership, to increase market share, to defeat the competitors and to achieve different motives, different pricing policies have to be adopted. In order to increase profit in the short run, high prices have to be fixed but to defeat competitors, lower prices are required to be charged.

(3) Place: Here the third element of marketing mix is place meaning thereby either place where goods are to be sold or it may also mean distribution channel. An important factor contributing to the success of a company's business is its distribution channel. How to send the goods to customers, how to reach them in distant small places or villages? For this purpose the company must prepare its distribution network. Dealers and stockists have to be appointed and given proper remuneration in the form of commission. The dealers have to dispatch goods to the retailers. Some companies avoid these intermediaries and reach directly to customers. But this arrangement requires a great deal of skill and expertise. In India, Tatas are selling their vacuum cleaners directly to customers. But generally, there are a number of intermediaries in distribution channels like wholesalers, semi- wholesalers, stockists, retailers etc. This distribution system requires a great deal of skill on the part of management. The Hindustan Lever Limited and Pan Parag have the best distribution system in India.

(4) Promotion In promotion, the main emphasis is on advertisement and sales promotion techniques. Advertisement has now become the main tool of sales promotion and all major companies spend crores of rupees on advertisement, particularly on television advertisement. They send a message to customers through such advertisements and so they treat advertisement as marketing communication, e.g. formerly, an outstanding advertising company of Ahmedabad was named Mudra Advertising, which is now known as Mudra Communication. Advertising science requires deep study, as each advertisement has to send a message to customers. A good deal of professional competence and skill is required in preparing such a message. The second part of promotion includes sales promotion techniques. It embraces discounts, commissions, prizes, competition among customers, gifts to customers etc. The third one is personal selling and mail order selling. Goods are sold through television programmes tele Asian Skyshop.

(5) Packaging: Authors have no unanimity about which is the fifth element of the marketing mix. Some say it is packaging. The packaging of a product is equally important as a product itself. If the package is attractive and colourful, the customers would be attracted to buy that product. Hence, packaging is called 'five second advertisement.' Some authors argue that as packaging is a part of a product, it should not be treated as a separate element.

(6) Public Relations: There is a group which considers public relations as a sixth element, which is nothing but maintaining good relations with various institutions, chambers of commerce, management associations, goverņument organisations etc.

7\) Political Power: While some experts believe that political power is the seventh element of marketing mix. A company to be successful, must have good political influence so that laws, rules and regulations affecting the company would be favourable to the company.  
A company which can prepare its marketing strategy on the basis of coordination and balancing of various elements of marketing mix.

(8) Probing: The author says that in his opinion eighth P is the probing meaning of Market Research. Market research is an integral part of marketing management. The producer who produces on a large scale on the basis of anticipated demand bears immense risks which he may avoid if he carefully estimates the magnitude of demand by gathering reliable information through systematic market research. In the modern age, market research has become an indispensable tool for decision making with respect to such matters as placing a new product in the existing market, placing existing product in a new market, preferences of the customers and their purchasing capacity etc. To formulate business policy merely on the basis of baseless information and inferences is extremely disastrous for the present day producers and firms. At present an intense competition prevails everywhere for capturing new markets for the products. Consequently, local as well as national markets have receded in the background and global markets have become a major goal for the progressive and dynamic companies. The whole world has turned into a unified market. People in major countries of the world begin their day with a delicious cup of tea produced in Assam in India.
