5\. PROCESS OF MARKETING CONTROL\*:

Let us now consider various stages involved in the process of marketing control.

(A) Establishing Standards of Performance: Actual performance has to be compared with some standard of performance. The standards of performance are necessary for two reasons: One, whether actual performance is up to the mark can be judged on the basis of the standard. Second, the marketing officials can know what is ' expected from them and therefore they can strive in a planned manner to fulfil this expectation.

There can be different standards for different aspects of marketing:

(i) Standards for Sales Volume: This standard can be very useful for sales control. The volume of sales for the forthcoming period can be fixed in the sales budget of the firm. It is then distributed among different regions and each salesman is assigned a definite quota of sales.

(ii) Standards for Sales Expenditure: These standards can also be useful for sales control. Total cost is classified into selling costs and distribution costs. This in turn may be divided into such categories as cost per product, cost per buyer, cost per region etc. Of course, the standard of profit per product must be laid down when the cost per product is determined. Otherwise, it is possible that while the sales expenditure is well within control, the standard of profit per product may not be achieved.

(iii) Standard for Share in Market: The higher the market share of the company, the better it is for its performance. Of course, the efficiency of the company in the field of marketing should be judged in the context of the cost incurred to increase market share of the company, resultant increase in profits and activities of the rival firms in the market.

(iv) Standards of Profits: Once standards of profits are established, it is easy to lay down the standard of profit per unit of products as also that of profit per sales region.

(B) Gathering Data on Performance: To compare actual performance with the standards already established by the comp company, it is necessary to gather all the data relevant to it. For this purpose, three methods are available: (1) Personal observation, (2) Oral reports, and (3) Written reports.

(i) Personal Observation: The sales managers or sales supervisors can obtain data through personal observation. However, this method is expensive and inconvenient. A company with a wide market area for its product will have to appoint a large number of sales supervisors. Though information obtained through this method will be more accurate, it is not practicable.

(ii) Oral Reports: The other method is that of gathering data through oral reports. This is possible only when the market is localised. To obtain information regarding sales in far off markets by way of oral reports can be possible sometimes only. This information can be obtained by organising conferences and sales meetings.

(3) Written Reports: This is the best method for gathering relevant data about sales. Especially, the reports obtained from the salesmen can be most authentic for sales control. The salesmen can be asked to prepare and send written reports in standardised forms regularly to convey information to the company.

(C) Analysing Performance: The information obtained through above methods should be compared with the established standards. If there is a difference between actual performance and optimum performance an attempt should be made to find out the causes responsible for it. There can be various causes for the weak performance of the salesmen, e.g., inability of the salesmen, lethargy of the salesmen, insincerity of the salesmen or change in customers' preferences in the market assigned to the salesmen and resultant fall in demand for the product, or weak sales efforts of the company and reduction in advertising etc.

(D) Taking Corrective Measures: Once the causes of low performance are identified, action should be initiated to remove them. Otherwise, marketing control will prove a fruitless proposition. There are three types of corrective measures. These are as follows:

(i) Changes in Sales Efforts For instance, salesmen may be instructed to raise sales efforts in their regions or it may be decided to reduce price to cope with the competitive forces in the market.

(ii) Augmenting Resources: If sales are below the standard in some regions, provision may be made to impart training to a larger number of salesmen or additional supervisors may be appointed to exercise stricter control over the activities of the salesmen.

(iii) Changes in Standards: Sometimes, it may be revealed that there is a discrepancy between actual performance and optimum performance because the standards of performance are not properly set. For example, the standard established for the number of customers to be approached by the salesmen may be unrealistic because of the geographical impediments which salesmen may have to face in a particular region.
