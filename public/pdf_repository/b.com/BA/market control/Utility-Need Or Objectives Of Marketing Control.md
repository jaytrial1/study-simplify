3\. UTILITY/NEED OR OBJECTIVES OF MARKETING CONTROL:\*

The only objective of control over any activity must be to see that all the activities are proceeding as planned, and if any deviation is observed, corrective measures should be devised to improve it so that balance is maintained in the activities resulting in minimisation of costs and maximisation of profits. The main objectives of marketing control may be elaborated at this point. In fact, marketing control is necessary to achieve the objectives as given below:

(1) Control Over Selling Costs: It is because of keen competition in the market that the proportion of selling costs in price of the majority of the products has gone up recently. It is estimated that selling and distribution costs account for about 25 per cent of the market price of the products. In the case of consumer durables, especially those used as luxuries, the proportion may be as high as 50 to 60 per cent. That is why any one of the following two measures may be suggested to reduce selling costs: (i) negative measures which aim at reducing selling costs, keeping total sales constant at their current level, and (ii) positive measures which aim at increasing total sales, keeping selling costs constant at their existing level.

(2) To Maintain Equilibrium in the Marketing Activities: In the absence of marketing control, the sales of only those products will be high which can be marketed easily and without much effort. On the other hand, there may be some products which a company can produce most efficiently and must therefore be produced compulsorily for technical reasons. But if their marketing is too tough to handle, the production plan of the company will be jeopardised. Such a disequilibrium in sales necessitates marketing control.

(3) To Maintain Equilibrium among different Sales Areas: How, in the absence of marketing control, can a balance in sales in different areas be ensured? In the absence of marketing control, salesmen will prefer only those areas where access to customers is easy or less difficult, where customer response is friendly and favourable, where contact with customers is less costly and less time-consuming. In this case, therefore, sales will be confined only to a few areas of the market. Gradually, the distance between easy areas and difficult areas will go on widening. The strong areas will get more and more strengthened, while the weak areas will get weaker day-by-day. These imbalances can be removed with the help of marketing control.

(4) Control Over the Performance of the Salesmen: It is necessary that salesmen perform their duties sincerely. It is also necessary that salesmen are allocated among different regions in the correct proportion. For this purpose, various measures may be devised, e.g., to distribute sales areas among the salesmen, to fix their sales quotas, to define their authority, to prepare their journey chart, and to demand daily or weekly reports of their performance. If complete information is gathered through conferences or reports or personal surveillance etc., corrective measures can be taken after evaluating it.

(5) To Make Sales Planning Effective: If actual sales are not in conformity with the anticipated sales, sales planning has no meaning. To ensure that this does not happen and profitability of the firm is not adversely affected, control must be exercised over the execution of the sales plan. Sales budget and sales ratios are useful tools which can be applied in this connection.
