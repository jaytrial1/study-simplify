5\. DEFINITION AND CHARACTERISTICS OF PUBLICITY:\*

George Black defines publicity in the following words:  
"Publicity involves securing editorial space, as divorced from paid space, in all media read, viewed or heard by a company's customers or prospects, for the specific purpose of assisting in the meeting of sales goals".

This definition implies following features of publicity:

(1) Diverse Tools: Various tools are available for publicity. Mainly, it is carried out through newspapers. But, as stated in the above definition, it can be effected through radio and television also.

(2) Cost-free Service: Advertising is expensive. A product can never be advertised free of cost. As against this, products can be publicised through radio television and newspapers free of cost. Of course, it is a part of public relations and the cost has to be borne to sustain public relations. But no direct payment is necessary for publicity.

(3) Sales Promotion not Direct Aim: Increase in sales is an explicit

objective of advertising, personal selling and sales promotion. But publicity aims at the enhancement of the company's goodwill in the market. True, that in the long run, the goodwill of the company does result in higher sales. Hence, sales expansion can be regarded as a long term objective of Publicity. For instance, to introduce a new product in the market, some distinguished person may be invited to its inaugural ceremony. If its news is published in the newspapers or relayed on radio or presented on television, people come to l.now about the product's existence. But manufacturer does not appeal to them to purchase it.

(4) Obtaining Editorial Space: The manufacturer has no command over the publicity that his product receives in the newspaper or on radio and television, as it is an unpaid publicity. It is the editor who decides where and how much space to allot to it in his newspaper. Similarly, its coverage and time to be allotted to it on radio and television is decided by the authority concerned.

(5) Part of Public Relations: Public relations means cultivating such relations with the parties concerned that goodwill of the company is built up in their eyes. Publicity is a part of such a public relations programme, because the aim of publicity is also that of enhancing the goodwill of the company. The only difference is that while, in the area of publicity, the initiative rests with the media authorities, ir: other measures of public relations, initiative is taken by the company itself.
