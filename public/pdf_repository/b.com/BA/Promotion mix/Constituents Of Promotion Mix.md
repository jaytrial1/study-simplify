3\. CONSTITUENTS OF PROMOTION MIX\*:

As stated above, there are four constituents of promotion mix. They are

(1) advertising, (2) personal selling, (3) sales promotion and (4) publicity.

Let us try to know more about them:

(1) Advertising: An attempt is made to increase sales of a product by informing buyers about the existence of a product, its usefulness and improvements in it through advertisement. Also a firm tries to build prestige and make its product popular through advertisement. For this purpose, such audio-visual media as newspapers, posters, postal services, radio and television, films etc. are used. In view of such diversity of the advertisement, it is difficult to derive distinct features thereof, but some of the features can be stated as under:

(a) Public Presentation: Advertisement differs from personal selling in one important respect. A standard uniform message can be conveyed to many persons at a time. An impression can be created that the product is of standard quality.

(b) Pervasiveness: It is a comprehensive medium of information. The same message can be repeated many times. It also allows the buyers to receive and compare the messages of various competitors. A seller gives information about all aspects of his product through large scale advertising.

(c) Dramatic Impact: A vivid presentation of various aspects of a product in a dramatic manner is possible through the use of different colours, print, sound etc. in the advertisements. For example, various uses of the Sumeet Mixture is demonstrated on the T.V. screen.

(d) Impersonal: A salesman can personally contact the customers. But this is not possible in the case of advertisement. The advertisement simultaneously appeals to a vast mass of customers simultaneously. In this sense, it is an impersonal medium. The buyers cannot be compelled to pay attention or respond to advertisements.

(2) Personal Selling Personal selling refers to the sales carried out through personal contact of the travelling salesman with buyers or through telephonic and postal contact with them or through sales counters in the shops. The basic object of personal selling may be to create product awareness, to arouse interest in it and to negotiate the price and other terms. Some peculiarities of personal selling are as follows:

(a) Personal Contact: Since two or more persons are face to face, personal contact is possible in this case. Both parties can try to understand each other's viewpoint and make immediate adjustments if necessary. This is a tough job when compared to impersonal selling, because just as there is the possibility of winning over the buyers through personal interest, there is also an equal chance of hurting the other party through lack of interest in his talk.

(b) Cultivation of Personal Relations: The salesmen can cultivate friendly relations also with selling relationships. It is only in some cases that they adopt a purely business approach.

(c) Response: Under personal selling, the buyers feel compelled to give- some response. Also, they can be asked to express their views in response to the salesman's arguments. On knowing buyers' opinions, salesmen can reshape their arguments.

(3) Sales Promotion: It refers to all devices aimed at sales promotion that are not included in the field of advertising, personal selling and publicity. More precisely it refers to three important devices of sales promotion: consumer oriented devices such as samples, coupons, contests, price-offs etc; trader oriented devices such as buying allowances, dealer sales contests etc, sales force promotion devices such as bonus or sales contests, sales conferences etc. Two notable peculiarities of sales promotion are as follows:

(a) Insistence on Instant Purchase In most of the sales promotion tools, buyers are persuaded to make a purchase immediately. An attempt is made to convince them that the firm has offered them something special that they won't again get. The buyers who are economy minded take advantage of this opportunity and consequently sales increase. But this type of buyers are not loyal to any particular brand in the long run. They immediately switch over to the other products, if they are cheaper.

(b) Unfavourable Impression (Product Demeaning): If sellers show their eagerness to sell out their products through frequent or careless use of such devices, buyers are likely to get the impression that their products may be inferior or price may be unreasonably high.

(4) Publicity: If'a company and its product can come to the attention of the public through newspapers, radio or television free of cost, it is called publicity. The main peculiarities of publicity are as follows:

(a) High Credibility: Articles or features published in newspapers or

magazines are considered authentic by the readers. Opinions about the company or its products in such media, have a higher degree of credibility than, if they are made by the seller himself.

(b) Appealing to Cautious Buyers: This method has a greater appeal to those buyers who avoid advertisement and salesmen. They receive a message not in the form of an advertisement, but in the form of a news or an article.
