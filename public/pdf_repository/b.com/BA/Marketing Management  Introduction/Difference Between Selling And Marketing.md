5\. DIFFERENCE BETWEEN SELLING AND MARKETING:

The term 'selling' is used in two different senses. One, it is used to mean exchange of goods against money. But this interpretation is too restricted. To convey a broader meaning, the word, 'marketing' is used in place of selling. The term marketing is interpreted to mean such activities as planning of production of goods and services, transporting goods from producers to the customers and meeting their needs, to get orders for the goods from them again (5 and again etc. Thus, marketing implies not only supplying goods to the customers in exchange of money, but it also includes advertisement and publicity of the product, market research, guiding the producers on that basis, sales promotion etc.

To put the same in other words, selling refers to only the activity of exchanging goods against money. The seller is concerned with only disposal of the goods he has. He does not bother to know whether customers are satisfied with what he sells. His only goal is to sell out the goods anyhow. One the other band, marketing, defined in a broader sense, means that the seller is concerned with the needs and preferences of the customers and also with the extent to which their needs are satisfied by the goods he supplies.

The major points of difference between selling and marketing may be enumerated as follows:

| Selling | Marketing |
| ----- | ----- |
| This term is used in a narrow sense. It is a part of marketing. | This term is used in a broader sense. Selling is only one of the activities included in marketing. |
| Selling has to do with the exchange of a product and hence the product and its production are given utmost importance. | In addition to exchange, the satisfaction of the customers is also given importance. Hence, customers' satisfaction is of utmost importance, |
| The starting point is production. How to sell and make profit is an issue which is considered only after the production process is over. | Customer's needs and preferences lie at the centre of marketing, consideration is given to measures to satisfy them and production is adjusted according to their needs and preferences. |
| Selling is a routine activity with a short term objective. | Marketing has long term objectives. It is not a routine job, rather, it is a strategic activity concerned with creating the image of the company. |
| The essence of selling is conversion of commodities into cash. | Marketing consists of demand forecasting, product planning, market research and such other activities. |
| Selling was the main activity during the period between 1875 and 1945\. | Marketing came into focus after 1950 when the business management had to face challenges of the new situation. |
