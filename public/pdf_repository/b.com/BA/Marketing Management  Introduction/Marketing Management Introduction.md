Marketing Management : Introduction

Every nation tries to accelerate economic progress so that the living standard of its people may improve. In the modern age, goods and services are produced in anticipation of demand and so the producer must try to create demand, otherwise, he will have to close down the business sooner or later. If the business is closed down, it will have adverse effects on the society. No business can last long unless it is able to sell goods effectively. They cannot prosper if they cannot sell their product profitably and without selling the goods, the production has no meaning.

With the advent of mass production in anticipation of demand, competition between firms has also become more intense. As a result of the progress of transport and communication, competition among the producers has crossed the national frontiers, because markets for most of the products have now become international. The development of technological skill has also played an important role in intensifying market competition by increasing the volume of demand for these products and services and then in turn expanding this demand."

This definition faithfully represents the modern approach to marketing. It contains three important points about marketing. First, the needs of the consumers are to be discovered and goods or services are to be produced in conformity with those needs. Thus it suggests that producers have to honour the needs of the consumers. Second, the producers have to create demand for the goods and services which they have produced. Here, the emphasis is on selling. Third, producers should not be content with creating demand, they must also make an attempt to gradually expand it. Here reference is to sales promotion measures such as advertisement, publicity etc.

(6) A very simple definition is given by Philip Kotler. According to him. "Marketing is human activity directed at satisfying needs and wants through exchange processes.

This definition lays stress on consumers' satisfaction, The word exchange implies the selling aspect. But the more important point is that the goal of exchange is to satisfy the needs and wants of the consumers. There are needs such as food and clothing. Similarly, there are wants such as the desire to make a show of one's riches. 'The producers provide different products to the consumer to satisfy their needs and wants.
