7\. IMPORTANCE OF MARKETING:

Marketing is of basic importance in the process of economic development. By enlarging markets, it encourages large scale production which results in low cost and low prices of goods and an improvement in the standard of living of the people to that extent. In the words of Peter Drucker 'Marketing occupies a critical role in respect of underdeveloped 'growth' countries. Indeed, marketing is the most improved 'multiplier' of such development. Its development makes possible economic integration and the fullest utilisation of whatever assets and productive capacity an economy already possesses. It mobilizes latent economic energy. It contributes to the greatest needs; that for the rapid development of entrepreneurs and managers, and at the same time it may be the easiest area of managerial work to get going. The reason is that, it is the most systematised and, therefore, the most learnable and the most teachable of all areas of business management and entrepreneurship."\*

To be more precise, the contribution of marketing to different g fields of economic development is as follows:

(1) Industrial Development: According to the modern concept of marketing, production should be according to consumers' needs and the o consumers should be satisfied with what they have bought. The industries which adopt the modern concept of marketing produce goods and services with a clear knowledge of what people need and prefer. Consequently, markets expand and it becomes possible to use economic resources in a better way. Industries can develop more and more rapidly due to product- planning and product-development.

(2) Conversion of Potential Needs into Effective Demand: As Drucker has stated, marketing can convert latent demand into effective demand Of course, marketing by itself cannot create purchasing power. But it can uncover and channelise purchasing power that exists.

(3) Development of Managers and Entrepreneurs: The contribution of marketing to the growth of managers and entrepreneurs in the society is invaluable. Drucker is of the opinion that economic development is not a force of nature. It is the result of the action, the purposeful, responsible, risk-taking action of men as entrepreneurs and managers, who can provide incentives to managers and entrepreneurs.

(4) Development of Small Scale Industries

Under-developed countries do not have the capacity to develop the giant industries using the latest technology. But they can modify their traditional techniques of production in the light of recent inventions in the technological field and develop their small scale industries. Thus they can speed up the rate of growth of their economy.

(5) Large Scale Consumption Consumption of goods on a large scale is necessary for rapid economic development. But consumption can be raised through marketing. This is what Drucker means by the term 'multiplier effect. If consumption can be raised through marketing, large scale industries wil. come up, producing goods in bulk. The costs and prices of goods decline due to the economies of large scale production. The low prices again encourage consumption and thus a cycle goes on giving encouragement to the process of economic development.

(6) High Living Standard: Not only does marketing help produce goods according to the needs of the consumers, but it improves the living standard of the people in one more respect. A large variety of goods becomes available to the people with the development of diversified industries.

(7) Development of Business Standards: Marketing helps not only in the development of product standards, but in the development of the standards of conduct of integrity, of reliability, of foresight and of planning.

(8) Division of Labour and Specialisation: Advantages of division of labour and specialisation can be availed of only when there is large scale production. Markets expand as a result of marketing. A local market turns into a regional market. A regional market turns into a national market and a national market may expand into an international market. The expansion of the market leads to large scale production.

(9) Growth of Exports: The underdeveloped countries have to import machinery and technical skill on a large scale for industrial development. They need foreign exchange to pay for these imports, It is only through exports that they can get foreign exchange. 'More exports' should only be the only watchword of these countries. Marketing can play an important role in this field. Information can be obtained through marketing as regards markets abroad, the preferences of the buyers in foreign markets and their expectations.

(10) Employment Generation: The underdeveloped countries are facing a serious problem of unemployment. Marketing can make a remarkable contribution in solving this problem. If proper training in modern techniques of marketing is given to the educated youth, they can pursue a promising career in salesmanship.
