4\. PRODUCT DEVELOPMENT:\*

In the age of intense competition, the existence and development of business depends upon quality of sales, which in turn depends upon customers. It is necessary that existing products must be modified to suit the changing needs of customers. New products must be constantly developed to meet their ever changing tastes and needs. The needs of customers go on changing and their habits, attitudes, fashion and buying habits also go on changing. Companies must go on developing new products in this fast changing world, in order to face competition and maintain the total number of customers. The companies that fail to develop new products are undertaking great risk of being thrown out of the market.

The development of new products is considered in the context of innovation.

There are two ways of innovation:

(1) Modification in Existing Product: When major changes are made in qualities, features, styles or packing of existing product to make it suitable to the changing needs and tastes of customers, it is this type of innovation.

(2) Development of New Product: The company has to frequently go on introducing a new product in the market in order to face competition in the market. This is innovation in the real sense of the term. e.g. in place of ink pens, ball pens of various types have been introduced; in place of simple television, L.C.D. T. Vs. have been introduced. Generally in modern days the Research and Development Department is constantly striving to develop new products. e.g. the well-known Indian company Escorts Ltd. started with manufacturing tractors and within a span of 25 years introduced motorcycles, scooters, land scrappers, shock pump units, control valves and cylinders etc. totalling 50 new items.

New product development is risky but risk has to be taken. Funds on a large scale have to be invested in developing new products and it will go to waste, if the customers do not accept the new product. There is no 100% guarantee that the new product will succeed. In highly advanced countries like the U.S.A., the new product failure rate was 40% for consumer products, 20% for industrial products and 18% for services.

(A) Classification of New Products:

Philip Kotler has identified six categories of new products, in terms of their newness to the company and to the market place.

(1) New-to-the-World Products: New products not so far produced by any company, that create an entirely new market.

(2) New-product Lines: They are the new products that allow a company to enter an established market for the first time.

(3) Additions to Existing Product Lines: These are the new products that supplement a company's established product lines.

(4) Improvements to Existing Products: These are new products that provide improved performance or greater perceived value and replace existing products.

(5) Repositioning: Existing products that are targeted to new markets or market segments.

(6) Cost Reductions: New products that provide similar performance at lower cost.

Another classification is as follows:

(1) Products which are really 'innovative': Such products are those for which there is a real need, but for which there are no existing substitutes e.g. computers.

(2) Replacement of existing products which differ significantly from the existing product: Here new products are put in the market which fulfils the need better than existing products. e.g. in place of a film projector, video is available.

(3) Imitative products: These are products new to the company, but not new to the market. In this case, new products feature familiar patterns and make life easier e.g. in place of ordinary refrigerators, defrost refrigerators were introduced.

(B) Need for Developing New Products: Just as social, economic, political and technological factors go on changing, the need for developing new products go on increasing. The following are basic factors for demand of such development:

(1) Changes in customers' tastes and fashions: The market is dynamic and so are customers' tastes. As the standard of living of customers rises, their tastes, fashions, like-dislikes also undergo changes. This requires the companies to develop new products.

(2) Changes in Marketing Techniques: As the marketing techniques go on improving, the companies are required to develop new products or improve existing products. c.g. As the existing brands become old, customers want new brands of the products. That is what the consumer product companies like Godrej, Hindustan Lever Ltd. etc are doing.

(3) Intense Competition In the regional, national and international markets, the competition is so severe that the companies have to always strive for new products, otherwise they will be thrown out of the market. It has to do it either to become market leader or to attract more and more customers etc.

(4) To Use Idle Capacity: A company may have spare production capacity and by developing new products, it can usefully and profitably use it. New production technology requires very large scale production units with ample spare capacity. Banks and other financial institutions grant loans on large scale for setting up or for new production units or for expansion etc. and this has also led to the existing spare capacity.

(5) Other Factors: There are other factors that make it necessary to develop new products: e.g. utilise waste or scrap from present production, to make more efficient use of marketing and production skills etc.
