The theory of the multiplier was first developed in the midst of the depression of the 1930s by Richard F. Kahn in his article "The Relation of Home Investment to Unemployment," published in the *Economic Journal* in June 1931. However, Kahn's multiplier was strictly speaking an employment multiplier rather than an investment multiplier. In 1936, Keynes developed the concept of the investment multiplier, which is considered one of the path-breaking contributions to economic analysis. The investment multiplier is an integral part of Keynesian theory of employment and, in fact, it has come to be regarded as the 'center piece of macro economic theory' today.
