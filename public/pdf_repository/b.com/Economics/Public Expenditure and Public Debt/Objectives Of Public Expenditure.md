# 2_Objectives of Public Expenditure
As said above, there has been a phenomenal expansion of governmental activity which has embraced almost all the major spheres of a nation's life. In fact the government has come to act as the 'social guardian' and in this role it performs numerous and ever-increasing functions. The broad objectives of public expenditure may be studies under the following heads:
(i) National Security
(ii) Maintenance of Law and Order or Internal Security
(iii) Economic Stability
(iv) Economic Growth
(v) Reduction of Economic Inequalities
(vi) Reduction of Regional Disparities
(vii) Other Objectives.
(i) National Security : One of the important objectives of public expenditure is to defend the country against foreign aggression and preserve its sovereignity and territorial integrity. Even Adam Smith and other classical economists who were vehement champions of the policy of laissez falre had emphasized defence as the primary function of the State. Adam Smith had said,' 'defence is more important than opulence'. The government must allocate and spend whatever amount is needed for ensuring the national security of the country. For this, the government must strive to develop the army, navy and air force on a very strong and sound footing

and ensure that all these three wings of defence are fully equipped with the latest arms, ammunitions and defence equipments. In short, the objective is to ensure complete national security of the country and make it fully well-equipped with the latest technology in all respects so as to enable it to face any eventuality.
(ii) Internal Security : Maintenance of Law and Order : The second important objecti re of public expenditure is the maintenance of law and order within the country and ensuring internal security, that is, security of life and property of the citizens. The classical economists too had emphasized this as one of the functions and objectives of public expenditure. In fact, the preservation of law and order and the maintenance of peace and tranquaillity is one of the pre-essential conditions for economic growth. The government, therefore, must-make adequate provision for the upkeep of the police force and other para-military forces and equip them with the latest techniques and equipments for handling any internal disturbances. Besides, a strong and independent judiciary and an efficient and functional jail department has also a significant role to play in this.
(iii) Economic Stability : The third important objective of public expenditure is to prevent the occurence of cyclical fluctuations and to maintain economic stability. Thus, for example, in times of depression, when there is a phenomenal decline in the level of effective demand and private investment and when even a substantial reduction in interest rates and tax rates fails to induce consumption and investment demand, a suitable public expenditure policy in the form of public investment on a massive scale on public works and social security schemes can go a long way in stimulating both consumption and investment. The merit of this policy is that it increases the levels of employment, income, consumption and output through its multiplier and acceleration effects and lift the economy out of the jaws of depression.

On the other hand, in times of inflation, a cut in public expenditure has an anti-inflationary effect. The government during this period should reduce its own expenditure to the minimum extent possible to contain aggregate demand. Non- developement and avoidable expenditure should be reduced to the bare minimum and utmost economy should be exercised in incurring public expenditure. Likewise, subsidies, bounties and other cash assistance given by the State to the private sector is selectively reduced or withdrawn.

Thus, public expenditure policy aims at controlling cyclical fluctuations and strives to maintain economic stability.
(iv) Economic Growth : Modern state is no longer a 'Police State'; it has accepted the concept of a Welfare State' and as a result there has been

a phenomenal increase in the scope of the governments' activities. $\frac{193}{195}$ expenditure is aimed at promoting rapid economic and social development of the country. The objective is to attain and maintain a high level of employment and income in the economy through the optimum utilisation of human, material and financial resources of the country. With this end in view, the government, in modern times, incurs large expenditure on the development of the different sectors of the economy like agriculture, industry, energy, transport and communications and on social sectors like education, health, housing, family welfare, etc. Public expenditure promtes economic development in the following ways:
(a) Public expenditure on the development of infrastructure like transport and communication facilities, power, irrigation, etc. helps to stimulate private investment and thereby ensure rapid economic growth. Likewise, private investment can also be encouraged by offering various types of subsidies.
(b) Similarly, public expenditure in the building up of a strong industrial base through the development of basic and heavy industries also plays a key role in sustaining a higher rate of economic growth.
(c) Public expenditure on education-general, vocational and technicaltraining and research, health and family welfare, housing and recreation etc. help in promoting economic development. This expenditure is also known as 'human investment' or 'investment in human capital' which is an essential ingredient of rapid economic growth.
(d) Likewise, public expenditure on various types of social security measures such as group insurance against unemployment, sickness and old age, health services, food and housing subsidies, family pensions, education grants, etc. have a favourable effect on people's ability to work and save. Such expenditure leads to an increase in the real income of the people, promotes their productive efficiency which ultimately results in more and better production.
(v) Reduction in Economic Inequalities : Still another important objective of public expenditure is to bring about reduction in economic and social inequalities to achieve the cherished goal of social justice. The nature and volume of public expenditure should be so devised as to reduce economic inequalities through the process of income redistribution.

A welfare-oriented public expenditure policy has an important role to play in bringing about reduction in income inequalities. For this, it is essential that-government expenditure helps directly and indirectly in increasing the income of the lower strata of the society. Thus, for example,

(i) Public expenditure incurred for providing education and health services, housing, etc to persons in the lower income groups- free of cost or at concessional rates will not only increase their productive efficiency but also bring about an increase in their real income.
(ii) Likewise, the award of scholarships, freeships, and other financial help to the wards of the poor also helps indirectly in increasing the real income of this group
(iii) Similarly, provision of essential articles at subsidised rates to lower income groups and public expenditure on various types of social security schemes also raises the level of real income of this class.
Thus, a suitable public expenditure policy designed to increase the real income of the weaker sections of the society can play an important role in reducing income inequalities. Of course, care, should be taken to see that the benefits of increasing public expenditure go really to the deserving and needy, or else it is likely to weaken the people's willingness to work and save.
(vi) Reducing Regional Disparities for Achieving Balanced Regional Growth : Besides reducing economic inequalities, public expenditure also aims at reducing regional disparities and ensure a balanced regional development. We know that-private sector being solely guided by profit motive confines its activities mainly to the developed areas of the country; it is rather unwilling to invest in backward and underdeveloped regions. This results in economic disparities as between the different regions of the country. The government, through its public expenditure policy can encourage the private sector to move to such undeveloped and backward regions by offering subsidies, bounties and other types of cash assistance besides developing social and economic overheads in these areas. And even inspite of this, if private enterprise does not come forward, the government has to take the lead and initiative and assume the responsibility for the development of these regions. This is sought to be done by the development of infrastructural facilities and by initiating direct investment in various developmental programmes and schemes. Thus, a well thought out public expenditure policy can play an effective role in reducing regional disparities.
(vii) Miscellaneous Objectives : The miscellaneous objectives of public expenditure include provision of public utility services at affordable rates to the citizens, protection of consumers' interests, debt-serving, maintenance of cordial foreign relations abroad, etc.
