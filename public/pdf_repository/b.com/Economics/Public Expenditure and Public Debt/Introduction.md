# 1_Introduction
Public expenditure is the expenditure incurred by public authorities like central and state governments, local bodies, public corporations etc, to satisfy those collective wants which the people in their individual capacity are unable to satisfy efficiently. Public expenditure, thus, tends to satisfy collective social wants. The volume of public expenditure in recent years has been growing in almost all the countries of the world partly because of a continuous expansion in the activities of the State and other public bodies and partly because of the realisation that public expenditure can affect economic life of a nation in various ways. Hence, the study of public expenditure, the causes of its rapid growth, its principles and its effects has come to occupy a very important place in modern times.

The earlier writers had not adequately recognised the importance of public expenditure and had therefore paid scant attention to it; rather they had focussed mainly on the study of the problems of public revenue. This was mainly due to two reasons :
(i) Adam Smith and other classical economists believed that the functions of the State should be restricted to,'Justice, Police and Arms', that is to say, to the provision of justice, maintenance of law-and-order and defence of the country. The concept of State prevalent at that time had come to be known as the 'Police State' (ii) The general belief prevalent at that time was that government expenditure was wasteful and that money would be utilised in a muth better way by private individuals than by the government.

Robert Peel had argued that money would "fructify more in the hands of the people than in those of the State." Going a step further, Parnell wrote that, "every particle of expense that is incurred beyond what necessity absolutely requires for the preservation of the social order and for protection against foreign attack, is a waste, unjust and oppressive imposition upon the public."
However, the situation today has entirely changed and none of the above two arguments are teneable. Modern state is no longer a Police State, instead the concept of Welfare State has come to be accepted by governments all over the world. Indeed, there is not a single aspect-of a modern citizen's life that is not affected by governmental activity. As a result there has been a phenomenal increase in public expenditure in recent years, firstly because the economic activities of the State have increased manifold and secondly, it has now been realised that the nature and volume of public expenditure have important bearings on the economic life of a nation- on production, distribution and the general level of economic activity.
