2_Disequilibrium in the Balance of Payments

There are two main types of disequilibrium in the balance of payments of a country :

(a) Temporary or Transitory Disequilibrium :

This type of disequilibrium is purely of a temporary nature and is selfcorrecting. It does not involve the complex problem of adjustment.

(b) Fundamental Disequilibrium :

When the balance of payments situation of a country progressively deteriorates and if it is chronic and long term, it is known as fundamental disequilibrium in the balance of payments. Such a disequilibrium requires correction and adjustment.

# Causes of Disequilibrium in Balance of Payments
The following are the main causes of disequilibrium in the balance of payments of a country.

## (1) Economic Factors
The economic factors which cause disequilibrium in the balance of payments include the following :

### (a) Cyclical Fluctuations
Business fluctuations arising out of the occurrence of trade cycles cause disequilibrium in the balance of payments of a country. Thus, for example, if there is a business recession in foreign countries, it will result in a fall in exports of the country concerned leading to disequilibrium in the balance of payemts.

### (b) Inflationary Pressures in the Economy
An inflationary rise in prices in the country also leads to disequilibrium in its balance of payments. Inflation, as we know, tends to reduce exports because of rise in prices of export goods and on the other hand due to rising money incomes of the people, during inflation, imports tend to rise there by causing disequilibrium in the balance of payments.

### (c) Capital Movements
If due to political instability and other factors, there is flight of capital from the country, it would cause disequilibrium in its balance of payments. Likewise, a country may tend to have an adverse balance when it borrows heavily from another country, while the lending country will tend to have a favourable balance.

### (d) Fall in Exports and Rise in Imports
A fall in exports and a rise in imports of a country causes a disequilibrium in its balance of payments. The fall in exports may be due to:

(i) Contraction of the economy.
(ii) Fall in domestic production and rising demand for exportable goods leading to a decline in exportable surplus.
(iii) Higher prices and inferior quality of export goods which reduce their competitive strength in the international markets.
(iv) Currency appreciation.
(v) Obstructive government policy.

The rise in imports may be due to :
(1) Initiation of the process of rapid economic development leading to rising demand for various types of developmental and maintenance imports
(2) Fall in domestic production
(3) Demonstration effect
(4) Cheaper prices and better quality of foreign goods.
(5) Increase in money incomes due to inflation policy.

### (e) Import restrictions and tariffs by the developed countries may also be mentioned as an important reason for disequilibrium in the balance of payments of less developed countries.

## (2) Natural Factors
Natural factors such as floods, famines, crop failures, etc. adversely affect agricultural and industrial production leading to fall in exports and rise in imports and hereby causing disequilibrium in the balance of payments.

## (3) Political Factors
Political instability, deterioration in the law and order situation within the country, fear of foreign aggression, lack of confidence in the currency of the country etc. disrupt the productive apparatus within the country causing a decline in exports and an increase in imports.

(b) Structural changes in the economy may alter the demand for exports and imports adversely.
