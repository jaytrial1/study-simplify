A trade cycle or a business cycle may be defined as a wave-like fluctuation of expansion and contraction chasing each other. According to Estey, "Cyclical fluctuations are characterised by alternating waves of expansion and contraction. They do not have a fixed rhythm, but they are cycles in that the phases of contraction and expansion recur frequently and in fairly similar patterns."

Keynes defines trade cycle as: "A trade cycle is composed of periods of good trade characterised by rising prices and low unemployment percentages altering with periods of bad trade characterised by falling prices and high unemployment percentages."

In the words of Mitchell and Burns: "Business cycles are a type of fluctuations found in the aggregate economic activity of nations that organise their work mainly in business enterprises. A cycle consists of expansions recurring at about the same time in many economic activities followed by similar general recessions, contractions and revivals which merge into the expansion phase of the next-cycle. This sequence of change is recurrent."

Haberler in his short definition of trade cycle says, "A business cycle in general sense may be defined as an alteration of periods of prosperity and depression, of good and bad trade."

In short, a trade cycle is marked by typical wave-like fluctuations marked by expansion and contraction in over all business activity as evidenced by changes in the level of aggregate economic activity such as the gross product, the index of industrial production, employment and income.
