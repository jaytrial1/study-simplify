
The classical theory of income and employment is based on a number of assumptions like perfect competition, wage-price flexibility, homogeneous labour, constant stock of capital and technology, etc. On the basis of these assumptions, the classical economists maintained that there is always full employment in the economy. Of course, there may exist some amount of voluntary and frictional unemployment, but otherwise the economy operates at a level of full employee. In case, if at any time, there is unemployment, this will lead to fall in wage rates which will increase the demand for labour till full employment is achieved in the economy and at this point wages will tend to be stable. Thus, according to classical economists, the existence of unemployment is a short-run phenomenon; in the long run the economy attains equilibrium at a level of full employment through wage-price flexibility. According to them, the main cause of unemployment is the monopoly of labour unions and State-interference in the labour market - both of which prevent the wage rates from coming down with the result that the demand for labour does not increase to enable the economy to attain full employment.

Thus, according to the classical economists, the free play of economic forces brings about full employment in the economy. If there is tendencey towards unemployment, it will be corrected by wage-price flexibility. They, therefore, argued that the government should keep its hands off from the economic field if there is to be full employment. During the periods of unemployment, the wage level should be reduced so that more labour could be demanded leading to elimination of unemployment. Whatever goods are produced are automatically demanded and so there is no possibility of general glut or over-production.
