In his illustrious book "The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money" published in 1936, Keynes has discussed the theory of employment and income which is considered as a landmark in the moden economic thinking. According to him, at any given time, the volume of employment in the economy depends on the aggregate effective demand. Employment does not depend on wages as assumed by Pigou and other writers. A policy of wage-cut will not lead to an inecrease in employment, on the contray, it may lead to increase in unempoyment. According to Keynes, a fall in wage rates will result in a fall in income and consequently to a fall in demand and employment. In order to increase employment, Keynes has advocated an increase in effective demand rather than a wagecut. He thought that the manipulation of wage rates is not an effective way to increase employment; manipulation of demand is a far more effective policy. Pigou also at a later stage, had accepted this fact when he wrote in his "Lapses from full Employment," that, "I am in favour of attacking the problem of unemployment by manipulating demand rather than by manipulating wages."

According to Keynes, given the technique of production, the level of employment in the economy depends on total production. There is a direct relationship between total output and total employment. If production increases, employment increases and vice-versa.

The total production in the economy depends on total effective demand. If the total effective demand increases, production will increase, while a fall in effective damand will lead to a fall in production. The total effective demand is classfied into two groups :
(i) Consumption demand. (ii) Investment demand.

Thus, the total effective demand is influenced by consumption expenditure and investment expenditure.

(i) Consumption Demand or Consumption Expenditure :

According to Keynes, consumption demand or consumption expenditure depends on the level of income and the propensity to consume. If income

increases, consumption also increases, but only upto a point beyond which consumption increases at a diminishing rate. In other words, it may be said the: the marginal propensity to consume tends to be less than unity or one. Thus, as income increases, after a point, the gap between income and consumption increases and therefore to maintain and increase employment in the economy it is necessary to bridge this gap by increasing investment demand or investment expenditure.

(ii) Investment Demand or Investment Expenditure : 

Investment demand or investment expenditure depends on the rate of interest and the marginal efficiency of captial. The rate of interest depends on liquidity preference and the quantity of money at a given time. Thus, according to Keynes, the three main determinant of employment are:
(a) Marginal propensity to consume (b) Rate of interest (c) Marginal efficiency of capital.

Of the above three factors, the marginal propensity to consume and the rate of interest are relatively sticky and therefore, investment mainly depends on the marginal efficiency of captial. Changes in production and employment, according to keynes, are in the main due to changes in the marginal efficiency of captial. As has been well said the initiative in modern times lies not with the 'saver' but with the 'investor.'

The classical economists in their analaysis had assumed that investment largely depends on the rate of interest and therefore, by manipulating interest rates, investment in the economy can be manipulated. Thus, for example, if the rate of interest is low, the entrepreneurs will be encouraged to invest more in the economy which would lead to increase in employment. Conversely, if the rate of interest is high, investment will decline leading to a fall in employment. Thus, according to classical economists, if there is unemployment in the economy, it can be dealt with by lowering the rate of interest. But the experience of the great depression of the thirties fully exposed the weakness of the calssical thinking by establishing the fact that when there is an alround atmosphere of pessimism prevailing in the economy, even a substantial reduction in the interest rate fails to stimulate investment. Thus, a policy of low interest rate or cheap money policy proves to be ineffective in dealing with the problem of unemployment.

As said earlier, according to Keynes, investment depends on the rate of interest which is relatively sticky and the marginal, efficiency of captial. Changes in the marginal efficency of captial cause changes in the volume of investment and employment. In other words, as the level of employment depends on effective demand, Keynes has advocated measures to increase the effective demand for increasing employment.

For increasing consumption demand, he has advocated a redistribution of income in favour of the poor whose marginal propensity to consume is high by caxing the rich whose marginal propensity to consume is low. Likewise, reduction in taxes on articles of mass consumption and the supply of essential goods to the poor at the subsidised rates too may help in increasing consumption demand.

On the other hand, to increase investment demand, Keynes is in favour of low interest rate policy though he fully recognises its limitations, particularly in times of depression when the marginal efficiency of capital declines to a very low level, almost to zero and sometimes becomes negative, even a substantial reduction in interest fails to revive private investment. Of course, Keynes is in favour of taking various measures to stimulate private investment through a cheap money policy and a contra - cyclical tax policy which includes tax reduction on personal incomes and corporate incomes alongwith the offer of a number of tax reliefs, tax concessions and tax incentives. And still, inspite of all this, if private investment is not forthcoming, it will be necessary for the government to adopt a contracyclical public expenditure policy for the purpose of attaining and maintaining full employment. In other words, when private invesment declines and shows no sings of revial inspite of all the monetary and other measures taken, the State should step in and increase public investments by undertaking a massive public works programme. This programme is to be financed not through raising taxes but by recourse to public borrowing and created money, that is, deficit financing. Such a programme would help in increasing total effective demand and employment in the economy.

[6.1] Gist of Keynesian Theory of Income and Employment : 

Keynesian theory of income and employment may be summarised as follows :
(1) The level of income and employment in an economy depends on the level of effective demand.
(2) Effective demand is governed by aggregate demand price and aggregate supply price. Keynes assumes aggregate supply price as given in the short run and concentrates wholly on aggregate demand price.
(3) Aggregate demand price or aggregate demand function is governed by consumption expenditure and investment expenditure.
(4) The consumption expenditure is determined by the size of the income and the individual's is propensity to consume. Keynes run, because the propensity to consume does not show major

changes in the short period. Employment, thus, mainly depends on investment expenditure or investment function.
(5) Investment expenditure depends on marginal efficiency of capital and the rate of interest.
(6) The marginal efficiency of captial, in its turn, depends on the prospective yield from capital assets (that is, expected rate of return) and the supply price of captial assets.
(7) The rate of interest depends on the liquidity preference of the community and the supply of money.
(8) The liquidity preference is governed by the transactions motive, precautionary motive and speculative motive, while the supply of money is directly controlled by the banking system.
