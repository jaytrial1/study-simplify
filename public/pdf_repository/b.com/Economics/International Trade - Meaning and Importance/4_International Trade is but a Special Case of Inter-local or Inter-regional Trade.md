4_International Trade is but a Special Case of Inter-local or Inter-regional Trade

As said earlier, according to the classical economists, in the context of the differences between inter-regional and international trade, there was a need for the separate theory of international trade. However, this view has been challenged in recent times of the eminent Sweedish economist Prof. Bertil Ohlin. According to Ohlin, there is no fundamental difference between international trade and inter - regional trade. As he puts it, `international trade is but a special case of inter-local or inter-regional trade.' All trade whether internal or international can be explained with the help of the relative cost theory. There is only one origin of all sorts of exchange and that is relative advantage in the production of one commodity as compared to another commodity. In Ohlin's words, `regions and nations specialise and trade with each other for the same reason that individual specialise and trade.'

As pointed out by the classical economists, the factors of production are more inobile in inter - regional trade than international trade and this gives rise to differences in internal and international trade. But according to Ohlin, differences in the mobility of production factors is not of a fundamental nature. Just as factors of production are not so much mobile between two countries, likewise within the same country too factors of production many a times tend to be immobile. Thus, for example, there are different groups in the total labour supply say like artisans, carpenters, black smiths, engineers, administrate officers, doctors, lawyers etc. All these are non-competing of groups. If there is a shortage of engineers, artisans cannot become engineers. Likewise, a mill-worker cannot become a manager. Thus, from the professional view point, labour is not mobile. Similarly, the workers do not move so easily from one city to another or from one region to another. As Adam Smith had pointed out long back, 'of all the luggage man is the most difficult luggage to be transported. Likewise, capital does not move freely say from Gujarat to North - Eastern States. In

this way, within the same country factors of production are not perfectly mobile nor these factors tend to be immobile between two countries. However, it may be accepted that factor mobility between two countries is relatively less than factor mobility within the same country. But this difference is only of degree and can hardly be called as fundamental because the existence of non-competing groups is found not only in international trade but in inter-regional trade as well.

Prof. Ohlin further points out that the classical economists had explained the difference between inter-regional and international trade in the context of different currency systems in the different countries and hence the need for a separate theory of international trade. But in the opinion of Ohlin, differences in the currency systems of different countries is not that much fundamental or basic that there is a need for a sperarate independent - discussion and debate of international trade. In recent years, a workable and efficient efficient mechanism has been evolved for determing exchange rate between different currencies of the world. More over, the International Moetary Fund too works for maintaining relative stability of exchange rate as between different currencies.

# Prof. Ohlin's View Regarding Differences between InterRegional and International Trade
We discuss below in brief Prof. Ohlin views regarding differences between inter - regional and international trade. According to Prof. Ohlin there are no fundamental or basic differences between inter regional and inter national trade. As he points out, the important difference between the two inter - regional and international trade is the operation of the value theory in one and the same market and the operation of the value theory in the different markets. The demand and supply analysis which is made applicatble to the inter - retional trade can also be applied to international trade as well. Thus, in Ohlin's view, international trade can but be regarded as a special of inter - local or inter - regional trade. Prof. Ohlin explains the concept of inter - regional trade as follows:

There is unequal distribution of natural resources as between different regions. Factor mobility is limited and because of transport costs and other factors there is not free movement of goods and commodities. We can conceive of different regions from the view point of distribution of production factors. A given region tends to be different from other regions and differences in the sub-parts of the region should not be less than that of other regions. Thus, the natural limitations as between different parts of the region should be considered as more important - than the limitations amongst the regions as such. Such parts are known as 'region' according to

Prof. Ohlin. Looked at from the point of view of production factors, we find that there are differences between various regions, while there is some sort of uniformity within the same region.

It is but natural that there is specialisation in the production of different goods in different regions, and this gives to exchange as between different regions. This is inter - regional trade. When these regions operate within the frontiers of the same country, we call it as internal trade and when the two trading regions operate in different countries it is known as international trade. However, the form and content of both are the same; both are forms of inter regional trade. In view of the special characteristics of inter-national trade, it is considered as a special case of inter - regional trade.

The form and content of international is almost like that of inter regional trade. This can be explained within the same region, while it is hardly the case in the sphere of international trade, then the analysis pertaining to inter - regional trade can help in understanding the genesis of international trade. The theory of inter regional trade explains the process of value determination in one and the same market, while in international trade, it studies value determination in different related markets. According to Prof. Ohlin, the theory of one and the same market can be used to study for more than one related markets.

Thus, according to Prof. Ohlin, international trade being a special case of inter - local or inter - regional trade, there hardly any need for a separate theory of international trade. Leading economists like Prof. Pareto, Edgeworth, Haberler etc. concur with the view of Prof. Ohlin. As Prof. Habler aptly puts it, strictly speaking, it is neither necessary nor possible to draw a dividing line between international trade and inter - regional trade. If we examine the so called characteristics of international trade, we shall find that there are no fundamental, basic and qualitative differences between international trade and inter - regional trade, whatever difference appear to us are only of quantum and not of kind.
