1_Introduction : Significance of the Study of International Trade
International trade has rightly been termed as an `engine of growth'. The size, composition and direction of foreign trade has a profound influence on the character and extent of development of a country. A study of the economic history of the various countries of the world reveals that international trade has played a very significant role in their rapid economic development. Thus, for example, the industrial revolution in Great Britain and its position as a super power in the comity of the nations of the world owes to a large extent to its international trade. Likewise, in the rapid economic development of countries like America, France, Germany, Japan etc. the role of international trade has been quite significant.

It is worthwhile mentioning here that international trade and economic development are complementary to each other. A country through imports can acquire the necessary machinery and equipment, capital goods, raw materials, spare parts and components, technical know-how and other goods and services. All these imports are essential to accelerate the pace of economic development in the country. Likewise, the earnings of foreign exchange through visible and invisible exports help in achieving selfreliance for the country. In this way, the various factors affecting import trade and export - trade play an important role in shaping internal economic situation. Similarly, the structure of international trade, direction of international trade, its composition and quantum gives an idea of the

economic situation of the country. Thus, for example, if the country is under - developed and predominantly agricultural, the quantum of its international trade is likely to remain relatively small and its main exports would largely comprise of agricultural products and raw materials. On the hand, its imports would mainly comprise of consumer goods and other finished products. Contrary to this, the main imports of developing industrial nations would mainly be capital goods, technical know-how etc.

Prof. Haberler has indicated four essential factors for economic development which become avialable through foreign trade :
(a) Availability of capital through international capital investment.
(b) Benefit through availability of raw materials as a means of development.
(c) Benefits of semi-finished goods and capital goods.
(d) Benefits arising from availability of technical skill and knowledge, skill development, marginal and administrative services and talents.
Thus, international trade helps in the economic development of a country through movement of physical goods and services. The various visible and invisible items entering the international trade keep the engine of economic development constantly moving and dynamic.

It should also be noted here that the World Trade Organisation reiterates these objectives. Thus, the main objectives of World Trade Organisation include among other the following :
(i) To remove impediments in the way of free flow of international trade and there by promote World Trade.
(ii) To raise the living standards and incomes of the people, attain full employment, expand production and trade, to make optimum use of world's resources and to apply all these objectives to the service sector.
(iii) To attain sustainable development in the context of optimisation of world's resources and to preserve and protect the environment consistent with the level of economic development of the country.
(iv) To initiate positive steps to ensure that the developing countries and in particular the least developed ones get a fair shape of the increase in international trade.
