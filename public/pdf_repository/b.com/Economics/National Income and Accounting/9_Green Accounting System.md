
**Introduction:** There are some imperfections in the measurement of national income, but why? Can it be said that the concept of economic growth has become obsolete? The answer is a clear 'no'. Gross national product and other concepts have been found to be inadequate for measuring economic welfare. But it is a fact that the measure of economic growth presents the whole picture with some limitations. The logic of suspending economic development to preserve natural resources is not acceptable. It is also of utmost importance that economic incentives should be provided for the maintenance of existing natural resources, as existing users pay less for the use of these natural resources than their actual social costs.

**Definition:** Green Accounting System – this term was probably first used by David Pearce. It is an accounting method in which the environmental impacts of production and consumption are taken into account in economic measurements.

Green accounting is sometimes referred to as natural resource accounting or environmental accounting. Here environmental cost benefit is incorporated into economic decision making. In particular, depreciations of natural resources and environment are taken into account in estimating Net Domestic Product or Net National Product. Green accounting thus incorporates the negative side effects of economic activity - especially environmental pollution effects - so that a true estimate of economic progress can be made. The nation's capacity for sustainable economic growth can be ascertained.

The two main uses of available information are the value of the depletion of natural resources and the negative effects of environmental pollution on economic welfare.

(1) (Green net national product) Green net national product is a guide for the formulation of economic policies in the national economy based on the concept of national product.

(2) Environmental economic information can be used in selected areas such as 'Pollution Emission Account' which has direct policy application.

**Conclusion:** Our economic measures like income, production consumption and investment cannot capture the long term view of social progress. It does not take into account environmental degradation that harms current and future quality of life, thereby overlooking sustainable economic development. In the traditional national accounting system, the color 'green' indicates the introduction of environmental impact and cost concepts. As a result, policymakers have a new kind of compass for steering the economy. This results in changes not only in the parameters of economic progress but also in environmental and natural resource policy. Thus green accounting is the best available tool for defining and evaluating the environmental dimension of sustainable development.
