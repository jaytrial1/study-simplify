
Various economists have presented different viewpoints regarding the meaning of National Income :

(1) Prof. Marshal has given a definition of 'National Income' from the point of view of Production.
(2) Prof. Pigou has given a definition of 'National Income' from the point of view of exchanges.
(3) Prof. Fisher has defined National Income from the point of view of consumption.

# Prof. Marshal's Definition
Prof. Marshal has thought about National Income from point of view of Production. "At the first look, national income is the total net production of all pricduction sources and the total amount being paid to those means of production. By combining capital and labour with the natural resources, whatever physical and nonphysical goods and services are produced every year is called `National Income."

From this point of view, National Income is the total net production and from another angle it is a flow of income, from which various instruments of production are paid compensation of their work. Thus, National Income is distributed as rent for land, buildings, salary of the workers, interest paid to the capitalists and entrepreneurs. Prof. Marshal concentrates more on the total production of the nation. The main point of his definition is that when a national produces certain level of production in a particular year, from which maintenence cost, use of national properties, half-made items etc. are deducted and what remains is called `Net Production' or also `National Income.'

The time period for calculation of National Income may be one year or more. While defining National Income, Prof. Marshall has used the term 'net Production' which suggests that from the total monetary value of the total national Income, costs attached with inpts like raw materials, semi ready goods and depreciation are to be deducted. Of course net income accrued from foreign investments is to be included in it.

Thus according to Prof. Marshal :

Net National Income = Total Production of goods and services - raw materials and half ready goods - depreciation of machines and capital + Net Income from Foreign investments.

# Prof. Pigou's Definition
In the opinion of Prof. Pigou, "Objective Income" of a society which can be measured through money, is the National Income." In other words, "the goods and services which are exchanged through money and whose can be easily stated in terms of money is called National Income.

Thus Prof. Pigou has given definition of National Income keeping in mind the factor of exchange. According to him in National Income all goods and service which can be exchanged through money, are included. This also includes, income earned from other countries.

Several economists have supported this definition given by Prof. Pigou. e.g. Accroding to Prof. Hicks, "National Income means, that aggregation of goods and services, which can be measured in terms of money - which is a Universal Standard." Similarly Prof. Sammuelson also calculates National Income by adding up monetary values of various goods and services produced in the country.

Of course, one should particularly remember a point that while discussing about the definition of National Income that the goods and services which are not paid through money i.e. the goods and services which are exchanged without expectation, are not counted when National Income is calculated. Thus, a car gifted to son by his father, necklace given to wife by her husband, typing work done by a Lawyer's wife, care taken by a Nurse for her sick father, wooden toys made by a carpented for his son, household work done by housewife, etc. are not included in calculation of National Income. Thus, the idea of calculation of National Income, according to Prof. Pigou can be presented as follows :

Net National Income $=$ Total Production goods and service which have monetary values and are exchanged + Net Income from foreign investment Depreciation - Indirect Taxes.

# Prof. Fisher's Definition
Prof. Fisher had defined 'National Income' from the point of view of consumption. According to him, during a particular year, that portion of Total Production is directly consumed, is called National Income.

In Prof. Fisher's opinion, the final objective of all the conomic activities is not production, but consumption. Moreover, `consumption' also includes 'production'. National Income is a measure of economic prosperity. So if the calculation of National Income, total consumption of the goods and services is kept in the centre, it can give a clear picture of the economic situtation in the country. In short, Prof. Fisher has made an attempt to give a logical definition of the National Income.

In addition, Prof. Fisher stated that the definition of National Income given by Prof. Marshal does not give the actually consumed income but also the income that will be consumed in future. Emphasizing this point he writes, "Actual consumption of goods and services only, should be counted as National Income. Thus, National Income is the set of goods and srvices going in the hands of the final consumers.
