
In order to describe various facets of National Income, Economists and Statisticians use various terms, using which one can obtain a measure of National Income during a given time period. There are two reasons for using various concepts for finding the National Income, instead of just one method.

(i) There are differences of opinion amongst economists regarding which details should be included in National Income. The Statistical Department puts aside such controversy and show calculation based on certain concepts, which may be used properly by a concerned person.

(ii) The second reason is that, for a particular objective, one concept may be more useful, while for another objective some other concept may be found to be more useful. Thus, more than one concept are presented.

# Net National Product - NNP $_{\text {mp }}$
Net National Product can be obtained by subtracting depreciation from the Total National Product. During production process when machiens break or get hard rubbing or scrub, in order to know net national product, depreciation values has to be deducted. Depreciation value can be known through original value of Capital instrument and time of its usage. If (i)preciation cost is zero, then there is no difference between Total and Net

National Product. But as there is always depreciation cost, NNP is always less than GNP. If depreciation cost is high, the difference between them is more.

Calculation of depreciation is difficult. It is not easy to estimate average life of a capital good and its price. Assumed working period may be wrong, and it is difficult to decide whether to consider original value of machine or its present value.

$$
\therefore \mathrm{NNP}_{\mathrm{mp}}=\mathrm{GNP}-\text { depreciation cost. }
$$

# Net National Product - NNP $_{\mathrm{fc}}$
Its calculation is based on the income of the inputs. Values of goods and services produced by inputs used by the citizens of the nation, is taken into account. These inputs may have been used within the boundary of the nation or outside the boundary of the nation.

This is known as Nation's Income.

$$
\therefore \mathrm{NNP}_{\mathrm{fc}}=\mathrm{NNP}_{\mathrm{mp}}-\text { net indirect business taxes. }
$$

Alternatively $\mathrm{MNP}_{\mathrm{fc}}$ may be expressed as $\mathrm{NDP}_{\mathrm{fc}}$ plus the net profit made by the inputs in foreign countries.

# Gross Domestic Product - GDP $_{\text {mp }}$
From the total value of goods and services produced during the year, within the boundari of the nation, the depreciation value of the physical tools has not been deducted. If depreciation value is deducted, then it is called 'Net Domestic Product'. In 'Domestic Product', production by nation's inputs is not included, but production through by foreign - owned inputs is included. In most of the countries difference between GNP and GDP is negligible.

Thus, when calculation of a nation's income is based on its internal inputs, it is called GDP ${ }_{m p}$. Here the time period is one year and production value is based on prevailing market prices.

Thus, $\therefore \mathrm{GDP}_{\mathrm{mp}}=\mathrm{GDP}_{\mathrm{fc}}+$ Tax on Net Business.

# **Gross Domestic Product (GDP $_{\mathrm{fc}}$ )
Here, calculation is based on total income of inputs. GDP is the addition of income of various inputs within the nation's boundary. In this estimate, depreciation cost or use of capital is considered. Thus it may also be defined as, total value added by the producers within national boundary plus depreciation.
$\therefore \mathrm{GDP}_{\mathrm{fc}}=$ Net Value Added in Production + Depreciation.
