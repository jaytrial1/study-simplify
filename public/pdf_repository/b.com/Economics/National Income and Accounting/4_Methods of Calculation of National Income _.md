National Income may be calculated using the following three methods:
(1) Production Method
(2) Income Method
(3) Consumption or Expenditure Method.

The correctness of these three methods depends on the availability of necessary and true statistics.

# Production Method

In this method, the net value of each good and service produced during a particular year is calculated, and then by adding up these figures, an estimate of National Income is obtained. In this calculation, the value of semi-produced goods and that of capital used is subtracted. Net income obtained from foreign trade and from capital assets are also added to National Income.

There are two methods of obtaining national income through the production method:
(1) Use of additional value in current production plus.
(2) Add up the increase in the value during each stage of production.

In short, using this method, the monetary value of annual production of various activities (Agriculture, Industries, transportation etc.) and the monetary value of the annual production of services is determined. From this value of total production, the annual depreciation is subtracted, and then net monetary income earned from other countries is added. Moreover, the amount of subsidies is added to it, and then indirect taxes are subtracted. In short,

National Income = total production at market price - indirect taxes + net income from foreign trade - depreciation + subsidy.

# Income Method

In this method, all the individual incomes of the nation are added. Incomes of firms and companies are also taken into account, and the income (profit) of government-owned enterprises are also added.

Here, while calculating National Income, payments made by entrepreneurs for inputs and reserve funds kept out of profit are added to the National Income. Of course, amounts of pensions, payments made towards unemployment insurance, benefits of sickness and other amounts received from the government which are called (Badla payments) are not included in the National Income. Similarly, the goods and services, for which money is not paid, are also not added to the National Income. Thus,

National Income = Individual Income and Incomes of firms and companies - Badla payments + undistributed profit + Balance of Public Enterprises - Depreciation.

# Use Method or Expenditure Method

Under this method, National Income is calculated through the expenditure incurred by individuals, firms, companies and the state, during the year.

The total expenditure in the economy may be distributed among the following five sections:

(1) Private Consumption Expenditure: A person spends money on various goods and services, and it is called 'Private Consumption Expenditure'.
(2) Private Investment Expenditure: During the year, producers, entrepreneurs, traders etc. incur expenditure on sources of capital, for producing goods and services, and it is called 'investment expenditure'. This type of investment expenditure should be increasing sources of expenditure. From this point of view, investment made in old shares or on old houses cannot be considered as investment expenditure because, only ownership of capital changes, and there is no increase in investment.
(3) Collection Expenditure on Produced goods and Capital Goods: Not the entire stock of produced goods and investment goods is sold during the year, some part of it is not sold which becomes unsold stock. Now, the stock of goods at the beginning of the year, and added stock during the year, becomes 'total investment expenditure'. Of course, if the stock of goods has reduced during the year, then the same will be deducted from total expenditure.
(4) Government Expenditure: During present time, the government also incurs many types of expenditure. For example, like other customers, the government also incurs expenditure on consumption goods and services and on capital goods. This way the government and services and on capital goods. This way the government expenditure can also be divided in two parts: consumption expenditure and capital expenditure.

Of course, it must be noted here that government expenditure includes only that type of expenditure, which results in the production of goods and services. Thus, unemployment allowance paid by the government or pension paid to the retired government servants is the type of expenditure which is not used for the production of goods or services. Thus, this type of government expenditure shows an exchange of money and so it is not included in the calculation of national income.
(5) Import Cost and Export Income: When a country imports foreign goods or services, it creates 'Import Cost'; on the other hand, when foreigners buy goods and services of this country, they earn income. Thus, in the calculation of Total National Income, the difference between 'Export Income' and 'Import Cost' should be added or subtracted.

# National Income is a Coin with Three Sides

It is clear from the above discussion that 'National Income' may be seen from three angles viz., production, income and expenditure. That is why it is said that 'National Income' is a three - sided coin. When a firm produces various goods, it has to use services of factors of production, such as land, labour, capital etc. Due to their total co-operation and efforts on one side various goods are produced and on the other side, these factors of production get rent, wages and interest as rewards or income and the entreprenour get income in form of profit.

Now the incomes obtained by these factors of production are spent by customers on buying goods produced by the firms. Thus, in the first stage, factors of production get income due to expenses incurred by the firms, while in the second stage these factors spend money for buying these goods, which results in income for the firms. From this income, firms again spend money on factors of production and thus the circular flow of National Income continues without stop.

Thus, as the idea of National Income can be seen from three angles viz., Production, Income and Expenditure, National Income is known as a three - sided coin. thus, there are three methods of measuring National Income of any country. If the basic data are properly obtained, then these three methods should provide the same level of National Income. e.g. the total value of produced goods and services will be equal to income accruing to the factors of production and these factors, as customers spend the same level of income towards production. So, one can derive the following equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\text { National Income } & =\text { Total Production during the year. } \\
& =\text { Total Income received by the factors of production. } \\
& =\text { Total Expenditure of Consumers (including the government)}
\end{aligned}
$$
