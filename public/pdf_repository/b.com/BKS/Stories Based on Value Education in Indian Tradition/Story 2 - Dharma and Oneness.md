
STORY-2 Dharma and Oneness : They Story of Yudhishthir and Yaksha (Mahabharat)

The Mahabharata is a grand epic poem that tells the story of the huge warfare between the Kauravas and the virtuous Pandavas, rooted in the historic Bharata dynasty. The Mahabharata is a truly remarkable work of literature, containing over 100,000 verses, divided into 18 captivating chapters, and tells the story of an epic battle between two groups of cousins for the throne of Hastinapur.

The Yaksha Prashna is a series of questions asked by a Yaksha to Yudhisthira. The answers to these questions aim to impart wisdom and knowledge.

The Story of Yudhishthir and Yaksha (The Yaksha Prashna)
The Pandavas were roaming in the forests during their thirteen-year exile. Then they once looked for water to quench their thirst. The responsibility of managing water was first handed over to Sahadeva.

Sahadeva saw a reservoir nearby and reached there to get water from it. The invisible Yaksha, the owner of the reservoir, stopped him through Akashvani and put a condition to answer some questions first. Ignoring the Yaksha, Sahadeva started taking water from the reservoir. Then the Yaksha made Sahadeva lifeless.

When Sahadeva did not return, Nakula, Arjuna, and then Bhima respectively took up the responsibility of bringing water. They reached

the same reservoir and became lifeless for disobeying the conditions of the Yaksha.

In the end, worried Yudhishthira himself reached that reservoir. The invisible Yaksha appeared and warned him and asked him to answer his questions. Yudhishthira showed patience and agreed to answer to all the questions.
Yaksha : Who makes the sun rise?
Yudhishthira : O Yaksha! Brahma makes the sun rise.
Yaksha : Who walks around it?
Yudhishthira : The gods walk around it.
Yaksha : Who sets him and in what sun is established?
Yudhishthira : Dharma sets him down and he is established in truth.
Yaksha : How does a man become a listener?
Yudhishthira : Man becomes listener through hearing.
Yaksha : By whom does he become Brahmaroop?
Yudhishthira : Through self-punishment
Yaksha : By whom does he become wise?
Yudhishthira : By the service of the Gurus, he becomes wise.
Yaksha : What is the divinity among Brahmins?
Yudhishthira : The self-study of the Vedas is the divinity among the brahmins.
Yaksha : What is the behavior of unscrupulous men?
Yudhishthira : Violent or Corrupt is the behavior of unscrupulous men.
Yaksha : Which one thing is sacrificial material?
Yudhishthira : Life is a sacrificial material.
Yaksha : Who am I?
Yudhishthira : You are neither this body, nor the senses, nor the mind, nor the intellect. You are pure consciousness, the consciousness that is omniscient.
Yaksha : What is the purpose of life?
Yudhishthira : The purpose of life is to know that consciousness that is free from the bondage of birth and death. Knowing it is salvation.

| Yaksha | : What is the cause of birth? |
| :--: | :--: |
| Yudhishthira | : Unsatisfied desires, desires, and results of actions are the cause of birth. |
| Yaksha | : Who is free from the bondage of birth and death? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who has come to know himself and the soul, is free from the bondage of birth and death. |
| Yaksha | : What is the relation between desire and birth? |
| Yudhishthira | : As the desires, so are the births. If the desires are like that of an animal, then birth as an animal. If the desires are like human, then birth as a human. |
| Yaksha | : What is the reason for sorrow in the world? |
| Yudhishthira | : Greed, selfishness, and fear are the causes of the sorrow of the world. |
| Yaksha | : Then why did God create sorrow? |
| Yudhishthira | : God created the world, and man created sorrow and happiness by his thoughts and deeds. |
| Yaksh | : Is there a God? Who is that? Is she a woman or a man? |
| Yudhishthira | : No action is without cause. This world is the proof of the existence of that cause. That's why you are there, that great reason has been called God in spirituality. It is neither a woman nor a man. |
| Yaksha | : What is the form of God? |
| Yudhishthira | : God is formless one who expresses himself in all forms. |
| Yaksha | : What does formless (God) Himself do? |
| Yudhishthira | : That God creates, maintains and destroys the world |
| Yaksha | : If God created the world, then who created God? |
| Yudhishthira | : He is the unborn nectar and without cause. |
| Yaksha | : What is fate / luck? |
| Yudhishthira | : Every action, every work has a result. The result can be good, it can also be bad. This result is luck. Today effort is tomorrow's luck. |

| Yaksha | : What is the secret of happiness and peace? |
| :--: | :--: |
| Yudhishthira | : Truth, virtue, love and forgiveness are the causes of happiness. Renunciation of untruth, incest, hatred, and anger is the way to peace. |
| Yaksha | : How is it possible to control the mind? |
| Yudhishthira | : Desires create agitation in the mind. Victory over desires is victory over mind. |
| Yaksha | : What is true love? |
| Yudhishthira | : To see oneself in all is true love. True love is to see oneself as omnipresent. True love is to see oneself as one with all. |
| Yaksha | : Then why does man not love everyone? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who cannot see himself in all cannot love all. |
| Yaksha | : What is attachment? |
| Yudhishthira | : In love, there is demand, expectation, possession, and attachment. |
| Yaksha | : What is intoxication? |
| Yudhishthira | : Attachment. |
| Yaksha | : What is liberation? |
| Yudhishthira | : Liberation is only non-attachment. |
| Yaksha | : Who is intelligent? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who has a conscience. |
| Yaksha | : Who is the thief? |
| Yudhishthira | : Attractions of the senses, those who take away the senses are thieves. |
| Yaksha | : What is hell? |
| Yudhishthira | : The slavery of the senses is hell. |
| Yaksha | : Who is asleep even while awake? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who does not know the soul is asleep even when he is awake. |
| Yaksha | : What is temporary like water lying in a lotus leaf? |
| Yudhishthira | : Youth, wealth, and life. |

| Yaksha | : What is the cause of misfortune? |
| :--: | :--: |
| Yudhishthira | : Pride and ego. |
| Yaksha | : What is the cause of good luck? |
| Yudhishthira | : Satsang and friendship towards all. |
| Yaksha | : Who can destroy all sorrows? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who is ready to give up everything. |
| Yaksha | : Who tortures till death? |
| Yudhishthira | : A crime committed in secret. |
| Yaksha | : Which enemy is unconquerable? |
| Yudhishthira | : Anger is the most invincible enemy. |
| Yaksha | : Who conquers the world? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who has truth and faith. |
| Yaksha | : How is it possible to get rid of fear? |
| Yudhishthira | : With dispassion. |
| Yaksha | : Who is free? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who is beyond ignorance. |
| Yaksha | : What is ignorance? |
| Yudhishthira | : Ignorance is the absence of self-knowledge. |
| Yaksha | : What is said to be grief? |
| Yudhishthira | : Lack of knowledge (of truth) leads to grief. |
| Yaksha | : Who is free from sorrows? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who never gets angry. |
| Yaksha | : What is that which exists or may not exist? |
| Yudhishthira | : Maya. |
| Yaksha | : What is Maya? |
| Yudhishthira | : The mortal world with name and form. |
| Yaksha | : What is the ultimate truth? |
| Yudhishthira | : Brahma |
| Yaksha | : By whose command does the sun rise? |
| Yudhishthira | : By the command of the Supreme Soul (Brahm). |
| Yaksha | : On what does one's being a Brahmin depend? On his birth or on his modesty? |

Yudhishthira : Brahminhood is not attained because of clan or education. Brahmanism is dependent on modesty and nature. One who does not have modesty cannot be a Brahmin. One who has bad addictions, no matter how educated he may be, is not a Brahmin.
Yaksha : Who supports man?
Yudhishthira : Patience is the companion of man.
Yaksha : What is called stability?
Yudhishthira : Firmness in one's religion is stability.
Yaksha : What is patience?
Yudhishthira : Controlling your senses is patience.
Yaksha : What is the real meaning of charity?
Yudhishthira : Charity consists in safeguarding all creatures.
Yaksha : Which scripture is studied by which a man becomes intelligent?
Yudhishthira : There is no such scripture. Man becomes wise only by the company of great people.
Yaksha : What is heavier than the earth?
Yudhishthira : The mother who holds the child in the womb is heavier than the land.
Yaksha : Who is higher than the sky?
Yudhishthira : Father.
Yaksha : Who is faster than the wind?
Yudhishthira : Mind.
Yaksha : What is lesser than grass?
Yudhishthira : Anxiety.
Yaksha : Who is the companion of a person travelling abroad?
Yudhishthira : Vidya (knowledge).
Yaksha : Who is the companion of the one who lives in the house?
Yudhishthira : Wife.
Yaksha : Who is the friend of a dying old man?

| Yudhishthira | : Donation, because it walks with the living being alone after death. |
| :--: | :--: |
| Yaksha | : Which is the largest of the pots? |
| Yudhishthira | : Land is the biggest vessel in which everything can be contained. |
| Yaksha | : What is happiness? |
| Yudhishthira | : Happiness is that which is based on modesty and honesty. |
| Yaksha | : What should a person leave behind to become dear to everyone? |
| Yudhishthira | : When egoism is released, a person becomes all-loving. |
| Yaksha | : When happens when something is lost? |
| Yudhishthira | : There is sorrow. |
| Yaksha | : By losing what does a man become rich? |
| Yudhishthira | : Greed. |
| Yaksha | : What is the biggest wonder in the world? |
| Yudhishthira | : Seeing how many creatures die in front of their eyes every day, human beings still dream of immortality. That's a great surprise. |
| Yaksha | : Who is blissful or happy? |
| Yudhishthira | : One who is able to be content with limited resources while living in the midst of his family is really happy. |
| Yaksha | : What is the surprise? |
| Yudhishthira | : Day after day countless creatures depart from this world to Yamaloka, yet those that remain behind believe themselves to be immortal is the biggest surprise, |
| Yaksha | : Who is condemned to everlasting hell? |
| Yudhishthira | : A person who invites a Brahmin to offer gifts but thereafter tells him that he has nothing to offer is condemned to everlasting hell. |
| Yaksha | : What is the grace of God? |
| Yudhishthira | : Grace of God is the fruit of our offerings to him. |

| Yaksha | : Who owns all kinds of wealth? |
| :--: | :--: |
| Yudhishthira | : A person who rates both the pleasant and the unpleasant, joy and sorrow, the past and the future as equal is wealthy. |
| Yaksha | : Which is the right way to live life? |
| Yudhishthira | : There is no well-established logic to determine the real way of life, Shrutis (Shastras and other sources) also talk of different types, there is no such sage/thinker whose words can be called proof. The right way to live life is with good character, perform his duties, be sensitive to others, devote himself for the interests of the society, etc. |
|  | In this way, the Yaksha asked Yudhishthira many questions and Yudhishthira gave proper answers to all those questions. |
|  | Pleased with this, the Yaksha said - "O Yudhishthira! You are the knower of all the meanings of religion. I am satisfied with your answers. So, I give life to one of your brothers. Tell which brother of yours should I bring alive? |
| Yudhishthira said : You bring Nakula back to life. |  |
| Yaksha | : Why didn't you ask for the life of Mahabali Bhima or Trilok victorious Arjuna, instead of asking for Nakula to be alive? |
| Yudhishthira | : "O Yaksha! Dharmatma Pandu had two queens - Kunti and Madri. Kunti was the mother of Me, Bhima, and Arjuna and Madri is the mother of Nakula and Sahadeva. Therefore, while I am Kunti's son and I am alive, Mother Madri's one son Nakula must also survive. That's why I have asked for Nakul's life donation." |
| Hearing the words of Yudhishthira, the Yaksha was pleased and said- "O Vatsa! I am very pleased with your thoughts, so I bring life of all your brothers. In fact, I am your father Dharma and came here to test you." |  |

After saying this, all the Pandavas got up as if they had woken up from a deep sleep. Yudhishthira touched the feet of his father Dharma and took his blessings.

Then Dharma said- "I am very pleased with you, so ask me for a boon as per your wish." Yudhishthira said - Our twelve years of exile is coming to an end and now we are going to go into exile for one year.

Therefore, you give this boon that no one can recognize us in that unknown abode. At the same time, give me this boon that my attitude should always be in you (religion). Dharma disappeared from there by giving. Yudhishthira both his desired boons.

# Moral of the story of Yudhishthira and the Yaksha from the Mahabharata: 

- From this story we learn that virtue, wisdom, and righteous conduct are important for navigating life's challenges.
- The story also emphasizes the value of reason, patience, and devotion to God.
- The story displays that success can only be achieved through truth, justice, and morality.
- The story teaches that one should be fair and equal with everyone and also follow Dharma in life.
