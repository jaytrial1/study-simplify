This section describes the three common states of matter - solid, liquid, and gas - and their properties based on particle arrangement and movement.

# States of Matter

Observe the different types of matter around you. What are their different states? We can see that matter around us exists in three distinct states: solid, liquid, and gas. These different states arise because of variations in the characteristics of the particles of matter.

Let's examine the properties of these three states in detail.

## The Solid State

### Activity: Properties of Solids

*   Collect the following articles: a pen, a book, a needle, and a piece of wooden stick.
*   Sketch the shape of these articles in your notebook by tracing around them with a pencil.
*   Do all these items have a definite shape, distinct boundaries, and a fixed volume?
*   What happens if they are hammered, pulled, or dropped?
*   Are these capable of diffusing into each other?
*   Try compressing them by applying force. Can you compress them?

> **Answer:**
> *   Yes, all these items (pen, book, needle, wooden stick) have a definite shape, distinct boundaries, and a fixed volume.
> *   If hammered or dropped, they may break, but their shape generally remains fixed unless the force is very strong. Pulling them might cause them to break (needle, stick) or change shape slightly if flexible (pen casing), but they resist changes in shape.
> *   Solids are generally not capable of diffusing into each other significantly under normal conditions.
> *   No, you are generally not able to compress them noticeably by applying force.

All the items above are examples of solids. We observe that they possess a definite shape, distinct boundaries, and fixed volumes, meaning they have negligible compressibility. Solids tend to maintain their shape when subjected to external force. While solids can break under force, changing their shape is difficult, which is why they are considered *rigid*.

Consider the following:
(a) What about a rubber band? Can it change its shape on stretching? Is it a solid?
(b) What about sugar and salt? When kept in different jars, they take the shape of the jar. Are they solid?
(c) What about a sponge? It is a solid, yet we can compress it. Why?

> **Answer:**
> (a) A rubber band changes shape when stretched and returns to its original shape when the force is removed. If excessive force is applied, it breaks. Despite changing shape under force, it is considered a solid because it has distinct boundaries and fixed volume, and it regains its shape when the force is removed.
> (b) Sugar and salt crystals are solids. The shape of each individual crystal of sugar or salt is fixed, regardless of the container it is in (hand, plate, or jar). The bulk substance appears to take the shape of the jar because it is made up of many small solid crystals.
> (c) A sponge is a solid. It can be compressed because it has many minute holes filled with air. When you press the sponge, the air is expelled from these holes, allowing the sponge itself (the solid material) to be compressed into a smaller volume temporarily.

These examples confirm that solids:
*   Have a definite shape (though some, like rubber bands, can change shape under force and regain it).
*   Have distinct boundaries.
*   Have fixed volumes.
*   Are generally rigid and have negligible compressibility.

## The Liquid State

### Activity: Properties of Liquids

Collect the following:
(a) water, cooking oil, milk, juice, a cold drink.
(b) containers of different shapes. Mark a 50 mL level on these containers using a measuring cylinder.
What will happen if these liquids are spilt on the floor?
Measure 50 mL of any one liquid and transfer it into different containers one by one. Does the volume remain the same?
Does the shape of the liquid remain the same?
When you pour the liquid from one container into another, does it flow easily?

> **Answer:**
> *   If spilt on the floor, these liquids will spread out and take the shape of the floor surface, not retaining a fixed shape.
> *   Yes, the volume of the liquid (50 mL) remains the same regardless of the container shape.
> *   No, the shape of the liquid does not remain the same; it takes the shape of the container it is in.
> *   Yes, when poured from one container to another, liquids flow easily.

We observe that liquids do not have a fixed shape but possess a fixed volume. They conform to the shape of the container they are placed in. Liquids flow and change shape, which means they are not rigid but are considered *fluid*.

Recall activities 1.4 and 1.5, which showed that solids and liquids can diffuse into liquids. Gases from the atmosphere can also diffuse and dissolve in water. Essential gases like oxygen and carbon dioxide are dissolved in water, which is vital for the survival of aquatic animals and plants.

Aquatic animals can breathe underwater because of the dissolved oxygen. Therefore, we can conclude that solids, liquids, and gases are all capable of diffusing into liquids. The rate of diffusion is higher for liquids compared to solids. This is because, in the liquid state, particles move more freely and have greater space between them than particles in the solid state.

## The Gaseous State

Have you seen a balloon seller filling many balloons from a single gas cylinder? Ask him how many balloons he can fill and what gas is in the cylinder.

We have observed that gases are significantly more compressible than solids and liquids. Compressed gas is used in everyday life, such as the liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) cylinders for cooking at home and oxygen cylinders in hospitals. Compressed natural gas (CNG) is used as vehicle fuel. The high compressibility of gases allows large volumes of gas to be stored in small cylinders for easy transport.

We can detect the smell of food cooking in the kitchen from a distance without entering the room. How does this smell reach us? The particles of the food aroma mix rapidly with air particles, spreading out from the kitchen and reaching our nostrils, even from afar. The smell of hot cooked food reaches us quickly; compare this speed to the diffusion rate of solids and liquids. Due to the high speed of particles and the large spaces between them, gases diffuse very quickly into other gases.

In the gaseous state, particles move randomly at high speeds. This random movement causes particles to collide with each other and with the walls of the container. The pressure exerted by a gas is a result of the force exerted by these gas particles per unit area on the container walls.

### Questions

1.  The mass per unit volume of a substance is called density (density = mass/volume). Arrange the following in order of increasing density - air, exhaust from chimneys, honey, water, chalk, cotton and iron.

> **Answer:** Density generally increases as the particles get closer together and the forces of attraction increase. Gases are less dense than liquids, and liquids are generally less dense than solids.
> Increasing order of density: air < exhaust from chimneys < cotton < water < honey < chalk < iron.
> (Note: Exhaust from chimneys is hot air/gas with particles, likely slightly denser than clean air but still much less dense than solids/liquids. Cotton is a solid but very porous, making its bulk density low. Honey is a liquid denser than water. Chalk and iron are solids, with iron being much denser than chalk).

2.  (a) Tabulate the differences in the characteristics of states of matter.
    (b) Comment upon the following: rigidity, compressibility, fluidity, filling a gas container, shape, kinetic energy and density.

> **Answer:**
> (a) Tabulated differences:
>
> | Characteristic    | Solid                     | Liquid                       | Gas                         |
> | :---------------- | :------------------------ | :--------------------------- | :-------------------------- |
> | Shape             | Definite shape            | No definite shape (takes container shape) | No definite shape (takes container shape) |
> | Volume            | Definite volume           | Definite volume              | No definite volume (fills container) |
> | Intermolecular Force | Very strong               | Moderate                     | Very weak                   |
> | Intermolecular Space | Very little               | Moderate                     | Very large                  |
> | Compressibility   | Negligible                | Slight                       | High                        |
> | Rigidity/Fluidity | Rigid                     | Fluid                        | Fluid                       |
> | Particle Movement | Vibrate about fixed positions | Move and slide past each other | Move randomly and rapidly   |

> (b) Comments:
> *   **Rigidity:** The property of a substance to resist change in its shape. Solids are rigid.
> *   **Compressibility:** The ability of a substance to be squeezed into a smaller volume. Gases are highly compressible, liquids are slightly compressible, and solids are negligibly compressible.
> *   **Fluidity:** The ability of a substance to flow. Liquids and gases are fluids; solids are not.
> *   **Filling a gas container:** Gases fill the entire volume of the container they are placed in because their particles move freely and occupy all available space.
> *   **Shape:** Solids have a fixed shape. Liquids take the shape of their container. Gases take the shape of their container.
> *   **Kinetic energy:** The energy of motion possessed by particles. Kinetic energy is lowest in solids (vibration), higher in liquids (sliding movement), and highest in gases (rapid random movement).
> *   **Density:** Mass per unit volume. Solids generally have the highest density, liquids have lower density than solids, and gases have the lowest density.

3.  Give reasons
    (a) A gas fills completely the vessel in which it is kept.
    (b) A gas exerts pressure on the walls of the container.
    (c) A wooden table should be called a solid.
    (d) We can easily move our hand in air but to do the same through a solid block of wood we need a karate expert.

> **Answer:**
> (a) A gas fills completely the vessel in which it is kept because the particles in a gas have very high kinetic energy and negligible forces of attraction between them, allowing them to move freely in all directions and occupy the entire available space.
> (b) A gas exerts pressure on the walls of the container because the particles of the gas are in constant, random motion and collide with the walls of the container, exerting a force per unit area.
> (c) A wooden table should be called a solid because it has a definite shape, distinct boundaries, and a fixed volume. It is rigid and cannot be compressed easily.
> (d) We can easily move our hand in air because air is a gas, and its particles are far apart with weak forces of attraction between them. We can easily overcome these forces and move through the spaces. To move through a solid block of wood, which has particles tightly packed with strong forces of attraction, requires applying a large force to break these bonds, which is why a karate expert is needed to break it.

4.  Liquids generally have lower density as compared to solids. But you must have observed that ice floats on water. Find out why.

> **Answer:** While most substances are denser in their solid state than in their liquid state, water is an exception. Ice (solid water) has a lower density than liquid water. This is because, in ice, water molecules arrange themselves in a crystal structure with open spaces between them, forming a cage-like structure. This causes ice to occupy more volume for the same mass compared to liquid water, resulting in lower density, which is why ice floats on water.
