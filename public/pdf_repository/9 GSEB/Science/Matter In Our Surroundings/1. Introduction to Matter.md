This section introduces the concept of matter and its historical classification by philosophers, contrasting it with modern scientific classification.

# Introduction to Matter

As we observe our surroundings, we encounter a vast array of things varying in shape, size, and texture. Scientists identify all these things as "matter." Matter includes everything from the air we breathe and the food we eat to stones, clouds, stars, plants, animals, and even a small drop of water or a particle of sand. A key characteristic of all these things is that they occupy space and possess mass. This means they have both mass and volume.

From ancient times, humans have sought to understand their environment. Early Indian philosophers categorized matter into five fundamental elements known as the "Panch Tatva": air, earth, fire, sky, and water. They believed that everything, whether living or nonliving, was composed of these five elements. Ancient Greek philosophers developed a similar classification system for matter.

Modern scientists have developed a classification of matter based on two aspects: physical properties and chemical nature.

This chapter will focus on understanding matter through its physical properties. The chemical aspects of matter will be discussed in later chapters.

*   The SI unit of mass is kilogram (kg).
*   The SI unit of volume is cubic metre (m³). The common unit for measuring volume is litre (L), where 1 L = 1 dm³, 1 L = 1000 mL, and 1 mL = 1 cm³.
