This section delves into the key properties of the particles that make up matter: having space between them, continuous movement, and attraction to each other.

# Characteristics of Particles of Matter

## Particles of Matter Have Space Between Them

Activities involving dissolving substances like sugar, salt, Dettol, or potassium permanganate in water showed that these substances distribute evenly throughout the water. Similarly, when preparing tea, coffee, or lemonade, the particles of one substance intermingle by fitting into the spaces between the particles of the other substance. This phenomenon indicates that there are significant spaces between the particles of matter.

## Particles of Matter Are Continuously Moving

### Activity: Incense Stick Diffusion

*   Place an unlit incense stick in a corner of your classroom. How close must you get to smell it?
*   Now, light the incense stick. What happens? Can you smell it from a distance?
*   Record your observations.

> **Answer:** When the incense stick is unlit, you have to go very close to smell it. When it is lit, the smell spreads quickly through the air, allowing you to smell it from a distance.

### Activity: Ink and Honey Diffusion

*   Take two glasses or beakers filled with water.
*   Carefully add a drop of blue or red ink along the side of the first beaker.
*   In the second beaker, carefully add a drop of honey in the same way.
*   Leave both undisturbed in your home or classroom.
*   Record your observations.
*   What do you observe immediately after adding the ink drop?
*   What do you observe immediately after adding a drop of honey?
*   How many hours or days does it take for the colour of ink to spread evenly throughout the water?

> **Answer:**
> *   Immediately after adding ink, you observe the ink starting to spread and mix with the water relatively quickly.
> *   Immediately after adding honey, you observe the honey settling at the bottom or spreading very slowly compared to the ink.
> *   It takes a few hours for ink to spread evenly throughout the water, depending on temperature and disturbance. Honey takes much longer, potentially days, to spread evenly without stirring.

### Activity: Diffusion in Hot and Cold Water

*   Drop a crystal of copper sulphate or potassium permanganate into a glass of hot water and another crystal into a glass of cold water. Do not stir. Allow the crystals to settle at the bottom.
*   What do you observe just above the solid crystal in the glass?
*   What happens as time passes?
*   What does this suggest about the particles of solid and liquid?
*   Does the rate of mixing change with temperature? Why and how?

> **Answer:**
> *   Just above the solid crystal, you observe a coloured streak or plume rising as the substance starts dissolving and spreading.
> *   As time passes, the colour spreads throughout the water in both glasses.
> *   This suggests that particles of both the solid (copper sulphate/potassium permanganate) and the liquid (water) are in motion and can intermix.
> *   Yes, the rate of mixing changes with temperature. The colour spreads faster in hot water than in cold water. This is because increasing the temperature increases the kinetic energy of the particles, causing them to move faster and diffuse more rapidly.

From these three activities (1.3, 1.4, and 1.5), we can conclude the following:

Particles of matter are constantly in motion; they possess *kinetic energy*. As temperature increases, the particles move faster, indicating that their kinetic energy also increases with rising temperature.

In these activities, we observe that particles of different types of matter mix with each other on their own. This happens because they move into the spaces between the particles. This spontaneous intermixing of particles of different matter types is called *diffusion*. We also notice that heating increases the rate of diffusion. This occurs because higher temperatures lead to faster particle movement.

## Particles of Matter Attract Each Other

### Activity: Forming Human Chains

Play this game in a field: form four groups and create human chains as described:
*   The first group should hold each other from the back and lock arms like Idu-Mishmi dancers (Fig. 1.3).
*   The second group should hold hands to form a human chain.
*   The third group should form a chain by touching each other with only their fingertips.
*   Now, the fourth group of students should run around and try to break the three human chains one by one into as many small groups as possible.
*   Which group was the easiest to break? Why?

> **Answer:** The group holding on by fingertips was the easiest to break because the force of connection (attraction) between the "particles" (students) was the weakest.

*   If we consider each student as a particle of matter, then in which group the particles held each other with the maximum force?

> **Answer:** The group holding each other from the back with locked arms represented the particles held together with the maximum force.

### Activity: Breaking Different Materials

Take an iron nail, a piece of chalk, and a rubber band.
Try breaking them by hammering, cutting, or stretching.
In which of the above three substances do you think the particles are held together with greater force?

> **Answer:** The particles are held together with greater force in the iron nail, making it the most difficult to break. The chalk is easier to break, and the rubber band can be stretched significantly before breaking, indicating weaker forces compared to iron.

### Activity: Cutting Water

Take some water in a container. Try cutting the surface of the water with your fingers.
Were you able to cut the surface of water?
What could be the reason behind the surface of water remaining together?

> **Answer:** You were not able to cut the surface of water cleanly with your fingers; it reforms after you pass through. The reason is that the particles of water are held together by forces of attraction, which keep the liquid surface intact.

These three activities (1.6, 1.7, and 1.8) suggest that there is a force acting between the particles of matter. This force is responsible for keeping the particles together. The strength of this force of attraction varies depending on the type of matter.

### Questions

1.  Which of the following are matter?
    Chair, air, love, smell, hate, almonds, thought, cold, lemon water, smell of perfume.

> **Answer:** The following are matter: Chair, air, almonds, lemon water. These occupy space and have mass. Love, smell, hate, thought, and cold are not considered matter; they are feelings, sensations, or concepts. The *smell* of perfume is the sensation caused by the particles of perfume in the air, so the perfume particles themselves are matter, but the sensation of smell is not.

2.  Give reasons for the following observation:
    The smell of hot sizzling food reaches you several metres away, but to get the smell from cold food you have to go close.

> **Answer:** Particles of matter are continuously moving, and their movement (kinetic energy) increases with temperature. Hot sizzling food releases particles (aroma) into the air at a higher temperature. These particles have higher kinetic energy and diffuse faster and further through the air than the particles released from cold food, which have lower kinetic energy and diffuse more slowly.

3.  A diver is able to cut through water in a swimming pool. Which property of matter does this observation show?

> **Answer:** This observation shows that the particles of water have spaces between them and that the forces of attraction between water particles are not very strong, allowing the diver to overcome them and move through the water.

4.  What are the characteristics of the particles of matter?

> **Answer:** The characteristics of the particles of matter are:
> *   They are very small.
> *   They have space between them.
> *   They are continuously moving.
> *   They attract each other.
