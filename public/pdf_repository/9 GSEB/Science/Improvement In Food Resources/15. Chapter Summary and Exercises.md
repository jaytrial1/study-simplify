This section provides a summary of the key concepts covered in the chapter and presents exercises to test understanding.

# What you have learnt

*   Crops need several essential nutrients: macro-nutrients (large quantities) and micro-nutrients (small quantities).
*   Manure and fertilizers supply nutrients to crops.
*   Organic farming minimizes chemical use, maximizing organic inputs, recycled waste, bio-agents, and healthy cropping systems.
*   Mixed farming combines crop production with livestock etc. on the same farm.
*   Mixed cropping is growing two or more crops simultaneously on the same land.
*   Intercropping is growing two or more crops in definite row patterns simultaneously.
*   Crop rotation is growing different crops in a planned sequence on the same land.
*   Varietal improvement aims for higher yield, quality, biotic/abiotic resistance, shorter maturity, wider adaptability, and desirable agronomic traits.
*   Animal husbandry is the scientific management of livestock, including shelter, feeding, breeding, and disease control.
*   Poultry farming raises domestic fowls for eggs and meat (broilers).
*   Poultry production is enhanced by cross-breeding Indian and exotic breeds.
*   Fish are obtained from marine and inland resources.
*   Fish production can be increased by culturing fish in marine (mariculture) and inland (aquaculture) ecosystems.
*   Marine fish capture uses nets, aided by echo-sounders and satellites.
*   Composite fish culture is common for intensive fish farming.
*   Beekeeping yields honey and wax.

# Exercises

1. Explain any one method of crop production which ensures high yield.

> **Answer:** One method is *inter-cropping*. By growing two or more crops with different nutrient needs in a specific row pattern, this method ensures maximum utilisation of nutrients from the soil, prevents the spread of pests and diseases throughout a single crop type, and can lead to higher overall yield from the same piece of land compared to monoculture.

2. Why are manure and fertilizers used in fields?

> **Answer:** Manure and fertilizers are used in fields to enrich the soil with essential nutrients that crops need for healthy growth and high yields. Manure also improves soil structure and water retention, while fertilizers provide concentrated nutrients quickly.

3. What are the advantages of inter-cropping and crop rotation?

> **Answer:** Advantages of *inter-cropping* include increased total yield from the land, reduced risk of complete crop failure, better utilisation of nutrients, and reduced spread of pests and diseases. Advantages of *crop rotation* include improving soil fertility (especially if legumes are included), controlling pests and diseases by breaking their life cycles, reducing weed problems, and allowing farmers to grow multiple crops per year for potentially higher income.

4. What is genetic manipulation? How is it useful in agricultural practices?

> **Answer:** Genetic manipulation is a method of improving crop varieties by introducing a gene that provides a desired characteristic, resulting in genetically modified crops. It is useful in agriculture because it can create varieties with enhanced traits like increased yield, resistance to diseases or pests, tolerance to environmental stresses (like drought or salinity), or improved nutritional quality, which can lead to better and more reliable crop production.

5. How do storage grain losses occur?

> **Answer:** Storage grain losses occur due to biotic factors such as insects, rodents, fungi, mites, and bacteria, which consume or spoil the grains. They also occur due to abiotic factors like inappropriate moisture levels and temperature, which can promote the growth of fungi and insects or lead to degradation of the grain quality.

6. How do good animal husbandry practices benefit farmers?

> **Answer:** Good animal husbandry practices benefit farmers by ensuring the health and well-being of livestock, which leads to increased productivity (e.g., higher milk yield, faster growth for meat, more eggs). Proper management of feeding, breeding, and disease control reduces losses due to illness or death, improves the quality of animal products, and can lead to increased income and sustained livelihood for the farmer.

7. What are the benefits of cattle farming?

> **Answer:** The benefits of cattle farming include the production of milk (from milch animals) which is a source of nutrition and income, and obtaining draught labour (from draught animals) for agricultural activities like tilling, irrigation, and carting, which are essential for crop production.

8. For increasing production, what is common in poultry, fisheries and bee-keeping?

> **Answer:** A common practice for increasing production in poultry, fisheries, and bee-keeping is the use of improved varieties or breeds. This is achieved through selecting or breeding varieties that have desirable traits like higher yield (more eggs, meat, fish, honey), better growth rates, or increased resistance to diseases.

9. How do you differentiate between capture fishing, mariculture and aquaculture?

> **Answer:** *Capture fishing* is obtaining fish by catching them from natural water bodies like rivers, lakes, or the sea. *Mariculture* is the culture or farming of marine organisms (like fish, shellfish, seaweed) specifically in seawater. *Aquaculture* is a broader term that refers to the farming of aquatic organisms in both freshwater and brackish water environments, including ponds, tanks, and enclosures. Mariculture is a type of aquaculture specifically in marine environments.
