This section discusses the importance of irrigation in Indian agriculture and various methods used to supply water to crops.

# Irrigation

Most Indian agriculture relies on rain (*rain-fed*), making it dependent on timely and sufficient monsoons. Poor monsoons cause crop failure. Ensuring water supply at the right growth stages can significantly increase yields. Therefore, expanding irrigation coverage is important.

Droughts, caused by scarce or irregular rain, threaten rain-fed areas. Light soils, with low water retention, are particularly vulnerable. Drought-tolerant crop varieties have been developed.

India's diverse water resources and climate necessitate various irrigation systems:
*   **Wells:**
    *   *Dug wells:* Collect water from water-bearing layers.
    *   *Tube wells:* Tap water from deeper layers. Water is pumped for irrigation.
*   **Canals:** Extensive systems receiving water from reservoirs or rivers. Main canals divide into branches and distributaries reaching fields.
*   **River Lift Systems:** Water is directly drawn from rivers in areas where canal flow is insufficient or irregular, supplementing irrigation near rivers.
*   **Tanks:** Small reservoirs storing run-off from small catchment areas.

Recent initiatives to increase water availability include *rainwater harvesting* and *watershed management*. Building small *check-dams* helps increase groundwater levels, stops rainwater run-off, and reduces soil erosion.
