# FISH PRODUCTION

Fish is an affordable source of animal protein, including true fish and shellfish (prawns, molluscs). There are two ways to get fish:
1.  *Capture fishing:* Obtaining fish from natural resources.
2.  *Culture fishery:* Fish farming.

Fish sources can be seawater or freshwater (rivers, ponds). Fishing occurs in both marine and freshwater ecosystems through capture and culture.

## Marine Fisheries

India's marine fishery resources include its coastline and deep seas. Popular marine fish include pomphret, mackerel, tuna, sardines, and Bombay duck. Fish are caught using nets from boats. Yields are improved by using satellites and echo-sounders to locate large fish schools.

Some high-value marine fish and shellfish are farmed in seawater, a practice called *mariculture*. This includes finned fish (mullets, bhetki, pearl spots), shellfish (prawns, mussels, oysters), and seaweed. Oysters are also cultivated for pearls.

## InLAND FISHERIES

Freshwater resources include canals, ponds, reservoirs, and rivers. Brackish water resources (estuaries, lagoons where seawater and freshwater mix) are also important. While capture fishing occurs, yields are generally not high. Most inland fish production comes from *aquaculture* (fish culture).

Fish culture is sometimes combined with rice farming, growing fish in paddy fields. More intensive farming uses *composite fish culture systems* with local and imported species.

In a composite system, 5-6 fish species with different food habits are raised in one pond. This ensures all parts of the pond are utilized for food without competition. Examples: Catlas (surface feeders), Rohus (middle-zone feeders), Mrigals and Common Carps (bottom feeders), Grass Carps (feed on weeds) (Fig. 12.6). This increases total fish yield from the pond.

A challenge in composite fish culture is that many fish breed only during the monsoon. Collecting fish seed from the wild can result in mixed species. Lack of good-quality, pure fish seed is a major problem. This has been addressed by breeding fish in ponds using *hormonal stimulation*, ensuring a supply of pure seed in desired quantities.

## Questions

1. How are fish obtained?

> **Answer:** Fish are obtained in two main ways: capture fishing, which involves catching fish from natural water bodies like rivers, lakes, and the sea; and culture fishery (aquaculture or mariculture), which involves farming fish in controlled environments like ponds, tanks, or cages in marine or freshwater.

2. What are the advantages of composite fish culture?

> **Answer:** The advantages of composite fish culture are that it maximizes the utilisation of food resources available in the pond by stocking multiple fish species with different feeding habits (surface, middle, bottom, weed feeders). This non-competitive feeding allows for efficient use of the pond's ecosystem and leads to a higher total fish yield from a single pond compared to raising just one species.
