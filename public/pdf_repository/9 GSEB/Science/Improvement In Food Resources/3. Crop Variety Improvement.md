This section explains how selecting and breeding different plant varieties can lead to improved crop characteristics and higher yields.

# Crop Variety Improvement

Improving crop yield starts with selecting varieties that can give good yields. Varieties or strains can be improved through breeding for desired characteristics like disease resistance, response to fertilisers, product quality, and high yields.

*Hybridisation* is one way to incorporate desirable characters by crossing genetically dissimilar plants. This can be:
*   *Intervarietal:* between different varieties.
*   *Interspecific:* between two different species of the same genus.
*   *Intergeneric:* between different genera.

Another method is introducing a gene for a desired characteristic, resulting in *genetically modified crops*.

For new varieties to be successful, they must produce high yields under diverse conditions found in different areas. Farmers need access to good quality seeds that are of the same variety and germinate uniformly.

Cultivation practices and yield are influenced by weather, soil quality, and water availability. Varieties that tolerate diverse climatic conditions (like drought or flood) are useful. Varieties tolerant to high soil salinity have also been developed.

Factors considered for variety improvement include:
*   **Higher yield:** Increasing productivity per acre.
*   **Improved quality:** Specific quality traits for different crops (e.g., baking quality in wheat, protein in pulses, oil quality in oilseeds, preserving quality in fruits/vegetables).
*   **Biotic and abiotic resistance:** Developing resistance to biotic stresses (diseases, insects, nematodes) and abiotic stresses (drought, salinity, water logging, heat, cold, frost) to prevent yield loss.
*   **Change in maturity duration:** Shorter durations allow for multiple crop rounds per year, reduce production costs, and enable easier, less loss-prone harvesting through uniform maturity.
*   **Wider adaptability:** Varieties adaptable to different environmental conditions help stabilize production across different areas and climates.
*   **Desirable agronomic characteristics:** Traits like tallness and profuse branching are good for fodder crops, while dwarfness is preferred in cereals to reduce nutrient consumption and improve yield efficiency.

## Questions

1. How do biotic and abiotic factors affect crop production?

> **Answer:** Biotic factors (diseases, insects, nematodes) and abiotic factors (drought, salinity, water logging, heat, cold, frost) are stresses that can significantly reduce crop production by damaging plants, inhibiting growth, or causing crop failure.

2. What are the desirable agronomic characteristics for crop improvements?

> **Answer:** Desirable agronomic characteristics include higher yield, improved quality, resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses, shorter maturity duration, wider adaptability to different conditions, and specific traits like dwarfness in cereals or tallness in fodder crops that improve productivity or efficiency.
