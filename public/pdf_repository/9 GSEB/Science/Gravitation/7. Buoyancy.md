This section explores the phenomenon of buoyancy, explaining the upward force exerted by fluids on immersed objects.

# Buoyancy

Have you noticed feeling lighter when in a swimming pool, or that a bucket of water feels heavier once lifted out of the water? These are experiences related to **buoyancy**.

Consider pushing an empty plastic bottle into a bucket of water. You feel an upward force resisting your push. The deeper you push, the harder it becomes. This indicates that water exerts an upward force on the bottle. This upward force is called **upthrust** or **buoyant force**. It increases as more of the object is immersed until it is fully submerged.

When you release the bottle, it bounces back up to the surface. This is because the upward buoyant force exerted by the water is greater than the downward force of gravity (the bottle's weight).

To keep the bottle fully immersed, you need to apply an additional downward force that balances the difference between the buoyant force and the bottle's weight.

All objects experience a buoyant force when immersed in a fluid. The magnitude of this buoyant force depends on the density of the fluid.
