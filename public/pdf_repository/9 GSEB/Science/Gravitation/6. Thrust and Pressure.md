This section introduces the concepts of thrust as force perpendicular to a surface and pressure as force per unit area, explaining how area affects the impact of a force.

# Thrust and Pressure

Why can camels walk easily in deserts? Why do cutting tools have sharp edges? These questions relate to the concepts of **thrust** and **pressure**.

Imagine pushing a drawing pin into a bulletin board. You apply force on the head of the pin. This force is directed perpendicular to the board's surface. This force is concentrated on the very small area of the pin's tip.

Now, consider standing on loose sand versus lying down on it. When standing, your weight (the force) is distributed over the small area of your feet, causing you to sink deeper. When lying down, the same weight is distributed over the much larger area of your body in contact with the sand, so you don't sink as deep.

In these examples, the force acting perpendicular to a surface is called **thrust**. The magnitude of the thrust is the same in both standing and lying-down cases (your weight). However, the *effect* of this thrust is different because it acts on different areas.

The effect of thrust depends on the area on which it acts. The thrust acting on a unit area is defined as **pressure**.

$$
\text { Pressure }=\frac{\text { thrust }}{\text { area }}
$$

The SI unit of thrust (force) is newton (N), and the SI unit of area is square meter ($\mathrm{m}^2$). Therefore, the SI unit of pressure is $\mathrm{N/m}^2$ or $\mathrm{N} \mathrm{m}^{-2}$. This unit is also called **pascal (Pa)**, in honor of Blaise Pascal.

### Example: Pressure from a Wooden Block

A wooden block with mass 5 kg and dimensions $40 \mathrm{~cm} \times 20 \mathrm{~cm} \times 10 \mathrm{~cm}$ is placed on a tabletop. Calculate the pressure exerted when it lies on sides of dimensions:
(a) $20 \mathrm{~cm} \times 10 \mathrm{~cm}$
(b) $40 \mathrm{~cm} \times 20 \mathrm{~cm}$

Given: Mass $m = 5 \mathrm{~kg}$. Dimensions = $40 \mathrm{~cm} \times 20 \mathrm{~cm} \times 10 \mathrm{~cm}$.
The thrust exerted by the block is its weight:
Thrust $F = m \times g = 5 \mathrm{~kg} \times 9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2} = 49 \mathrm{~N}$.

(a) Area = $20 \mathrm{~cm} \times 10 \mathrm{~cm} = 200 \mathrm{~cm}^2 = 0.02 \mathrm{~m}^2$.
Pressure = $\frac{\text { thrust }}{\text { area }} = \frac{49 \mathrm{~N}}{0.02 \mathrm{~m}^2} = 2450 \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{-2}$.

(b) Area = $40 \mathrm{~cm} \times 20 \mathrm{~cm} = 800 \mathrm{~cm}^2 = 0.08 \mathrm{~m}^2$.
Pressure = $\frac{\text { thrust }}{\text { area }} = \frac{49 \mathrm{~N}}{0.08 \mathrm{~m}^2} = 612.5 \mathrm{~N} \mathrm{~m}^{-2}$.

This example shows that the same force (thrust) produces a larger pressure when acting on a smaller area and a smaller pressure when acting on a larger area. This principle explains why sharp tools cut well (force concentrated on a small area) and why buildings have wide foundations (weight distributed over a large area).

### Pressure in Fluids

Liquids and gases are collectively called **fluids**. Just like solids, fluids have weight and exert pressure. Fluids exert pressure on the base and the walls of the container they are in. A key property of fluids is that pressure applied to a confined fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.
