This section explains how the density of an object relative to the density of a fluid determines whether the object will float or sink.

# Why Objects Float or Sink

Whether an object floats or sinks in a fluid depends on the balance between the downward gravitational force (weight) and the upward buoyant force.

Consider placing an iron nail on the surface of water. The nail sinks. The Earth's gravitational force pulls the nail down. The water exerts an upward buoyant force. However, the downward force on the nail is greater than the upthrust from the water, so it sinks.

Now, compare a piece of cork and an iron nail of equal mass placed on water. The cork floats, while the nail sinks. This difference in behavior is due to their **densities**.

Density is defined as mass per unit volume.

The density of cork is less than the density of water. When the cork is placed on water, the buoyant force exerted by the water on the cork is greater than the weight of the cork, causing it to float.

The density of an iron nail is greater than the density of water. When the iron nail is placed on water, the buoyant force exerted by the water on the nail is less than the weight of the nail, causing it to sink.

Therefore:
*   Objects with a density **less than** the density of the fluid will **float**.
*   Objects with a density **greater than** the density of the fluid will **sink**.

This principle also explains why a ship made of iron and steel floats (its overall density, including the air inside, is less than water) while a solid piece of iron sinks.

### Questions

1. Why is it difficult to hold a school bag having a strap made of a thin and strong string?
> **Answer:** A thin strap has a small surface area. When the weight of the bag (thrust) is distributed over this small area, it exerts a large pressure on your shoulder, making it uncomfortable and feel difficult to hold. A wider strap distributes the same weight over a larger area, reducing the pressure.

2. What do you mean by buoyancy?
> **Answer:** Buoyancy is the upward force exerted by a fluid (liquid or gas) on an object that is immersed in it, either partially or fully.

3. Why does an object float or sink when placed on the surface of water?
> **Answer:** An object floats if the upward buoyant force exerted by the water is equal to or greater than the object's weight (the downward gravitational force). It sinks if its weight is greater than the maximum buoyant force the water can exert (which depends on the volume of water displaced). This is related to the object's density compared to the fluid's density: objects less dense than the fluid float, while objects denser than the fluid sink.
