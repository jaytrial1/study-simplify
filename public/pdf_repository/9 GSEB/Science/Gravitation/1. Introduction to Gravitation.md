This section introduces the concept of gravitation as the force responsible for the motion of objects on Earth and celestial bodies like the Moon and planets.

# Introduction to Gravitation

We have previously explored the motion of objects and the role of force in causing changes in speed or direction. It is observed that objects dropped from a height fall towards the Earth. Similarly, planets orbit the Sun, and the Moon orbits the Earth. These movements are all attributed to a fundamental force called the **gravitational force**.

Isaac Newton recognized that the same force causing an apple to fall is also responsible for keeping the Moon in orbit around the Earth. He reasoned that the Moon is constantly "falling" towards the Earth, but its tangential motion prevents it from hitting the surface.

Consider the motion of an object swung in a circle on a string. The force pulling the object towards the center is called the **centripetal force**. Without this force, the object would move off in a straight line, tangent to the circle. The Earth's gravitational attraction provides the centripetal force that keeps the Moon in its orbit.

An apple is attracted to the Earth, but does the apple also attract the Earth? According to Newton's third law of motion, it does. However, the Earth's mass is vastly larger than the apple's mass. By Newton's second law ($F=ma$), for the same force, the acceleration is inversely proportional to mass. Thus, the Earth's acceleration towards the apple is negligible and unobservable. This same logic explains why the Earth does not visibly move towards the Moon.

Newton concluded that this force of attraction exists between *all* objects in the universe, not just Earth and objects near it. This universal force of attraction is called the gravitational force.
