# Exercises and Questions

1.  How does the force of gravitation between two objects change when the distance between them is reduced to half?
    > **Answer:** According to the universal law of gravitation, $F \propto \frac{1}{d^2}$. If the distance ($d$) is reduced to half ($d/2$), the new force ($F'$) is proportional to $\frac{1}{(d/2)^2} = \frac{1}{d^2/4} = \frac{4}{d^2}$. So, the new force $F'$ is 4 times the original force $F$. The force of gravitation increases by a factor of 4.

2.  Gravitational force acts on all objects in proportion to their masses. Why then, a heavy object does not fall faster than a light object?
    > **Answer:** While the gravitational *force* is proportional to mass ($F = Gm M/d^2$), the *acceleration* produced by this force is given by Newton's second law ($a = F/m$). Substituting the gravitational force, we get $a = (Gm M/d^2) / m = GM/d^2$. This shows that the acceleration due to gravity ($g = GM/d^2$) is independent of the object's mass ($m$). Therefore, in the absence of air resistance, all objects fall with the same acceleration regardless of how heavy or light they are.

3.  What is the magnitude of the gravitational force between the earth and a 1 kg object on its surface? (Mass of the earth is $6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$ and radius of the earth is $6.4 \times 10^{6} \mathrm{~m}$.)
    > **Answer:** This is the weight of the object. Using $F = G \frac{M \times m}{R^2}$:
    $F = (6.67 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}) \times \frac{(6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}) \times (1 \mathrm{~kg})}{(6.4 \times 10^6 \mathrm{~m})^2}$
    $F \approx 9.8 \mathrm{~N}$.
    The magnitude of the gravitational force is approximately 9.8 N.

4.  The earth and the moon are attracted to each other by gravitational force. Does the earth attract the moon with a force that is greater or smaller or the same as the force with which the moon attracts the earth? Why?
    > **Answer:** The Earth attracts the Moon with a force that is *the same* in magnitude as the force with which the Moon attracts the Earth. This is according to Newton's third law of motion, which states that for every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. The gravitational force is a mutual force of attraction between any two objects.

5.  If the moon attracts the earth, why does the earth not move towards the moon?
    > **Answer:** The Earth *does* move towards the Moon, but its acceleration is much smaller than the Moon's acceleration towards the Earth. According to Newton's second law ($a=F/m$), for the same force, the acceleration is inversely proportional to mass. The mass of the Earth is much greater than the mass of the Moon. Therefore, the acceleration of the Earth towards the Moon is much smaller and less noticeable than the Moon's acceleration towards the Earth. Both bodies actually orbit around their common center of mass.

6.  What happens to the force between two objects, if
    (i) the mass of one object is doubled?
    (ii) the distance between the objects is doubled and tripled?
    (iii) the masses of both objects are doubled?
    > **Answer:** The gravitational force is given by $F = G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}$.
    (i) If the mass of one object (say $M$) is doubled (to $2M$), the new force $F' = G \frac{(2M) \times m}{d^2} = 2 \times (G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}) = 2F$. The force is doubled.
    (ii) If the distance ($d$) is doubled (to $2d$), the new force $F' = G \frac{M \times m}{(2d)^2} = G \frac{M \times m}{4d^2} = \frac{1}{4} (G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}) = \frac{1}{4} F$. The force becomes one-fourth. If the distance is tripled (to $3d$), the new force $F'' = G \frac{M \times m}{(3d)^2} = G \frac{M \times m}{9d^2} = \frac{1}{9} (G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}) = \frac{1}{9} F$. The force becomes one-ninth.
    (iii) If the masses of both objects are doubled (to $2M$ and $2m$), the new force $F' = G \frac{(2M) \times (2m)}{d^2} = G \frac{4Mm}{d^2} = 4 \times (G \frac{M \times m}{d^2}) = 4F$. The force is quadrupled (becomes four times).

7.  What is the importance of universal law of gravitation?
    > **Answer:** The universal law of gravitation is important because it explains various phenomena like the force holding us to the Earth, the motion of the Moon around the Earth, the motion of planets around the Sun, and the occurrence of tides. It provided a single framework to understand seemingly unrelated celestial and terrestrial events.

8.  What is the acceleration of free fall?
    > **Answer:** The acceleration of free fall is the acceleration experienced by an object falling under the sole influence of gravity. It is denoted by $g$ and is approximately $9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$ near the Earth's surface.

9.  What do we call the gravitational force between the earth and an object?
    > **Answer:** The gravitational force between the Earth and an object is called the **weight** of the object.

10. Amit buys few grams of gold at the poles as per the instruction of one of his friends. He hands over the same when he meets him at the equator. Will the friend agree with the weight of gold bought? If not, why? [Hint: The value of $g$ is greater at the poles than at the equator.]
    > **Answer:** No, the friend will likely not agree with the *weight* of the gold, although the *mass* is the same. Amit bought a certain *mass* of gold at the poles. When he takes it to the equator, the mass remains the same. However, the value of $g$ is slightly less at the equator than at the poles. Since weight = mass $\times$ $g$, the weight of the gold will be slightly less at the equator than it was at the poles. If the friend measures the weight of the gold, it will be less than the weight measured at the poles for the same mass.

11. Why will a sheet of paper fall slower than one that is crumpled into a ball?
    > **Answer:** A flat sheet of paper has a larger surface area compared to the same sheet crumpled into a ball. When falling through the air, the larger surface area of the flat sheet experiences significantly more air resistance (a type of fluid friction) than the smaller, denser crumpled ball. This greater air resistance opposes the downward gravitational force more effectively for the flat sheet, causing it to fall slower.

12. Gravitational force on the surface of the moon is only $\frac{1}{6}$ as strong as gravitational force on the earth. What is the weight in newtons of a 10 kg object on the moon and on the earth?
    > **Answer:**
    On Earth: Mass $m = 10 \mathrm{~kg}$, $g_{earth} = 9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2}$. Weight on Earth $W_{earth} = m \times g_{earth} = 10 \mathrm{~kg} \times 9.8 \mathrm{~m} \mathrm{~s}^{-2} = 98 \mathrm{~N}$.
    On the Moon: The gravitational force (and thus $g$) is $\frac{1}{6}$ that on Earth. So, $g_{moon} = \frac{1}{6} g_{earth}$. Weight on Moon $W_{moon} = m \times g_{moon} = m \times (\frac{1}{6} g_{earth}) = \frac{1}{6} (m \times g_{earth}) = \frac{1}{6} W_{earth}$.
    $W_{moon} = \frac{1}{6} \times 98 \mathrm{~N} \approx 16.33 \mathrm{~N}$.
    The weight of a 10 kg object is 98 N on Earth and approximately 16.33 N on the Moon.

13. A ball is thrown vertically upwards with a velocity of $49 \mathrm{~m/s}$. Calculate
    (i) the maximum height to which it rises,
    (ii) the total time it takes to return to the surface of the earth.
    > **Answer:** Given: $u = 49 \mathrm{~m/s}$ (upwards), $g = 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. For upward motion, $a = -g = -9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. At the maximum height, final velocity $v = 0 \mathrm{~m/s}$.
    (i) Maximum height ($s$): Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
    $0^2 = (49)^2 + 2(-9.8)s$
    $0 = 2401 - 19.6s$
    $19.6s = 2401$
    $s = \frac{2401}{19.6} = 122.5 \mathrm{~m}$. The maximum height is 122.5 m.
    (ii) Total time: Time to reach maximum height ($t_{up}$): Using $v = u + at$:
    $0 = 49 + (-9.8)t_{up}$
    $9.8 t_{up} = 49$
    $t_{up} = \frac{49}{9.8} = 5 \mathrm{~s}$.
    The time to fall back from the maximum height to the surface ($t_{down}$) is equal to the time taken to go up, assuming air resistance is negligible. $t_{down} = t_{up} = 5 \mathrm{~s}$.
    Total time = $t_{up} + t_{down} = 5 \mathrm{~s} + 5 \mathrm{~s} = 10 \mathrm{~s}$. The total time is 10 s.

14. A stone is released from the top of a tower of height 19.6 m. Calculate its final velocity just before touching the ground.
    > **Answer:** Given: Initial velocity $u = 0 \mathrm{~m/s}$ (released from rest), height $s = 19.6 \mathrm{~m}$ (downwards), $g = 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. For downward motion, $a = +g = 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. We need to find final velocity $v$. Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
    $v^2 = 0^2 + 2(9.8)(19.6)$
    $v^2 = 19.6 \times 19.6$
    $v = \sqrt{(19.6)^2} = 19.6 \mathrm{~m/s}$. The final velocity is 19.6 m/s.

15. A stone is thrown vertically upward with an initial velocity of $40 \mathrm{~m/s}$. Taking $g=10 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$, find the maximum height reached by the stone. What is the net displacement and the total distance covered by the stone?
    > **Answer:** Given: $u = 40 \mathrm{~m/s}$ (upwards), $g = 10 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. For upward motion, $a = -g = -10 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. At maximum height, $v = 0 \mathrm{~m/s}$.
    Maximum height ($s$): Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$:
    $0^2 = (40)^2 + 2(-10)s$
    $0 = 1600 - 20s$
    $20s = 1600$
    $s = \frac{1600}{20} = 80 \mathrm{~m}$. The maximum height is 80 m.
    Net displacement: The stone starts from the ground, goes up to 80 m, and returns to the ground. The final position is the same as the initial position. Therefore, the net displacement is 0 m.
    Total distance covered: The stone travels 80 m upwards to the maximum height and then travels 80 m downwards back to the ground. Total distance = Distance up + Distance down = 80 m + 80 m = 160 m.

16. Calculate the force of gravitation between the earth and the Sun, given that the mass of the earth $=6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$ and of the Sun $=2 \times 10^{30} \mathrm{~kg}$. The average distance between the two is $1.5 \times 10^{11} \mathrm{~m}$.
    > **Answer:** Given: $M_{earth} = 6 \times 10^{24} \mathrm{~kg}$, $M_{sun} = 2 \times 10^{30} \mathrm{~kg}$, $d = 1.5 \times 10^{11} \mathrm{~m}$, $G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \mathrm{Nm}^2 \mathrm{~kg}^{-2}$.
    Using $F = G \frac{M_{sun} \times M_{earth}}{d^2}$:
    $F = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \times \frac{(2 \times 10^{30}) \times (6 \times 10^{24})}{(1.5 \times 10^{11})^2}$
    $F = (6.67 \times 10^{-11}) \times \frac{12 \times 10^{54}}{2.25 \times 10^{22}}$
    $F = 6.67 \times 10^{-11} \times 5.33 \times 10^{32}$
    $F \approx 3.56 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~N}$. The force of gravitation between the Earth and the Sun is approximately $3.56 \times 10^{22} \mathrm{~N}$.

17. A stone is allowed to fall from the top of a tower 100 m high and at the same time another stone is projected vertically upwards from the ground with a velocity of $25 \mathrm{~m/s}$. Calculate when and where the two stones will meet.
    > **Answer:** Let the height of the tower be $H = 100 \mathrm{~m}$. Let $t$ be the time when they meet. Let the meeting point be at a distance $s_1$ from the top (for the falling stone) and $s_2$ from the ground (for the projected stone). The total height is $s_1 + s_2 = H = 100 \mathrm{~m}$.
    For the falling stone: Initial velocity $u_1 = 0$. $a_1 = +g = 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. Distance covered $s_1 = u_1 t + \frac{1}{2} a_1 t^2 = 0 \times t + \frac{1}{2} (9.8) t^2 = 4.9t^2$.
    For the projected stone: Initial velocity $u_2 = 25 \mathrm{~m/s}$ (upwards). $a_2 = -g = -9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$. Distance covered $s_2 = u_2 t + \frac{1}{2} a_2 t^2 = 25t + \frac{1}{2} (-9.8) t^2 = 25t - 4.9t^2$.
    They meet when $s_1 + s_2 = 100$:
    $4.9t^2 + (25t - 4.9t^2) = 100$
    $25t = 100$
    $t = \frac{100}{25} = 4 \mathrm{~s}$. The stones will meet after 4 seconds.
    Where they meet:
    Distance from the top ($s_1$) = $4.9t^2 = 4.9 \times (4)^2 = 4.9 \times 16 = 78.4 \mathrm{~m}$.
    Distance from the ground ($s_2$) = $25t - 4.9t^2 = 25(4) - 4.9(4)^2 = 100 - 78.4 = 21.6 \mathrm{~m}$.
    Check: $s_1 + s_2 = 78.4 + 21.6 = 100 \mathrm{~m}$ (which is the height of the tower).
    The stones will meet after 4 seconds, at a height of 21.6 m from the ground (or 78.4 m from the top of the tower).

18. A ball thrown up vertically returns to the thrower after 6 s. Find
    (a) the velocity with which it was thrown up,
    (b) the maximum height it reaches, and
    (c) its position after 4 s.
    > **Answer:** Given: Total time of flight $T = 6 \mathrm{~s}$. Time to reach maximum height $t_{up} = T/2 = 6/2 = 3 \mathrm{~s}$. Let $g = 9.8 \mathrm{~m/s}^2$.
    (a) Velocity thrown up ($u$): At maximum height (at $t=3$ s), $v=0$. Using $v = u + at_{up}$ (with $a=-g$ for upward motion):
    $0 = u + (-9.8)(3)$
    $0 = u - 29.4$
    $u = 29.4 \mathrm{~m/s}$. The ball was thrown up with a velocity of 29.4 m/s.
    (b) Maximum height ($s$): Using $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$ (with $a=-g$):
    $0^2 = (29.4)^2 + 2(-9.8)s$
    $0 = 864.36 - 19.6s$
    $19.6s = 864.36$
    $s = \frac{864.36}{19.6} = 44.1 \mathrm{~m}$. The maximum height reached is 44.1 m.
    (c) Position after 4 s: After 3 seconds, the ball is at its maximum height (44.1 m). In the next 1 second (from $t=3$ s to $t=4$ s), it is falling from the maximum height.
    Consider motion from the top (initial velocity from top is 0):
    Time from top = $4 \mathrm{~s} - 3 \mathrm{~s} = 1 \mathrm{~s}$.
    Distance fallen from top ($s_{fall}$): Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2} at^2$ (with $u=0$, $a=+g=9.8$):
    $s_{fall} = 0 \times 1 + \frac{1}{2} (9.8)(1)^2 = 4.9 \mathrm{~m}$.
    The position after 4 seconds is $4.9 \mathrm{~m}$ below the maximum height.
    Position from the ground = Maximum height - Distance fallen = $44.1 \mathrm{~m} - 4.9 \mathrm{~m} = 39.2 \mathrm{~m}$.
    The position after 4 seconds is 39.2 m above the ground.

19. In what direction does the buoyant force on an object immersed in a liquid act?
    > **Answer:** The buoyant force on an object immersed in a liquid acts vertically upwards.

20. Why does a block of plastic released under water come up to the surface of water?
    > **Answer:** A block of plastic comes up to the surface because its density is less than the density of water. According to Archimedes' principle, the buoyant force acting on the plastic block (equal to the weight of the water displaced) is greater than the weight of the plastic block itself. This net upward force causes the block to accelerate upwards until it reaches the surface and floats, displacing only enough water so that the buoyant force equals its weight.

21. The volume of 50 g of a substance is $20 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$. If the density of water is $1 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$, will the substance float or sink?
    > **Answer:** First, calculate the density of the substance:
    Density = Mass / Volume = $50 \mathrm{~g} / 20 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} = 2.5 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$.
    The density of the substance ($2.5 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$) is greater than the density of water ($1 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$). Therefore, the substance will sink in water.

22. The volume of a 500 g sealed packet is $350 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$. Will the packet float or sink in water if the density of water is 1 g $\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$? What will be the mass of the water displaced by this packet?
    > **Answer:** First, calculate the density of the packet:
    Density of packet = Mass / Volume = $500 \mathrm{~g} / 350 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} \approx 1.43 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$.
    The density of the packet ($1.43 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$) is greater than the density of water ($1 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3}$). Therefore, the packet will sink in water.
    When an object sinks, it is fully immersed and displaces a volume of fluid equal to its own volume.
    Volume of water displaced = Volume of packet = $350 \mathrm{~cm}^{3}$.
    Mass of water displaced = Density of water $\times$ Volume of water displaced
    Mass of water displaced = $1 \mathrm{~g} \mathrm{~cm}^{-3} \times 350 \mathrm{~cm}^{3} = 350 \mathrm{~g}$.
    The packet will sink, and it will displace 350 g of water.
