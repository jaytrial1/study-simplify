# Exercises

1. Compare the properties of electrons, protons and neutrons.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Electron:** Symbol 'e', negative charge (-1), mass is negligible (approx. 1/2000th of a proton), found outside the nucleus in orbits/shells.
> *   **Proton:** Symbol 'p', positive charge (+1), mass is approximately 1 unit, found in the nucleus.
> *   **Neutron:** Symbol 'n', no charge (neutral), mass is approximately 1 unit, found in the nucleus (except in Protium).

2. What are the limitations of J.J. Thomson's model of the atom?
> **Answer:** The main limitation of Thomson's model was that it could not explain the results of the alpha-particle scattering experiment conducted by Rutherford. Rutherford's experiment showed that the positive charge and mass were concentrated in a very small nucleus, which contradicted Thomson's idea of a positive charge spread throughout the atom.

3. What are the limitations of Rutherford's model of the atom?
> **Answer:** The main limitation of Rutherford's model was that it could not explain the stability of an atom. According to classical physics, a charged particle (like an electron) revolving in a circular orbit would lose energy by radiation and eventually spiral into the nucleus. This would make the atom unstable, but atoms are known to be stable.

4. Describe Bohr's model of the atom.
> **Answer:** Bohr's model of the atom proposed that electrons revolve around the nucleus in specific, fixed orbits or energy levels, called discrete orbits or shells. He stated that electrons do not radiate energy while revolving in these special orbits. Electrons can only move from one energy level to another by absorbing or emitting a fixed amount of energy.

5. Compare all the proposed models of an atom given in this chapter.
> **Answer:**
> *   **Dalton's Model:** Proposed atoms were indivisible and indestructible. Failed after the discovery of sub-atomic particles.
> *   **Thomson's Model:** Proposed atoms were a positive sphere with embedded electrons ("plum pudding" model). Explained neutrality but failed to explain scattering experiments.
> *   **Rutherford's Model:** Proposed a central, dense, positively charged nucleus with electrons orbiting it. Explained scattering but failed to explain atomic stability.
> *   **Bohr's Model:** Proposed electrons orbit in specific, quantized energy levels/shells and do not radiate energy in these orbits. Explained stability and the spectrum of hydrogen, but had limitations for multi-electron atoms.

6. Summarise the rules for writing of distribution of electrons in various shells for the first eighteen elements.
> **Answer:** The rules for electron distribution (Bohr-Bury scheme) are:
> (i) The maximum number of electrons in a shell is given by $2n^2$, where n is the shell number (K=1, L=2, M=3, ...). Max electrons: K=2, L=8, M=18.
> (ii) The outermost shell can hold a maximum of 8 electrons.
> (iii) Shells are filled in a step-wise manner; inner shells must be filled before electrons occupy outer shells.

7. Define valency by taking examples of silicon and oxygen.
> **Answer:** Valency is the combining capacity of an atom, which is determined by the number of electrons in its outermost shell (valence electrons) and its tendency to gain, lose, or share electrons to achieve a stable electron configuration (usually an octet).
> *   **Silicon (Si):** Atomic number 14. Electron configuration is 2, 8, 4. It has 4 valence electrons. Silicon can either lose 4 electrons or gain 4 electrons to achieve stability. Its valency is 4.
> *   **Oxygen (O):** Atomic number 8. Electron configuration is 2, 6. It has 6 valence electrons. Oxygen tends to gain 2 electrons to complete its octet (8-6=2). Its valency is 2.

8. Explain with examples (i) Atomic number, (ii) Mass number, (iii) Isotopes and (iv) Isobars. Give any two uses of isotopes.
> **Answer:**
> (i) **Atomic Number (Z):** The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It defines the element. Example: Carbon has 6 protons, so its atomic number is 6 (Z=6).
> (ii) **Mass Number (A):** The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom. Example: A carbon atom with 6 protons and 6 neutrons has a mass number of 12 (A=12).
> (iii) **Isotopes:** Atoms of the same element (same atomic number) but with different mass numbers (different number of neutrons). Example: Carbon-12 (${ }_{6}^{12} \mathrm{C}$) has 6 protons and 6 neutrons. Carbon-14 (${ }_{6}^{14} \mathrm{C}$) has 6 protons and 8 neutrons. Both are carbon because Z=6, but they have different mass numbers.
> (iv) **Isobars:** Atoms of different elements (different atomic numbers) but with the same mass number. Example: Argon-40 (${ }_{18}^{40} \mathrm{Ar}$) has Z=18, A=40 (18 protons, 22 neutrons). Calcium-40 (${ }_{20}^{40} \mathrm{Ca}$) has Z=20, A=40 (20 protons, 20 neutrons). They are different elements but have the same mass number (40).
> **Two uses of isotopes:**
> 1. An isotope of Uranium is used as fuel in nuclear reactors.
> 2. An isotope of Cobalt is used in the treatment of cancer.

9. $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$has completely filled K and L shells. Explain.
> **Answer:** A neutral sodium atom (Na) has atomic number 11, meaning it has 11 protons and 11 electrons. Its electron configuration is 2, 8, 1 (K shell=2, L shell=8, M shell=1). A sodium ion ($\mathrm{Na}^{+}$) is formed when a neutral sodium atom loses its outermost electron (from the M shell). This leaves the ion with 11 protons and 10 electrons. The electron configuration of $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$becomes 2, 8. In this configuration, the K shell has 2 electrons (which is full) and the L shell has 8 electrons (which is full). Thus, $\mathrm{Na}^{+}$has completely filled K and L shells.

10. If bromine atom is available in the form of, say, two isotopes ${ }_{35}^{79} \mathrm{Br}(49.7 \%)$ and ${ }_{35}^{81} \mathrm{Br}(50.3 \%)$, calculate the average atomic mass of bromine atom.
> **Answer:** Average atomic mass = [(Mass of isotope 1 × % abundance) + (Mass of isotope 2 × % abundance)] / 100
> Average atomic mass = $[(79 \text{ u} \times 49.7) + (81 \text{ u} \times 50.3)] / 100$
> Average atomic mass = $[3926.3 + 4074.3] / 100$
> Average atomic mass = $8000.6 / 100$
> Average atomic mass = $80.006 \text{ u}$

11. The average atomic mass of a sample of an element X is 16.2 u. What are the percentages of isotopes ${ }_{8}^{16} \mathrm{X}$ and ${ }_{8}^{18} \mathrm{X}$ in the sample?
> **Answer:** Element X has atomic number 8, so it is Oxygen. Let the percentage abundance of ${ }_{8}^{16} \mathrm{X}$ be $y\%$ and the percentage abundance of ${ }_{8}^{18} \mathrm{X}$ be $(100-y)\%$.
> Average atomic mass = [(Mass of isotope 1 × % abundance) + (Mass of isotope 2 × % abundance)] / 100
> $16.2 = [(16 \times y) + (18 \times (100-y))] / 100$
> $1620 = 16y + 1800 - 18y$
> $1620 = 1800 - 2y$
> $2y = 1800 - 1620$
> $2y = 180$
> $y = 90$
> So, the percentage of ${ }_{8}^{16} \mathrm{X}$ is 90% and the percentage of ${ }_{8}^{18} \mathrm{X}$ is (100 - 90)% = 10%.

12. If Z=3, what would be the valency of the element? Also, name the element.
> **Answer:** If Z=3, the element has 3 protons and, in a neutral atom, 3 electrons. The electron configuration is 2, 1. The outermost shell (L shell) has 1 electron. To achieve a stable configuration, it is easier for this atom to lose 1 electron. Therefore, its valency is 1. The element with atomic number 3 is Lithium (Li).

13. Composition of the nuclei of two atomic species X and Y are given as under
    X: Protons = 6, Neutrons = 6
    Y: Protons = 6, Neutrons = 8
    Give the mass numbers of X and Y. What is the relation between the two species?
> **Answer:**
> *   Mass number of X = Protons + Neutrons = 6 + 6 = 12.
> *   Mass number of Y = Protons + Neutrons = 6 + 8 = 14.
> The atomic number of X is 6 (number of protons). The atomic number of Y is also 6 (number of protons). Since both species have the same atomic number (6) but different mass numbers (12 and 14), they are **isotopes** of the same element (Carbon).

14. For the following statements, write T for True and F for False.
