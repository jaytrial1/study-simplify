This section details the rules for distributing electrons into the different energy levels or shells around the nucleus, based on the Bohr-Bury scheme.

# How are Electrons Distributed in Different Orbits (Shells)?

The distribution of electrons into different orbits of an atom was suggested by **Bohr and Bury**.

The following rules are followed for writing the number of electrons in different energy levels or shells:
(i) The maximum number of electrons present in a shell is given by the formula $2n^2$, where 'n' is the orbit number or energy level index (1, 2, 3, ...).
Hence the maximum number of electrons in different shells are as follows:
*   first orbit or K-shell (n=1) will be = $2 \times 1^2 = 2$
*   second orbit or L-shell (n=2) will be = $2 \times 2^2 = 8$
*   third orbit or M-shell (n=3) will be = $2 \times 3^2 = 18$
*   fourth orbit or N-shell (n=4) will be = $2 \times 4^2 = 32$, and so on.

(ii) The maximum number of electrons that can be accommodated in the outermost orbit is 8.
(iii) Electrons are not accommodated in a given shell unless the inner shells are filled. That is, the shells are filled in a step-wise manner.

## Questions

1. Write the distribution of electrons in carbon and sodium atoms.
> **Answer:**
> *   Carbon (Atomic Number 6): The distribution is 2 electrons in the K shell and 4 electrons in the L shell (2, 4).
> *   Sodium (Atomic Number 11): The distribution is 2 electrons in the K shell, 8 electrons in the L shell, and 1 electron in the M shell (2, 8, 1).

2. If K and L shells of an atom are full, then what would be the total number of electrons in the atom?
> **Answer:** The K shell can hold a maximum of 2 electrons ($2 \times 1^2$). The L shell can hold a maximum of 8 electrons ($2 \times 2^2$). If both shells are full, the total number of electrons in the atom would be 2 + 8 = 10.
