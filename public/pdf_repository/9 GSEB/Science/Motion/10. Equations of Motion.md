This section introduces the three equations that relate velocity, acceleration, time, and distance for objects moving with uniform acceleration along a straight line.

# Equations of Motion

When an object moves along a straight line with uniform acceleration, it is possible to relate its velocity, acceleration during motion and the distance covered by it in a certain time interval by a set of equations known as the *equations of motion*. For convenience, a set of three such equations are given below:

1.  $v = u + at$ (Velocity-Time Relation)
2.  $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$ (Position-Time Relation)
3.  $2as = v^2 - u^2$ (Position-Velocity Relation)

where $u$ is the initial velocity of the object which moves with uniform acceleration $a$ for time $t$, $v$ is the final velocity, and $s$ is the distance travelled by the object in time $t$. Eq. (1) describes the velocity-time relation and Eq. (2) represents the position-time relation. Eq. (3), which represents the relation between the position and the velocity, can be obtained from Eq. (1) and Eq. (2) by eliminating $t$. These three equations can be derived by graphical method.

### Example 7.5

A train starting from rest attains a velocity of 72 km h⁻¹ in 5 minutes. Assuming that the acceleration is uniform, find (i) the acceleration and (ii) the distance travelled by the train for attaining this velocity.

#### Solution:

We have been given:
Initial velocity, $u = 0$ (starting from rest)
Final velocity, $v = 72 \text{ km h}^{-1}$
Convert to m s⁻¹: $72 \times \frac{1000}{3600} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 72 \times \frac{5}{18} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 4 \times 5 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 20 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
Time, $t = 5 \text{ minutes} = 5 \times 60 \text{ s} = 300 \text{ s}$.

(i) From the first equation of motion, $v = u + at$:
$20 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1} + a \times 300 \text{ s}$
$20 = 300a$
$a = \frac{20}{300} \text{ m s}^{-2} = \frac{1}{15} \text{ m s}^{-2}$.

(ii) From the third equation of motion, $2as = v^2 - u^2$:
$2 \times (\frac{1}{15} \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times s = (20 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2 - (0 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2$
$\frac{2}{15} s = 400$
$s = \frac{400 \times 15}{2} \text{ m} = 200 \times 15 \text{ m} = 3000 \text{ m}$.
Convert to km: $3000 \text{ m} = 3 \text{ km}$.

The acceleration of the train is $\frac{1}{15}$ m s⁻² and the distance travelled is 3 km.

### Example 7.6

A car accelerates uniformly from 18 km h⁻¹ to 36 km h⁻¹ in 5 s. Calculate (i) the acceleration and (ii) the distance covered by the car in that time.

#### Solution:

We are given that:
Initial velocity, $u = 18 \text{ km h}^{-1}$
Convert to m s⁻¹: $18 \times \frac{1000}{3600} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 18 \times \frac{5}{18} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 5 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
Final velocity, $v = 36 \text{ km h}^{-1}$
Convert to m s⁻¹: $36 \times \frac{1000}{3600} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 36 \times \frac{5}{18} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 2 \times 5 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 10 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
Time, $t = 5 \text{ s}$.

(i) From the first equation of motion, $v = u + at$:
$10 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 5 \text{ m s}^{-1} + a \times 5 \text{ s}$
$10 - 5 = 5a$
$5 = 5a$
$a = 1 \text{ m s}^{-2}$.

(ii) From the second equation of motion, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
$s = (5 \text{ m s}^{-1}) \times (5 \text{ s}) + \frac{1}{2} \times (1 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (5 \text{ s})^2$
$s = 25 \text{ m} + \frac{1}{2} \times 1 \times 25 \text{ m}$
$s = 25 \text{ m} + 12.5 \text{ m}$
$s = 37.5 \text{ m}$.

The acceleration of the car is 1 m s⁻² and the distance covered is 37.5 m.

### Example 7.7

The brakes applied to a car produce an acceleration of 6 m s⁻² in the opposite direction to the motion. If the car takes 2 s to stop after the application of brakes, calculate the distance it travels during this time.

#### Solution:

We have been given:
Acceleration, $a = -6 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ (negative because it's opposite to motion/brakes applied)
Time, $t = 2 \text{ s}$.
Final velocity, $v = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ (car stops).

From the first equation of motion, $v = u + at$:
$0 \text{ m s}^{-1} = u + (-6 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (2 \text{ s})$
$0 = u - 12 \text{ m s}^{-1}$
$u = 12 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
So, the initial velocity of the car before applying brakes was 12 m s⁻¹.

From the second equation of motion, $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
$s = (12 \text{ m s}^{-1}) \times (2 \text{ s}) + \frac{1}{2} \times (-6 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (2 \text{ s})^2$
$s = 24 \text{ m} + \frac{1}{2} \times (-6) \times 4 \text{ m}$
$s = 24 \text{ m} - 12 \text{ m}$
$s = 12 \text{ m}$.

Thus, the car will move 12 m before it stops after the application of brakes. Can you now appreciate why drivers are cautioned to maintain some distance between vehicles while travelling on the road?

## Questions

1. A bus starting from rest moves with a uniform acceleration of 0.1 m s⁻² for 2 minutes. Find (a) the speed acquired, (b) the distance travelled.
> **Answer:**
> Initial velocity, $u = 0$ m s⁻¹ (starting from rest).
> Acceleration, $a = 0.1$ m s⁻².
> Time, $t = 2 \text{ minutes} = 2 \times 60 \text{ s} = 120 \text{ s}$.
>
> (a) Speed acquired (final velocity, v):
> Using $v = u + at$:
> $v = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1} + (0.1 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (120 \text{ s})$
> $v = 12 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
> The speed acquired is 12 m s⁻¹.
>
> (b) Distance travelled (s):
> Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
> $s = (0 \text{ m s}^{-1}) \times (120 \text{ s}) + \frac{1}{2} \times (0.1 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (120 \text{ s})^2$
> $s = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 0.1 \times 14400 \text{ m}$
> $s = 0.05 \times 14400 \text{ m}$
> $s = 720 \text{ m}$.
> The distance travelled is 720 m.

2. A train is travelling at a speed of 90 km h⁻¹. Brakes are applied so as to produce a uniform acceleration of -0.5 m s⁻². Find how far the train will go before it is brought to rest.
> **Answer:**
> Initial velocity, $u = 90 \text{ km h}^{-1}$
> Convert to m s⁻¹: $90 \times \frac{1000}{3600} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 90 \times \frac{5}{18} \text{ m s}^{-1} = 5 \times 5 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 25 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
> Final velocity, $v = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ (brought to rest).
> Acceleration, $a = -0.5 \text{ m s}^{-2}$.
> Distance travelled (s):
> Using $2as = v^2 - u^2$:
> $2 \times (-0.5 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times s = (0 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2 - (25 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2$
> $-1 \times s = 0 - 625 \text{ m}^2 \text{ s}^{-2}$
> $-s = -625 \text{ m}$
> $s = 625 \text{ m}$.
> The train will go 625 m before it is brought to rest.

3. A trolley, while going down an inclined plane, has an acceleration of 2 cm s⁻². What will be its velocity 3 s after the start?
> **Answer:**
> Initial velocity, $u = 0 \text{ cm s}^{-1}$ (assuming it starts from rest).
> Acceleration, $a = 2 \text{ cm s}^{-2}$.
> Time, $t = 3 \text{ s}$.
> Final velocity (v):
> Using $v = u + at$:
> $v = 0 \text{ cm s}^{-1} + (2 \text{ cm s}^{-2}) \times (3 \text{ s})$
> $v = 6 \text{ cm s}^{-1}$.
> The velocity of the trolley 3 s after the start will be 6 cm s⁻¹.

4. A racing car has a uniform acceleration of 4 m s⁻². What distance will it cover in 10 s after start?
> **Answer:**
> Initial velocity, $u = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ (assuming 'after start' implies starting from rest).
> Acceleration, $a = 4 \text{ m s}^{-2}$.
> Time, $t = 10 \text{ s}$.
> Distance travelled (s):
> Using $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$:
> $s = (0 \text{ m s}^{-1}) \times (10 \text{ s}) + \frac{1}{2} \times (4 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times (10 \text{ s})^2$
> $s = 0 + \frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 100 \text{ m}$
> $s = 2 \times 100 \text{ m}$
> $s = 200 \text{ m}$.
> The racing car will cover a distance of 200 m in 10 s after the start.

5. A stone is thrown in a vertically upward direction with a velocity of 5 m s⁻¹. If the acceleration of the stone during its motion is 10 m s⁻² in the downward direction, what will be the height attained by the stone and how much time will it take to reach there?
> **Answer:**
> Initial velocity, $u = 5 \text{ m s}^{-1}$ (upward).
> Acceleration, $a = -10 \text{ m s}^{-2}$ (downward, opposite to initial velocity).
> At the highest point, the final velocity $v = 0 \text{ m s}^{-1}$.
>
> Height attained (s):
> Using $2as = v^2 - u^2$:
> $2 \times (-10 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times s = (0 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2 - (5 \text{ m s}^{-1})^2$
> $-20s = 0 - 25$
> $-20s = -25$
> $s = \frac{-25}{-20} \text{ m} = \frac{5}{4} \text{ m} = 1.25 \text{ m}$.
> The height attained by the stone is 1.25 m.
>
> Time taken to reach the highest point (t):
> Using $v = u + at$:
> $0 \text{ m s}^{-1} = 5 \text{ m s}^{-1} + (-10 \text{ m s}^{-2}) \times t$
> $0 = 5 - 10t$
> $10t = 5$
> $t = \frac{5}{10} \text{ s} = 0.5 \text{ s}$.
> It will take 0.5 s to reach the highest point.
