This section explains the difference between physical and chemical changes based on whether the chemical composition of the substance changes.

# Physical and Chemical Changes

In the previous chapter, we have learnt about a few physical properties of matter. The properties that can be observed and specified like colour, hardness, rigidity, fluidity, density, melting point, boiling point etc. are the **physical properties**.

The interconversion of states is a physical change because these changes occur without a change in composition and no change in the chemical nature of the substance. Although ice, water and water vapour all look different and display different physical properties, they are chemically the same substance (H₂O).

Both water and cooking oil are liquid but their chemical characteristics are different. They differ in odour and inflammability. We know that oil burns in air whereas water extinguishes fire. It is this chemical property of oil that makes it different from water. Burning is a **chemical change**. During this process, one substance reacts with another to undergo a change in chemical composition. Chemical change brings change in the chemical properties of matter and we get new substances. A chemical change is also called a **chemical reaction**.

During burning of a candle, both physical and chemical changes take place. Can you distinguish these?

> **Answer:** When a candle burns, both physical and chemical changes occur.
> *   **Physical Change:** The wax melts (solid to liquid) and then vaporizes (liquid to gas). This is a change of state, which is a physical change.
> *   **Chemical Change:** The wax vapor reacts with oxygen in the air to produce new substances like carbon dioxide, water vapour, soot, heat, and light. This process of combustion is a chemical change.

### Questions

1.  Classify the following as chemical or physical changes:
    *   cutting of trees,
    *   melting of butter in a pan,
    *   rusting of almirah,
    *   boiling of water to form steam,
    *   passing of electric current, through water and the water breaking down into hydrogen and oxygen gases,
    *   dissolving common salt in water,
    *   making a fruit salad with raw fruits, and
    *   burning of paper and wood.
2.  Try segregating the things around you as pure substances or mixtures.

> **Answer to Q1:**
> *   cutting of trees: **Physical change** (The wood is still wood, just in smaller pieces)
> *   melting of butter in a pan: **Physical change** (Butter changes state from solid to liquid)
> *   rusting of almirah: **Chemical change** (Iron reacts with oxygen and moisture to form iron oxide, a new substance)
> *   boiling of water to form steam: **Physical change** (Water changes state from liquid to gas)
> *   passing of electric current, through water and the water breaking down into hydrogen and oxygen gases: **Chemical change** (Water decomposes into two new substances, hydrogen and oxygen)
> *   dissolving common salt in water: **Physical change** (Salt and water mix; they retain their chemical properties and can be separated)
> *   making a fruit salad with raw fruits: **Physical change** (Fruits are cut and mixed; they remain chemically the same fruits)
> *   burning of paper and wood: **Chemical change** (Paper/wood reacts with oxygen to form ash, smoke, gases, etc., new substances)

> **Answer to Q2:** This requires observation of the student's surroundings. Examples:
> *   **Pure Substances:** Water (if distilled), sugar, salt, iron nail, copper wire, gold jewellery (if pure).
> *   **Mixtures:** Air, tap water, soil, wood, milk, juice, rock, salt solution, sugar solution.
