# Summary and Exercises

## What you have learnt

*   During a chemical reaction, the sum of the masses of the reactants and products remains unchanged. This is known as the **Law of Conservation of Mass**.
*   In a pure chemical compound, elements are always present in a definite proportion by mass. This is known as the **Law of Definite Proportions**.
*   An **atom** is the smallest particle of an element that cannot usually exist independently and retains all its chemical properties.
*   A **molecule** is the smallest particle of an element or a compound capable of independent existence under ordinary conditions. It shows all the properties of the substance.
*   A **chemical formula** of a compound shows its constituent elements and the number of atoms of each combining element.
*   Clusters of atoms that act as an ion are called **polyatomic ions**. They carry a fixed charge on them.
*   The chemical formula of a molecular compound is determined by the **valency** of each element.
*   In ionic compounds, the **charge** on each ion is used to determine the chemical formula of the compound.

1. A 0.24 g sample of compound of oxygen and boron was found by analysis to contain 0.096 g of boron and 0.144 g of oxygen. Calculate the percentage composition of the compound by weight.
    > **Answer:**
    > Total mass of compound = 0.24 g
    > Mass of boron = 0.096 g
    > Mass of oxygen = 0.144 g
    > Percentage of boron = (Mass of boron / Total mass of compound) $\times$ 100
    > Percentage of boron = (0.096 g / 0.24 g) $\times$ 100 = 40%
    > Percentage of oxygen = (Mass of oxygen / Total mass of compound) $\times$ 100
    > Percentage of oxygen = (0.144 g / 0.24 g) $\times$ 100 = 60%
    > The percentage composition is 40% boron and 60% oxygen by weight.

2. When 3.0 g of carbon is burnt in 8.00 g oxygen, 11.00 g of carbon dioxide is produced. What mass of carbon dioxide will be formed when 3.00 g of carbon is burnt in 50.00 g of oxygen? Which law of chemical combination will govern your answer?
    > **Answer:**
    > In the first reaction, 3.0 g of carbon reacts with 8.00 g of oxygen to produce 11.00 g of carbon dioxide. This shows that carbon and oxygen combine in a fixed ratio by mass to form carbon dioxide (3:8).
    > According to the Law of Constant Proportions, carbon and oxygen will always combine in the ratio 3:8 by mass to form carbon dioxide.
    > When 3.00 g of carbon is burnt in 50.00 g of oxygen, only 8.00 g of oxygen will react with the 3.00 g of carbon (as per the 3:8 ratio). The remaining oxygen (50.00 g - 8.00 g = 42.00 g) will be left unreacted.
    > The mass of carbon dioxide formed will be the sum of the mass of carbon that reacted and the mass of oxygen that reacted.
    > Mass of carbon dioxide = Mass of reacted carbon + Mass of reacted oxygen
    > Mass of carbon dioxide = 3.00 g + 8.00 g = 11.00 g.
    > The law governing this answer is the **Law of Constant Proportions**.

3. What are polyatomic ions? Give examples.
    > **Answer:** Polyatomic ions are groups of atoms that are chemically bonded together and carry a net positive or negative charge. They behave as a single unit in chemical reactions.
    > Examples include:
    > *   Ammonium ion ($\mathrm{NH}_{4}^{+}$)
    > *   Hydroxide ion ($\mathrm{OH}^{-}$)
    > *   Nitrate ion ($\mathrm{NO}_{3}^{-}$)
    > *   Carbonate ion ($\mathrm{CO}_{3}^{2-}$)
    > *   Sulphate ion ($\mathrm{SO}_{4}^{2-}$)
    > *   Phosphate ion ($\mathrm{PO}_{4}^{3-}$)

4. Write the chemical formulae of the following.
    (a) Magnesium chloride
    (b) Calcium oxide
    (c) Copper nitrate
    (d) Aluminium chloride
    (e) Calcium carbonate.
    > **Answer:**
    > (a) Magnesium chloride: Mg (2+), Cl (1-). Formula: $\mathrm{MgCl}_2$.
    > (b) Calcium oxide: Ca (2+), O (2-). Formula: CaO (simplified from $\mathrm{Ca}_2\mathrm{O}_2$).
    > (c) Copper nitrate: Copper can be Cu(I) or Cu(II). Assuming Copper(II) which is more common in simple examples: Cu (2+), $\mathrm{NO}_3$ (1-). Formula: $\mathrm{Cu(NO_3)}_2$. If assuming Copper(I): Cu (1+), $\mathrm{NO}_3$ (1-). Formula: $\mathrm{CuNO_3}$. Using Copper(II) is a reasonable assumption for this level.
    > (d) Aluminium chloride: Al (3+), Cl (1-). Formula: $\mathrm{AlCl}_3$.
    > (e) Calcium carbonate: Ca (2+), $\mathrm{CO}_3$ (2-). Formula: $\mathrm{CaCO}_3$.

5. Give the names of the elements present in the following compounds.
    (a) Quick lime
    (b) Hydrogen bromide
    (c) Baking powder
    (d) Potassium sulphate.
    > **Answer:**
    > (a) Quick lime (Calcium oxide, CaO): Calcium (Ca) and Oxygen (O).
    > (b) Hydrogen bromide (HBr): Hydrogen (H) and Bromine (Br).
    > (c) Baking powder (commonly Sodium hydrogen carbonate, $\mathrm{NaHCO}_3$): Sodium (Na), Hydrogen (H), Carbon (C), and Oxygen (O).
    > (d) Potassium sulphate ($\mathrm{K}_2\mathrm{SO}_4$): Potassium (K), Sulphur (S), and Oxygen (O).

6. Calculate the molar mass of the following substances. (Note: The question asks for molar mass, but the context of the chapter before this only covered molecular/formula unit mass in 'u'. Assuming it means molecular/formula unit mass in 'u' as per the chapter's scope.)
    (a) Ethyne, $\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{2}$
    (b) Sulphur molecule, $\mathrm{S}_{8}$
    (c) Phosphorus molecule, $\mathrm{P}_{4}$ (Atomic mass of phosphorus = 31)
    (d) Hydrochloric acid, HCl
    (e) Nitric acid, $\mathrm{HNO}_{3}$
    > **Answer:** (Calculated as molecular/formula unit mass in 'u')
    > Atomic masses: H=1u, C=12u, S=32u, P=31u, Cl=35.5u, O=16u, N=14u
    > (a) Ethyne ($\mathrm{C}_{2}\mathrm{H}_{2}$): $(2 \times 12) + (2 \times 1) = 24 + 2 = 26 \text{ u}$
    > (b) Sulphur molecule ($\mathrm{S}_{8}$): $8 \times 32 = 256 \text{ u}$
    > (c) Phosphorus molecule ($\mathrm{P}_{4}$): $4 \times 31 = 124 \text{ u}$
    > (d) Hydrochloric acid (HCl): $1 \times 1 + 1 \times 35.5 = 1 + 35.5 = 36.5 \text{ u}$
    > (e) Nitric acid ($\mathrm{HNO}_{3}$): $1 \times 1 + 1 \times 14 + 3 \times 16 = 1 + 14 + 48 = 63 \text{ u}$

For example:
Formula for sodium sulphate:
Sodium ion ($\mathrm{Na}^{+}$) has valency 1 (1 dip). Sulphate ion ($\mathrm{SO}_{4}^{2-}$) has valency 2 (2 dips).
You need two sodium ions to balance one sulphate ion.
2 sodium ions can be fixed on one sulphate ion.
Hence, the formula will be: $\mathrm{Na}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$

Do it yourself: Now, write the formula of sodium phosphate.
> **Answer:**
> Sodium ion ($\mathrm{Na}^{+}$) has valency 1.
> Phosphate ion ($\mathrm{PO}_{4}^{3-}$) has valency 3.
> To balance the charges, you need three sodium ions for every one phosphate ion (3 x 1+ = 3+ charge, balances the 3- charge).
> Using the criss-cross method: Na (1) and $\mathrm{PO}_4$ (3) -> $\mathrm{Na}_3(\mathrm{PO}_4)_1$.
> The formula will be: $\mathrm{Na}_{3}\mathrm{PO}_{4}$.
