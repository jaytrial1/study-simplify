This section explains how to write chemical formulae for compounds, introducing the concept of valency and how it is used to determine the ratio of atoms in a compound.

# Chemical Formulae and Valency

The **chemical formula** of a compound is a symbolic representation of its composition, showing the elements present and the number of atoms of each element. Writing chemical formulae requires knowing the symbols and the combining capacity of elements.

The combining power or capacity of an element is called its **valency**. Valency helps determine how atoms of one element combine with atoms of another to form a compound. We can think of valency like the 'arms' of an atom that connect to other atoms.

Imagine a human with two arms and an octopus with eight arms. If one octopus needs to hold humans so all arms are linked, how many humans can it hold? Represent octopus as O and human as H. The octopus (O) has 8 arms, each human (H) has 2 arms. The octopus needs to connect all 8 arms. Each human can connect 2 arms. To balance the connections, the octopus's 8 arms need 8 human arms, which means 8 / 2 = 4 humans. So, one octopus combines with 4 humans. The "formula" would be $\mathrm{OH}_4$. The subscript '4' indicates the number of humans held.

### Rules for Writing Chemical Formulae:

1.  The valencies or charges on the ions must balance, resulting in a neutral compound.
2.  When a compound consists of a metal and a non-metal, the metal's name or symbol is written first (e.g., CaO, NaCl, FeS). Nonmetals are written on the right.
3.  In compounds with polyatomic ions, if there is more than one polyatomic ion, enclose its formula in brackets and write the number of ions outside the bracket (e.g., $\mathrm{Mg}(\mathrm{OH})_{2}$). If there is only one polyatomic ion, brackets are not needed (e.g., NaOH).

## Formulae of Simple Compounds

The simplest compounds, made of two different elements, are called **binary compounds**. We can use the valencies from the table to write their formulae. Write the symbols of the constituent elements and their valencies, then "criss-cross" the valencies to determine the subscripts.

### Questions

1. Write down the formulae of
    (i) sodium oxide
    (ii) aluminium chloride
    (iii) sodium sulphide
    (iv) magnesium hydroxide
    > **Answer:**
    > (i) Sodium oxide: Na (Valency 1), O (Valency 2). Criss-cross gives $\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{O}$.
    > (ii) Aluminium chloride: Al (Valency 3), Cl (Valency 1). Criss-cross gives $\mathrm{AlCl}_3$.
    > (iii) Sodium sulphide: Na (Valency 1), S (Valency 2). Criss-cross gives $\mathrm{Na}_2\mathrm{S}$.
    > (iv) Magnesium hydroxide: Mg (Valency 2), OH (Valency 1). Criss-cross gives $\mathrm{Mg(OH)}_2$.

2. Write down the names of compounds represented by the following formulae:
    (i) $\mathrm{Al}_{2}\left(\mathrm{SO}_{4}\right)_{3}$
    (ii) $\mathrm{CaCl}_{2}$
    (iii) $\mathrm{K}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$
    (iv) $\mathrm{KNO}_{3}$
    (v) $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3}$.
    > **Answer:**
    > (i) $\mathrm{Al}_{2}\left(\mathrm{SO}_{4}\right)_{3}$: Aluminium sulphate
    > (ii) $\mathrm{CaCl}_{2}$: Calcium chloride
    > (iii) $\mathrm{K}_{2}\mathrm{SO}_{4}$: Potassium sulphate
    > (iv) $\mathrm{KNO}_{3}$: Potassium nitrate
    > (v) $\mathrm{CaCO}_{3}$: Calcium carbonate

3. What is meant by the term chemical formula?
    > **Answer:** A chemical formula is a symbolic representation of the composition of a chemical compound. It shows the elements present in the compound and the relative number of atoms of each element in a molecule or formula unit of the compound.

4. How many atoms are present in a
    (i) $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ molecule and
    (ii) $\mathrm{PO}_{4}^{3-}$ ion?
    > **Answer:**
    > (i) In a $\mathrm{H}_{2}\mathrm{S}$ molecule, there are 2 atoms of Hydrogen (H) and 1 atom of Sulphur (S). Total number of atoms = 2 + 1 = 3 atoms.
    > (ii) In a $\mathrm{PO}_{4}^{3-}$ ion, there is 1 atom of Phosphorus (P) and 4 atoms of Oxygen (O). Total number of atoms = 1 + 4 = 5 atoms.
