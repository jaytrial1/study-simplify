This section explains the concept of single citizenship in India and describes the federal structure, including the division of powers through lists and residuary powers.

# Citizenship and Federal Structure

## Single Citizenship

Unlike countries like America which have dual citizenship (one for the United States and another for the State), India has only **single citizenship** irrespective of any region or State within the country.

## The Centre with Strong Federal Structure

India is described as a **Union of States**. The word 'Federal' is not explicitly used in the Constitution. The term 'Union of States' indicates a permanent and irreversible relationship between the Union and its Component States. India is a Union of States, and no State has the right to secede. Thus, India is a Union of States, yet it has some elements of a Federal Government.

In the Indian structure, there are two levels of Government: the **Union Government** and the **State Governments**. The Constitution clearly defines their areas of functioning. The powers are demarcated into different Lists of Subjects:

### Union List

The Union List comprises **97 subjects**. Subjects of national importance like defence, foreign affairs, atomic energy, banking, railway, communication, and post and telegraph are included.

### State List

The State List comprises **66 subjects**. These are important subjects on which the State Legislature can pass laws, such as law and order, State government institutions, agriculture and irrigation, health, land, and inter-state trade and commerce. If law and order break down in a State, the Union Government can send Reserved Police Force with or against the State's consent.

### Concurrent List

The Constitution also provides for a third list called the **Concurrent List**, which consists of **47 subjects** of common concern to both the Central and State Governments. Both the Central and State Governments can pass laws on these subjects. This list includes subjects like criminal and civil procedure, marriage and divorce, education, economic planning, and trade unions.

### Residuary Powers

Subjects not specifically allocated to the Union or State Lists are included in **'Residuary Powers'**. Matters not included in the division of powers are known as residuary powers. The Central Government is given the power to legislate on these residuary subjects.

### Financial Distribution

Financial resources are distributed between the Union Government and State Governments. Major taxes like excise and custom, export-import, and income tax are decided by the Central Government. Taxes with fewer financial resources, such as sales tax, revenue tax, entertainment tax, and educational tax, are decided by the State Government.
