This section delves into the detailed meaning and implications of the three main pillars mentioned in the Preamble: Democracy, Socialism, and Secularism.

# Key Pillars of the Preamble

The words inscribed in the Preamble include: We, the people of India SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST, SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC, REPUBLIC, JUSTICE, LIBERTY, FRATERNITY AND EQUALITY. Here, we will study the following three main pillars:

## Democracy

According to the Indian Constitution, the ultimate sovereignty of power is with the **people**. There is no particular regime of any one person; power resides with the people.

The word **'Democracy'** is derived from the Greek words 'Demos' meaning people and 'Kratos' meaning power. The country's power is not within a group of people but within the people of the country. A Democratic State administration is an arrangement providing equal justice (social, economic, and political) and equal rights of participation to the people.

In democratic India, people elect their leaders by casting votes to represent them in Parliament. These elected representatives can then assume positions like Prime Minister and Cabinet Ministers. Executives do not have absolute power and are given a specific time limit. Similarly, voters elect State and Local level Governments. Hence, Democracy means **by the people, for the people, and of the people**.

The Cabinet consists of Members of Parliament and is responsible to the Parliament. Any eligible citizen can contest elections; ministerial positions are not hereditary. Each elected Government has a 5-year tenure. This government works under the principles of the Constitution and is therefore known as a **'Responsible Government'**. The democratic government is based on liberty, equality, and fraternity, and continuously works to achieve these goals. The Constitution empowers people to cast their vote according to their free will, instilling confidence in the Constitution.

Provisions like Fundamental Rights, Principles for Political directions, Parliament, Member of Assembly, Independent and Impartial Justice, and Election Commission make India democratic in every sense.

**Adult Suffrage** means citizens above 18 years of age can cast their vote for their chosen leaders without discrimination based on caste, creed, religion, language, gender, education, income, or birthplace. It is essential that citizens are enlisted in the Electoral list.

## Socialist

The word **'Socialist'** was added to the Constitution in the year 1976 by the **42nd Indian Constitution Amendment**. Most provisions in the Constitution directly or indirectly reflect a 'Social Revolution' aimed at achieving social and economic equality and furthering the goal of a 'Welfare State'.

The principle of the socialistic pattern directs social, economic, and political equality for citizens as stated in the Preamble. It is now a prime feature of the State, reflecting India's commitment to securing social, economic, and political justice for all. India aims to abolish all forms of exploitation and secure equitable distribution of income, resources, and wealth. The goal is to prevent any single person from having power over all assets and ensure everyone has equal opportunity by accessing facilities and provisions from society, thereby eliminating discrimination between rich and poor. Thus, the Constitution of India is considered a socialistic document.

## Secularism

The word **'Secular'** was inserted in the year 1976 by the **42nd Indian Constitution Amendment**. India is a secular country. According to the Constitution, India can never become a country of one particular religion. India does not follow or promote any particular religion. The country cannot involve any secular activity with religious activity.

Citizens have the freedom to choose their religion, and the country cannot discriminate against a citizen based on religion. Equal job opportunities and political rights are given without religious discrimination. Secularism is a basic and mandatory feature of the Constitution.

The principles of **'Sarv Dharm Samdrishti'** (Equal view for all religions) and **'Sarv Dharm Sambhav'** (Equality of all religions) are incorporated. No particular religion is encouraged by the state. Citizens are free to propagate their religious belief, trust, and faith.
