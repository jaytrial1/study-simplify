This section details how the Indian Constitution was created, including the Constituent Assembly, key members, and the timeline.

# The Process of Framing the Constitution

Before independence, the British Government assigned the task of finding a solution for India's independence to the **Cabinet Mission** of three members on March 25, 1946. Based on the Cabinet Mission's recommendations, the **Constituent Assembly** was formed, and the structure of the Constitution was decided.

The Constituent Assembly had 389 members from various religions, castes, genders, geographical areas, political backgrounds, and expert fields. Notable members included Jawaharlal Nehru, Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel, Maulana Abdul Kalam Azad, Shyamprasad Mukerjee, H.P. Modi, H.V. Kamanth, Frank Anthony, Kanaiyalal Munshi, Krishna Swami Iyer, Baldev Singh, and women representatives like Sarojini Naidu and Vijay Lakshmi Pandit.

**Dr. Rajendra Prasad** was the President of the Constituent Assembly. A **Drafting Committee** was appointed under the Chairmanship of **Dr. Bhimrao Ambedkar** to draft the Constitution.

The Constituent Assembly began its work on November 9, 1946. It met for 166 sessions over 2 years, 11 months, and 17 days. Members discussed every detail of the provisions, considering the Constitutions of different countries.

Originally, the Constitution had 295 Articles and 8 Appendices. After amendments, it grew to 395 Articles and 9 Appendices (Note: The text later mentions 12 Appendices, indicating further changes).

The Constitution was unanimously passed in the Constituent Assembly on November 26, 1949. It came into existence on **January 26, 1950**, when India was declared a **'Republic Nation'**. Republic Day is celebrated every year on January 26th.

The Indian Constitution includes the four lions as the **'National Emblem'** and the national slogan **'Satyamev Jayate'** (Truth will always win). It contains provisions for citizenship, rights and duties, directive principles of State policy, Union-state relations, elections, and emergency provisions. This makes the Constitution of India a highly detailed and elaborated national document.
