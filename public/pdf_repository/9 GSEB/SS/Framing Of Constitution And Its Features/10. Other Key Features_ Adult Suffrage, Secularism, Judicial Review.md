This section discusses universal adult suffrage, reiterates secularism as a constitutional feature, and explains the concept of judicial review.

# Other Key Features

## Universal Adult Suffrage

India has a flexible system of **Adult Suffrage**. Any citizen who is 18 years of age or above has the right to vote in elections for Parliament, Legislative Assemblies, or Local Self-Government Bodies, without any discrimination based on education, property, or economic standard.

## Secularism

As discussed earlier in the Preamble section, India has been declared a **secular State** in the Constitution. People of different religions reside in India. The State cannot discriminate against any citizen on the basis of religion. No citizen can be given special rights or deprived of certain rights based on religion. The State must be absolutely impartial and neutral in respect of religion. Only a secular state can offer proper or equal treatment towards all citizens. Secularism means that the state does not interfere in matters of religion and is not biased towards any specific religion. The State must be secular and indifferent. Followers of every religion are free to follow and practice their religion of choice.

## Judicial Review

**Judicial review** is a chief characteristic of the Constitution. Judicial review administers the working of the Union and State within their jurisdiction. The Constitution attempts to harmonize the principles of judicial review without disrespecting the powers of Parliament. Parliamentary amendments, external orders, ordinances, and judicial judgments are subject to Judicial Review. If the court finds any discrepancy in external orders, ordinances, or judicial judgments, it can stop them by declaring them **unconstitutional**.
