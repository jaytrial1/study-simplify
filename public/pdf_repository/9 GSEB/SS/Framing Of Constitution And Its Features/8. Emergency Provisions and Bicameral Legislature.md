This section explains the provisions for managing emergencies in India and describes the bicameral structure of the Indian Parliament.

# Emergency Provisions and Bicameral Legislature

## Unified Arrangement during Crisis

The Constitution of India provides for three types of Emergencies:

*   **National Emergency:** Declared during war, external attack, or armed rebellion.
*   **Constitutional Emergency:** Declared when a State cannot function according to the Constitution due to a breakdown of law and order.
*   **Financial Emergency:** Declared due to a monetary value erosion caused by an increase in prices.

The Central Government has been given more powers to deal with these emergencies. During an emergency, India almost turns into a **unitary system**.

## Bicameral Practice

India has a **Parliamentary system of Governance**. In this system, the Parliament is the supreme authority representing the people. The Legislature at the Union level is the Parliament, which is **bicameral**, meaning it has two houses:

*   **Upper House:** Called the **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States).
*   **Lower House:** Called the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People).

Although the Government is carried on in the name of the President at the Union and the Governor in the States, the actual administration is carried out by the **Council of Ministers** headed by the Prime Minister at the Union and the Chief Minister in the States. The Council of Ministers is responsible to the Legislature, which comprises representatives of the people.

The President selects 12 experts from various fields for the Rajya Sabha. The Rajya Sabha is a **permanent house** where one-third of the members retire every two years, and new members are elected. Each member has a six-year tenure in the Rajya Sabha. The powers of the Rajya Sabha are less compared to the Lok Sabha, which is considered special, superior, and decisive. The Rajya Sabha cannot be completely dissolved.
