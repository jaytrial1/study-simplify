# The Principle of Division of Power

The functions of the State, carried out by the government, are diverse and require specific expertise. The principle of division of powers suggests that the functions of the three organs of government (Legislature, Executive, Judiciary) should be distinct and administered by different people. Each organ should primarily carry out its own functions.

This division helps prevent the misuse of power. By dividing powers, the functioning of the government becomes easier and more efficient. Although each organ has primary powers (e.g., Legislature frames laws), other organs are given some powers to control and check potential misuse by another organ. For example, the Executive and Judiciary have powers to check the Legislature, and the Legislature and Executive have powers to check the Judiciary.
