This section introduces the three main branches of the Indian government and their fundamental roles in maintaining law, order, and citizen welfare.

# Organs of Government

The government in India is composed of three main organs: the **Legislature**, the **Executive**, and the **Judiciary**. These organs work together to achieve the welfare of the nation, maintain law and order, and protect the freedom and fundamental rights of citizens.

*   The **Legislature** is responsible for framing laws.
*   The **Executive** is responsible for implementing the framed laws.
*   The **Judiciary** is responsible for maintaining law and order by punishing those who violate the law.

For the effective and efficient administration of the State, it is essential that these three organs function with mutual unity, cooperation, and coordination.

The structure of these organs in India is as follows:

*   **Legislature:**
    *   At the Centre: Parliament (Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha)
    *   At the State level: Legislative Assemblies and, in some states, Legislative Councils.
*   **Executive:**
    *   At the Centre: President, Prime Minister and Cabinet Ministers, and the administrative system.
    *   At the State level: Governor, Chief Minister and Cabinet, and the administrative system.
*   **Judiciary:**
    *   At the top: Supreme Court
    *   In the middle: High Courts of the States
    *   At the bottom: Subordinate courts, District courts, and Special Courts.

The Judiciary is a separate, independent, and organized entity, distinct from the Legislature and the Executive. India is a Union of States, and while no State is completely independent, the parliamentary system divides powers between the Centre and the State. The Legislature and Executive are interdependent, while the Judiciary remains separate, independent, and impartial.
