This section explains the concept of decentralization and describes the structure and functions of local self-governing bodies in rural and urban areas.

# Governance at the Local Level

Governing an entire nation from a single place is challenging due to the Central Government's vast responsibilities. **Decentralization** of power aims to solve local problems, needs, expectations, and aspirations in a timely and efficient manner. By decentralizing power, administration becomes easier and more efficient.

At the local level, **Institutes of Local Self Governance** function. These organizations administer villages, towns, or cities through representatives elected by the local people. This division of governmental responsibility and functions to local bodies enhances administrative efficiency for specific geographical regions.

Examples:

*   **Urban Areas:** Municipality, Municipal Corporation, Mega City Nigam.
*   **Rural Areas:** Gram Panchayat, Taluka Panchayat, District Panchayat.

Institutions of Local Self Governance provide valuable training in electoral processes, voting, duties, responsibilities, administration, and even acting as opposing parties. They offer opportunities for self-development. As policies are often implemented at the local level first, these institutions help identify and improve potential issues in policies designed at higher levels. Therefore, Local Self Rule Organizations are often called **training schools and laboratories of constitutional improvement in a democracy**.

In 1992, 'Panchayati Raj' and 'Urban Institutions of Local Self Governance' were established nationwide through constitutional amendments ($73^{\text{rd}}$ and $74^{\text{th}}$ respectively, though not explicitly stated as 73rd in the text, this is general knowledge).

### Three Tier Structure of the Panchayati Raj (Rural)

This is an administrative structure connecting from bottom to top:

1.  **Gram Panchayat:** At the village level.
    *   Elected representative: Sarpanch (elected by the people).
    *   Administrative head: Talati-cum-Minister (handles administration).
    *   Villages where Sarpanchs and members are elected unanimously are called 'Samras Villages' and receive recognition/prizes.
2.  **Taluka Panchayat:** At the block/taluka level.
    *   Leader of the elected wing: President.
    *   Administrative head: Taluka Development Officer (TDO).
3.  **District Panchayat:** At the district level.
    *   Chief of the elected wing: President.
    *   Chairmen of various committees.
    *   Administrative head: District Development Officer (DDO).
    *   Main office is at the District headquarter.

These organizations are provided with funds and resources to carry out local development tasks, schemes, and welfare programs efficiently.

### Urban Institute of Self Governance

Established as per the $74^{\text{th}}$ Constitutional Amendment based on population:

1.  **Municipality:** For smaller urban areas.
2.  **Municipal Corporation:** For larger urban areas.
3.  **Maha Nagar (Mega City) Nigam:** For very large urban areas (though "Maha Nagar Megacity Nigam" is an unusual term, the concept is for largest cities).

In these urban bodies:

*   A **Mayor** is elected from the majority wing.
*   There are Chairmen of various Committees and Corporators representing different wards.
*   Reservation for women and reserved castes is maintained ward-wise.

Various committees handle responsibilities in a decentralized manner, including:

*   City Planning, land reforms, roads, bridges, flyovers.
*   Water management, sewage management.
*   Civil development, environmental facilities.
*   Fire department service.
*   Education, health, culture, entertainment.
*   City scape development.
*   Development of morgues, crematoria, graveyards.
*   Slum removal, overall development.

These activities are funded through taxes collected from people, government grants, and Public Private Partnerships (PPP).

*   The administrative head of the municipal corporation is the **Municipal Commissioner**.
*   Under the Commissioner are zonal heads and various officers with technical and administrative knowledge.

Cordial relations between the elected wing and the administrative wing are crucial for effective work related to public welfare.
