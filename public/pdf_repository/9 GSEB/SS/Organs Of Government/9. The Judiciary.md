This section describes the structure and role of the Indian Judiciary, emphasizing its function in upholding justice and interpreting the Constitution.

# Judiciary

The Constitution of India aims to ensure social, economic, and political justice for all citizens, thereby establishing a just and equitable social order. The Judiciary plays a crucial role in this by:

*   Punishing or fining individuals who break the law.
*   Checking if laws are in accordance with the constitutional provisions.
*   Declaring laws unconstitutional and void if they are not consistent with the Constitution.

In a federal system, where powers are divided between the Centre and States, the Judicial System ensures that both levels of government operate within their defined spheres without interfering with each other. Disputes between the Centre and a State, or between States, are resolved by the Supreme Court.

The Indian judiciary has a **continuous and linear structure**:

*   **Top Level (Central):** Supreme Court
*   **Middle Level (State):** High Courts
*   **Bottom Level (Lower Courts):** Trial Courts, Civil Courts, Criminal Courts, and various subordinate Courts.

This hierarchical structure allows for appeals from lower courts to higher courts, ensuring a system of checks and balances within the judiciary itself.
