This section covers the Executive branch at the state level, including the Governor and Chief Minister, and discusses the role of the administrative executive (bureaucracy).

# The State Executive

The Executive branch at the state level includes the Governor, the Chief Minister, and the Council of Ministers, supported by the administrative system.

## The Governor

The **Governor** is the constitutional and formal head of the State.

*   **Appointment:** Appointed by the President of India on the advice of the Central Council of Ministers headed by the Prime Minister. A person can be appointed Governor of more than one state.
*   **Qualification:** Must be a citizen of India and 35 years of age or above.
*   **Term:** Typically 5 years, but holds office during the "Pleasure" of the President. Can be removed or transferred to another state before the term expires.

### Powers and Functions

All executive powers of the State government are vested in the Governor, though exercised in reality by the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers.

*   **Executive Powers:**
    *   Appoints the Chief Minister (leader of the party with a clear majority).
    *   Appoints other members of the Council of Ministers on the advice of the Chief Minister.
    *   Appoints the Advocate General, the Chairman, and members of the State Public Service Commission.
    *   Is consulted by the President for the appointment of High Court judges.
*   **Legislative Powers:**
    *   Summons and prorogues sessions of the State Legislature.
    *   Can dissolve the State Legislative Assembly before its full term.
    *   Can issue ordinances when the legislature is not in session.
    *   A Bill passed by the State Legislature becomes law only after the Governor's assent.
*   **Other Roles:**
    *   Normally, the Governor is the *ex-officio* Chancellor of all universities in the State.

While the Governor's position is similar to the President's at the Centre, their powers are largely exercised by the state's Council of Ministers. However, the Governor can exercise certain powers independently. The Governor is expected to perform duties firmly and impartially, regardless of political affiliation.

# The Chief Minister and Council of Ministers

The **Chief Minister** is the real head of the State Government.

*   **Appointment:** Appointed by the Governor. The leader of the majority party or coalition in the Legislative Assembly is appointed as Chief Minister. If no single party gets a majority, the leader of the largest party with majority support may be appointed.
*   **Council of Ministers:** Other members of the Council of Ministers are appointed by the Governor on the advice of the Chief Minister.
*   **Authority:** While administration is formally in the Governor's name, the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers exercise most powers.

The Chief Minister distributes portfolios among the Cabinet Ministers. The Chief Minister is responsible for policy making, transparent administration, addressing public concerns, providing leadership to the State Legislature, and guiding the state's growth policies. During calamities, the Chief Minister ensures basic amenities and support for the people.

The State Cabinet Ministry can include different types of Ministers: Cabinet, State Level, Deputy Level, and Parliamentary Secretary. The Governor administers the oath of secrecy to the Chief Minister and Council of Ministers.

## Administrative System (Administrative Executive)

The Administrative Executive implements the policies framed by the Political Executive. They advise and guide the political executive, providing necessary information and data, though the political executive is not bound by their advice.

Effective implementation by the Administrative Executive is crucial for the success of policies and laws. The administrative system supports the Executive in formulating and implementing policies. Experienced public servants in various fields like foreign relations, defence, finance, trade, etc., are considered the backbone of the government.

*   At the District Level, the **Collector** is the administrative head.

Their expertise, insight, experience, and efficiency are vital for effective administration. The State Executive acts as the "brain" for people's welfare, while the Administrative Executive is like the "limbs". Amicable relations between the political and administrative executives are essential for smooth governance.

### Challenges and Controls

The bureaucracy's power and influence are significant. Evils like nepotism, corruption, dishonesty, inefficiency, misconduct, and escapism can affect it. To control corruption, mechanisms like **Lokpal or Lokayukt** are needed. The Gujarat State Government has an Anti-Corruption Bureau with a toll-free number (18002334 4444) for complaints.

The Administrative System provides political stability, especially in governments formed by different parties, by implementing developmental schemes and programs consistently.
