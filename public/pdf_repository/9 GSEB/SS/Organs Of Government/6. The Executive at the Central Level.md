This section describes the Executive branch of the government, focusing on the Central Executive, including the President, Vice President, and Prime Minister.

# The Executive

The Executive is considered a highly efficient, effective, and central organ of the government. The **Union Executive** (Central Government) includes the political executive (President, Vice President, Prime Minister, and Cabinet Ministers) and the administrative executive (bureaucrats).

The Executive implements the laws framed by the Legislature. The administrative executive specifically carries out policies, programs, and planning formulated by the government to meet the aspirations and expectations of the people.

### Political vs. Administrative Executive

*   **Political Executive:** Changes periodically (typically every five years or when the government changes). Members are elected based on public choice and party affiliation. Their primary qualification is getting elected.
*   **Administrative Executive:** Composed of permanently appointed bureaucrats (civil servants). They remain in service until retirement (age varies by state). They can be removed before retirement for misbehaviour or inefficiency after an inquiry. Their appointment requires educational qualifications, quality, experience, and passing competitive examinations. This service is known as the **Civil Service**.

At the Centre, the Political Executive consists of the President, Vice President, Prime Minister, and Cabinet of Ministers as per the Constitution.

## President

The **President** is the Constitutional Head of India and the first citizen of the Republic. All executive powers of the federal government are vested in the President by the Constitution, and the administration of the Central government is carried out in their name.

### Qualification

To be a Presidential candidate, a person must:

*   Be a citizen of India.
*   Be 35 years of age or above.
*   Not be a salaried government employee or hold any office of profit.
*   Not be a member of either House of Parliament or a State Legislative Assembly (if elected while holding such a position, they must vacate it).

### Election and Term

The President is elected indirectly by an electoral college consisting of:

*   Elected members of both Houses of Parliament.
*   Elected members of the State Legislative Assemblies.

The President is elected for a term of **5 years** and can contest the election again after their term ends. While in office, no criminal case can be framed against the President, nor can an order of arrest or imprisonment be issued.

### Functions and Powers

The President holds vast executive, administrative, and other powers:

*   **Executive Powers:**
    *   Appoints the leader of the majority party in the general election as the Prime Minister.
    *   Appoints other Cabinet Ministers in consultation with the Prime Minister, administers oaths, and distributes portfolios.
    *   Cannot dissolve the Cabinet as long as it has majority support in Lok Sabha.
    *   Is the Head of the defence forces (Supreme Commander).
    *   Has the power to declare war, cease war, or conclude treaties with other countries.
    *   Appoints Judges of the Supreme Court and High Courts, Governors of States, Attorney General, Comptroller and Auditor General, Head of defence forces, Chairman of Union Public Service Commission, and Ambassadors to various nations.
*   **Legislative Powers:**
    *   Summons and prorogues sessions of both Houses of Parliament.
    *   Can dissolve the Lok Sabha.
    *   A Bill becomes an Act only after the President's assent.
*   **Judicial Powers:**
    *   Can grant pardon, reduce, postpone, or change the nature of punishment for a convicted person.
*   **Emergency Powers:**
    *   Can declare a **National Emergency** if the security of India or any part is threatened by war, external aggression, or armed rebellion (cannot be challenged in court).
    *   Can declare a **Constitutional Emergency (President's Rule)** in a State based on the Governor's report if the state government cannot function constitutionally, dissolving the State Cabinet Ministry.
    *   Can declare a **Financial Emergency**, potentially reducing salaries of all government officials, including judges.

While the President possesses these extensive powers constitutionally and administration is carried out in their name, in reality, these powers are largely exercised by the **Prime Minister and the Council of Ministers**.

## The Vice President

The **Vice President** performs the functions of the President in the President's absence.

*   **Election:** Elected by the members of both Houses of Parliament.
*   **Term:** 5 years.
*   **Role:** Is the *ex-officio* Chairman of the Rajya Sabha.
*   **Resignation:** The President submits their resignation to the Vice President.

## Prime Minister

The **Prime Minister** is the real head of the Central Government.

*   **Appointment:** Appointed formally by the President, typically the leader of the party or coalition that has achieved a majority in the general elections.
*   **Role in Council of Ministers:** Appoints Cabinet Ministers on the counsel of the Prime Minister, distributes portfolios, and decides who is included or excluded from the Cabinet.
*   **Leadership:** Presides over Cabinet meetings, looks after the functioning of various departments, and takes pivotal decisions.
*   **Head of Niti Aayog:** The Prime Minister is the head of the Niti Aayog (formerly Planning Commission).

There are typically three levels of ministers under the Prime Minister: Cabinet Ministers, State Ministers, and Deputy Ministers. The Prime Minister must be a member of any one House of the Parliament.

### Responsibility of Ministers

*   Ministers are individually responsible for their Ministries and departments.
*   The **Council of Ministers** is **collectively responsible and accountable to the Lok Sabha**. If the Lok Sabha rejects a government policy on an issue, the entire Council of Ministers must accept responsibility and resign, not just the minister concerned.
