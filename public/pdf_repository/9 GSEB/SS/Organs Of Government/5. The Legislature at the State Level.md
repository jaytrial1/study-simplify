This section covers the legislative bodies operating at the state level in India.

# Legislature at the State Level

Each State has a Legislature.

*   **Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha):** This is the Lower House in states with a bicameral legislature and the sole house in states with a unicameral legislature.
*   **Legislative Council (Vidhan Parishad):** This is the Upper House in states with a bicameral legislature (e.g., Bihar, Maharashtra, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu). Gujarat does not have a Legislative Council.

### Composition of Legislative Assembly

The number of members varies by state, as per constitutional provisions.

*   Minimum members: 60
*   Maximum members: 500
*   Gujarat Legislative Assembly currently has 182 members.

### Qualification for Membership of the State Legislature

To be a member of the Legislative Assembly, a person must:

*   Be a citizen of India.
*   Be 25 years of age or more.
*   Not be in a state of unsound mind, insolvent, or a criminal.

### Time Period

The Legislative Assembly is **not a permanent House**. Its term is **5 years**, after which it is dissolved. In unusual situations, if the state government cannot function constitutionally or a government cannot be formed, the Governor can recommend to the President of India to dissolve the Assembly and impose **'President's Rule'** in the state. During President's Rule, the Governor administers the state.

The Legislative Assembly elects a Speaker and Deputy Speaker from among its members for smooth functioning.

### Composition and Term of Legislative Council

Members of the Legislative Council are elected from various electorates:

*   Institutions of Local Self Governance.
*   Registered graduates.
*   Teachers of secondary and higher secondary schools.

To be eligible for the Legislative Council, a candidate must be a citizen of India and 30 years of age or more.

The Legislative Council is a **permanent House**.

*   Term of a member: 6 years.
*   Retirement: $\frac{2}{3}$rd of its members retire every 2 years.

Each state decides whether to have a Legislative Council. Gujarat does not have one.

### Powers

Similar to the Lok Sabha at the Centre, the Legislative Assembly generally has more power than the Legislative Council (where it exists). Ordinary and Money Bills can be presented in the State Legislature. After passing through the required stages, Bills are sent to the **Governor** for assent. A Bill becomes an Act after the Governor signs it.
