This section outlines the various impacts of the war, including human and material losses, social changes, and the controversial peace treaty.

# Effects of the First World War

The First World War had profound and lasting effects.

## Immediate Effects

*   **Destruction of Men and Material:** Approximately 65 million people participated in the war. At least 10 million died, about 20 million were injured, and around 7 million were permanently handicapped. Many more died from starvation, epidemics, and other post-war issues. The total cost of the war was immense.
*   **Social Change:** With most men away at the war front, women took on responsibilities for families and occupations. They entered various professional fields, boosting their self-confidence and sense of equality with men. This led to increased demands for women's suffrage (the right to vote). The war also caused scarcity of basic necessities, leading to problems like unemployment, starvation, strikes, and lockouts. People suffered greatly, and the condition of children was particularly dire.
*   **The Treaty of Versailles (June 1919):** The war ended with Germany's unconditional surrender to the Allied Nations. The "Peace Process" was conducted in Paris, involving 58 commissions and 145 sessions. The agreement specifically with Germany was signed in the Mirror Palace at Versailles, hence known as the **Treaty of Versailles**. The treaty had four main provisions:
    1.  Regional arrangements (territorial changes).
    2.  Reduction in troops and disarmament.
    3.  Arrangements for distributing war gains and imposing war fines.
    4.  Other provisions.

    Key figures involved included American President **Woodrow Wilson**, British Prime Minister **Lord George**, French President **Clemenceau**, and Italian President **Orlando**. Germany was held responsible for the war and faced severe penalties:
    *   A war fine of 6.5 billion pounds.
    *   Ceding the Ruhr region to France.
    *   The Rhine river being opened for international navigation.
    *   The Rhineland region bordering France being demilitarised and its mineral wealth given to France for 15 years.
    *   Most German organisations being confiscated.
    *   Alsace and Lorraine being returned to France.
    *   Germany required to annually provide large quantities of coal and iron to France and other Allied Nations as war benefits.

    Germany was forced to sign these harsh terms, causing bitterness and desperation among its population and leading to a severe failure of the German economy.

## Long Term Effects

The punitive nature of the treaties signed with the defeated nations fostered a sense of revenge. The peace established by the Treaty of Versailles was fragile. Socialist Russia was excluded from the newly formed **League of Nations**, and America did not join it either. Thus, it is often argued that the terms of the First World War peace process contained the seeds of the Second World War.
