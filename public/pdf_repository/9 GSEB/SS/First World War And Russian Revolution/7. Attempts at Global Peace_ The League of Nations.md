This section discusses the international efforts made after the First World War to prevent future conflicts, focusing on the establishment and goals of the League of Nations.

# Efforts for World Peace

The terrible experiences of the World War highlighted the absolute necessity of achieving world peace. This led to the recognition of a need for an international organisation dedicated to this goal. Active and immediate thinking about world peace became essential. American President **Woodrow Wilson** played a substantial role in advocating for the establishment of such an organisation. His **14 points** were presented during the "Paris Peace Process". On January 10, 1920, the **League of Nations** was formally established.

## Aims of the League of Nations

The primary goals of the League of Nations were:

*   To maintain international peace and security.
*   To ensure every nation respects the unity and integrity of other nations.
*   To encourage nations to abandon the policy of war.
*   To foster the development of positive international relations.
*   To resolve international disputes peacefully through negotiation or mediation.
*   To declare any nation ignoring the League or its mediation as a 'rebel' nation.

Despite being established to promote world peace, the League of Nations proved unable to control the imperialist policies of powerful nations. Consequently, the **Second World War** began in 1939, indicating the League's failure to prevent another major global conflict.
