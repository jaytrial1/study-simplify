This section details how European powers established and expanded their colonies across Asian and African continents, highlighting key examples and conflicts.

# Colonial Expansion in Asia-Africa

Western European countries sought to expand their empires not only overseas but also by taking control of neighbouring countries. Spain, for instance, controlled the Netherlands, Belgium, and Luxembourg. France later captured some of these provinces. When the Portuguese king died without an heir, Portugal came under Spanish rule due to a blood relation. This gave Spain a monopoly on establishing colonies outside Europe for six decades.

Similarly, Italian and German states were targets of French and Austrian ambitions. As European powers tightened their grip on Asian and African nations, many recently independent European nations followed England's example, turning Afro-Asian nations into their colonies.

## Colonialism in Asia

After consolidating its control over India, England expanded its empire to Sri Lanka, Myanmar (Burma), Singapore, and Malaysia. England also entered China, leading to the **Opium Wars** (1839-1842) due to the opium trade. China lost these wars, ceding five ports to England for trade and expansion. Other nations like Japan, Russia, Germany, France, Belgium, and America also gained trade and political rights in China, exploiting its weakened state.

The vast oil resources in West Asia's desert regions also attracted European powers. England, Germany, Russia, and America established oil companies in Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Saudi Arabia, and Bahrain to secure their interests.

## Colonialism in Africa

The Dutch established the first European colony in South Africa at the end of the 15th century. Subsequently, England colonised the Cape region and France colonised Algeria in North Africa. Post-Industrial Revolution, Africa's large territories were ideal for acquiring raw materials and selling finished goods, intensifying European competition for colonies.

*   **King Leopold of Belgium** seized vast areas of Congo.
*   England established colonies in Egypt, parts of East Africa, and South Africa.
*   France colonised Tunisia, Morocco, and provinces in West Africa.
*   Germany established its empire in parts of East and West Africa.
*   Italy captured African provinces around the Red Sea.
*   Spain and Portugal also took control of some African provinces.

Amidst this competition, a conference was held in Berlin (1884-85) where African territories were divided among European nations. This solidified the colonial dominance of various European nations across the entire continent of Africa.

While European nations were colonising Asia and Africa, Germany and Italy completed their political unification processes. Unified Germany quickly entered the competition for industrialisation, commercialisation, and colonisation. These rapid global changes politically, economically, and socially exploited some countries, hindering their development and contributing to the conditions that led to the First World War.
