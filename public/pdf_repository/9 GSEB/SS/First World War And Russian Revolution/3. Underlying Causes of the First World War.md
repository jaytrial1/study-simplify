This section explores the various factors and tensions that accumulated and ultimately triggered the First World War.

# Factors Leading to the First World War

The First World War is a significant and tragic event in modern history, caused by multiple factors. The war began on August 1, 1914. A key underlying issue was the outcome of the **Sedan War**, where Germany defeated France. France was forced to sign the **Frankfurt Treaty** (1871), paying a war fine and ceding the provinces of Lorraine and Alsace to Germany. France's inability to overcome this humiliation meant that the treaty itself sowed the seeds for a future conflict; the end of one war led to the potential for another.

## Economic Factors

In the 19th century, England built a vast empire in Asia and Africa, exploiting its colonies economically to enrich itself. Driven by industrialisation and rapid colonisation, Europe needed large quantities of raw materials. In the late 19th century, Germany began competing for markets in Asia and Africa, supplying cheaper goods than England and France. This challenged the dominance of England and France in Afro-Asian markets, leading to intense economic competition between Germany and England.

## Militarism

Military strength became crucial for European nations competing for expansion. Countries like England, France, Austria, and Russia began expanding their military power. Nations like Japan, Italy, and Germany introduced compulsory military training. Weapon production increased under the guise of self-defence. This rise in militarism, combined with economic competition, created a volatile atmosphere ripe for war.

## Groupism and Secret Treaties

The formation of alliances and secret treaties played a major role in the First World War. Before the war, the world was divided into two main groups:
*   **Central Powers:** Germany, Austria, Hungary, Bulgaria, and Turkey
*   **Allied Powers:** England, France, Russia, and Japan

A climate of jealousy, enmity, mistrust, fear, and hatred developed between these groups, significantly contributing to the outbreak of the war.

## Feeling of Fierce Nationalism

Initially, nationalism led to the independence of Belgium and Greece, and the unification of Germany and Italy. However, nationalism in Europe later took an extreme and narrow form. Economic rivalries, competition, and colonialism intensified to the point where national interests clashed fiercely. Leading European nations promoted extreme, destructive nationalism, encouraging love for one's own nation and hatred for others. The German Chief **Kaizer William** was a key proponent of this aggressive nationalism and colonialism, seeking "world leadership" through military power. This spread a warlike sentiment across nations, contributing to the First World War.

## Role of Newspapers

Newspapers in European nations fuelled hatred among people with derogatory, provocative, exaggerated, and false reports against rival nations. This made it difficult for rulers to pursue peace and harmony.

## Philosophy about War

The belief that "War is Welfare" gained traction in Europe. German writer **Trotsky** published maxims like "Only the mighty deserve to live" and "War alone is the national need". Another German writer, **Nietzsche**, described war as "a sacred work".

## The Immediate Cause

The assassination of the Prince of Austria and his wife by a member of the Serbian terrorist organisation "Black Hand" served as the immediate trigger. Austria blamed Serbia and issued an ultimatum demanding the culprit be handed over within 48 hours. Serbia denied involvement. Without further negotiation, Austria declared war on Serbia, initiating the First World War.
