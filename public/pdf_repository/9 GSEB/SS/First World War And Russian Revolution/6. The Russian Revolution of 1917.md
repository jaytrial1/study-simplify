This section describes the causes, key events, and outcome of the Russian Revolution, a pivotal moment in world history.

# The Russian/Bolshevik Revolution (1917)

The **Russian Revolution** (1917) is considered a highly significant event in world history. For centuries, Russians had been oppressed by the **Czar** (tsar) rulers, who were autocratic dictators with absolute power and denied people any rights. The Czars were known for their cruelty; anyone demanding rights faced torture, suppression, severe punishment, or exile to the harsh, cold region of Siberia. This oppressive rule caused widespread grief, poverty, and suffering. Russian farmers, labourers, and peasants worked hard but received insufficient income, leading to extreme poverty.

On January 22, 1905 (a Sunday), a large rally led by **Father Gapon** marched to the Czar's residence, the Winter Palace. The protestors were unarmed, some carrying pictures of the Czar with loyal captions. The Czar's army opened fire on these innocent people, killing thousands and staining the snow in Petersburg (where the palace was) red with blood. This day became known as "**Bloody Sunday**".

Around this time, the weakness of the Czardom was exposed when a small nation like Japan defeated the massive Russian Empire in the **Russia-Japan War** (1904-05), provoking and unsettling many Russians. To pacify the angry populace, the Czar announced the calling of a **Duma** (Legislative Assembly), which had not been convened for years. Four Dumas were eventually called, but each was dissolved before it could implement significant changes to satisfy the citizens.

On March 8, 1917, oppressed workers in Petrograd called for a strike. The Czar sent troops to suppress the strike, but the army refused to fire on the people. This refusal effectively marked the beginning of the revolution. After the fall of the Czardom, power initially went to **Karensky**, leader of the **Menshevik** group (the minority faction). Most people in Russia were happy with the Czar's overthrow, except for **Lenin**.

Lenin, influenced by **Karl Marx**, believed in the central importance of the labourers, whereas the Mensheviks focused on the middle class. Consequently, Lenin incited the **Bolsheviks** against the Mensheviks. In November 1917, the Bolsheviks launched their final revolution, seizing power. This event became known as the **Socialist Bolshevik Revolution**.

Thus, the 300-year-old Czardom ended, and Russia was, for the first time, without a Czar.
