# Impact of Disasters

This section details the various ways disasters affect human life, property, infrastructure, the economy, and social structures.

Disasters affect almost everyone, but their worst effects are felt by the poor and underprivileged. The impacts of disasters can be categorized as follows:

## Effects on Physical Features:

*   Heavy damage or total loss of movable and immovable property.
*   Severe damage to infrastructure like roads, railways, bridges, electricity, gas, and telecommunication facilities, which cannot be immediately restored.
*   Loss of fertile agricultural land due to floods, a loss that is difficult to compensate for even over a long time.

## Effects on Human Life:

*   Many people die or become permanently handicapped.
*   The health of citizens deteriorates.
*   Those who lose relatives suffer intense **trauma** and despair, requiring significant effort to help them recover.
*   The situation of orphaned children and elderly people becomes difficult when their support system is lost; their rehabilitation requires intense effort.
*   People face numerous difficulties in their daily lives.

## Effects on Economy:

*   Huge capital is needed for rehabilitation work after a disaster, affecting routine developmental projects.
*   Projects are delayed due to capital shortages.
*   Unemployment emerges until industrial units are reactivated.
*   The economy of the disaster-affected area weakens.

## Social Effects of Disasters:

*   Migration or fleeing by people affects the social structure of the area.
*   Social festivals and public celebrations become less engaging compared to before. Restoring the original charm takes many years.
*   Social organizations weaken due to changes in the social network.
