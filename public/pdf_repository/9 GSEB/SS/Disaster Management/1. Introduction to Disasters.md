# Introduction to Disasters

This section defines disasters and categorizes them into natural and man-induced types, highlighting their devastating impact.

A disaster can be **natural** or **man-induced**. It can be **catastrophic** (sudden, violent) or **diastrophic** (slow, gradual changes). Disasters can originate from within the earth or in the atmosphere, affecting large areas and causing widespread devastation. Phenomena like volcanic eruptions, earthquakes, and tsunamis can result from a combination of natural processes and, sometimes, human activities.

Some events cause pre-planned destruction, such as the atomic bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki during World War II. Others are results of human negligence or mistakes, like the **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** or the **Chernobyl Nuclear Disaster**.

## Types of Disasters

Experts divide disasters into two main categories:

1.  **Natural Disasters:** These include floods, cyclones, tsunamis, droughts, earthquakes, volcanoes, and forest fires. Forecasts are possible for some (floods, cyclones, tsunamis, drought) but not others (earthquake, volcanic eruptions, forest fire).
2.  **Man-induced Disasters:** These include fires, industrial accidents, bomb explosions, riots, and viral diseases.
