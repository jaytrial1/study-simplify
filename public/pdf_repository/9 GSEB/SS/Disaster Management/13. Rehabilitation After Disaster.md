# Rehabilitation After Disaster

This section focuses on the complex process of rehabilitation that follows rescue and relief operations after a disaster, highlighting the challenges involved.

After a disaster, the sequence of operations is rescue, followed by compensation or relief, and finally, **rehabilitation**, which varies depending on the type of disaster.

*   After earthquakes, floods, or cyclones, many buildings need to be reconstructed.
*   After a drought, new employment opportunities must be created, and provisions for farm implements are needed for agriculture.
*   After viral epidemics, mass education and public awareness programs are necessary for future safety.

Rehabilitation work faces many obstacles because infrastructural facilities are damaged. It is particularly difficult to rehabilitate families with only one or two survivors. Timely psychological support from a psychiatrist is crucial for individuals suffering trauma after witnessing a disaster; lack of such support can have adverse results. Training and employment are necessary for survivors who become permanently handicapped.

Thus, rehabilitation after a disaster is a very challenging task. It can only be achieved through proper, step-by-step planning and implementation.
