# Natural Disasters: Earthquakes

This section describes earthquakes, their cause, the difficulty in forecasting them, and crucial safety actions during and after an earthquake.

An **earthquake** is the shaking of the Earth's surface. It is caused by tectonic activities in the Earth's interior. Major earthquake regions are often areas with weaker relief on the surface. While probable earthquake regions can be identified, earthquakes cannot be accurately forecast, leading to significant damage due to lack of preparation time.

### What to do:

*   During an earthquake, stay beneath a sturdy object like a large bench or table.
*   If in school, sit beneath a bench.
*   If in an open space, stay away from buildings, balconies, electric power lines, and electric poles.
*   If driving, bring your vehicle to a halt away from bridges (if possible, or under if structure is known to be safe), electric poles, power lines, and traffic signals.
*   Remain seated in your vehicle until the tremors are over.
*   Even after tremors stop, things like fridges, wall hangings, or ceiling fans might fall; stay away from them.
*   Listen to the local radio for instructions and guidance.
*   New buildings should be constructed to be **earthquake-proof**.

### What not to do:

*   Do not shout or flee in panic.
*   Do not try to prevent things from falling down after tremors.
*   Do not use elevators.
*   If in a house, do not stand near wooden cabinets, safes, mirrors, or glass chandeliers.
*   Do not light a matchstick or lighter, or switch on electric gadgets, before confirming that cooking gas is not leaking, as leaking gas can explode.
*   Do not make phone calls unless it's for emergency medical help or fire. Excessive calls can overload the network and obstruct relief work.
