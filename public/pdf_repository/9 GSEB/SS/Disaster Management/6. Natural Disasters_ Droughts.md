# Natural Disasters: Droughts

This section discusses droughts, their connection to water and food scarcity, and strategies for managing their impact.

A **drought** is a highly destructive hazard with long-lasting impacts, primarily related to the scarcity of water and food essential for life. Droughts occur in regions with monsoon climates characterized by irregular and scanty rainfall. Since agriculture, food grains, and organisms depend heavily on water, a water crisis leads to significant shortages. This causes heavy damage to agriculture and natural vegetation. While in the past, many people died from starvation during droughts, improved transportation and planned management have reduced such fatalities.

### What to do:

*   Intensify the use of **drip irrigation** in agriculture.
*   Plan food rationing and water use based on estimated availability.
*   Arrange for fair price distribution of food grains and relief work.
*   Stop construction activities unless absolutely necessary.

### What not to do:

*   To prevent food wastage, avoid holding large-scale dinner parties.
*   Citizens should not hoard food grains or fodder.
