# Natural Disasters: Forest Fires

This section describes forest fires, their causes (mostly man-induced), the conditions that facilitate their spread, and safety measures.

A **forest fire** is a fire that occurs in forests and causes devastation. Except for lightning, most forest fires are **man-induced**. Major causes include carelessly discarded burning cigarette butts or matchsticks, and burning items left by tourists, pilgrims, or shepherds.

Forest fires are more likely during dry seasons after autumn when dry grass and fallen leaves act as fuel. The fire spreads faster during windy days with high temperatures and low humidity when both wind and fuel are dry. Some trees that ooze oily, inflammable material also contribute to faster spread.

Once started, a forest fire spreads in the direction of the wind, potentially advancing at about 15 km per hour on all sides. If not promptly doused, it stops only under two conditions: (i) it extinguishes completely on its own, or (ii) heavy rain occurs. Smoke and sparks rising with warm air currents during a forest fire pose a risk to nearby settlements.

### What to do:

*   Act according to instructions from the Forest Department.
*   Arrange special patrolling in forests during dry seasons.
*   Train Forest Department employees in extinguishing forest fires.

### What not to do:

*   Do not throw burning bidis or other lighted items while travelling through a forest.
*   People living near forest fire areas should not ignore instructions from the Forest Department.
