# Natural Disasters: Tsunamis

This section defines tsunamis, explains how they are generated, their destructive nature, and safety measures.

**Tsunami** refers to destructive and powerful sea waves. They are generated at the sea bottom by earthquakes rating 7 or more on the Richter scale, underwater volcanic eruptions, or large-scale landslides on ocean floors. The word "Tsunami" means '**destructive waves**' in Japanese. They are often called oceanic earthquake waves as they are frequently caused by underwater earthquakes.

These waves travel very fast in a circular pattern from their origin. In the deep sea, they are not very noticeable due to short wave heights. However, as they approach coasts and shallow areas, they slow down but increase significantly in height, turning into a devastating wall of water near the coast.

A highly destructive tsunami on December 26, 2004, affected countries like Thailand, India, Indonesia, and Sri Lanka, victimizing over 2 lakh people. Modern equipment allows for forecasting and predicting the arrival time of tsunamis, enabling prevention of more deaths through precautions.

### What to do:

*   With a tsunami warning, move to a safer place away from the sea coast.
*   Keep a radio handy and follow instructions from authorities.
*   New buildings and settlements near the coast should be constructed considering the potential impact of tsunamis.
*   Attempts should be made to expand **mangrove** forests, as they help reduce the impact of tsunamis.

### What not to do:

*   Do not take shelter in tall buildings near the coast, as destructive waves can collapse them.
*   After the tsunami recedes, do not approach the sea coast until authorities give instructions.
