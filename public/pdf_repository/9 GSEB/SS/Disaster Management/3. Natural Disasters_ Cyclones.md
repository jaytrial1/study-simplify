# Natural Disasters: Cyclones

This section explains cyclones (and similar storms by other names), their formation, and safety guidelines for areas prone to them.

**Cyclones** are severe storms that strike suddenly due to atmospheric disturbances. They are known by different names globally: Cyclone in the Indian sub-continent, Hurricane and Tornado in the USA, and Typhoon along the coasts of China and Japan. These storms form due to unequal atmospheric pressure and cause devastation in the areas they pass over, particularly affecting coastal regions like eastern India and Kachchh-Saurashtra.

### What to do:

*   Keep watching news bulletins on TV and radio for anticipated storm times.
*   If you have a radio, keep an extra battery ready.
*   Keep your mobile phone fully charged; charge power banks in advance if available.
*   Listen carefully to and implement instructions and warnings broadcast over radio.
*   Stay away from rumours.
*   Store additional food, dry breakfast, and drinking water.
*   Keep provisions of food and medicines necessary for children and elderly people.
*   If a rescue team instructs you to vacate your home, follow their instructions immediately.
*   Use **pure and safe drinking water**.
*   If sheltered by the government or an NGO, follow their instructions and do not leave without permission.
*   Do not keep domesticated animals tied; if free, they can defend themselves better.

### What not to do:

*   Do not pay attention to gossips or rumours; only trust instructions from the Weather Department on Radio.
*   Do not go into open areas if the atmosphere suddenly calms down, rain stops, and wind becomes still, as wind or rainfall may strike suddenly again.
*   Do not touch electric poles or loose wires; stay away from them.
*   Do not take shelter near large hoardings or big trees.
