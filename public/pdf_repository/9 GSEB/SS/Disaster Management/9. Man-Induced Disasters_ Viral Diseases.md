# Man-Induced Disasters: Viral Diseases

This section discusses viral diseases spreading as epidemics, their dangers, and safety measures to prevent their spread.

When a large number of people over a wide area become victims of a disease, it is called an **epidemic**. Epidemics can cause many deaths. Viral diseases, like dengue, ebola, swine flu, and influenza, can spread faster than general diseases and are very dangerous. Historically, diseases like the plague caused millions of deaths, but scientific advancements and vaccines have helped control them. However, new viral diseases or those resistant to traditional medicines pose a constant threat of widespread deaths.

Examples include the plague epidemic in Surat in September 1994 and recent swine flu and dengue epidemics, where various agencies took preventive steps.

### Safety Remedies against Viral Diseases:

*   Being vigilant about infection is an important step.
*   Educate people about the causes, effects, and safety measures through propaganda and transmissions.
*   Take anti-disease vaccines.
*   Arrange for special, separate wards for treating patients.
*   Follow guidelines and instructions issued by the World Health Organization (**WHO**) to curb viral diseases.
