This section details the administrative aspects of elections in India, focusing on the role and functions of the independent Election Commission and the timeline of the electoral process.

# The Election System and Administration

India has the largest number of voters globally. The **Election Commission** is the body responsible for the administration, control, and monitoring of the entire election process. For any democracy, it is crucial that its Election Commission is independent, impartial, and autonomous. Maintaining the autonomy of the Election Commission is mandatory.

The Constitution includes special provisions regarding the appointment, service conditions, removal, or suspension of Election Commissioners. The responsibility and authority for conducting elections for the Parliament, State Assemblies, Legislative Councils, President, and Vice President lie with the Election Commission. Elections in India are conducted by **secret ballot**.

The Legislative Assembly or Parliament is dissolved after every five years, necessitating fresh general elections. An election held to fill a vacancy created by the death or resignation of a representative is called a **by-election**. The Election Commission's work includes announcing election dates, managing advertisements, handling the filing and scrutiny of nomination forms, setting dates for withdrawal of nominations, declaring the final list of eligible candidates, and allotting election symbols.

From the moment election dates are announced, the Election Commission implements a **protocol** or Model Code of Conduct. The government is bound by this protocol and cannot undertake activities or campaigns that could improperly influence public opinion.

Candidates and political parties present their **election manifestos**, which outline their proposed plans and policies on economic issues, defence, foreign policy, and other problems.
