# Elections: The Foundation of Democracy

Elections are the foundation upon which democracy rests. They often create a festive atmosphere, providing citizens an opportunity to evaluate their representatives. Any eligible person can contest elections, whether they belong to a political party or are an independent candidate.

In an election, the winning candidate from a party or a coalition of parties comes to power. In a democracy, power is attained only with the support of the people. While Parliament is the apex body in India's democracy, the **Gram Panchayat** serves as a basic foundational block. Elections are also conducted for institutions of **Local Self Government**, such as the Taluka Panchayat and District Panchayat, as well as Municipalities in towns and Municipal Corporations in cities. Elections are truly the lifeline of a nation's democracy.

Elections hold immense importance for political parties and candidates alike. They act as a barometer of democracy, offering voters a chance to assess contesting candidates. Elections can usher in new political landscapes or waves and provide insight into a country's social and economic environment. Ultimately, elections determine the future of the government.

Elections are contested by political parties, independent candidates, and coalitions. In a Parliamentary Democracy, the party or coalition securing a majority of seats forms the government and assumes power. This power is both gained and maintained through public support. Given that elections are the soul of a democracy, their **transparency** is essential.
