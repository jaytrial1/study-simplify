This section explains the concept of the secret ballot, its importance in ensuring voter privacy, and the different methods used for casting votes in Indian elections, including the introduction of EVMs and the NOTA option.

# Secret Ballot and Voting Methods

Elections in India are conducted in a completely independent and secret manner. The Indian parliamentary elections are considered the largest election process in the world. Various political parties, organizations, and independent candidates contest, presenting their work, ideas, and future plans to voters. Voters are expected to vote without any expectation of benefit, greed, or fear.

The **'Secret Ballot'** is given utmost importance in a democracy. A voter has the right to maintain the secrecy of their vote, and no one can legally ask a voter how they voted. This process ensures the secrecy of each voter's choice. The Election Commission also facilitates voting for individuals involved in the election process and army personnel.

Two primary processes are used for voting:
1.  Voting by **ballot paper**
2.  Voting through an **Electronic Voting Machine (EVM)**

Counting ballot papers is time-consuming. Nowadays, most voting is done using EVMs, which significantly reduces the time and labour required for counting. Voting with EVMs is also considered environmentally friendly as it reduces paper usage.

If a voter wishes to exercise their right to vote but does not want to vote for any of the contesting candidates, they have the option to select **NOTA (None of the Above)**.
