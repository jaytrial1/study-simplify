# Disintegration of Soviet Union

In the final decade of the 20th century, the liberal policies of Soviet Union's Prime Minister **Mikhail Gorbachev** contributed to its disintegration. The peaceful dissolution of the Soviet Union is considered a momentous event in international politics. Mikhail Gorbachev became Prime Minister on March 11, 1985, introducing liberal reforms. Although Russia had made significant progress in science and technology and could compete with superpowers like America, its internal system faced challenges.

Gorbachev introduced two key policies: **'Glasnost'** (openness) and **'Perestroika'** (restructuring). These economic and political reforms aimed to move away from single-party communist rule. This process weakened the stronghold of the communist party, the Soviet bureaucracy, and the Red Army.

The disintegration process began in 1990. By December 1991, 14 out of the 15 states of the Soviet Union became independent, marking the end of the Soviet Union.
