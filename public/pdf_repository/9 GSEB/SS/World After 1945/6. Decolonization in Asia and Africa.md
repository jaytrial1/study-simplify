This section discusses the process by which countries in Asia and Africa gained independence after World War II.

# Rise of Independent Nations in Asia

Following a long struggle, India achieved independence in 1947, though it was partitioned into India and Pakistan due to Britain's divide and rule policy and other factors. Similarly, Sri Lanka and Myanmar gained independence from the British in 1948 after continuous struggles. The United Nations played a role in Indonesia's independence in 1949. Other Asian countries like Laos, Cambodia, and Vietnam also gained independence.

# Rise of Independent Nations in Africa

World War II was beneficial for enslaved African nations. After the war, countries like Abyssinia (Ethiopia), South Africa, and Egypt were independent. The war and the freedom movements in Asia inspired other African countries. This led to a period between 1951 and 1966 where 40 small and large African countries became free. Although imperialist countries tried to maintain control, these newly independent nations resisted, sometimes aligning with superpowers or using military resources to secure their freedom.
