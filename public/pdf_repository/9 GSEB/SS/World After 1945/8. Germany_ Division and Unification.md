This section details the division of Germany after World War II, the creation of two separate German states, the Berlin Blockade and Wall, and eventually the reunification of Germany.

# Partition and Unification of Germany

Germany was a primary cause of both World Wars and suffered a humiliating defeat in World War II, leading to the collapse of its state administration and economy. With no major party capable of ruling and fears of its resurgence as a military power, the defeated Germany was divided into four administrative zones. The Soviet Red Army had captured the eastern part, which remained under Soviet control. America controlled the southwestern part, France an area near its border, and Britain the areas bordering Belgium and Holland.

America, France, and Britain considered alternative arrangements amidst growing distrust between the two power blocs. The western zones controlled by America, Britain, and France were united to form the **'Federal Republic of Germany'** (West Germany), administered jointly by these three powers. In response, the Soviet Union declared its zone the **'Democratic Republic of East Germany'** (East Germany). Thus, after the war, two independent German states emerged: democratic West Germany and communist East Germany.

When the three western sectors of Berlin were united, the Soviet Union responded with the **'Berlin Blockade'** in April 1948, causing significant tension. Later, a high, 42 km long wall was constructed separating West and East Berlin. This **Berlin Wall** was built by the communist government to prevent East German citizens from escaping to West Germany, and many people died attempting to cross it.

Over the next four decades, West Germany experienced remarkable economic growth, termed the **'German Miracle'**. East Germany's economic condition saw little change, and its communist government suppressed aspirations for political freedom and rapid economic development.

By 1990, significant changes in international politics, including the end of the Cold War and cracks within the Soviet Union, led to the Soviet Union's disintegration. Consequently, on October 3rd, 1990, both Germanys were united. People celebrated by tearing down the Berlin Wall, a symbol of division and the Cold War era. Unified Germany is now a leading economic power in Europe and an important nation in the European Union.
