This section covers the competition in developing weapons, particularly nuclear arms, the near-nuclear conflict during the Cuban Missile Crisis, and early efforts towards disarmament treaties.

# Armament, Disarmament, Nuclear Weapon Creation and its Application

The Cold War, driven by the desire for global dominance, fueled a fierce competition in the **armament race**. America first used the atomic bomb on the Japanese cities of Hiroshima and Nagasaki in 1945, demonstrating its supremacy. Within four years, the Soviet Union proved its capability by conducting its own nuclear test, creating a **Power Balance** between the superpowers.

A critical moment occurred when America declared a blockade of communist Cuba. To defend Cuba, the Soviet Union sent ships carrying nuclear missiles into the Caribbean Sea. This brought the superpowers to the brink of nuclear war. For the first time, leaders from America and the Soviet Union communicated directly via a 'Hotline'. The Soviet Union agreed to withdraw its ships, and America agreed to remove nuclear missiles aimed at Cuba. This event is known as the **Cuban Missile Crisis** and is considered a significant turning point in world politics, initiating communication channels between the two powers and marking a step towards the process of nuclear disarmament. The Cuban Crisis is often seen as the beginning of the end of the Cold War.

America, the Soviet Union, and Britain agreed to an agreement controlling nuclear production, experiments, and proliferation, known as the **Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty**. France refused to sign this treaty. China also conducted a nuclear test in 1964 and did not sign the Treaty. This treaty banned nuclear tests in the open atmosphere. At this time, America, the Soviet Union, Britain, and France were permanent members of the United Nations Security Council with veto power; China gained this status later.

Beyond nuclear weapons, superpowers also developed chemical and biological weapons, considered weapons of mass destruction capable of causing widespread death without destroying property.

Many significant changes occurred in international politics during these decades. The Cold War eventually ended for various reasons, and treaties like the Nuclear Ban Treaty contributed to this. India has always supported such treaties and is a strong advocate for complete disarmament, believing global danger persists as long as lethal weapons exist.
