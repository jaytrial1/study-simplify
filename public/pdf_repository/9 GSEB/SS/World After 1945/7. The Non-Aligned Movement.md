This section explains the policy adopted by many newly independent nations to stay out of the superpower blocs and its significance.

# Non-Aligned Movement

Some newly independent nations chose a policy of not aligning with either of the two power blocs or military alliances. They decided to maintain equal distance from both blocs. These nations were identified as **'Non-Aligned Nations'**, and their foreign policy was called the **'Non-Aligned Policy'**.

These neutral nations, unwilling to adopt the ideologies of either superpower, aimed for comprehensive development while preserving their unique identity. The movement gained significant support under the leadership of prominent figures like India's first Prime Minister, Jawaharlal Nehru; Indonesia's first President, Sukarno; Egypt's second President, Gamal Abdel Nasser; Ghana's first President, Kwame Nkrumah; and Yugoslavia's President, Josip Broz Tito. The founding conference, held in Belgrade in 1961, included 23 countries from Asia and 6 from Africa, establishing the **Non-Aligned Movement (NAM)**.

Under Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru's leadership, the policies of the Non-Aligned Movement were formulated. Nehru believed the movement remained relevant because he saw the world's poorest nations still exploited and marginalized, not by opposing superpowers, but by a uni-polar world dominated by Western influence and neo-colonialism. NAM opposes foreign occupation, interference in internal affairs, and aggressive unilateral actions. It has also shifted focus to socio-economic challenges faced by member states, particularly inequalities from globalization and neo-liberal policies. The Non-Aligned Movement identifies economic underdevelopment, poverty, and social injustices as growing threats to peace and security.

More countries have joined NAM over time. The movement has played a significant role, especially after the disintegration of the Soviet Union and the decline of imperial policies, contributing to world politics, peace, stopping wars, maintaining human society, and encouraging disarmament among superpowers.
