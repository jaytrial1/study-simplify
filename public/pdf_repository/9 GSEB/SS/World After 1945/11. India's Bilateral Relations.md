This section provides an overview of India's relationships with key countries, including the United States, Russia, and its immediate neighbors.

# India's Relations with Other Countries

## Relation between India and United States (US)

Both India and the United States are democratic countries. Despite similarities, their relationship has seen fluctuations. The US initially disapproved of India's non-aligned policy after independence, hoping India would join its bloc. However, India prioritized its economic growth through the Non-Aligned Movement. Another point of friction was the US policy of favoring Pakistan, particularly on the Jammu-Kashmir issue. Pakistan joined the US-led military bloc, while India remained non-aligned. Disagreements over nuclear weapons treaties have also been significant. The US urged India to sign the 'Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty' and 'Comprehensive Test Ban Treaty'. When India conducted nuclear tests in Pokhran in 1998, the US imposed financial and technical sanctions.

Relations improved significantly after the terrorist attack on New York's World Trade Centre on September 11, 2001. The US now acknowledges India's rapid economic growth, and both countries desire closer ties. Visits by leaders, like Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit to the US and addressing the UN in 2014-15, and President Barack Obama being the Chief Guest at India's Republic Day in 2015, indicate a more cordial relationship. Terrorism remains a shared concern.

## Relation between India and the Soviet Union (Russia)

The Soviet Union provided significant assistance to India in establishing heavy and key industries. The Soviet Union supported India on the Jammu-Kashmir issue, often using its veto power in the UN Security Council to block proposals against India. This has fostered close friendly relations between Russia and India.

# India's Relation with its Neighbouring Countries

## India-Pakistan

Relations between India and Pakistan have been strained since India's independence, leading to three wars in 1948, 1965, and 1971, in which Pakistan was defeated. Despite treaties like the Tashkent and Shimla Agreements, Pakistan has not always adhered to them. The Kargil war occurred in 1999. Leaders from both countries have expressed willingness to resolve issues peacefully through negotiation.

## India - China

India and China established relations in 1954. Relations became difficult when China displayed maps showing Indian territories as its own. India strongly opposed this. China's rejection of the McMahon Line as the border further strained relations. China attacked India in 1962 and occupied areas shown on its maps. India deployed troops to its border regions. China ended the war unilaterally. Numerous meetings to resolve border issues have been unsuccessful. However, in recent decades, India and China have developed cooperative and friendly relations in other areas. High-level visits, like Prime Minister Narendra Modi inviting Chinese President Xi Jinping to Ahmedabad in 2014, signify efforts to improve ties.

## India - Bangladesh

Bangladesh gained independence in 1971 after struggles, becoming a sovereign country after being part of Pakistan. India provided financial, technical, and physical aid for its development. Issues exist, such as the utilization and channelizing of water from the River Ganga. India has provided immense help during natural calamities like floods and cyclones. Concrete talks in 2015 addressed disputed land regions and citizenship issues.

## India - Bhutan

A peaceful and cordial friendship treaty was signed between India and Bhutan in 1949. Bhutan has trusted India on its security and foreign policies. India has assisted Bhutan in telecommunication and transportation. Visits by Indian leaders, including Prime Minister Jawaharlal Nehru in 1958 and the Indian President in 1970, strengthened ties. India helped Bhutan become a member of the United Nations in 1971. Relations have consistently been untroubled. Prime Minister Narendra Modi's visit in 2014 emphasized that India's progress benefits its neighbors, and security cooperation is satisfactory.

## India - Srilanka

India's relationship with Sri Lanka is long-standing. Many Tamils from India settled in Sri Lanka, and their citizenship was a major issue, which has been largely resolved through continuous talks. India prioritized peaceful talks between Tamil organizations and the Sri Lankan government regarding the problems faced by the Tamil population. Prime Minister Narendra Modi visited Tamil-affected regions in Jaffna in 2015 and provided financial assistance for rebuilding houses for rehabilitation. Today, India and Sri Lanka share cordial relations.

## India - Nepal

Relations between India and Nepal began with a treaty signed in 1950. This treaty recognizes both countries as sovereign and allows for free movement of citizens and preservation of regional unity. India has assisted Nepal socially and economically, and Nepali students often study in India. Rivers flowing from the Himalayas through Nepal can cause floods in India. Talks are ongoing to manage these rivers. India provided aid for relief and rehabilitation after a destructive earthquake in Nepal on April 25, 2015.

## India - Afghanistan

India has very friendly relations with Afghanistan, contributing significantly to its reconstruction in areas like construction, health, and education. India has provided financial aid for economic and social development and during natural calamities. India is involved in constructing Afghanistan's parliament building.

## India - Myanmar

India and Myanmar (Burma) have cordial relations. Myanmar gained independence in 1948, shortly after India. Since then, the relationship has been friendly. Myanmar has sought and received financial aid from India, as India supports Myanmar's financial stability.

India's foreign policy aims for world peace and security and fostering friendly relations with neighboring countries. These relationships have experienced fluctuations over time due to various reasons.
