# Cold War (1945-1962) - Reasons and Consequences

Although World War II ended, it led to the rise of America and Russia as superpowers, replacing England and France. While allied during the war, differences arose, particularly concerning Russian policies, leading to a split between America/England and Russia. A strong competition for global supremacy emerged between the two superpowers, America and Russia. This period saw power centralized around these two poles, creating a **bi-polar world order**. Due to extremely tense relations between these two power blocs, this phase is known as the **'Cold War Phase'**. At various points, the superpowers were on the verge of direct conflict.

A fundamental change occurred in the relationships among powerful nations after the war. America and the Soviet Union, former allies against Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy, became leaders of two rival power blocs and military powers, initiating the Cold War. Britain and France, though victorious, lost their great power status due to war damage and the end of imperialism. Germany, Italy, and Japan were defeated and economically, politically, and militarily ruined. After the war, the Soviet Union gained control over Eastern Germany and other Eastern European countries like Austria, Albania, Hungary, Yugoslavia, Czechoslovakia, Poland, Romania, Bulgaria, and the Baltic states, later supporting communist parties in power to maintain influence. In 1949, the Soviet Union challenged America's nuclear monopoly by conducting its own nuclear test. Meanwhile, democratic systems were gradually established in Western European countries liberated by British and American forces.
