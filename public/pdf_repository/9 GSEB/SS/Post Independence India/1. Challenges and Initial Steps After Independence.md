# Challenges and Initial Steps After Independence

India gained independence with the passing of the Article for India's Independence in the British Parliament in 1947, leading to the creation of India and Pakistan. The newly independent nation faced two primary challenges:

1.  Framing the **constitution** of India.
2.  Ending the sovereignty of the British Empire over the **princely states** and integrating them into a unified India.

At the time of independence, there were 562 princely states. These states accounted for 48% of India's total area and were home to 20% of its population. Some were large, like Kashmir, Hyderabad, and Mysore, while others were as small as villages. Convincing the rulers (Kings and Nawabs) of these states to join the Union of India was a difficult task that needed to be accomplished quickly.

After independence, Maharaja Krushna Kumarsinh of Bhavnagar was the first King to establish a responsible government (January 15, 1948). Bhavnagar State was subsequently merged with the United Saurashtra State, formed through the efforts of Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel (February 15, 1948). Jawaharlal Nehru described the formation of the United Saurashtra State as a significant merger in contemporary history.
