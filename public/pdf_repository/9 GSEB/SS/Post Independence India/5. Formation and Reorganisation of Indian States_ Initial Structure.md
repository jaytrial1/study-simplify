# Formation and Reorganisation of Indian States: Initial Structure

After achieving independence and integrating the princely states, India adopted a union system of governance rather than a unitary one. This raised the issue of forming and reorganizing the constituent units of the union. The former British provinces and princely states were initially divided into four categories:

*   **Class A states:** Mumbai, Assam, Andhra, Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Madras, Orissa, and West Bengal.
*   **Class B states:** Jammu-Kashmir, Hyderabad, Mysore, Central India, Rajasthan, Saurashtra, Travancore-Cochin, and Pepsu (Patiala and East Punjab States Union).
*   **Class C states:** Ajmer, Bhopal, Coorg, Delhi, Bilaspur, Kutch, Himachal Pradesh, Tripura, Manipur, and Vindhya Pradesh.
*   **Class D states:** Andaman and Nicobar islands.

States in these four classes did not have equal status but were integral parts of India. This classification was in effect when the Constitution of India came into force, but it was considered a temporary arrangement.

Following this, a strong demand emerged for the reorganization of states on a **linguistic basis**.
