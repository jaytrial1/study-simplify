This section focuses on the specific processes and difficulties involved in integrating the three princely states that initially resisted joining India: Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir.

# Integrating the Challenging States: Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir

While most princely states joined India smoothly, Hyderabad, Junagadh, and Kashmir presented unique challenges.

## Merger of Hyderabad

The Nizam of Hyderabad declared independence on August 15, 1947. Due to its geographical location, surrounded by Indian territory, it was expected to join India. The Nizam, however, stated that while he couldn't join Pakistan due to geography, he wouldn't join India for ideological reasons and preferred independence.

Negotiations with the Nizam began. Sardar Patel firmly stated that the people of Hyderabad wished to join India. Meanwhile, the Nizam's officers began committing atrocities against the populace. Ultimately, Hyderabad was merged with the Union of India on September 18, 1948, through the use of 'police force'. Sardar Patel assured the Nizam that his interests would be protected. Kanaialal Munshi played a crucial role in this process. After the reorganization of states in 1956, Hyderabad was included in Andhra Pradesh.

## Merger of Junagadh

On Independence Day, the Nawab of Junagadh signed an instrument of accession to join Pakistan, which Pakistan accepted. Sardar Patel decided to take countermeasures. People from Junagadh living in Mumbai formed an 'Aarzi Hakumat' (Provisional Government) in Mumbai. Simultaneously, Sheikhs of Mangrol and Manavadar, regions within Junagadh, declared their intention to join India.

Indian soldiers and navy positioned to protect Manavadar and Mangrol subsequently besieged the Junagadh State. The Nawab of Junagadh fled to Pakistan. On November 9, 1947, a **referendum** (plebiscite) was held among the people of Junagadh, who overwhelmingly voted to join India. Thus, Junagadh's merger was achieved due to Sardar Patel's resolve and the strong will of its people.

## Merger of Kashmir

The issue of Kashmir proved to be the most complex. Raja Harisingh of Kashmir was initially undecided about which country to join. While he hesitated, Pakistan attacked Kashmir. Raja Harisingh then requested military assistance from India to defend Jammu and Kashmir.

The Government of India conditioned military aid on Raja Harisingh first signing the instrument of accession. One clause in the agreement stated that after normalcy was restored, a **plebiscite** would be held for permanent merger with India. Immediately after the signing, the Indian army was sent and protected most of Kashmir. However, Pakistan had already occupied about one-third of the region.

India lodged a complaint against Pakistan in the United Nations Security Council. The Security Council suggested a cease-fire. Even today, the Kashmir issue remains a contentious point between India and Pakistan. By the end of 1948, the political unity of India was largely established through these historical and often challenging processes.
