This section examines the issue of uneven development across different regions of India, its historical roots, and the factors that perpetuate it.

# Regional Inequality

Regional inequality refers to the uneven distribution of development across different areas within a country. The text suggests that foreign rulers did not focus developmental activities in areas where they saw no economic profit, leading to historical regional imbalances.

After independence, the **Planning Commission** was formed with the aim of social and economic development, including the balanced development of different regions. However, the text points out that factors like regional pressure, political reasons, competition to bring development programs to specific regions, and development priorities have contributed to continued regional inequality. Differences in access to development resources and grants are also cited as factors.

Significant internal inequality exists even within states categorized as developed, moderately developed, or less developed. For instance, Maharashtra, considered economically developed, has backward regions like Vidarbha and Marathwada. Similarly, some areas in Andhra Pradesh are well-developed, while Rayalseema and Telangana were considerably backward (prior to Telangana's formation). Some regions excel in agricultural development, while others lead in industrial development.

Regionalism is also visible in disputes between states, such as boundary issues between Maharashtra and Karnataka, and Punjab and Haryana. Narrow-mindedness is observed in regions competing for resources like water, minerals, industrial raw materials, and agricultural sectors. This regional inequality and narrow-minded focus are considered highly detrimental to the unity of the country. The text advocates for planning focused on preserving national dignity and position rather than solely on regional development.
