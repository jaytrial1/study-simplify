This section details the process of reorganizing Indian states based on language, including the establishment and recommendations of the States Reorganisation Commission.

# Reorganisation of States: Linguistic Basis and the Commission

The demand for states based on language grew significantly. In 1953, Jawaharlal Nehru addressed this issue and appointed the **States Reorganisation Commission**. The commission was chaired by retired Chief Justice of the Supreme Court, Justice Fazal Ali. Its other two members were Mr. Hridaynath Kunzru and Mr. K.M. Panikkar.

The commission solicited recommendations from all states through public opinions, views, and postal interactions. An interesting outcome was the initial formation of a bilingual Mumbai state, which included Maharashtra, Gujarat, and Saurashtra, with Mumbai as its capital.

Finally, a Bill was passed making changes to the Constitution and reorganizing states. This reorganization was implemented from 1956. The old classification of states (Class A, B, C, D) was abolished. Amendments were made to the First Schedule of the Indian Constitution, replacing the earlier classification with two categories: Constituent States and Union Territories.

The fourteen states formed were: Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Mumbai, Jammu-Kashmir, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Madras, Mysore, Orissa, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, and West Bengal. The six Union Territories included: Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Manipur, Tripura, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, and Lakshadweep.
