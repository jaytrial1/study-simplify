# Scientific and Technological Development

India has made remarkable progress in science and technology over the past half-century. Given its relatively limited resources and lower initial literacy rates compared to developed countries, India's growth in this sector is considered unique.

After independence, distinguished leaders and scientists contributed significantly. Notable figures include Dr. Homi Bhabha, Dr. Raja Ramanna, Dr. Vikram Sarabhai, Dr. C.V. Raman (in science), and Sir M. Vishwariya, Sam Pitroda, Dr. A.P.J. Abdul Kalam, and E. Sreedharan (in engineering/technology).

Key institutions like the Indian Institute of Science, Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO), and Physical Research Laboratory (PRL) are highlighted as examples of this progress.

## Key Areas of Development

*   **Agriculture:** At independence, India was dependent on food grain imports. Significant infrastructure like dams, lakes, and canals, along with the use of modern agricultural technology, have been crucial in achieving self-reliance through the **Green Revolution**. Agricultural institutions, laboratories, and universities have been established to advance agricultural science.
*   **Nuclear Science:** India has made significant headway in nuclear science and technology, establishing research institutes and laboratories primarily for the peaceful use of nuclear power. Scientists and engineers have built modern nuclear reactors and power stations. India has also successfully conducted nuclear tests twice at Pokhran, Rajasthan, demonstrating its capability in making nuclear bombs.
*   **Space Research:** India's achievements in space research technology in a short time are considered spectacular. The development of launch vehicles like the Geosynchronous Satellite Launch Vehicle (GSLV) allows India to launch its own satellites. Satellites such as 'Aryabhatta' (1975), 'Bhaskar' (1979), and 'Rohini' (1979) have been useful for various purposes including telecasting, education, weather forecasting, and remote sensing for mineral information.
*   **Telecommunication and Information Technology:** In the last decade, India has made significant progress in telecommunication and IT through the widespread use of computers, the internet, mobile phones, smartphones, tablets, fax, email, Twitter, and WhatsApp. This progress places India in the race of developed nations. The application of computers is widely used in administration.
*   **Biotechnology and Other Fields:** Research and development in biotechnology (industries, energy, agriculture, health), marine research, human resources development, and harbor development have led to initiatives like **'Digital India'** and **'Make in India'**, gaining international recognition. The declaration of June 21st as **'World Yoga Day'** by the United Nations is also mentioned as a sign of India's global standing.
