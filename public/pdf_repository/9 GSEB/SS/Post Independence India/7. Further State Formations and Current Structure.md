This section describes the subsequent creation of new states after the 1956 reorganisation and outlines the current administrative structure of the Indian Union.

# Further State Formations and Current Structure

The process of state formation continued after 1956. The Central Government created two separate states, Maharashtra and Gujarat, on May 1, 1960. Maharashtra was recognized as a state on May 25, 1960, with Mumbai as its capital. May 1, 1960, is considered the Foundation Day of Gujarat, which included the regions of Saurashtra and Kutch.

Assam, Meghalaya, Manipur, Tripura, Arunachal Pradesh, Mizoram, and Nagaland are collectively known as the **'Land of Seven Sisters'** in the Northeast.

There has been a continuous demand for the formation of smaller states from larger ones. In the year 2000, three new states were created:
*   Jharkhand (capital: Ranchi) was separated from Bihar.
*   Chhattisgarh (capital: Raipur) was separated from Madhya Pradesh.
*   Uttarakhand (capital: Dehradun) was separated from Uttar Pradesh.

In 2014, Telangana was recognized as a separate state from Andhra Pradesh. Demands for various separate states continue even today, such as the demand for the Vidarbha region from Maharashtra.

Currently, the Indian Union consists of 28 states and 8 Union Territories (Note: The text lists 7 UTs and Delhi separately, but the current count is 8 UTs including Delhi). The states are: Assam, Bihar, Goa, Gujarat, Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, Karnataka, Kerala, Jammu-Kashmir, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Rajasthan, Odisha, Punjab, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Uttar Pradesh, Sikkim, Tripura, West Bengal, Chhattisgarh, Uttarakhand, Arunachal Pradesh, and Jharkhand. The Union Territories listed are Chandigarh, Puducherry, Lakshadweep, Daman, Diu, Dadra and Nagar Haveli, and Delhi (National Capital Territory).
