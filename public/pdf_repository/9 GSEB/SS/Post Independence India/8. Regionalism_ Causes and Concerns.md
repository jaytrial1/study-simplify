This section defines regionalism in the Indian context, explores its underlying causes, and discusses its potential negative impacts on national unity.

# Regionalism: Causes and Concerns

India is characterized by **unity in diversity**, with people of diverse religions, languages, castes, races, and cultures coexisting for centuries. This level of diversity is unique globally. Cultural progress in India is seen as a unified effort of all its people, not tied to any single group.

However, the feeling of **regionalism** can arise. One of the main reasons cited for its creation is the actions of certain politicians and communal groups who exploit people's feelings for their own motives. Those who instigate regionalism are considered harmful to national unity and integrity. While personal development aspirations are positive, actions that endanger society or the country are not. It is emphasized that working for the country should take precedence over working solely for one's state after independence.

It is natural for people sharing a common language, culture, and geographical region over time to develop an emotional bond. Language, Caste, and Religion are identified as three main factors contributing to regionalism.

## Factors Contributing to Regionalism

*   **Language:** Language is a significant driver of regionalism. Demands for new regions based on language have arisen, particularly between Hindi-speaking areas in the North and non-Hindi-speaking areas in the South. The States Reorganisation Commission already implemented divisions based on linguistic boundaries to some extent.
*   **Caste and Religion:** Different religious groups coexist in India, but in certain regions, a sense of superiority of one religion may exist. Regions like Nagaland, Mizoram, Manipur, and Arunachal Pradesh have distinct customs and lifestyles. Demands for separate regions, such as by the Bodo people in Assam, have led to movements and conflicts, including ULFA terrorism in Assam.

Regionalism also played a role in the separation of Punjab and Haryana, Jharkhand from Bihar, Chhattisgarh from Madhya Pradesh, and Uttarakhand from Uttar Pradesh. Current demands like that for a separate Vidarbha region from Maharashtra are also cited. The text argues that prioritizing regional demands over national unity is not in the nation's interest. The Constitution declares India a sovereign, socialist, secular, democratic, and republic country, emphasizing that national unity should be the primary goal during state reorganization.
