This section describes how the remaining foreign-controlled territories in India, specifically those under French and Portuguese rule, were integrated into the Indian Union.

# Integration of French and Portuguese Colonies

Although India became an independent and sovereign state on January 26, 1950, certain colonies of the French and Portuguese empires remained under their control. The Union of India appealed to the French-controlled areas (like Pondicherry) and Portuguese-controlled areas (like Diu, Daman, and Goa) to merge with India.

The people in these colonies strongly desired to join India and launched vigorous movements for this purpose. The French attempted to suppress these movements. In a large meeting in Pondicherry in 1948, people gave the French government the slogan **'Hind chhodo'** (Leave India).

The Indian government began negotiations for a peaceful resolution with the French government, but the people were not satisfied with the pace. In Yanam, the People's Liberation Army took control of the administration on June 13, 1954. Recognizing the people's strong desire, the French government was compelled to negotiate with India. All French colonies (Karaikal, Mahe, Yanam, and Chandranagar) were handed over to India on October 31, 1954, and were later incorporated into the Union Territory of Pondicherry. With the reorganization of states, Pondicherry includes Karaikal (in Tamil Nadu), Mahe (in Kerala), Yanam (in Andhra Pradesh), and Chandranagar (in West Bengal), with Pondicherry city as the administrative center.

## Merger of Diu, Daman and Goa

On August 15, 1947, political leaders and Satyagrahis entered Diu, Daman, and Goa. Thousands of volunteers died in the **'Free Goa Agitation'**. As negotiations, persuasion, and Satyagrahas proved unsuccessful, the Indian government decided to launch **'Operation Vijay'** to liberate Goa. On the midnight of December 18, 1961, the Portuguese Governor, De-Silva, surrendered to the Indian government. The Indian army captured Goa, Daman, and Diu and hoisted the Indian flag, marking the end of Western colonial rule in India.

On March 12, 1962, Goa, Dadra, Nagar Haveli, Daman, and Diu were merged with the Union of India and designated as Union Territories. On May 30, 1987, Daman and Diu were separated from Goa. Silvassa became the capital of Dadra and Nagar Haveli, Daman became the capital of Daman and Diu, and Panaji became the capital of Goa.
