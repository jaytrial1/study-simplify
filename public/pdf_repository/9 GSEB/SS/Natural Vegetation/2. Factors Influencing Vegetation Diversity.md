This section explains the various geographical and climatic factors that contribute to the rich diversity of natural vegetation found across India.

# Factors Influencing Vegetation Diversity

The diversity observed in India's natural vegetation is primarily due to the following reasons:

*   **Relief features:** The varied landscape, including mountains, plateaus, plains, and deserts, results in different vegetation patterns.
*   **Soil:** India has diverse soil types such as alluvial, black, mountain, and desert soils, which support different kinds of vegetation.
*   **Temperature:** Variations in temperature and humidity, particularly between the cold Himalayan regions and the southern peninsular region, lead to differences in vegetation.
*   **Insolation (sunshine):** The amount of sunlight a place receives depends on its latitude and altitude. Vegetation grows faster with more sunshine and rain.
*   **Rainfall:** Unequal distribution of rainfall across India contributes significantly to vegetation diversity.
*   **Humidity:** Along with temperature, humidity levels influence the type of vegetation that can thrive in a region.

India is home to approximately 5000 varieties of trees, with about 450 having commercial value. Additionally, around 15,000 flowering plants grow in India, accounting for about 6% of the world's total. Non-flowering plants like fern, algae, and moss are also found. India has a long history of using herbal plants, with about 2000 medicinal plants described in Ayurveda. This underscores the vast diversity in India's vegetation.
