This section describes the five main categories of natural vegetation found in India, based on climatic conditions, altitude, soil, rainfall, and temperature variations.

# Types of Natural Vegetation

The existence and growth of vegetation are strongly influenced by the climate of a region. Areas with similar climates tend to have similar vegetation, forming what are called Natural Vegetation Regions.

Based on factors like altitude, soil, rainfall, and temperature, India's natural vegetation can be broadly classified into five types:

1.  Tropical Rain Forests
2.  Tropical Deciduous Forests
3.  Tropical Desert Vegetation
4.  Temperate Forests and Grasslands (Himalayan Vegetation)
5.  Mangrove (Tidal) Forests

## Tropical Rain Forests

*   **Distribution:** Found in hot and humid areas with annual rainfall exceeding 200 cm and temperatures above $22^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$. These include heavy rainfall areas of the Western Ghats, Lakshadweep, Andaman and Nicobar Islands, upper parts of Assam, and coastal Tamil Nadu.
*   **Trees:** Common trees include *mahogany*, *ebony*, *rosewood*, and *rubber*.
*   **Characteristics:** Trees often reach heights of 60 metres or more. The presence of *scrubs* contributes to high humidity. There is no distinct *autumn* season, as trees are evergreen and do not shed leaves simultaneously. These forests are also known as **Evergreen Forests**.

## Tropical Deciduous Forests

*   **Distribution:** Found in regions receiving 70 to 200 cm of rainfall. These cover significant parts of India, including North-Eastern States, Himalayan foothills, Western Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, eastern slopes of the Eastern Ghats, and the Vindhya and Satpura ranges.
*   **Trees:** Major trees include *teak*, *saal*, *sesame*, *sandalwood*, *kher* (*acacia catechu*), and *bamboo*.
*   **Characteristics:** A key feature is that trees shed their leaves for 6 to 8 weeks during the dry season (*autumn*). Different species shed leaves at different times, so the forest is never completely bare. Because trees shed leaves seasonally, these are also called **Monsoon Forests**.

## Thorny Vegetation (Tropical Desert Vegetation)

*   **Distribution:** Found in regions receiving less than 70 cm of rainfall, primarily in the North-Western region, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh.
*   **Trees:** Common trees include *jujube*, *acacia*, *cactus*, and *khijdo*.
*   **Characteristics:** Plants have long, deep, and widespread roots to find water. Leaves are small or modified into thorns to reduce water loss through *evapotranspiration*.

## Temperate Forests and Grasslands (Himalayan Vegetation)

Vegetation in the Himalayas varies significantly with altitude:

| Height                 | Areal Span                                     | Forests / Vegetation             | Trees / Major Vegetation          |
| :--------------------- | :--------------------------------------------- | :------------------------------- | :-------------------------------- |
| 1000 to 2000 metres    | High mountains of North-East, West Bengal, and mountainous Uttarakhand | Tropical forests                 | *Oak* and *chestnut*              |
| 1500 to 3000 metres    | Southern slopes of Himalayas, higher areas of South and North-East | Coniferous forests               | *Pine*, *deodar*, *silver fir*, *spruce* |
| 3600 metres and more   | Higher altitude in Himalaya near snow line     | Alpine and short grass (Tundra)  | *Silver fir*, *Junifur*, *birch*  |

A major characteristic of *coniferous forests* is the conical shape of the trees. This shape, with branches leaning downwards, helps snow slide off easily. The leaves are long, pointed, and sticky, which helps conserve moisture.

## Tidal Forests (Mangroves)

*   **Distribution:** Located in the delta regions of rivers along the coast and in marshy coastal lands. Found along the Gujarat coast and the Bay of Bengal coast, notably in the Sundarbans.
*   **Trees:** Common trees include *Sundari* and *cher*.
