This section discusses the reasons behind the destruction of forests and the negative consequences that result from it, highlighting the need for conservation.

# Challenges to Forest Conservation

The ecosystem is a complex interaction between the biosphere and mankind. However, human activities and selfishness have disturbed this balance. The increasing human desire for land is a major cause of forest destruction. Other factors contributing to deforestation include:

*   Increasing population pressure.
*   Establishing industrial units away from residential areas, often encroaching on forest land.
*   Urbanization.
*   Large multi-purpose projects (dams, etc.).
*   Construction of roads.
*   *Jhoom cultivation* (shifting cultivation).
*   Logging for timber and fuel wood.
*   Forest fires.

The destruction of forests disrupts the ecological balance, leading to adverse effects such as:

*   Decrease in rainfall.
*   Drought conditions.
*   Global warming and the greenhouse effect.
*   Advancing deserts.
*   Loss of habitat for wild animals.

According to the National Forest Policy of 1952, 33% of a nation's total geographical area should be under forest cover. In India, forests cover about 23% of the area, while in Gujarat, it is only about 10%. This highlights the critical need to prevent forest destruction and prioritize protection and conservation efforts.

### Key Concepts & Examples

*   **Ecosystem:** A community of living organisms interacting with each other and their physical environment.
*   **Jhoom Cultivation:** A traditional agricultural practice, especially in hilly areas, where a patch of forest is cleared by cutting and burning, crops are grown for a few seasons, and then the land is abandoned for a new patch, allowing the old one to regenerate (though often slowly). This practice contributes to deforestation.
*   **Red Data Book:** A list maintained by the IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature) that categorizes species based on their risk of extinction. Plants like white *khakhro*, *gugal*, *nilsoti*, *sesame*, *amli*, *attak*, and *harde* from Gujarat are listed as 'on the verge of extinction'.
