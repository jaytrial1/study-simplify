This section details the rise of Fascism under Benito Mussolini in Italy following dissatisfaction with the outcome of World War I and describes Italy's aggressive foreign policy under Fascist rule.

# Fascism in Italy

Italy fought alongside the winning nations in the First World War, incurring significant costs (12 billion dollars expenditure, 6 lakh soldiers lost). Despite its contribution, Italy felt ignored by other winning nations who claimed desirable territories, leaving Italy angry and frustrated. This hurt nationalistic sentiment led Italians to blame their government and seek quick revenge for the perceived humiliation.

To address this crisis, **Benito Mussolini** launched the **Fascist Party**. His party symbol, "a bundle of sticks and axe," represented the supremacy of the Roman emperor. The term **Fascism** derives from the Italian word 'fascismo' or 'fasces', meaning absolute control of the state over all aspects of life. Mussolini's guiding principle was "**One party, one leader**". He provided military training to his volunteers, known for their black uniforms.

After gaining power, Mussolini pursued an expansionist policy, capturing Rhodes and Dodecanese islands from Turkey, and later Albania, Abyssinia, and Fume port in 1924. Following these successes, Mussolini withdrew Italy from the League of Nations and joined the "**Rome - Berlin - Tokyo**" Axis. Italy's aggressive actions and alignment contributed to the tensions leading to the Second World War.
