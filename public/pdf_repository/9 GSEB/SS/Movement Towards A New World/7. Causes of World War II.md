This section details the major underlying causes of the Second World War, including intense nationalism, formation of alliances, military buildup, the failure of the League of Nations, the harshness of the Treaty of Versailles, and Hitler's expansionist aims, culminating in the invasion of Poland.

# Causes of World War II

## Fierce Nationalism

After the First World War, intense nationalism surged in Germany, Japan, and Italy. Germans could not forget the humiliation of the **Treaty of Versailles**. Following his Nazi ideology, Hitler annexed German-speaking provinces in Austria and Czechoslovakia. Italy, feeling slighted by the Treaty, pursued aggressive imperialist policies under Mussolini and the Fascist Party. Japan, from Asia, also embraced imperialism. These nationalistic and expansionist drives threatened world peace.

## Groupism

Fear and mistrust led nations to form alliances. France, fearing Germany, formed treaties with Belgium, Poland, Romania, and Czechoslovakia. Italy signed treaties with various Balkan and Central European nations. Russia made treaties with Germany, Turkey, Lithuania, and Iran. Italy, Germany, and Japan formed the "**Rome-Berlin-Tokyo**" Axis. England and France formed another group based on democratic values. Germany also signed a non-aggression treaty with Russia. This complex web of alliances created an atmosphere of fear and contributed to the impetus for war.

## Militarism

Despite efforts for peace after WWI, European nations engaged in an arms race. The production of weapons increased, with nations competing to develop superior armaments. Countries like Russia and Germany introduced compulsory military training. All nations expanded their armies, navies, and air forces. Japan also increased its military strength in the East. The widespread military buildup meant that when England and France also began equipping themselves, the stage was set for war.

## Failure of the League of Nations

The **League of Nations** was established after WWI to maintain peace. However, it lacked the authority and military power to enforce its principles. Member nations were not obligated to bring disputes to the League or accept its rulings. Many nations withdrew, including Russia, Germany, Italy, and Japan. The League failed to act effectively when Italy invaded Abyssinia, Japan attacked Manchuria, and Germany annexed parts of Czechoslovakia. The inability to curb the power struggles and groupism among nations was a major failure of the League.

## Treaty of Versailles

The **Treaty of Versailles**, finalized during the Paris Peace Process, held Germany responsible for WWI. Germany was forced to pay a massive war fine (6.5 billion pounds), lost its Ruhr province, and had the Rhine river opened for international shipping. This unfair and humiliating treaty deeply angered Germans. Adolf Hitler famously called it "a piece of paper" and vowed to reject it, encouraging the German population to support war. Japan was also dissatisfied with the treaty. Thus, the injustices of the Treaty of Versailles were a fundamental cause of the Second World War.

## Imperialistic Ambition of Adolf Hitler

Hitler's fierce nationalist and militarist nature was a direct cause of the war. He was determined to achieve German unity, stability, and prosperity through expansion. His first target was Austria, which he annexed on March 12, 1938. The day after the Munich Convention, Germany captured Czechoslovakia (October 1, 1938), followed by the Mamal Port of Lithuania in March 1939. Hitler's goal was to create a grand German nation, and his aggressive imperialist policy was a primary cause of the Second World War.

## German attack on Poland - an immediate cause

The cumulative effect of the preceding factors had created a highly volatile global situation. The world was poised for conflict, and a small incident could trigger it. This spark came when Germany attacked Poland on September 1, 1939, in the early morning. This act marked the beginning of World War II. Britain and France warned Germany to cease hostilities, but Germany ignored the warning, leading Britain and France to declare war and enter the conflict, which then spread worldwide.
