This section explains the establishment of the United Nations after the failure of the League of Nations, its purpose, and key steps in its formation process.

# United Nations

The **League of Nations** was formed after World War I to ensure world peace, but its failure was evident with the outbreak of World War II. To address this, at the end of World War II, the **United Nations** was established on October 24, 1945. Its purpose was to promote peace, security, and coexistence globally. Its headquarters was designated to be in New York.

Efforts by America to establish the United Nations began during its involvement in World War II. American President Roosevelt outlined four key freedoms in a message to Congress, aiming for human freedom, peace, and security:
1.  Freedom of speech and thought
2.  Freedom of religion
3.  Economic freedom
4.  Right to freedom from fear

Later, President Roosevelt and British Prime Minister Churchill drafted an eight-point agreement aboard a ship in the Atlantic Ocean, known as the **Atlantic Agreement**. This agreement included principles like maintaining national freedom and sovereignty, peace, security, social and economic welfare, and disarmament. Subsequent meetings, like the Moscow Declaration (foreign ministers of Britain, America, Russia, China) and a conference in Tehran (three superpowers), furthered the process. Representatives from 50 nations prepared the UN agreement in Washington (September 1944). On October 24, 1945, 51 member nations declared the establishment of the United Nations. India was one of the original members. Since then, October 24th is celebrated as United Nations Day worldwide. Currently, the United Nations has 193 member nations.
