This section describes the worldwide economic crisis that began in 1929, its impact on global economies and trade, and how it contributed to the instability leading up to World War II.

# Global Depression 1929-32

Many European leaders believed that the end of World War I would resolve the world's post-war problems. However, this proved incorrect. A sudden, massive sale of shares on the "**Wall Street**" stock market caused it to collapse (October 24, 1929). This event, known as the "**Wall Street crisis**", shook the world and severely damaged national economies, triggering the **Great Global Depression**.

Most nations were affected. Even powerful countries like Great Britain had to abandon its policy of maintaining gold reserves against its currency, the 'pound', which impacted other nations and international trade. Prosperous nations like America also implemented strict economic measures. Global trade was halved. This economic turmoil created further instability and contributed to the conditions that led to World War II.
