This section discusses Japan's dissatisfaction with the post-WWI treaties, the rise of militaristic nationalism, and Japan's expansionist policies in Asia, leading to its alignment with Germany and Italy.

# Militarism in Japan

Japan was dissatisfied with the outcome of the Treaty of Versailles (1919) because it did not gain control over Chinese provinces as expected. England and France divided many former German territories among themselves. Further discontent arose from the 1921-22 Washington Meet, where Japan was limited to only 35% of the naval force of England and America and had to withdraw from the islands of Sakhalin and Siberia. This angered the Japanese youth.

During this period, elections in Japan resulted in a majority for parties supporting **Militarism**. This Military Nationalism adopted an expansionist policy. Japan was not granted a permanent seat in the League of Nations, and Western countries were unable to curb its imperialist activities. Japan captured Manchuria, establishing the Manchukuo government (1932), and also took control of Korea, Mongolia, Shantung, and other Chinese provinces.

Japan improved its relations with Germany and Italy, intensifying its imperialistic pursuits. Emperor Hirohito (1936), following Emperor Meiji, also encouraged these expansionist activities. Consequently, Japan withdrew from the League of Nations in 1933.
