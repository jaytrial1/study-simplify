This section explains the conditions in Germany after World War I, the rise of Nazism under Adolf Hitler, and the core policies and actions of the Nazi regime, including persecution and expansionism.

# Nazism in Germany

Germany, having lost the First World War, was economically devastated. The Paris Peace Process culminated in the Treaty of Versailles, which Germans viewed as unjust and deeply resented. **Adolf Hitler** joined the National Socialist German Workers Party in 1919, which became known as the **Nazi Party**. The Nazi philosophy combined elements of nationalism and socialism, though its implementation was characterized by extreme nationalism and authoritarianism.

Upon the death of President Hindenburg, Hitler assumed power, establishing a dictatorial rule. He implemented stern and aggressive policies, promoting a narrow form of nationalism among Germans, who saw him as "**Fuhrer**" (Saviour).

Nazi soldiers wore blue military uniforms with a red arm strip and the symbol of the **swastika**. Hitler's authoritarian and militarist nature became apparent after his rise to power. His primary goal was the re-emergence of Germany as a major power. Under the guise of purifying the German race, his policies targeted the extermination of Jews, gypsies, and mentally challenged people. The mass murder of European Jews during the Second World War is known as the **Holocaust** (genocide), resulting in innumerable deaths. Hitler's expansionist ambitions, military aggression against neighboring countries, and genocidal policies were major factors leading to the Second World War.
