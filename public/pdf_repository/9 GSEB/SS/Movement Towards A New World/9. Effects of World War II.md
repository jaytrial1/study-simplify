This section outlines the immediate and long-term consequences of World War II, focusing on economic devastation, the rise of communism in China, and the beginning of the Cold War between the two new superpowers.

# Effects of World War II - Immediate Effects

## Economic Effects

The war caused tremendous destruction globally. Involved nations incurred immense costs; America spent 350 billion dollars, and other nations spent over a thousand billion dollars. Property damage was equally staggering, with England losing property worth two thousand crore and Germany facing unimaginable losses. Nations prioritized arms production, leading to scarcity of basic necessities, reduced production, and increased inflation. People struggled for livelihood, markets lacked essential goods, and economic life was shattered, pushing nations into another economic depression. The war resulted in major shifts in global politics and economy.

## Communism in China

Many nations were impressed by Russia's rapid progress under communism following the Bolshevik Revolution (1917). China was among them. After World War II, Japan weakened, reducing its control over China. Taking advantage of this, **Mao Tse-Tung** led a revolution that established communist rule in China in 1949.

## The Beginning of Cold War - long term effects

Following World War II, the world became dominated by two superpowers: America and Russia. Although allies during the war, their differences grew afterward. The democratic America and the communist Russia became opposing blocs. Smaller nations aligned with one of these groups. The ideological conflict and war of words between the superpowers created the atmosphere of the **Cold War**. This tension frequently brought the world to the brink of a potential third world war.
