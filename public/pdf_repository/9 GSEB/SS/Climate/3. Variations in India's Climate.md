# Variations in India's Climate

There are many variations in the climate of India.

*   South India has a peninsular shape and experiences a moderate **maritime climate** due to its proximity to the sea.
*   The climate becomes more **continental** as one moves away from the sea shore. A large part of North India is away from the sea shore, so it experiences a continental climate.
*   The Tropic of Cancer passes almost through the middle of India. The area to the south of it falls in the **torrid zone**, while the area to the north of it is in the **temperate zone**.

As a result, there is a large difference in temperature and rainfall in different parts of the country:

*   Temperatures in Leh and Dras in north of Ladakh can be as low as -45°C during winter.
*   Sri Ganganagar and Alwar in Rajasthan can record high temperatures of 51°C during summer.
*   Cherrapunji in Meghalaya gets about 1200 cm rainfall annually, which is among the highest in the world. Mawsynram, about 16 km away, is famous for receiving maximum rainfall during a twenty-four-hour period.
*   In contrast, the deserts of Rajasthan receive only 10 to 12 cm rainfall annually.

These contradicting climatic conditions mean that while one region might face devastating floods, another faces drought due to intense heat and scanty or absent rainfall. Some regions also face devastating effects of thunderstorms, sandstorms, and tropical cyclones. Thus, seasonal weather has diversified effects across India.
