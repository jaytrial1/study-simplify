This section explains specific wind and storm phenomena common during the hot weather season in India.

# Summer Phenomena

## Norwester

A low-pressure pocket over north and north-east India, extending up to Bihar, can cause some parts of Odisha and Jharkhand to become occasionally hotter. This phenomenon is known as **Norwester**. These winds are locally called **'kaal Baisakhi'** in eastern India and can cause significant damage.

## Loo

In the arid regions of west and north-west India, sandstorms or thunderstorms are common. In summer, especially in May, very hot winds blow over North India known as **'Loo'**. Excessive heat from the Loo can be fatal to people and animals.

During this season, tropical cyclones can develop over the Bay of Bengal and the Arabian Sea. They advance towards coastal areas, causing devastating effects. Most of the country experiences hot and dry weather during summer, with little to no rain in most places. However, the Malabar coast sometimes receives rain which benefits the mango crop, hence called **'AmraVrushti'**. This rain is useful for mango and coffee crops.
