This section focuses on the characteristics of the winter season in India, including temperature, winds, and regional variations.

# Cold Weather Season - Winter (December to February)

The period from December to February is considered winter in India. During this time, the sun shines vertically over the southern hemisphere (between September 22nd and March 21st). Since India is in the northern hemisphere, it receives slant sun rays between December and February, resulting in low temperatures.

*   Winds blow from the north-east direction from Central Asia. These winds are dry and cold, leading to dry and cold weather.
*   The sky is generally clear (cloudless) during this season.

North-West India is comparatively cooler because it is far from the sea and includes desert areas. This region develops high pressure during winter, deflecting winds. Dry and cool winds generated by high pressure reduce temperatures.

*   In Delhi, temperature often drops below 10°C.
*   It remains around 16°C in Allahabad and 18°C in Kolkata.
*   Temperatures in the Himalayas are much lower; Shimla and Darjeeling have temperatures around 5°C in January.

After snowfall in the Himalayas, cold and heavy winds rush towards the North Indian plains, causing a cold wave in this region, along with Gujarat and Rajasthan. Temperatures decrease suddenly. Frost can occur in some areas, damaging crops like cotton.

Temperatures generally do not fall below freezing point during winter, except in high mountainous regions. The Central Himalayas act as a natural barrier, obstructing extremely cold winds from Central Asia and protecting India from severe cold.

Conditions in South India during winter differ from North India. South India is in the torrid zone, near the equator, and has a peninsular shape with interior areas not far from the sea. Thus, it does not experience severe cold like North India. There is no snowfall, and temperatures do not drop very low (e.g., Kochi ~26°C, Madurai ~25°C, Chennai ~24°C in January). Mountainous areas in South India do experience lower temperatures. Temperature generally decreases towards the north in India during this season.

Winter is considered a pleasant and healthy season in India. Due to longer and cooler nights, dew and fog are common in the early morning. The day is shorter and night is longer.

Dry winds from landmass generally do not bring rain, but North-East winds coming from the Bay of Bengal contain humidity and give significant rainfall over the Coromandel coast. North-east India gets some rainfall due to western disturbances and thunderstorms, which is useful for the *Rabi* crop in Punjab and Haryana. These winds occasionally bring rain to Gujarat, known locally as 'mavthu'.
