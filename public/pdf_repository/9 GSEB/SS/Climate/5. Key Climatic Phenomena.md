This section explains specific atmospheric and oceanic phenomena that can influence Indian weather patterns, often mentioned in weather reports.

# Key Climatic Phenomena

Certain phenomena, sometimes occurring far from India, can cause temporary changes in the long-term climatic pattern.

## Jet Stream

These are very powerful, high-speed winds blowing in a pipe-shaped belt at an altitude of 8 to 15 km, typically around 30° latitude in both hemispheres. Their average velocity is about 150 km/hour, reaching about 400 km/hour in the central part. During winter, they blow over the southern slopes of the Himalayas; in summer, they are stationed over peninsular India. High altitude winds like Jet Streams can influence rainfall patterns.

## Western Disturbances

Jet Streams formed over West Asia blow eastward, affecting West Asian countries, North-West India, Pakistan, and Afghanistan. Dust storms accompanying these disturbances can reach up to Bangladesh. They often disrupt the pleasant winter weather in North India, causing snowfall in very high mountains and some rain in the plains, which is useful for the *Rabi* crop. This can also cause unseasonal rain, locally known as 'mavthu', in Gujarat, potentially damaging crops.

## El-Nino

This is a Spanish word meaning 'small child', named by Peruvian fishermen because its impact is often felt around Christmas. It is a warm current originating west of Peru along the Pacific coast in South America due to atmospheric and oceanic conditions. This current flows westwards, and its impact can be felt up to India. The El-Nino phenomenon occurs occasionally and can cause changes in the duration and amount of monsoon rainfall in India.

## ITCZ (Inter Tropical Convergence Zone)

A large low-pressure area develops over the equator where the Trade Winds converge. This zone is called the Inter Tropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ). The Trade winds rise upwards as air currents in this zone. In July, the ITCZ is typically stationed over 20° to 25° north latitude, stable over the Ganga plains in India. The low pressure here draws winds from the southern hemisphere oceans towards this area, causing rain in parts of North India. During the colder season, the ITCZ shifts further south, causing winds to change direction to north-east.
