# India's Appeal and the Search for a New Sea Route

Since ancient times, India has held a significant position globally due to its rich economic, cultural, and religious heritage. This prosperity attracted people from various nations. Indian goods like spices, muslin, silk cloth, and indigo were highly sought after in European countries. Trade between India and Europe historically occurred via land and sea routes, with Istanbul (Constantinople) in Turkey serving as a central point.

However, the conquest of Constantinople by the Turkish Muslims in 1453 A.D. closed the traditional sea route for Europeans. As spices were essential, Europeans were compelled to find a new sea route, initiating an era of geographical discoveries.
