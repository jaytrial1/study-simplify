# Arrival and Establishment of European Trade Centers

The Portuguese were the first Europeans to arrive in India for trade after the new sea route was discovered. Within a century, they controlled territories like Diu, Daman, Goa, Cochin, and Malacca.

Inspired by the Portuguese success, the **Dutch** (from Holland) and the **Danish** (from Denmark) also came to India for trade.

The **English** traders formed the **East India Company** to trade with eastern countries. In 1600 A.D., Queen Elizabeth of England granted the Company permission to trade. The first English ship, led by Captain William Hawkins, arrived in Surat. Initially, the Portuguese dominance prevented them from establishing trade there.

Eventually, the British East India Company received permission from the Mughal Emperor **Jehangir** and established its first trade center (**Kothi**) in Surat in 1613 A.D. Initially, they also set up centers in Bharuch and Ahmedabad.

Facing strong opposition and potential danger from the Marathas in the region, the Company shifted its focus towards the south and east, establishing centers in Machlipattanam (Andhra Pradesh), Saint George (Chennai), and Fort William (Kolkata). Mumbai became their headquarters in 1687 A.D.

The **French East India Company** arrived in India in 1668 A.D. and established trade centers in Mahe, Karaikal, Pondicherry (Puducherry), Chandranagar, and Machlipattanam.
