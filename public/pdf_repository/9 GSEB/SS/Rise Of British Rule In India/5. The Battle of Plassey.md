This section details the events leading up to and the outcome of the Battle of Plassey, marking a significant step in the British rise to power.

# The Battle of Plassey

**Siraj-ud-Daula** was the ruler of Bengal. His impulsive nature created opponents within his state. The British East India Company, without the Nawab's permission, built a fortress around their trade center in Kolkata, citing security reasons. Nawab Siraj-ud-Daula ordered the destruction of this fortification.

Upon hearing this news, the Company in Madras (Chennai) sent a small army led by **Robert Clive** to Bengal to support their Kolkata center. Despite having a strong army, the Company realized defeating the Nawab's forces directly would be difficult.

Robert Clive resorted to **treachery**. A conspiracy was hatched involving the Nawab's commander-in-chief, **Mir Jafar**, and a merchant, **Seth Amichand**, who were persuaded to betray the Nawab. Using the pretext that the Nawab was harassing the people, the British declared war.

The battle took place near a village called Plassey.

*   The **Battle of Plassey** was fought on **23rd June, 1757 A.D.**
*   The battlefield is located 38 km from Murshidabad in West Bengal.

Following the conspiracy, Mir Jafar deliberately lost the battle. Siraj-ud-Daula was defeated. Due to Clive's treachery, the battle concluded in just half a day.

As a result, the Company gained control over 24 Parganas. Mir Jafar was installed as the new Nawab of Bengal. The Battle of Plassey in 1757 is considered the **foundation of the Company's rule in India**.
