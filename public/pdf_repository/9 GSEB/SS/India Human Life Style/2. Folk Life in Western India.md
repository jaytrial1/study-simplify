This section details the lifestyle aspects, including food, dress, dwellings, language, festivals, and fairs, of the states and union territories in Western India.

# Folk Life - Western India

Major states in Western India include Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Goa, along with the Union Territories of Diu-Daman, Dadra and Nagar Haveli.

## Food

The staple food in Rajasthan is *millet* and *dal-bati*. *Marwadi kachori* is a popular breakfast item. In Gujarat, the main food includes *rotli-bhakhari*, vegetables, dal and rice, *khichdi*, and *kadhi*. *Khaman* and *ganthia* are preferred savoury snacks. Maharashtrians often prefer *sev-usal*. People living along the sea coasts of Gujarat, Maharashtra, and Goa primarily eat *rice* and *fish*.

Gujaratis, being historically traders who travel long distances, often favour durable food items for travel. These include *thepla*, *ganthia*, dry *kachori*, *khakhro*, and *sukhdi*.

## Dress

Rajasthan, being a desert and dry region with less vegetation diversity, compensates this through colourful dresses.
*   Men typically wear *dhoti*, *angarkhu*, and a colourful headgear.
*   Women wear a *petticoat* with a large circumference, a *blouse*, and an *odhani*.
Footwear like *mojadi* and other items made from camel hides are common due to the large camel population.

Traditional attire in Gujarat:
*   Men wear *dhoti*, *kurta*, and a white cap or headgear (*turban*).
*   Women wear *sari*, *petticoat*, and *blouse*.

Traditional attire in Maharashtra:
*   Men wear *dhoti* and *paheran*, with a cap or *turban* on the head.
*   Women wear *sari* in a typical Maharashtrian style.

The traditional dressing in Madhya Pradesh is similar to that of neighboring states like Maharashtra and Gujarat. In Goa, men wear *dhoti* and *paheran*, while women wear *sari*, *petticoat*, and *blouse*. However, Goan dressing shows a distinct Western influence. Thus, each region has unique traditional dressing characteristics.

## Dwelling

In Rajasthan, due to low rainfall and deserts, most houses have flat roofs. Rural houses are often made of grass and mud. In Gujarat, people live in modern buildings constructed with brick and cement. Urban areas in Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh, and Goa also feature well-planned houses with modern facilities. In all states, forest dwellers in forests or mountainous areas live in scattered huts. In the Konkan region, which receives heavy rainfall, roofs are slanted.

Dwellings in Kachchh (Gujarat) are known as *Bhunga*. These round, mud-walled houses with conical thatched roofs were noted for being least damaged during the earthquake on January 26, 2001.

## Language

Hindi is the main language spoken in Rajasthan, with *Marwadi* dialect spoken in Marwad. Gujarati is the primary language in Gujarat, and *Kachchhi* dialect is spoken in Kachchh. Hindi is the main language in Madhya Pradesh. Marathi is spoken in Maharashtra, and Konkani is spoken in Goa. Regional dialects are also used depending on the specific area within each state.

## Festivals and Celebrations

Indians celebrate numerous festivals and holy days. Rajasthan is known for its distinctive folk songs and dances. Festivals like *Holi* and *Ganagaur* are celebrated with great enthusiasm. Famous folk dances of Rajasthan include *Ghummar*, *Kachchighodi*, and *Kalbelia*. Gujarat is renowned globally for its *raas-garba* dances. Maharashtra celebrates *Ganesh Chaturthi* with great pomp, and its *Lavni* dance is also very famous. In Ujjain, Madhya Pradesh, *Shiv Ratri* is celebrated with much enthusiasm. Common festivals celebrated across Western India include *Diwali*, *Navratri*, *Shivratri*, *Dashera*, *Ganesh Chaturthi*, *Eid*, *Moharram*, *Christmas*, *Mahavir Jayanti*, *Pateti*, *Chetti Chand*, and *Buddha Jayanti*.

## Fairs

Pushkar in Rajasthan and Siddhpur in Gujarat are famous for their camel trade fairs held on *Kartik Purnima*. A fair for trading donkeys is held at Vautha in Dholka taluka, Gujarat. Other notable fairs in Gujarat include the Tarnetar Fair near Thangadh, Bhavnaath Fair in Junagadh, and Dang Darbar Fair in Dangs. Ujjain in Madhya Pradesh and Nashik in Maharashtra are famous for hosting *Kumbh* and *Ardha Kumbh* Fairs, while Goa is known for its Carnivals.

In the *Adhik Bhadarvo* month, which occurs approximately every 18 years, a fair is organized at Bhadbhut in Vagra taluka of Bharuch district, Gujarat.
