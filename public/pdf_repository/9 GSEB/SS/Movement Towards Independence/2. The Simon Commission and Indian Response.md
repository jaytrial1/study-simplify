This section details the appointment of the Simon Commission, its composition, and the widespread Indian opposition it faced due to the absence of Indian members.

# Simon Commission (1927)

The *Montford (Montague-Chelmsford 1919) reforms* had stipulated that a commission would be sent to India after ten years to review the constitutional reforms and suggest further changes. However, the government appointed the *Simon Commission* two years early. The commission was led by Sir John Simon and included seven other members, all of whom were members of the British Parliament. Crucially, *no Indian* was included as a member.

Indians strongly recommended the inclusion of Indian members to ensure a proper understanding of the country's issues, but this was rejected by the British. Consequently, Indians decided to oppose the commission. The arrival of the Simon Commission was met with widespread *strikes*, *assemblies*, *processions*, loud slogans of *"Simon, Go Back"*, and the unfurling of *black flags*.

The British government responded with brutal suppression. Leaders like *Lala Lajpat Rai*, *Govind Vallabh Pant*, and *Jawaharlal Nehru* were subjected to oppressive measures. During a procession in Lahore, the Assistant Police Superintendent ordered a *lathi charge* on Lala Lajpat Rai's procession, seriously injuring him. He passed away a few months later. His death enraged *Veer Bhagat Singh*, *Sukdev*, and *Rajguru*, leading them to kill Saunders in retaliation.
