This section details the Mountbatten Plan, its acceptance, the passing of the Indian Independence Act, the formal transfer of power, and the reactions to independence and partition.

# Mountbatten Plan (1947) and Partition of India

On *March 24, 1947*, Viceroy *Mountbatten* presented his scheme, concluding that there was no alternative but the *Partition of India*. National leaders like Sardar Patel understood the gravity of the situation. After extensive discussions and deliberation, Viceroy Mountbatten finalized the plan for the partition of India on *June 3, 1947*. This plan is known as the *Mountbatten Plan*.

Both the Congress Leaders and the Muslim League accepted this plan. As the Mountbatten Plan was implemented, the people of East Bengal and the Sylhet province of Assam voted to join *East Pakistan*. West Bengal and East Punjab remained with India based on decisions by their respective assemblies. West Punjab joined *Pakistan*. It was also decided that the Northwest Frontier Province, Sindh, and Baluchistan would affiliate with Pakistan.

## Indian Independence Act (July 1947)

The British Parliament passed the *Indian Independence Act* in July 1947 based on the Mountbatten Plan. According to this Act, Lord Mountbatten became the first Governor General of India, and Muhammad Ali Jinnah became the first Governor General of Pakistan. This Act formally ended India's dependence on British rule. Gandhiji considered this Act as "The one and only, best and beneficial Act given by the Britishers to India".

The last viceroy, Lord Mountbatten, organized a function in New Delhi at midnight on *August 14*. The Union Jack was lowered, and the Indian National Flag, the tricolour, was hoisted. The documents transferring independence to India were handed over by Mountbatten to *C. Rajagopalachari*, who became the first Governor General of Independent India. The atmosphere was filled with excitement, and the transfer of power was completed.

*August 15, 1947*, marked an excellent dawn for India. The first independence festival was celebrated joyously across the country. People hung 'Torans' (decorative hangings) on doors, sweets were distributed in schools, and many felt relief as their vows were fulfilled. However, Gandhiji was deeply shocked and saddened by the partition of India, plunging into grief and meditation.
