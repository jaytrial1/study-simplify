This section describes the historic Dandi March led by Mahatma Gandhi, the breaking of the Salt Law, and the initiation of the wider Civil Disobedience Movement.

# Dandi Yatra (From March 12 to April 6, 1930)

As a key part of the *Civil Disobedience Movement*, *Gandhiji* decided to march to the village of Dandi on the sea-coast to symbolically break the *Salt Law*. On the evening of March 11, a large assembly heard his message of *Satyagraha*. Despite the risk of arrest, people were urged to continue the non-violent struggle with firm determination.

On *March 12, 1930*, Gandhiji commenced the *'Dandi Yatra'* with his supporters from Harijan Ashram (now Gandhi Ashram), singing the hymn 'Vaishnav jan to tene re kahiye je, peed parayi jaane re'. Gandhiji emphasized the bravery required for the struggle, stating, "Brave never run away from struggle, the one who runs by seeing the struggle is not brave." Prominent figures like *Sarojini Naidu* and *Mahadevbhai Desai* also joined the march.

The march covered 370 kms, passing through various towns and regions like Aslali, Bareja, Nadiad, Anand, Boriavi, Ras, Jambusar, Bharuch, Surat, and Navsari. In these areas, people were educated about the purpose of the Civil Disobedience Movement and the reason for breaking the Salt Law. At Bhat (March 29, 1930), Gandhiji declared, "I shall die the death of crow and dog, but I will not return to this Ashram without attaining Poorna Swaraj."

As the Dandi March progressed, people cleaned village roads, decorated houses, and showed strong support for the *satyagraha*. The Yatra significantly boosted *faith*, *unity*, and *consciousness* among the people, spreading a wave of satyagraha across the nation.

Gandhiji and his companions reached Dandi after 24 days on *April 5, 1930*. Early the next morning, on *April 6, at 6:30 a.m.*, Gandhiji broke the Salt Law by picking up a handful of salt from the sea-coast. He declared loudly, "I have broken the Salt law" and addressed the gathering, stating, "I, by this event, am applying salt to the foundation of building in the form of British Empire." Shri Mahadevbhai Desai compared this act to 'Mahabhinishkraman' (the great renunciation).

Journalists, photographers, writers, and historians from India and abroad documented the event, publishing reports in their newspapers and booklets. The Dandi Yatra, starting from Sabarmati Ashram, gained national acclaim and symbolically began to undermine the British Empire's foundation.

The *Civil Disobedience Movement* sparked new national consciousness. Despite brutal suppression by the British Police involving *lathi charges*, *arrests*, *shooting*, and oppression on women and children, the agitation persisted.

During the Dandi March and the wider movement, numerous *constructive programmes* were undertaken, including:
*   Boycott of foreign clothes
*   Prohibition of liquor
*   Removing untouchability
*   Promoting Hindu-Muslim unity
*   Oath to do social services

The Dandi Yatra and the breaking of the Salt Law not only raised nationwide consciousness but also brought global fame to the concept of non-violent struggle. Further agitations included boycotting foreign goods, picketing liquor shops, refusing to pay revenue tax, and satyagraha for removing untouchability. The government responded with increased atrocities. In turn, people sometimes reacted by damaging government property like railway stations, post offices, police stations, and government buildings.

Notable incidents included the agitation led by *Abdul Gafar Khan* ('Frontier Gandhi') at Vadala, Bombay; the struggle against foreign liquor led by *Kasturba Gandhi* in Bombay; and agitations in Gharsana (Surat) and Viramgam during the Salt Law movement. Many Indians resigned from government services, and students left government schools.
