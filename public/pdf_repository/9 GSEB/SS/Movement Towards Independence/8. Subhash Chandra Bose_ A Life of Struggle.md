This section introduces Subhash Chandra Bose, tracing his early life, education, entry into politics, and his growing differences with the Congress leadership.

# Azad Hind Fauj and Subhash Chandra Bose

*Subhash Chandra Bose* was born in Cuttack, Orissa, on January 23, 1897. His father, Rai Bahadur Janikinath Bose, was a government lawyer, and his mother, Parvatidevi, was a philanthropist. Despite early predictions that he would become a loyal servant of the British, he grew up to be a staunch freedom fighter.

After excelling at Calcutta's Presidency College, he went to England for further studies. He passed the difficult *Indian Civil Service (ICS)* examination, securing the fourth rank, and returned to Calcutta. The insulting attitude of British professors towards Indians at Presidency College had instilled revolutionary ideas in him.

He was deeply interested in the Indian Independence struggle and became an active member of the *Indian National Congress*. He joined the *Swarajya Party* in 1923 and quickly became a popular youth leader. He was prominent in the Civil Disobedience Movement and was jailed multiple times.

In 1938, at the age of forty-one, he was elected President of the Congress convention at Haripura (Surat), showcasing his capabilities. However, increasing differences of opinion with Gandhiji led him to leave the Congress. He then established his own party, the *Forward Bloc*. To mobilize public opinion against the British, he traveled extensively across India. He was arrested under the *Indian Defence Code* and imprisoned.

During his imprisonment, he was ill-treated. His health deteriorated while he was fasting 'Unto the Death', leading to his imprisonment at his residence.

He then cleverly escaped from Calcutta in the disguise of a Pathan, traveling via Peshawar, Kabul, Iran, and Russia, eventually reaching *Berlin, Germany* on March 28, 1942. Indians residing in Germany gave him a warm welcome. He used *Berlin radio* to urge Indians to fight against the British Empire. He also met *Hitler* in Germany to discuss India's independence. He later went to Rome and Paris to establish military units for Indian independence, recruiting 3000 Indians.

Meanwhile, *Rasbihari Bose*, a revolutionary living in Japan, consolidated various organizations and established the *Indian Independence League*. The *Azad Hind Fauj* (Indian National Army) was set up to fight for India's freedom. The organization included 100 Indian delegates from various Southeast Asian regions. *Mohan Singh*, a war prisoner captured by Japan, was also part of this effort. Rasbihari Bose eventually retired as the head of the INA.
