This section covers the Indian response to the Simon Commission's challenge, the formulation of the Nehru Report, and the subsequent demand for complete independence.

# Nehru Committee

In response to the opposition against the Simon Commission, the British India Secretary, Lord Birkenhead, challenged Indian leaders to draft a constitution that all parties could agree upon. Accepting this challenge, the *Indian National Congress* constituted the *Nehru Committee* under the chairmanship of *Motilal Nehru*.

The report submitted by this committee, known as the *Nehru Report*, proposed key features such as *Dominion Status*, an *Independent Judiciary*, *Fundamental Rights*, and *Adult Suffrage*. However, the British government ultimately rejected these recommendations.

# Demand for Complete Independence, 1929

During this period, younger leaders like *Jawaharlal Nehru* and *Subhash Chandra Bose* became prominent national figures, advocating strongly for *Complete Independence* rather than just Dominion Status, which the youth of India increasingly desired.

At the *Lahore session* of the Indian National Congress in 1929, held on the banks of the river Ravi, a resolution for *Complete Independence* (*Poorna Swaraj*) was passed under the leadership of Jawaharlal Nehru. Following this, on *January 26, 1930*, members took an oath of Complete Independence and celebrated this day as *Independence Day*. This date later became significant for independent India, as the Constitution was implemented on this day, marking *Republic Day*.
