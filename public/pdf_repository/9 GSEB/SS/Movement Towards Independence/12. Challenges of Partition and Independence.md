This section outlines the immediate and complex problems faced by India and Pakistan following the partition and the granting of independence.

# India becoming Independent and the immediate problems of it

With India and Pakistan emerging as two separate, independent nations based on the Mountbatten Plan and the Indian Independence Act, 1947, several immediate problems arose that required urgent resolution:
*   Reorganization of military forces.
*   Disputes over assets and properties.
*   Division of debts and credits.
*   Problem and settlement of refugees.
*   Public services and the arrangement of civil servants and administrative staff.
*   Demarcation of international boundaries on the eastern and western sides of India.

A five-member committee, consisting of two representatives each from India and Pakistan under the chairmanship of Lord Mountbatten, was formed to address these issues. This committee appointed expert commissions for relevant subjects. In case of disagreements, both nations agreed to accept the final decision of an arbitrator, comprising one representative from India and one from Pakistan, chaired by the former Chief Justice of India.

The Military Commandant General of India managed the partition of the army, military weapons, etc. The British army left India by February 28, 1948. Officers and employees in administration, military, and judiciary were given the choice to serve either India or Pakistan and were assured of their pay, allowances, service security, and pension.
