This section focuses on the Quit India Movement, its context during World War II, the call for immediate independence, and the arrest of leaders.

# Quit India Movement

Considering the situation during *World War II*, the Congress Working Committee ended the *Individual Satyagraha* in January 1942. The British Government sent the *Cripps Mission* to India, but it was perceived by Indians as an attempt to deceive them, leading to anger and dejection. People lost faith in the British rulers, believing they had no intention of granting independence.

Gandhiji urged the people to overcome despair and prepare for a final decisive struggle. On *August 8, 1942*, the resolution for the *'Quit India Movement'* was passed in the Congress Working Committee meeting held in Mumbai.

The very next day, prominent leaders including *Gandhiji*, *Vallabhbhai Patel*, *Jawaharlal Nehru*, *Maulana Azad*, and many others were arrested. Newspapers were banned, and regional and district-level Congress leaders were simultaneously apprehended. The arrest of leaders triggered nationwide strikes. Gandhiji gave the powerful slogan *"Do or Die"*, emphasizing that this was the final struggle for independence and people should make every effort to achieve it.

## Strikes during Quit India Movement

The movement saw participation from all sections of society across rural and urban areas, including *workers*, *farmers*, *youth*, *students*, *teachers*, *businessmen*, and *women*. Workers went on *strike* in factories, including iron factories in Jamshedpur and cotton mills in Mumbai and Madras. In Ahmedabad, 1 lakh mill workers from 75 cotton mill industries conducted a peaceful strike for 105 days. Schools and colleges also observed strikes. Markets in Ahmedabad remained closed for nearly three and a half months.

## Harmful activities during Quit India Movement

Anger against the British Government was expressed throughout the country. The brutal suppression of peaceful protesters by the British led to violence. People not only *ransacked* post offices, telegram departments, police stations, railway stations, government buildings, and educational institutions but also damaged railway lines, bridges, and roads, looting assets and setting government properties on fire. The railways incurred the heaviest losses. In many areas, *bombs* were used.
