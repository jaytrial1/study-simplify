This section details Subhash Chandra Bose's journey to Japan, his leadership of the Azad Hind Fauj and Indian Independence League, the establishment of the Provisional Government, and its activities.

# Azad Hind Fauj and Indian Independence League

Breaking through tight British security, *Subhash Chandra Bose* reached Japan from Berlin by submarine in 1943. *Rasbihari Bose* of the Indian Independence League invited Subhash Chandra Bose to take over the leadership. It was decided that the *Azad Hind Fauj* would consist solely of Indian soldiers and officers fighting for India's independence, with full support from Japan. It was promised that as the Japanese army advanced into India, capturing provinces, provisional governments would be formed and recognized by Japan.

On July 2, 1943, Subhash Chandra Bose arrived in Singapore from Japan. On July 4, 1943, he was elected President of the Indian Independence League. Rasbihari Bose voluntarily retired as head of the Indian National Army (Azad Hind Fauj) and appointed Subhash Chandra Bose as its head. Indians in the region found a capable leader in him for freeing their homeland, and he was affectionately called '*Netaji*'.

In his speeches and on radio broadcasts, he gave inspiring slogans like *"Chalo Delhi"* (Let's march to Delhi) and *"Tum Muje khoon do, Mein tumhe Azadi doonga"* (Give me blood, I will give you freedom). Under his skilled guidance, he formed a *parallel government*. He urged Indians residing in Southeast Asia to sacrifice everything for India's independence struggle.

## Establishment of Provisional Government by Subhash Chandra Bose

Subhash Chandra Bose formed a *Provisional Indian Government* (Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind), becoming its Prime Minister and head of the army. He assigned other departments to his companions, forming a cabinet. This government received recognition from Japan, Germany, Italy, Nationalist China, and Burma. It recognized *Hindi* as the national language and the *tri-colour* as the national flag. Funding was planned through assistance from Japan, independent initiatives, and property tax. Netaji visited the *Andaman and Nicobar islands*, renaming them '*Shaheed*' (Martyr) and '*Swarajya*' (Independence) respectively.

## Last step and Martyrdom of Subhasbabu

In May 1944, under Netaji's leadership, his military units captured Rangoon, Prom, and Kohima (now the capital of Nagaland). They then advanced towards Imphal (now the capital of Manipur). However, after the dropping of two atomic bombs on Nagasaki and Hiroshima in Japan, financial assistance to the provisional government ceased. British aircraft began attacking the Azad Hind Fauj, making their situation critical.

On August 18, 1945, Netaji reportedly departed from Taipei airport in Formosa (Taiwan) for Tokyo (Japan). However, his plane is said to have caught fire, causing him serious burns. He reportedly died while undergoing treatment. The mystery surrounding his death remains unsolved to this day.
