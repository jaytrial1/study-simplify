This section describes the attempts at negotiation between the Indian leaders and the British government following the Civil Disobedience Movement, including the Round Table Conferences and the Gandhi-Irwin Pact.

# Gandhi-Irwin Pact, Round Table Conference and Moratorium of Satyagraha

Following the widespread Civil Disobedience Movement, the British government invited Gandhiji for a *Round Table Conference* in London to discuss constitutional structure and reforms. The *First Round Table Conference* was held, but it was deemed futile due to the absence of Congress leaders.

To secure Congress participation, a pact was made between Gandhiji and Viceroy *Lord Irwin* in March 1931, known as the *Gandhi-Irwin Pact*. Key points included:
*   Permission for people near the sea-coast to freely collect or manufacture salt.
*   Permission for peaceful picketing.

Gandhiji attended the *Second Round Table Conference* as the sole representative from Congress. However, he was deeply disappointed by the Britishers' approach, particularly their emphasis on separate electorates for various religious communities, which seemed designed to divide Indians. Consequently, the conference dissolved without significant progress.
