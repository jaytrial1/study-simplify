This section explains the concept of a Court of Records and its implications for the Supreme Court and High Courts.

# Court of Records

A **Court of Records** holds an evidential value and when any records from the court are presented, no objection can be taken against it. The judgments and decisions of the Supreme Court are considered as permanent documents and all are bound to accept it. They are used as references by the subordinate courts while giving judgments and decisions. Any court showing its contempt or disapproval can be punished.

The High Court occupies the place as the Court of Records. The High Court publishes its judgments, decisions in an organised form. These Judgments and decision are binding and final for they can use these court decisions and judgments as reference while taking decisions on various cases in future. The lawyers use these published judgments and decisions as citations while pleading their cases. The court has a power to punish for contempt of Court.
