This topic describes the traditional farming practices and the self-sufficient nature of rural communities in India before independence.

# Ancient Self-Reliant Rural Economy

Before independence, Indian farmers primarily practiced **traditional farming** using oxen. They were largely self-reliant, not depending on mechanical equipment. Manure and other farming resources were available at the village level, often derived from cattle rearing. The joint family system ensured the availability of labor. Farmers also saved seeds and improved them using their own knowledge, making them self-reliant in this area as well.

Villagers were interdependent, contributing to the self-sufficiency of the rural economy. Goods were often exchanged through the **barter system**, reducing the need for currency, and life's necessities were easily accessible within the village.
