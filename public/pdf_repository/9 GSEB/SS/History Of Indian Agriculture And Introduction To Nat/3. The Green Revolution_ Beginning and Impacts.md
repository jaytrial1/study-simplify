This section discusses the advent of the Green Revolution in India, its initial successes in increasing food grain production, and the subsequent negative consequences of intensive chemical farming.

# The Green Revolution: Beginning and Impacts

After India gained independence, the population grew continuously, leading to the need to import food grains. To achieve self-reliance in food production, the **Green Revolution** was initiated. This involved using hybrid seeds, chemical fertilizers, and pesticides developed by agricultural scientists, which significantly increased grain production, particularly wheat.

However, the pursuit of higher yields through intensive farming led farmers to use chemical fertilizers, pesticides, and irrigation excessively and haphazardly. This has resulted in numerous side effects in agriculture:

*   **Increased Cost of Farming:** Excessive chemical use increases farming costs annually. The need for micronutrient fertilizers and repeated cropping on the same land also adds to expenses. Soil hardening from chemical farming increases the cost of mechanized operations, and increased water salinity from excessive irrigation reduces production.
*   **Decreased Soil Fertility:** Reduced use of organic fertilizer and reliance solely on chemicals gradually decreases soil fertility and productivity.
*   **Reduced Soil Moisture and Water Pollution:** Inadequate organic manure reduces soil moisture retention, requiring more water for irrigation. Excessive chemical use also pollutes groundwater.
*   **Nutrient Deficiency in Food:** Products grown with only chemical fertilizers often lack balanced nutrients, leading to deficiencies in vitamins and minerals in humans, which can cause diseases.
*   **Contribution to Global Warming:** Excessive irrigation and chemical use produce greenhouse gases like methane and nitrous oxide, contributing to global warming.
*   **Impact on Agricultural Products and Farmer Income:** Agrochemical residues affect agricultural products, potentially hindering exports. High costs of chemical resources increase product prices, decreasing farmer income.
*   **Harm to Beneficial Organisms:** Pesticides kill useful insects, birds, and microorganisms essential for environmental balance. For example, the destruction of bees reduces pollination, leading to lower crop yields.

The negative effects of excessive chemical use in farming are evident. Just as the transition from traditional to chemical farming was accepted, there is a need to accept natural farming instinctively. Given the side effects of chemical farming, **natural farming** is presented as a strong alternative.
