This section describes the significant efforts undertaken by the government to protect specific endangered species through dedicated projects.
# Major Wildlife Conservation Projects

Laws to protect wildlife have existed in India since ancient times, notably during the reign of the Maurya King Ashok. The Indian constitution includes provisions for wildlife protection in Fundamental Duties and Directive Principles. The Parliament passed the Wild Life Conservation Act based on recommendations from the Indian Wild Life Board.

As of 2014, India had 503 Sanctuaries, 102 National Parks, and 14 Bio-reserves. Gujarat has 22 sanctuaries, 4 national parks, and 1 bio-reserve zone. Specific protection schemes have been launched for species facing extinction.

Some key projects include:

*   **Project Tiger:** Launched in 1973 to counter hunting and declining numbers. Initially covered 9 reserved areas, now expanded to 48.
*   **Lion Project:** Started in 1972 in Gir to protect Asiatic Lions, whose numbers had dropped below 100. As a result, the 2015 census recorded 523 lions.
*   **Hangool Project:** For the rare *Barasinga deer* in Kashmir.
*   **Crocodile Project:** For saline water crocodiles.
*   **Rhino Project:** For the protection of the Indian Rhino.
*   **Snow Leopard Project:** Focused on conserving snow leopards.

Effective wildlife conservation depends on the strong determination of both society and the Government to take the right steps, balancing development with environmental preservation.
