# The Need for Wildlife Conservation

Over the past few decades, the existence of wildlife has faced significant danger. Historically, India had thousands of tigers. According to the Forest and Environment Ministry figures from 2014, the number stood at 2226. The increase in tiger numbers, evident from recent foot imprints, is considered a positive sign.

However, some species are facing extinction. *Vultures* are critically endangered primarily due to consuming meat contaminated with *diclofenac*, a drug used in sick milch cattle. *Asiatic cheetahs*, present in forests at the beginning of the twentieth century, are now extinct in India. *Asiatic Lions*, once spread widely, are now confined almost exclusively to the Gir forests. Conservation efforts have helped their numbers increase to 523. Cranes, once numerous in Gujarat, have also seen a decline.

The reduction in wildlife numbers affects the quality of the environment and is a serious concern. Local examples include the *Shyam Garud* (eagle) in Gujarat's mountainous forests and the *Chilotro* in Vijaynagar taluka (Sabarkantha District), both now rarely seen.

Human greed and the pursuit of progress have led to environmental imbalance with potentially adverse results. It is crucial to take proper actions now, or future generations may only see wildlife in pictures.
