This section explains what Human Rights are, their declaration by the United Nations, and how certain important Human Rights are incorporated into the Constitution of India as Fundamental Rights, allowing citizens to live in a dignified way.

# Human Rights

**Human Rights** are those rights which not only give a person basic existence but also overall development. Society helps construct an environment that enables a person to grow. In every democratic country, citizens are conferred with basic rights. A person is not discriminated against on the basis of caste and can experience basic rights, the protection of which is assured by society and the nation. A person owns rights by birth, which are his/her basic rights, known as Human Rights.

The **United Nations General Assembly** declared Human Rights on December 10, 1948, and December 10 is celebrated as '**Human Rights Day**'. Following this global announcement, certain basic and important Human Rights were added to the **Constitution of India**. These rights are protected in a dignified way. Thus, those Human Rights included by the Constitution of India are known as **Fundamental Rights**.

These Human Rights proclaim that human beings should live in a dignified way. For the existence of human beings, basic needs (*Food, Clothes, Shelter, Education and Health*) should be received in a dignified and respectable way. A democratic country allows its citizens to enjoy equal rights for multifaceted development. The State guarantees a conducive environment where one has protection of Fundamental Rights, which is a true identity of a democratic system.
