This section describes the Peninsular Plateau, highlighting its ancient origin, general characteristics, and its division into the Malwa Plateau and the Deccan Plateau.

# Peninsular Plateau

This is the oldest region of India, appearing as an inverted triangle. Its average height is about 600 to 900 metres. The northern part slopes towards the north-east, as shown by the flow of Chambal, Son, and Damodar rivers. The southern part slopes towards the south-east. Most of its area is in the south, hence it is also called the Southern Plateau. Being surrounded by sea on three sides, it is known as a **Peninsular Plateau**.

The Peninsular Plateau is divided into two parts:
1.  Malwa Plateau
2.  Deccan Plateau

## Malwa Plateau

The **Aravalli Range**, one of the oldest fold mountains in the world, is situated to the north-west of the Malwa plateau. **Mt. Abu** is a famous hill station on this range. Its highest peak is **Gurushikhar** (1722 metres).

To the south of this region, rivers Chambal and Betwa originate from Vindhyachal and flow northwards to meet river Yamuna. River Son flows northwards to meet river Ganga. The direction of these rivers indicates that the region slopes northwards. The north-eastern part of this central upland is known as Bundelkhand.

Rivers Luni and Banas originate in the Aravalli range in the north-east; they vanish in the Rann of Kachchh. Sabarmati and Mahi rivers meet the Gulf of Khambhat. The flow of these rivers indicates that the western part of the Malwa Plateau slopes towards the south-west. Rajmahal Hills and Shillong plateau are considered part of the Chhota Nagpur plateau, which includes the Ranchi plateau.

## Deccan Plateau

The **Deccan Plateau** is located south of the Satpuda, Mahadev, and Maikal ranges, which are south of the Malwa plateau. The north-western parts of the plateau are made of lava deposits.

Its western boundary is marked by the **Western Ghats**, which run north-south along the Arabian Sea coast. The Western Ghats have several local names:
*   Sahyadri in Maharashtra and Karnataka
*   Nilgiri in Tamil Nadu
*   Annamalai and Cardamum ranges along the border of Kerala and Tamil Nadu

The southern portion of the Western Ghats is higher.

The average height of the Deccan Plateau is generally 900 to 1000 metres, but some areas are higher. Isolated hills over 900 metres form the eastern boundary, known as the **Eastern Ghats**. The general slope of the Deccan Plateau is towards the south-east, evident from the direction of river flow. Most rivers flow eastwards into the Bay of Bengal, except for the Narmada and Tapi rivers, which flow westwards.
