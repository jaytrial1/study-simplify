This section classifies and describes the six major types of soils found in India, highlighting their characteristics and distribution.

# Soil: Types in India

Soils in India are classified into six major types:
1.  Alluvial Soil
2.  Black (or regur) soil
3.  Red soil
4.  Laterite soil
5.  Mountainous soil
6.  Desert soil

## Alluvial Soil

Alluvial soil is divided into two types: **Khadar** and **Bangar**.
*   **Khadar soil** is formed from fresh alluvial deposits, typically found near rivers due to floods. It is generally sandy and very fertile.
*   **Bangar soil** contains old alluvium, found in the upper valley regions. It is sticky and has a dark colour.

Alluvial soil is found in many parts of the country, including Punjab, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, and West Bengal. Its fertility varies by location, but generally, it is very fertile.

## Black Soil (Regur Soil)

This soil is found mostly in Maharashtra, western Madhya Pradesh, Gujarat, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, and Tamil Nadu. It is considered a 'gift' of the peninsular plateau. **Black soil** is very sticky and fertile and can retain humidity for a long time. It is formed from igneous rocks and is highly useful for cotton cultivation, hence its name **Black Cotton Soil**. It is also known as **regur soil**.

## Red Soil

Found in regions with igneous and metamorphic rocks. Its characteristic red colour comes from ferrous and other humus contents. **Red soil** is porous and fertile. It is seen in Goa, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Odisha, and Jharkhand.

## Laterite Soil

**Laterite soil** develops due to excessive erosion by rain. Heavy rainfall causes leaching, where humus contents from the top soil descend into lower layers. As it contains less humus, it is less fertile. The red colour results from the erosion of red sandstones containing iron and aluminium. Such soil is found in the mountainous regions of the Deccan, Karnataka, Kerala, Odisha, and parts of the North-East.

## Mountainous Soil

Found in the mountainous regions like Meghalaya, Arunachal Pradesh, eastern hill ranges, Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, Jammu-Kashmir, and Ladakh. **Mountainous soil** often has more humus content due to forests, though this varies. Soil on the Shivalik Range is less fertile and developed, being sandy, porous, and lacking humus.

## Desert Soil

Found in arid and semi-arid regions of Gujarat, Rajasthan, Punjab, and Haryana. **Desert soil** is typically alkaline and has low humus content. Agriculture is possible in this soil primarily through irrigation.

The diversity in soils across India is a result of the diverse climate and relief features of the nation.
