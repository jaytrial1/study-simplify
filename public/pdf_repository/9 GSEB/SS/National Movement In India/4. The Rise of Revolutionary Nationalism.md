This section explores the emergence and spread of fierce revolutionary activities in India and abroad, highlighting key figures and organizations involved in the armed struggle for freedom.

Fierce revolutionary movements began in India and spread to other countries. The circumstances fueling these movements included the cancellation of the partition of Bengal, the split between "Jahal" (radicals) and "Maval" (moderates) at the Congress Conference in Surat (1907), and strong opposition to the Congress's practice of relying solely on resolutions and requests. The *Lal-Bal-Pal trio* (Lala Lajpat Rai, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Bipin Chandra Pal) adopted a radical attitude, invigorating young Indian activists and giving a new direction to the policies of the Indian National Congress.

*Lokmanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak* firmly declared, "**Freedom is my birthright and I will achieve it anyhow**". This became a guiding principle for revolutionary freedom fighters.

At that time, a group of Indian youth undertook activities to gain freedom from foreign rule. These revolutionaries were ready to sacrifice their lives for their motherland, willing to give or take lives to attain freedom.

Revolutionary activities spread across Maharashtra, Bengal, Punjab, Bihar, Orissa, Gujarat, Rajasthan, Madras province, Uttarpradesh, and central India. Organizations like '*Mitramela Society*', '*Abhinav Bharat Samaj*', '*Anushilan Samiti*', '*Anjuman-e-Muhillane Vatan*', and '*Indian Patriots Association*' (later '*Bharatmata*') gave impetus to these activities.

In the first stage of the movement, newspapers and magazines such as '*Sandhya*', '*Yugantar*', '*Navshakti*', '*Vandematram*', '*Kesari*', and '*Maratha*' provided constant support. Events like the Alipur massacre (where about 34 revolutionaries were prosecuted), the Howrah massacre, and the Dhaka massacre involved many arrests. Attempts to kill Lord Hardinge by bombing in Delhi and dropping a bomb on Viceroy Minto outside Raipur Darwaja in Ahmedabad on November 13, 1909, are examples of these activities.

In the second stage (1920-42), events like the '*Kakori Loot Case*', '*Lahore massacre*', and the dropping of a bomb on the Central Parliament (Delhi) took place. Other revolutionaries involved in these activities have been studied previously.

# Fierce Revolutionary Movement in Gujarat

*Sri Aurobindo Ghosh* was the pioneer of armed revolution in Gujarat, though he remained behind the scenes. His brother, *Baarindrakumar Ghosh*, was in the forefront (1902). He traveled to Gujarat, particularly the southern part, where he met able companions *Shri Chotubhai* and *Shri Ambubhai Purani*. *Sakaria Swami*, who was associated with the Queen of Jhansi during the 1857 revolt, also met him on the banks of the Narmada. Baarindrakumar involved middle-class youth from Vadodara, the Charotar region (Kheda), Ahmedabad, and Mehsana in the revolution. The bombing of Viceroy Minto's buggy outside Raipur Darwaja in Ahmedabad was related to these activities.

*Shri Aurobindo Ghosh* described the scheme for revolution in his book '*Bhavani Mandir*', which was translated into Gujarati and published in the journal '*Dakshina*'. To spread revolutionary thoughts without alerting the British, booklets with titles like '*Deshi Vanaspati Davao*' (Indigenous Herbal Medicines), '*Nahavana Sabu Banavvani Reet*' (Method of Making Bathing Soap), '*Kasrat*' (Exercise), '*Gulab no Kisso*' (Story of Rose), and '*Kayada no Sangrah*' (Collection of Laws) were published. These booklets secretly contained methods for preparing bombs. '*Gangnath Vidyalay*' was established near Chandod - Karnali, where secret revolutionary activities were carried out. Many Gujarati youth joined these activities and faced stern government action. However, the fight continued, and the government eventually realized that suppression alone would not suffice to rule India.

# Revolutionary Movement in Foreign Countries

The revolutionary movement for India's freedom also began in foreign countries, spreading to England, Canada, America, Germany, France, Myanmar (Burma), Malaya, Singapore, Afghanistan, and Russia.

## The active revolutionaries who took part in revolutionary activities in foreign countries are:

*Shyamji Krishna Verma*, *Madanlal Dhingra*, *Vir Savarkar*, *Lala Hardayal*, *Udhamsingh*, *Raja MahendraPratap*, *Madam Bhikhaji Cama*, *Sardarsingh Rana*, *Maulana Abdulla*, *Maulana Basir*, *Champak Raman Pillai*, and *Dr. Mathursinh "Khudabaksh"* were associated with these activities.

Revolutionary activities abroad gave impetus to those in India. Pistols were secretly sent to India from England hidden in the bedding of cooks. *Madanlal Dhingra* killed *William Wylie*, who criticized the revolutionaries. In 1907, the '*Indian Independence League*' was established in California, America, later renamed the '*Gadar Party*' by *Lala Hardayal*. A weekly called '*Gadar*' was published in four languages. *Taraknath Das* and *Kartarsingh* also joined this effort. *Champak Raman Pillai* formed the '*Hind Rastriya Swaymsevak Dal*' in Germany. A plan was made to attack India using Iraq as the headquarter (1907).

At the '*International Socialist Conference*' held in Stuttgart, Germany, the national flag prepared by *Madam Cama* was hoisted for the first time. *Raja Mahendra Pratap* formed a Provisional Independent Government in Afghanistan with himself as 'Rastra Pramukh'. *Barkatulla*, *Abidulla*, *Maulana Bashir*, *Shamshersingh*, and *Dr. Mathursingh* also joined this government. This government sought help from Russia, Iran, and Turkey, meeting with figures like the Turkish chief *Anwar Pasha*. The conspiracy known as '*Galibnama*', written on a silk handkerchief (*reshmi rumal*) urging Muslims to unite against Christians, was exposed. *Raja Mahendra Pratap* sent a golden strip signed by him to the Russian Czar, requesting him to break ties with England. Russian revolutionary *Trotsky* pledged support to the Indian revolutionaries. *Sohanlal Pathak* was active in Burma, and *Parmanand* in Singapore. Additionally, the *Komagata Maru* and *Tosa Maru* steamer incidents inspired Indian revolutionaries living abroad to fight against the British.

The Indian youth, primarily aged 18-24 years, were heavily involved in the radical freedom movement. They undertook daring acts without fearing death. When caught, they readily embraced death, shouting slogans like '*Inquilab Zindabad*' and '*Vande Mataram*' for their motherland's freedom. Their sacrifices continue to inspire younger generations.
