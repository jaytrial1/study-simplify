This section describes the formation of the Swarajya Party following the withdrawal of the Non-Co-operation Movement, its objectives, activities within the legislatures, and eventual decline.

# Swarajya Party

With the aim of maintaining national awareness among the people, *Chittaranjan Das Munshi* and *Motilal Nehru* formed the *Swarajya Party* after the withdrawal of the Non-Co-operation Movement. Their objective was to enter the legislative assemblies and oppose the government's unfair policies from within. The leaders of the Swarajya Party also adopted Gandhiji's constructive activities, such as the abolition of untouchability, Hindu-Muslim unity, revival of the spinning wheel, and liquor prohibition.

The Swarajya Party contested the legislative elections held in November 1923. Its candidates were elected in the central legislature and achieved a clear majority in several provincial legislatures. While it did not get a majority in Bengal, it emerged as a strong party. *Motilal Nehru* was elected as the leader of the party in the central Legislature, and *Chittaranjan Das* was elected in the Bengal Province.

The Swarajya Party disapproved of government budgets and proposals and voiced resentment against the government's unjust policies. It compelled the government to consider the problems of the people. The work of the Swarajya Party was instrumental in the government appointing the Simon Commission two years earlier than planned.

The Swarajya Party functioned in a disciplined manner and established high parliamentary practices, demonstrating to the British government that Indians were capable of constitutional and democratic rule. This attracted the educated class of India to the party and reignited national awareness among the people. The party played a crucial role in bringing the achievement of independence closer, highlighting its significance.

With the death of *Chittaranjan Das* in June 1925, the Swarajya Party weakened. Some members began supporting the government, while others formed a new party called the National Party. Consequently, the prestige of the Swarajya Party declined. In the 1926 election, most of its members suffered significant losses, except in the Madras Province. Figures like Bipinchandra Pal and Surendranath Bannerji strongly criticized the Party.
