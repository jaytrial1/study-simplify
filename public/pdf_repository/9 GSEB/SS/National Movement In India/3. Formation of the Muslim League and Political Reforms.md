This section covers the British strategy to divide Hindus and Muslims, leading to the formation of the Muslim League and the introduction of the Morley-Minto Reforms.

# Muslim League (1906)

Adopting the 'divide and rule' policy, the English aimed to create conflict between Hindus and Muslims. Viceroy Lord Minto and the Indian Vizier (Diwan/Vazir) Morley planned together to suppress Indian nationalism. As the national struggle grew stronger, the English began favoring upper-class Muslims. The English successfully convinced a group of Muslims that they should form a Muslim organization to demand separate voting rights and voting centers. This led to the formation of the *Muslim League* in 1906.

Key figures involved in its establishment included the religious head of the Muslims *Agakhan*, the Nawab of Dhaka *Salimullakhan*, Viceroy Minto, and his personal minister *Dunlop Smith*. To discourage Muslims from attending the annual conference of the Congress, the Muslim League's annual conference was deliberately scheduled on the same day. Thus, the demand for separate representation is seen as a result of English policy rather than an inherent desire of the Muslims. Minto initiated the policy of involving Muslims as associates, leading many writers to consider him the *father of Muslim communalism*. A historian even suggested that the real creator of Pakistan was Minto, not Mohammad Ali Jinnah or Rehmatullah. Since then, the conflict between Hindus and Muslims intensified, eventually leading to the division of India.

# Morley - Minto Reforms (1909)

The English government adopted the policy of 'Divide and Rule' to create animosity between Hindus and Muslims, which became evident in 1906. Under the leadership of Agakhan, a Muslim delegation met Viceroy Minto. Morley was the Indian Vizier at that time (1909). The reforms introduced during this period are known as the *Morley-Minto reforms*.
