This section explains the typical journey of a river from its source in the mountains to its mouth, describing the features formed at each stage.

# Different Stages of a River's Course

A river's journey can be divided into three main stages:

1.  **Upper Course:**
    *   Starts in mountainous regions.
    *   Causes intense erosion due to steep slopes and fast flow.
    *   Carries a huge volume of sand and sediments.
2.  **Middle Course:**
    *   The river's flow decreases.
    *   Meanders (winding curves) are commonly seen.
    *   Oxbow lakes can form due to meandering and floods.
3.  **Lower Course:**
    *   Near its mouth, a river divides into many **distributaries**.
    *   The area between distributaries forms a highly fertile, triangular plain called a **Delta**.

**Peninsular Rivers:**
*   Seasonal, dependent on rainwater.
*   Flow is reduced or stops completely during the dry season.
*   Generally shorter and shallower compared to Himalayan rivers.
*   Most originate in the Western Ghats and flow eastwards to the Bay of Bengal due to the slope of the land.
*   The Western Ghats act as a major water divide for peninsular rivers.
