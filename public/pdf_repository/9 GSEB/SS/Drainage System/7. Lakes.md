This section discusses the various types of lakes found in India, their formation, and their significance.

# Lakes

India has many lakes. Fresh water lakes are more common in the Himalayan region. Most lakes contain water during the rainy season.

**Lake Formation:**
*   In Himalayan region, many lakes are formed from glaciers (glacial lakes). Water fills depressions left by retreating glaciers.
*   Some lakes are formed by wind, rivers, and human actions.
*   **Ox-bow lakes** are formed in rivers with many meanders.
*   **Lagoon lakes** are created by sea tides cutting off parts of the sea; examples include Chilka, Kolleru, and Pulicat.
*   **Saline lakes** exist, like Sambhar lake in Rajasthan, used for salt production.
*   **Tectonic lakes** are formed by movements in the Earth's crust, like Wular Lake in Kashmir.
*   Other fresh water lakes in the Himalayas include Dal, Bhimtal, Loktal, and Badapani.

**Importance of Lakes:**
*   Sources for some rivers.
*   Accumulate excess rainwater, useful for irrigation and other purposes.
*   Provide water during drought.
*   Reservoirs and lakes formed by dams are used for generating hydel power.
*   Enhance natural beauty and are developed as tourist spots.
*   Important for fishery and recreation.
