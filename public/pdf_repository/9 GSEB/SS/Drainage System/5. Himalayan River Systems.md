This section provides detailed information about the three major river systems originating in the Himalayas: Sindhu, Ganga, and Brahmaputra.

# Himalayan River Systems

Sindhu, Ganga, and Brahmaputra are major rivers originating in the Himalayas. These rivers are long and have many tributaries. When a large river and its many tributaries meet, they form a **System**.

## Sindhu (Indus) River System:
*   Emerges from near Mansarovar in Tibet.
*   Flows west and then north-westwards, entering Leh district in Ladakh.
*   Has formed many inaccessible gorges.
*   Tributaries: Zaskar, Nubra, Shyok, Hunza (meet in the mountainous region); Satluj, Beas, Ravi, Chenab, Jhelum (main tributaries, meet near Mithankot in Pakistan).
*   The combined flow meets the Arabian Sea.
*   Flows slower in the plains.
*   About one third of its basin is in the mountainous region of Jammu-Kashmir.
*   River Sindhu is 2900 km long.
*   In 1960, the Indus Waters Treaty was signed with Pakistan regarding water distribution. India can use 20% of its water.
*   Irrigation in Punjab, Haryana, and south-western Rajasthan is possible by harnessing the waters of Satluj, Ravi, and Beas.

## Ganga River System:
*   Rivers Bhagirathi and Alaknanda emerge from the Gangotri area in the Himalayas and merge near Devprayag (Uttarakhand).
*   Ganga enters the plains near Haridwar.
*   Many Himalayan rivers meet Ganga: Ghaghra, Gandak, and Kosi are major ones.
*   Yamuna emerges from Yamunotri, flows to the right of Ganga, and merges near Allahabad.
*   Ghaghra, Gandak, and Kosi originate in Nepal, causing annual flood disasters in the northern plain, but also forming fertile plains crucial for agriculture.
*   Peninsular rivers like Chambal, Betwa (meet Yamuna), and Son (meets Ganga) originate in semi-arid areas, are shorter, and have limited water volume.
*   Downstream, the combined flow divides into two branches: Padma (enters Bangladesh) and Bhagirathi-Hugli (in West Bengal).
*   These two branches meet the Bay of Bengal.
*   In Bangladesh, Padma merges with the Brahmaputra (known as Jamuna there), and their combined flow is called Meghna.
*   The Ganga-Brahmaputra delta is the most fertile delta, known as Sundarvan.
*   The delta is called 'Sundarvan' because of the large number of 'sundari' trees. It is also famous as mangrove forests.
*   Total length of Ganga exceeds 2500 kilometres.
*   Ambala City acts as a water divide between Ganga and Sindhu rivers.
*   The distance from Ambala to Sundarvan is about 1800 km, with a very gentle slope (less than one metre per 6 km), causing many meanders.

## Brahmaputra River System:
*   Originates near Mansarovar in Tibet, close to the sources of Sindhu and Satluj.
*   Most of its flow is outside India.
*   Flows east parallel to the Himalayas.
*   Near Namcha Barva, it takes a 'u' turn and enters Arunachal Pradesh, known there as Dihang.
*   In Assam, it is collectively known as Brahmaputra with tributaries like Lohit and Kenula.
*   Brahmaputra is about 2900 kilometres long.
*   It is known as Tsang Po in Tibet and Jamuna in Bangladesh.
*   'Mazuli', an island in Brahmaputra, is the largest riverine island in the world.
*   Less sedimentation in Tibet; more water and sediments in India due to heavy rainfall in regions it passes through.
*   Many tributaries meet in Assam, causing the river to flow in a braided form.
*   Annual increase in water during the rainy season leads to devastating floods in Assam and Bangladesh.
*   Heavy deposition of sand raises the river bed, causing frequent changes in its course.
