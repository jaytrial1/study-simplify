# Extended Learning - Activities and Projects

1. Visit the music room of your school. You may also visit musicians in your locality. Make a list of musical instruments. Note down the parts of these instruments that vibrate to produce sound.
2. If you play a musical instrument, bring it to the class and demonstrate how you play it.
3. Prepare a list of famous Indian musicians and the instruments they play.
4. Take a long thread. Place your hands over your ears and get some one to place this thread round your head and hands. Ask her to make the thread taut and hold its ends in one hand. Now ask her to draw her finger and thumb tightly along the thread. Can you hear a rolling sound like that of a thunder? Now repeat the activity while another friend stands near both of you. Can he hear any sound?

> **Answer:** Yes, you can hear a rolling sound through the thread because the vibrations produced by drawing the finger along the taut thread travel through the solid medium (the thread) directly to your ears. Your friend standing nearby will likely not hear the sound or hear it very faintly, as the sound waves travelling through the air from the thread are much weaker. This demonstrates that sound travels well through the solid thread.

5. Make two toy telephones. Use them as shown in Fig. 13.20. Make sure that the two strings are taut and touch each other. Let one of you speak. Can the remaining three persons hear? See how many more friends you can engage in this way. Explain your observations.

> **Answer:** In the setup shown (Fig. 13.20), where multiple strings are connected and taut, if one person speaks into their can, the vibrations travel along their string to the central connection point. If the other strings are also taut and connected well, the vibrations can transfer to those strings and travel to the other cans. The other three persons *might* hear the sound, but it will likely be fainter than in a simple two-can telephone because the vibration energy is being shared among multiple strings and connection points. You can potentially engage more friends as long as the strings are kept taut and connections are good, but the signal will weaken with more divisions. This activity reinforces the idea of sound travelling through solids (strings) and how energy transfer works in this medium.

6. Identify the sources of noise pollution in your locality. Discuss with your parents, friends and neighbours. Suggest how to control noise pollution. Prepare a brief report and present it in the class.
