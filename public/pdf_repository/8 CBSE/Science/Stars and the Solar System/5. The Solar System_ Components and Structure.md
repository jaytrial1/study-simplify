# The Solar System

The **Solar System** consists of the Sun and the celestial bodies revolving around it, including planets, comets, asteroids, and meteors. The Sun's gravitational attraction keeps these objects in orbit.

Earth revolves around the Sun and is a planet in the solar system. There are seven other planets orbiting the Sun. In order of distance from the Sun, they are: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune.

**Q: I have read that there are nine planets in the solar system.**
> **Answer:** While there were nine planets recognized in the solar system until 2006 (including Pluto), the International Astronomical Union (IAU) adopted a new definition of a planet in 2006, and Pluto no longer fits this definition, so there are now considered to be eight planets.

## Did you know?

Until 2006, there were nine planets, with Pluto being the farthest. In 2006, the International Astronomical Union (IAU) redefined "planet," and Pluto does not fit, so it's no longer considered a planet of the solar system.

Let's learn about some members of the solar family.

## The Sun

The Sun is our nearest star, continuously emitting vast amounts of heat and light. It is the source of almost all energy on Earth and the main source of heat and light for all planets.

## The Planets

Planets look like stars but do not have their own light; they reflect sunlight.

**Q: Can you distinguish between planets and stars?**
> **Answer:** The simplest way to distinguish planets from stars is that stars twinkle, while planets do not. Also, planets change their positions relative to stars over time.

A planet follows a definite path called an **orbit** as it revolves around the Sun. The time for one revolution is its **period of revolution**, which increases with distance from the Sun.

**Q: I wonder why the planets do not collide while revolving around the Sun.**
> **Answer:** Planets do not collide because they revolve around the Sun in their own definite orbits and maintain their paths due to the balance between their forward motion and the Sun's gravitational pull.

The time for one rotation is its **period of rotation**.

Some planets have moons or satellites revolving around them. Any celestial body revolving around another is called its **satellite**.

**Q: The Earth revolves around the Sun. Does it make Earth a satellite of the Sun?**
> **Answer:** While technically Earth revolves around the Sun and could be considered a satellite of the Sun, the term "satellite" is generally used for bodies revolving around planets. Earth is typically called a planet of the Sun.

The Moon is a natural satellite of the Earth. There are also many man-made satellites, called **artificial satellites**, orbiting Earth.
