# The Planets of the Solar System

## Mercury (Budh)

Mercury is the planet nearest to the Sun and the smallest. It's hard to observe due to the Sun's glare but can be seen just before sunrise or after sunset near the horizon. It has no satellites.

## Venus (Shukra)

Venus is Earth's nearest planetary neighbor and the brightest planet in the night sky. It appears in the eastern sky before sunrise or western sky after sunset, often called a morning or evening star (though it's a planet). Venus has no moon. Its rotation is unusual; it rotates from east to west, opposite to Earth's west-to-east rotation.

## Activity: Observing Venus

Find out when Venus is visible. Recognize it by its brightness. It's not seen high in the sky; look 1-3 hours before sunrise or after sunset.

**Q: Does it mean that on Venus, the Sun would rise in the west and set in the east?**
> **Answer:** Yes, because Venus rotates from east to west, the Sun would appear to rise in the west and set in the east from its surface.

If you use a telescope, you can see that Venus shows phases like the moon.

## The Earth (Prithvi)

Earth is the only planet where life is known to exist. Special environmental conditions support life: the right distance from the Sun for suitable temperature, presence of water, a suitable atmosphere, and an ozone layer. We must protect our environment to preserve life on Earth.

From space, Earth appears blue-green due to light reflection from water and land.

**Q: If I am 13 years old, how many times have I gone round the sun?**
> **Answer:** If you are 13 years old, you have completed 13 revolutions around the Sun.

## Mars (Mangal)

Mars is the planet next to Earth's orbit. It appears reddish and is called the red planet. Mars has two small natural satellites.

## Mangalyaan

The Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) launched India's first Mars orbiter mission, Mangalyaan, on November 5, 2013. It successfully entered Mars' orbit on September 24, 2014, making India the first country to do so on its first attempt.

## Jupiter (Brihaspati)

Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system. About 1300 Earths could fit inside it, though its mass is only about 318 times Earth's mass. It rotates very rapidly. Jupiter has many satellites and faint rings. It's easily recognized as a bright object. A telescope can show its four large moons.

## Saturn (Shani)

Beyond Jupiter is Saturn, yellowish in color. Its unique feature is its beautiful rings, not visible to the naked eye but observable with a small telescope. Saturn also has many satellites. An interesting fact is that Saturn is the least dense planet, less dense than water.

## Uranus and Neptune

These are the outermost planets, visible only with large telescopes. Like Venus, Uranus rotates from east to west. Its most remarkable feature is a highly tilted rotational axis.

**Image:**

*The image depicts a simplified model showing a planet with a highly tilted axis rotating and orbiting a central star, illustrating Uranus's unusual orientation.*

As a result of its tilt, Uranus appears to roll on its side during its orbit.

The first four planets (Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars) are the **inner planets**, much nearer the Sun. They have very few moons.

The planets outside Mars' orbit (Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, Neptune) are the **outer planets**, much farther from the Sun. They have ring systems and a large number of moons.
