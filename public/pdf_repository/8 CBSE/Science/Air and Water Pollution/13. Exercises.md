# Exercises

## Exercises

1.  What are the different ways in which water gets contaminated?
    > **Answer:** Water gets contaminated by harmful substances such as sewage, toxic chemicals from industries, silt, agricultural chemicals like pesticides and weedicides, and hot water from power plants and industries.
2.  At an individual level, how can you help reduce air pollution?
    > **Answer:** At an individual level, you can help reduce air pollution by using cleaner fuels, reducing the use of vehicles by walking, cycling, or using public transport, planting trees, composting dry leaves instead of burning them, and generating awareness about air pollution.
3.  Clear, transparent water is always fit for drinking. Comment.
    > **Answer:** Clear, transparent water is not always fit for drinking. Water that looks clean may still contain disease-carrying microorganisms and dissolved impurities that are harmful to health. It needs to be purified before drinking.
4.  You are a member of the municipal body of your town. Make a list of measures that would help your town to ensure the supply of clean water to all its residents.
    > **Answer:** Measures to ensure the supply of clean water include treating water in municipal treatment plants before supplying it to households, strictly implementing laws for industrial units to treat waste before discharge, installing water treatment plants in all industrial areas, and promoting public awareness about preventing water pollution and conserving water.
5.  Explain the differences between pure air and polluted air.
    > **Answer:** Pure air is a mixture of gases, mainly about 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen, with small amounts of other gases like carbon dioxide, argon, methane, ozone, and water vapour. Polluted air is air that is contaminated by unwanted substances (pollutants) which have a harmful effect on living organisms and non-living things.
6.  Explain circumstances leading to acid rain. How does acid rain affect us?
    > **Answer:** Acid rain is caused when pollutants like sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide, released from sources such as industries and power plants, react with water vapour in the atmosphere to form sulphuric acid and nitric acid. These acids then fall to the earth with rain. Acid rain affects us by corroding buildings, monuments (like marble structures), and statues. It can also affect soil and plants.
7.  Which of the following is not a greenhouse gas?
    (a) Carbon dioxide
    (b) Sulphur dioxide
    (c) Methane
    (d Nitrogen
    > **Answer:** (b) Sulphur dioxide
8.  Describe the 'Green House Effect' in your own words.
    > **Answer:** The Greenhouse Effect is the process where certain gases in the Earth's atmosphere, called greenhouse gases (like carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour), trap some of the heat radiation reflected by the Earth's surface. This trapped heat warms the atmosphere, similar to how glass traps heat in a greenhouse, making life on Earth possible.
9.  Prepare a brief speech on global warming. You have to deliver the speech in your class.
10. Describe the threat to the beauty of the Taj Mahal.
    > **Answer:** The beauty of the Taj Mahal, which is made of white marble, is threatened by air pollution. Pollutants like sulphur dioxide and nitrogen dioxide from industries and refineries cause acid rain, which corrodes the marble, a phenomenon called "Marble cancer". Suspended particulate matter like soot also contributes to the yellowing of the marble.
11. Why does the increased level of nutrients in the water affect the survival of aquatic organisms?
    > **Answer:** Increased levels of nutrients in water, often from agricultural runoff, cause excessive growth of algae (algae bloom). When these algae die, decomposers like bacteria consume them and use up a large amount of the dissolved oxygen in the water body. This decrease in oxygen level makes it difficult for aquatic organisms to survive, potentially leading to their death.

## Extended Learning - Activities and Projects

1.  In some cities, a pollution check has been made compulsory for vehicles. Visit a petrol pump in order to learn about the process of conducting a pollution check. You may systematically record your findings in the following areas:
    -   Average number of vehicles checked per month.
    -   Time taken to check each vehicle.
    -   Pollutants checked for.
    -   The process of testing.
    -   Permissible levels of emission of various gases.
    -   Measures taken if the emitted gases are above the permissible limits.
    -   How frequently is a pollution check required?
2.  Conduct a survey in your school to investigate various environment related activities that have been undertaken. The class can divide itself into two groups, with each group looking at a different area. For example, one group can find out whether there is an environment club in the school. What are its objectives? What is its calendar of events? How can you become a member?
    If your school does not have such a club, you even think of starting one along with a few of your friends.
3.  Organise a field visit to a river in or around your town with the help of your teachers.
    Observations followed by discussion could focus on
    -   the history of the river.
    -   cultural traditions.
    -   role of the river in meeting the town's water needs.
    -   pollution concerns.
    -   sources of pollution.
    -   effects of pollution on the people living by the riverside as well as those living far away.
4.  Find out with the help of your teachers and the internet (if possible), whether there are any international agreements to control global warming. Which are the gases covered under these agreements?
