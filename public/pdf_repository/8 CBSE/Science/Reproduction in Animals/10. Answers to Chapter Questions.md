# Answers to Chapter Questions

1.  Explain the importance of reproduction in organisms.
    > **Answer:** Reproduction is essential for the continuation of a species. It ensures that new individuals are produced, preventing the species from becoming extinct and maintaining the population generation after generation.

2.  Describe the process of fertilisation in human beings.
    > **Answer:** Fertilisation in human beings is internal. It occurs when sperms, released by the male into the female reproductive tract, come into contact with an ovum (egg) released from the ovary. One sperm fuses with the ovum, combining their nuclei to form a single nucleus. This fusion results in the formation of a zygote, the first cell of a new individual.

    (a) Internal fertilisation occurs

4.  Indicate whether the following statements are True (T) or False (F).
    (a) Oviparous animals give birth to young ones.
    > **Answer:** False (F)
    (b) Each sperm is a single cell.
    > **Answer:** True (T)
    (c) External fertilisation takes place in frog.
    > **Answer:** True (T)
    (d) A new human individual develops from a cell called gamete.
    > **Answer:** False (F) - A new human individual develops from a zygote, which is formed by the fusion of gametes.
    (e) Egg laid after fertilisation is made up of a single cell.
    > **Answer:** False (F) - The zygote (fertilised egg) begins to divide into multiple cells soon after fertilisation, even before being laid in some cases (like hen's egg development).
    (f) Amoeba reproduces by budding.
    > **Answer:** False (F) - Amoeba reproduces by binary fission.
    (g) Fertilisation is necessary even in asexual reproduction.
    > **Answer:** False (F) - Fertilisation is the fusion of gametes, which occurs in sexual reproduction, not asexual reproduction.
    (h) Binary fission is a method of asexual reproduction.
    > **Answer:** True (T)
    (i) A zygote is formed as a result of fertilisation.
    > **Answer:** True (T)
    (j) An embryo is made up of a single cell.
    > **Answer:** False (F) - An embryo develops from a zygote through repeated cell division, so it is made up of multiple cells.

5.  Give two differences between a zygote and a foetus.
    > **Answer:**
    > 1.  **Number of Cells:** A zygote is a single cell formed by the fusion of sperm and egg, whereas a foetus is a multicellular structure composed of many cells, tissues, and organs.
    > 2.  **Developmental Stage:** A zygote is the very first stage after fertilisation, while a foetus is a later stage of embryonic development where all major body parts are identifiable.

6.  Define asexual reproduction. Describe two methods of asexual reproduction in animals.
    > **Answer:** Asexual reproduction is a mode of reproduction in which a single parent produces new individuals without the fusion of gametes. Two methods of asexual reproduction in animals are:
    > 1.  **Budding:** In organisms like Hydra, a bud (an outgrowth) develops from the parent body. This bud grows into a new individual and eventually detaches from the parent to live independently.
    > 2.  **Binary Fission:** In single-celled organisms like Amoeba, the parent cell divides into two equal halves, each containing a nucleus, resulting in the formation of two new daughter individuals.

7.  In which female reproductive organ does the embryo get embedded?
    > **Answer:** The embryo gets embedded in the wall of the uterus.

8.  What is metamorphosis? Give examples.
    > **Answer:** Metamorphosis is the transformation of a larva into an adult through drastic changes in structure and form. Examples include the life cycle of a frog (tadpole to adult frog) and the life cycle of a silkworm (larva/caterpillar and pupa to adult moth).

9.  Differentiate between internal fertilisation and external fertilisation.
    > **Answer:**
    > *   **Internal Fertilisation:** This type of fertilisation takes place inside the body of the female. It occurs in animals like humans, cows, dogs, and hens.
    > *   **External Fertilisation:** This type of fertilisation takes place outside the body of the female, typically in water. It occurs in animals like frogs, fish, and starfish.
