## WHAT YOU HAVE LEARNT

*   Microorganisms are too small and are not visible to the unaided eye.
*   They can live in all kinds of environment, ranging from ice cold climate to hot springs and deserts to marshy lands.
*   Microorganisms are found in air, water and in the bodies of plants and animals.
*   They may be unicellular or multicellular.
*   Microorganisms include bacteria, fungi, protozoa and some algae. Viruses, though different from the above mentioned living organisms, are considered microbes.
*   Viruses are quite different from other microorganisms. They reproduce only inside the host organism: bacterium, plant or animal cell.
*   Some microorganisms are useful for commercial production of medicines and alcohol.
*   Some microorganisms decompose the organic waste and dead plants and animals into simple substances and clean up the environment.
*   Protozoans cause serious diseases like dysentery and malaria.
*   Some of the microorganisms grow on our food and cause food poisoning.
*   Some microorganisms reside in the root nodules of leguminous plants. They can fix nitrogen from air into soil and increase the soil fertility.
*   Some bacteria present in the soil fix nitrogen from the atmosphere and convert into nitrogenous compounds.
*   Certain bacteria convert compounds of nitrogen present in the soil into nitrogen gas which is released to the atmosphere.

4. Can microorganisms be seen with the naked eye? If not, how can they be seen?
> **Answer:** No, microorganisms cannot be seen with the naked eye because they are very small. They can be seen with the help of a microscope.

5. What are the major groups of microorganisms?
> **Answer:** The major groups of microorganisms are bacteria, fungi, protozoa, and some algae. Viruses are also considered microbes but are different from other microorganisms.

6. Name the microorganisms which can fix atmospheric nitrogen in the soil.
> **Answer:** The microorganisms which can fix atmospheric nitrogen in the soil are certain bacteria (like Rhizobium) and blue green algae (cyanobacteria).

7. Write 10 lines on the usefulness of microorganisms in our lives.
> **Answer:**
> 1. Microorganisms are used in the preparation of curd, bread, and cake.
> 2. They are used for the large scale production of alcohol, wine, and vinegar.
> 3. Bacteria like Lactobacillus help in setting curd from milk.
> 4. Yeast is used in the baking industry for making breads and pastries.
> 5. Microorganisms are the source of antibiotics like streptomycin.
> 6. Vaccines are prepared using weakened or dead microbes to prevent diseases.
> 7. Some bacteria and blue green algae fix atmospheric nitrogen, increasing soil fertility.
> 8. Microorganisms decompose organic waste and dead organisms, cleaning the environment.
> 9. They help in the digestion of food in animals, including humans (beneficial bacteria in the gut).
> 10. They are used in making cheese and pickles.

8. Write a short paragraph on the harmful effects of microorganisms.
> **Answer:** Microorganisms can be harmful in several ways. Some, called pathogens, cause diseases in humans, plants, and animals, such as cholera, tuberculosis, anthrax, citrus canker, and rust of wheat. These diseases can spread from an infected person or animal to a healthy one through various means like air, water, food, or carriers like insects. Harmful microorganisms also cause food spoilage, which can lead to food poisoning due to the toxic substances they produce. They can also spoil clothing and leather.

9. What are antibiotics? What precautions must be taken while taking antibiotics?
> **Answer:** Antibiotics are medicines that kill or stop the growth of disease-causing microorganisms.
> Precautions to be taken while taking antibiotics:
> 1. Antibiotics should be taken only on the advice of a qualified doctor.
> 2. You must complete the course prescribed by the doctor.
> 3. Do not take antibiotics when not needed or in wrong doses, as it may make the drug less effective in the future and can kill beneficial bacteria in the body.
> 4. Antibiotics are not effective against cold and flu, as these are caused by viruses.

# Extended Learning - Activities and Projects

1. Pull out a gram or bean plant from the field. Observe its roots. You will find round structures called root nodules on the roots. Draw a diagram of the root and show the root nodules.
> **Explanation:** This activity encourages observation of root nodules, which are structures on leguminous plant roots where nitrogen-fixing bacteria like Rhizobium live symbiotically. Drawing helps in understanding their appearance and location.

2. Collect the labels from the bottles of jams and jellies. Write down the list of contents printed on the labels.
> **Explanation:** This activity helps identify common preservatives (like sugar, sodium benzoate, sodium metabisulphite) listed on food labels, linking textbook knowledge to real-world applications of food preservation.

3. Visit a doctor. Find out why antibiotics should not be overused. Prepare a short report.
> **Explanation:** This activity encourages students to seek information from a professional about antibiotic resistance and the importance of proper usage, reinforcing the precautions mentioned in the text.

4. Project : Requirements -2 test tubes, marker pen, sugar, yeast powder, 2 balloons and lime water.
Take two test tubes and mark them A and B. Clamp these tubes in a stand and fill them with water leaving some space at the top. Put two spoonfuls of sugar in each of the test tubes. Add a spoonful of yeast in test tube B. Inflate the two balloons incompletely. Now tie the balloons on the mouths of each test tube. Keep them in a warm place, away from sunlight. Watch the setup every day for next 3-4 days. Record your observations and think of an explanation.

*The image shows two test tubes, A and B, in a stand. Test tube A is clear with a stopper. Test tube B contains a cloudy liquid and has an inflated balloon on its mouth, indicating gas production.*

Now take another test tube filled $1/4$ with lime water. Remove the balloon from test tube B in such a manner that gas inside the balloon does not escape. Fit the balloon on the test tube and shake well. Observe and explain.
> **Explanation:**
> **Observations:** In test tube B, the liquid will appear cloudy and the balloon will inflate over time. Test tube A will show no significant change. When the gas from the balloon in test tube B is passed into lime water, the lime water will turn milky.
> **Explanation:** Test tube B contains sugar and yeast. Yeast is a microorganism that performs fermentation, converting sugar into alcohol and carbon dioxide gas. The carbon dioxide gas produced causes the balloon to inflate. Test tube A is a control without yeast, so no fermentation occurs and no gas is produced. The lime water turning milky is a test for carbon dioxide, confirming that the gas produced by yeast fermentation is carbon dioxide.

# Did You Know?

Bacteria have lived on the earth for much longer than human beings. They are such hardy organisms that they can live under extreme conditions. They have been found living in boiling mudpots and extremly cold icy waters. They have been found in lakes of caustic soda and in pools of concentrated sulphuric acid. They can survive at depths of several kilometres. They probably can survive in space, too. A kind of bacterium was recovered from a camera which stood on the moon for two years. There is probably no environment in which bacteria cannot survive.
