# Exercises

1. Indicate whether the following statements are True (T) or False (F).
(a) Unicellular organisms have one-celled body.
(T/F)
> **Answer:** T
(b) Muscle cells are branched.
(T/F)
> **Answer:** F (Nerve cells are branched; muscle cells are spindle-shaped)
(c) The basic living unit of an organism is an organ.
(T/F)
> **Answer:** F (The basic living unit is a cell)
(d) Amoeba has irregular shape.
(T/F)
> **Answer:** T
2. Make a sketch of the human nerve cell. What function do nerve cells perform?
> **Answer:** Nerve cells (neurons) are long and branched. They receive and transfer messages, helping to control and coordinate the working of different parts of the body.
3. Write short notes on the following.
(a) Cytoplasm
> **Answer:** Cytoplasm is the jelly-like substance present between the cell membrane and the nucleus. It contains various other components or organelles of the cell.
(b) Nucleus of a cell
> **Answer:** The nucleus is an important, generally spherical component of the living cell, usually located in the centre. It is separated from the cytoplasm by a nuclear membrane. It contains a smaller nucleolus and thread-like chromosomes which carry genes. The nucleus acts as the control centre of the cell's activities and is involved in inheritance.
4. Which part of the cell contains organelles?
> **Answer:** The cytoplasm contains organelles.
5. Make sketches of animal and plant cells. State three differences between them.
> **Answer:** Sketches would show a plant cell with a regular shape, cell wall, large central vacuole, and chloroplasts, while an animal cell would be irregularly shaped, lack a cell wall, have smaller vacuoles, and no chloroplasts. Three differences are: 1) Plant cells have a cell wall, animal cells do not. 2) Plant cells typically have one large central vacuole, animal cells have smaller vacuoles. 3) Plant cells have plastids (like chloroplasts), animal cells do not.
6. State the difference between eukaryotes and prokaryotes.
> **Answer:** Eukaryotes have cells with a well-organised nucleus enclosed by a nuclear membrane. Prokaryotes have cells where the nuclear material is not enclosed by a nuclear membrane.
7. Where are chromosomes found in a cell? State their function.
> **Answer:** Chromosomes are found in the nucleus of a cell. Their function is to carry genes and help in the inheritance or transfer of characteristics from parents to offspring.
8. 'Cells are the basic structural units of living organisms'. Explain.
> **Answer:** Just as bricks are assembled to make a building, cells are assembled to make the body of every organism. All living organisms, whether unicellular or multicellular, are made up of cells. Cells are the smallest living parts that perform basic functions necessary for life. Tissues are formed from groups of cells, and organs are formed from tissues, demonstrating that the cell is the fundamental building block.
9. Explain why chloroplasts are found only in plant cells?
> **Answer:** Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll, the green pigment essential for photosynthesis. Photosynthesis is the process by which plants make their own food using sunlight, water, and carbon dioxide. Animal cells do not perform photosynthesis and obtain energy by consuming food, so they do not need chloroplasts.

# Extended Learning - Activities and Projects

1. Visit a laboratory for senior secondary students in your school or in a neighbouring school. Learn about the functioning of a microscope in the laboratory. Also observe how a slide is observed under the microscope.
2. Talk to the senior biology teacher in your school or a neighbouring school. Find out if there are diseases which are passed on from parents to the offspring. Find out how these are carried and also if these diseases can be treated. For this you can also visit a doctor.
3. Visit an agriculture extension centre in your area. Find out about genetically modified (GM) crops. Prepare a short speech for your class on this topic.
4. Find out about Bt cotton from an agriculture expert. Prepare a short note on its advantages/disadvantages.

## Did You Know?

The cells in the outermost layer of our skin are dead. An average adult carries around about 2 kg of dead skin. Billions of tiny fragments of the skin are lost every day. Every time you run your finger on a dusty table, you shed a lot of old skin.
