# Electroplating Process

## Activity 14.7: Electroplating Copper

We will need copper sulphate and two copper plates of size around $10 \mathrm{~cm} \times 4 \mathrm{~cm}$. Take 250 mL of distilled water in a clean and dry beaker. Dissolve two teaspoonfuls of copper sulphate in it. Add a few drops of dilute sulphuric acid to copper sulphate solution to make it more conducting. Clean copper plates with sand paper. Now rinse them with water and dry them. Connect the copper plates to the terminals of a battery and immerse them in copper sulphate solution (Fig. 14.7).

Allow the current to pass for about 15 minutes. Now remove the electrodes from the solution and look at them carefully. Do you find any difference in any one of them? Do you find a coating over it? What colour is the coating? Note down the terminal of the battery with which this electrode is connected.
> **Answer:** You should find a coating on the copper plate connected to the negative terminal of the battery. The coating will be reddish-brown, which is the color of copper.

After doing the electroplating activity, Paheli interchanged the electrodes and repeated the activity. What do you think she would observe this time?
> **Answer:** If Paheli interchanges the electrodes, the copper will now be deposited on the copper plate that is connected to the negative terminal in the *new* setup. The plate that was previously connected to the positive terminal (and lost copper) will now gain copper if it is connected to the negative terminal.
