# Activities on Reflection

This section describes experiments to understand the behavior of light reflection and establish the laws that govern it.

## Activity 16.1

Fix a white sheet of paper on a drawing board or a table. Take a comb and close all its openings except one in the middle using a strip of black paper. Hold the comb perpendicular to the sheet of paper. Throw light from a torch through the opening of the comb from one side (Fig. 16.1). Adjust the torch and comb to see a ray of light along the paper. Keep the comb and torch steady. Place a strip of plane mirror in the path of the light ray (Fig. 16.1). Observe what happens.

Do you see any relation between the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection. Are they approximately equal?

> **Answer:** Yes, if the experiment is carried out carefully, the angle of incidence is always equal to the angle of reflection.

This is one of the **laws of reflection**.

What would happen if I threw the light on the mirror along the normal.

> **Answer:** If light is incident along the normal, the angle of incidence is $0^{\circ}$. According to the law of reflection, the angle of reflection will also be $0^{\circ}$. This means the reflected ray will travel back along the same path as the incident ray, but in the opposite direction.

## Activity 16.2

Perform Activity 16.1 again using a sheet of stiff paper or chart paper. Let the sheet project a little beyond the edge of the table (Fig. 16.4). Cut the projecting portion of the sheet in the middle. Look at the reflected ray, ensuring it extends to the projected portion. Bend the projected portion where the reflected ray falls. Can you still see the reflected ray? Bring the paper back to the original position. Can you see the reflected ray again? What do you infer?

Fig. 16.4 (a), (b) : Incident ray, reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane

When the whole sheet of paper is spread on the table, it represents one plane. The incident ray, the normal at the point of incidence and the reflected ray are all in this plane. When you bend the paper you create a plane different from the plane in which the incident ray and the normal lie. Then you do not see the reflected ray. What does it indicate? It indicates that the incident ray, the normal at the point of incidence and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane. This is another law of reflection.

These two observations constitute the **laws of reflection**:
1.  The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.
2.  The incident ray, the normal at the point of incidence and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.

## Activity 16.3

A source of light $O$ is placed in front of a plane mirror PQ. Two rays $O A$ and OC are incident on it (Fig. 16.5). Can you find out the direction of the reflected rays? Draw normals to the surface of the mirror PQ, at the points A and C. Then draw the reflected rays at the points A and C using the laws of reflection. Call the reflected rays $A B$ and $C D$, respectively. Extend them further. Do they meet? Extend them backwards. Do they meet now? If they meet, mark this point as I. For a viewer's eye at E (Fig. 16.5), do the reflected rays appear to come from the point I.
