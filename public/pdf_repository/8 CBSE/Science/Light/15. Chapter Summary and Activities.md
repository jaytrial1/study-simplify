## WHAT YOU HAVE LEARNT

*   Light is reflected from all surfaces.
*   Regular reflection takes place when light is incident on smooth, polished and regular surfaces.
*   Diffused or irregular reflection takes place from rough surfaces.
*   Two laws of reflection are:
    *   The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.
    *   Incident ray, reflected ray and the normal drawn at the point of incidence to the reflecting surface, lie in the same plane.
*   Image formed in a plane mirror undergoes lateral inversion.
*   Two mirrors inclined to each other give multiple images.
*   Beautiful patterns are formed in a kaleidoscope because of multiple reflections.
*   Sunlight, called white light, consists of seven colours.
*   Splitting of light into its constituent colours is known as dispersion.
*   Parts of the eye are cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina and optic nerve.
*   A normal eye can see nearby and distant objects clearly.
*   Visually impaired persons can read and write using the Braille system.
*   Visually impaired persons develop their other senses more sharply to improve their interaction with their environment.

# Exercises

1.  Suppose you are in a dark room. Can you see objects in the room? Can you see objects outside the room. Explain.
    > **Answer:** You cannot see objects in a dark room because there is no light reflecting off them to enter your eyes. You can see objects outside the room if there is light outside that is reflected by the objects and enters your eyes, or if the objects themselves are luminous.
2.  Differentiate between regular and diffused reflection. Does diffused reflection mean the failure of the laws of reflection?
    > **Answer:** Regular reflection occurs when light reflects from a smooth surface, resulting in parallel incident rays reflecting as parallel rays. Diffused reflection occurs when light reflects from a rough surface, causing parallel incident rays to reflect in different directions. Diffused reflection does not mean the failure of the laws of reflection; the laws of reflection are still valid at each point on the rough surface, but the surface's irregularities cause the reflected rays to scatter.
3.  Mention against each of the following whether regular or diffused reflection will take place when a beam of light strikes. Justify your answer in each case.
    (a) Polished wooden table
    > **Answer:** Regular reflection. A polished surface is relatively smooth.
    (b) Chalk powder
    > **Answer:** Diffused reflection. Chalk powder consists of many small, irregularly shaped particles, creating a rough surface.
    (c) Cardboard surface
    > **Answer:** Diffused reflection. Cardboard has an irregular surface.
    (d) Marble floor with water spread over it
    > **Answer:** Regular reflection. The water surface is smooth and acts like a mirror.
    (e) Mirror
    > **Answer:** Regular reflection. A mirror is a smooth, polished surface specifically designed for regular reflection.
    (f) Piece of paper
    > **Answer:** Diffused reflection. Paper surfaces appear smooth but have microscopic irregularities that cause light to scatter.
4.  State the laws of reflection.
    > **Answer:** The laws of reflection are: (i) The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection. (ii) The incident ray, the normal at the point of incidence, and the reflected ray all lie in the same plane.
5.  Describe an activity to show that the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.
    > **Answer:** Fix a sheet of paper on a table, letting a portion project over the edge. Use a comb and torch to create a ray of light incident on a mirror placed on the paper. Draw the incident ray, reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence on the paper. Bend the projecting portion of the paper where the reflected ray falls. The reflected ray will no longer be visible on the bent part of the paper, indicating it has moved out of the original plane formed by the incident ray and the normal. When the paper is brought back to its original position, the reflected ray becomes visible again, showing that all three lie in the same plane.
6.  Fill in the blanks in the following.
    (a) A person 1 m in front of a plane mirror seems to be $\qquad$ m away from his image.
    > **Answer:** 2
    (b) If you touch your $\qquad$ ear with right hand in front of a plane mirror it will be seen in the mirror that your right ear is touched with $\qquad$ .
    > **Answer:** left, left hand
    (c) The size of the pupil becomes $\qquad$ when you see in dim light.
    > **Answer:** larger
    (d) Night birds have $\qquad$ cones than rods in their eyes.
    > **Answer:** fewer
Choose the correct option in Questions 7 - 8
7.  Angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection.
    (a) Always
    (b) Sometimes
    (c) Under special conditions
    (d) Never
    > **Answer:** (a) Always
8.  Image formed by a plane mirror is
    (a) virtual, behind the mirror and enlarged.
    (b) virtual, behind the mirror and of the same size as the object.
    (c) real at the surface of the mirror and enlarged.
    (d) real, behind the mirror and of the same size as the object.
    > **Answer:** (b) virtual, behind the mirror and of the same size as the object.
9.  Describe the construction of a kaleidoscope.
    > **Answer:** A kaleidoscope is constructed using three rectangular mirror strips of equal size joined together to form a triangular prism. This mirror assembly is placed inside a tube (e.g., cardboard). One end of the tube is closed with a disc having a small central hole for viewing. The other end, touching the mirrors, has a plane glass plate on which small pieces of colored glass or bangles are placed. This end is closed with a ground glass plate, allowing the colored pieces space to move. When viewed through the hole while rotating the tube, multiple reflections of the colored pieces create beautiful, changing patterns.
10. Draw a labelled sketch of the human eye.
    > **Answer:** This question requires a drawing and cannot be answered in text format. A sketch would include labels for the cornea, iris, pupil, lens, retina, optic nerve, and ciliary muscles.
11. Gurmit wanted to perform Activity 16.8 using a laser torch. Her teacher advised her not to do so. Can you explain the basis of the teacher's advise?
    > **Answer:** The teacher advised against using a laser torch because too much light, especially from a powerful source like a laser torch, can injure the retina of the eye.
12. Explain how you can take care of your eyes.
    > **Answer:** To take care of your eyes, you should: have regular eye checkups; use suitable spectacles if advised; avoid too little or too much light when reading; never look directly at the Sun or powerful light sources; never rub your eyes and wash them with clean water if dust enters (see a doctor if no improvement); always read at a comfortable distance (about 25 cm); and ensure your diet includes foods rich in vitamin A.
13. What is the angle of incidence of a ray if the reflected ray is at an angle of $90^{\circ}$ to the incident ray?
    > **Answer:** The laws of reflection state that the angle of incidence ($\angle i$) equals the angle of reflection ($\angle r$), and the incident ray, reflected ray, and normal lie in the same plane. The angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray is the sum of the angle of incidence and the angle of reflection ($\angle i + \angle r$). If this total angle is $90^{\circ}$, then $\angle i + \angle r = 90^{\circ}$. Since $\angle i = \angle r$, we have $2 \times \angle i = 90^{\circ}$. Therefore, the angle of incidence ($\angle i$) is $45^{\circ}$.
14. How many images of a candle will be formed if it is placed between two parallel plane mirrors separated by 40 cm?
    > **Answer:** When an object is placed between two parallel plane mirrors, a very large number of images are formed due to multiple successive reflections. In theory, an infinite number of images are formed.
15. Two mirrors meet at right angles. A ray of light is incident on one at an angle of $30^{\circ}$ as shown in Fig. 16.19. Draw the reflected ray from the second mirror.
    > **Answer:** This question requires drawing. Based on the laws of reflection and the diagram: The incident ray strikes the first mirror at an angle of incidence of $30^{\circ}$. It reflects off the first mirror at an angle of reflection of $30^{\circ}$ to the normal. Since the mirrors are at right angles, the reflected ray from the first mirror will be incident on the second mirror. The angle of incidence on the second mirror can be determined from the geometry; it will be $60^{\circ}$ (the reflected ray from the first mirror makes a $60^{\circ}$ angle with the first mirror's surface, and since the mirrors are at $90^{\circ}$, this ray makes a $30^{\circ}$ angle with the second mirror's surface, meaning a $60^{\circ}$ angle with the second mirror's normal). The ray will then reflect off the second mirror at an angle of reflection of $60^{\circ}$ to the normal of the second mirror.

16. Boojho stands at A just on the side of a plane mirror as shown in Fig. 16.20. Can he see himself in the mirror? Also can he see the image of objects situated at P, Q and R?
    > **Answer:** Boojho at A cannot see himself in the mirror because his line of sight does not reflect back to his eyes from the mirror's surface at his position. He can see the image of object P because rays from P reflect off the mirror and reach his eyes at A. He cannot see the images of objects Q and R because rays from Q and R, after reflecting off the mirror, do not reach his eyes at A; Q and R are outside his field of view reflected by the mirror from position A.

17. (a) Find out the position of the image of an object situated at A in the plane mirror (Fig. 16.21).
    (b) Can Paheli at B see this image?
    (c) Can Boojho at C see this image?
    (d) When Paheli moves from B to C, where does the image of A move?
    > **Answer:**
    > (a) The image of object A is formed behind the plane mirror at the same perpendicular distance from the mirror as A is in front of it, and it will be laterally inverted relative to A.
    > (b) Yes, Paheli at B can see this image because rays from A reflect off the mirror and reach her eyes at B.
    > (c) Yes, Boojho at C can see this image because rays from A reflect off the mirror and reach his eyes at C.
    > (d) When Paheli moves from B to C, the image of A does not move. The position of the image of A is fixed relative to the mirror and the object A itself, regardless of the observer's position.

# Extended Learning - Activities and Project

1.  Make your own mirror. Take a glass strip or glass slab. Clean it and put it on a white sheet of paper. See yourself in the glass. Next put the glass slab on a black sheet of paper. Again look into the glass. In which case do you see yourself better and why?
2.  Make friends with some visually impaired students. Enquire from them how they read and write. Also find out how they are able to recognise objects, hurdles and currency notes.
3.  Meet an eye specialist. Get your eye sight checked and discuss how to take care of your eyes.
4.  Survey your neighbourhood. Find out how many children below the age of 12 years use spectacles. Find out from their parents what, in their view, could be the reason for the weak eyesight of their children.

## Did You Know?

Eyes can be donated by any person as an invaluable gift to visually impaired persons suffering from corneal blindness. The person may be:
*   a male or female.
*   of any age.
*   of any social status.
*   using spectacles.
*   suffering from any normal disease but not AIDS, Hepatitis B or C, rabies, leukemia, lymphoma, tetanus, cholera, encephalitis.

The eyes have to be donated within 4-6 hours after death at any place, home or hospital. A person who wants to donate the eyes may pledge eyes during his/her lifetime to any registered eye bank. He/she should also inform his/her relatives about this pledge so that they can take necessary action after his/her death.

You can also donate a Braille kit.
