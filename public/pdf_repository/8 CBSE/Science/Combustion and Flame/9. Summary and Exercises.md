## WHAT YOU HAVE LEARNT

*   The substances which burn in air are called combustible.
*   Oxygen (in air) is essential for combustion.
*   During the process of combustion, heat and light are given out.
*   Ignition temperature is the lowest temperature at which a combustible substance catches fire.
*   Inflammable substances have very low ignition temperature.
*   Fire can be controlled by removing one or more requirements essential for producing fire.
*   Water is commonly used to control fires.
*   Water cannot be used to control fires involving electrical equipment or oils.
*   There are various types of combustions such as rapid combustion, spontaneous combustion, explosion, etc.
*   There are three different zones of a flame: dark zone, luminous zone and non-luminous zone.
*   An ideal fuel is cheap, readily available, readily combustible and easy to transport. It has high calorific value. It does not produce gases or residues that pollute the environment.
*   Fuels differ in their efficiency and cost.
*   Fuel efficiency is expressed in terms of its calorific value which is expressed in units of kilojoule per kg ($\text{kJ/kg}$).
*   Unburnt carbon particles in air are dangerous pollutants causing respiratory problems.
*   Incomplete combustion of a fuel gives poisonous carbon monoxide gas.
*   Increased percentage of carbon dioxide in air has been linked to global warming.
*   Oxides of sulphur and nitrogen produced by the burning of coal, diesel and petrol cause acid rain which is harmful for crops, buildings and soil.

## Exercises

1.  List conditions under which combustion can take place.
    > **Answer:** The conditions under which combustion can take place are: presence of a combustible substance, presence of a supporter of combustion (like oxygen), and reaching the ignition temperature of the combustible substance.
2.  Fill in the blanks.
    (a) Burning of wood and coal causes $\qquad$ of air.
    (b) A liquid fuel, used in homes is $\qquad$ .
    (c) Fuel must be heated to its $\qquad$ before it starts burning.
    (d) Fire produced by oil cannot be controlled by $\qquad$ .
    > **Answer:**
    > (a) Burning of wood and coal causes **pollution** of air.
    > (b) A liquid fuel, used in homes is **kerosene oil** (or LPG, which is stored as liquid).
    > (c) Fuel must be heated to its **ignition temperature** before it starts burning.
    > (d) Fire produced by oil cannot be controlled by **water** .
3.  Explain how the use of CNG in automobiles has reduced pollution in our cities.
    > **Answer:** The use of CNG in automobiles has reduced pollution in cities because CNG produces harmful products like oxides of sulphur and nitrogen, and unburnt carbon particles in very small amounts compared to petrol and diesel. It is described as a cleaner fuel.
4.  Compare LPG and wood as fuels.
    > **Answer:**
    > **LPG:** Gaseous fuel, higher calorific value (55000 kJ/kg), burns easily, produces heat and light, leaves no residue, burns cleanly producing fewer harmful pollutants (mainly $\text{CO}_2$ and water vapour) compared to wood, stored as liquid under pressure, easy to transport via pipelines/cylinders.
    > **Wood:** Solid fuel, lower calorific value (17000-22000 kJ/kg), burns with smoke, leaves solid residue (ash), produces harmful pollutants like smoke (unburnt carbon particles), carbon monoxide (due to incomplete combustion), and contributes to respiratory problems and deforestation. It is readily available and cheap in rural areas but less efficient and more polluting than LPG.
5.  Give reasons.
    (a) Water is not used to control fires involving electrical equipment.
    > **Answer:** Water is not used to control fires involving electrical equipment because water can conduct electricity. Using water could result in electric shock to the person trying to extinguish the fire and can also damage the electrical equipment.
    (b) LPG is a better domestic fuel than wood.
    > **Answer:** LPG is a better domestic fuel than wood because it has a much higher calorific value, meaning it produces more heat per unit mass. It burns cleanly without producing smoke and harmful residues like ash and unburnt carbon particles that cause respiratory problems and air pollution. It is also easier to control and use.
    (c) Paper by itself catches fire easily whereas a piece of paper wrapped around an aluminium pipe does not.
    > **Answer:** Paper by itself catches fire easily because its ignition temperature is low and the heat from a flame quickly raises its temperature to this point. When paper is wrapped around an aluminium pipe, the heat supplied to the paper is conducted away by the aluminium pipe (aluminium is a good conductor of heat). This prevents the paper from reaching its ignition temperature, so it does not catch fire.
6.  Make a labelled diagram of a candle flame.
    > **Answer:** A labelled diagram of a candle flame should show the different zones. Starting from the wick outwards:
    > *   **Dark Zone (Innermost zone):** Region of unburnt wax vapours. It is the least hot part.
    > *   **Luminous Zone (Middle zone):** Region where incomplete combustion takes place. Unburnt carbon particles glow, making it luminous (yellow). This zone produces soot.
    > *   **Non-luminous Zone (Outermost zone):** Region of complete combustion. It is the hottest part of the flame and is usually blue or invisible.
7.  Name the unit in which the calorific value of a fuel is expressed.
    > **Answer:** The unit in which the calorific value of a fuel is expressed is kilojoule per kg ($\text{kJ/kg}$).
8.  Explain how $\text{CO}_2$ is able to control fires.
    > **Answer:** $\text{CO}_2$ is able to control fires because it is heavier than oxygen and forms a blanket around the fire, cutting off the supply of oxygen to the fuel. Additionally, when released from a cylinder, it expands and cools down, which helps to bring down the temperature of the fuel below its ignition temperature.
9.  It is difficult to burn a heap of green leaves but dry leaves catch fire easily. Explain.
    > **Answer:** It is difficult to burn a heap of green leaves because they contain a lot of moisture (water). The water in the leaves absorbs the heat supplied, preventing the leaf material from reaching its ignition temperature. Dry leaves, on the other hand, have very little moisture. The heat supplied quickly raises their temperature to the ignition point, allowing them to catch fire easily.
10. Which zone of a flame does a goldsmith use for melting gold and silver and why?
    > **Answer:** A goldsmith uses the outermost zone (non-luminous zone) of a flame for melting gold and silver. This is because the outermost zone is the hottest part of the flame due to complete combustion occurring there, providing the high temperature needed to melt metals like gold and silver.
11. In an experiment 4.5 kg of a fuel was completely burnt. The heat produced was measured to be 180.000 kJ . Calculate the calorific value of the fuel.
    > **Answer:**
    > Calorific Value = (Heat produced) / (Mass of fuel burnt)
    > Heat produced = 180.000 kJ
    > Mass of fuel burnt = 4.5 kg
    > Calorific Value = 180.000 kJ / 4.5 kg
    > Calorific Value = 40 kJ/kg
12. Can the process of rusting be called combustion? Discuss.
    > **Answer:** Based on the definition of combustion provided in the chapter (a chemical process in which a substance reacts with oxygen to give off heat), rusting is a reaction with oxygen. However, rusting is a very slow process that does not produce heat and light rapidly like combustion. Therefore, although it is a reaction with oxygen, it is generally not considered combustion according to the definition given in this context.
13. Abida and Ramesh were doing an experiment in which water was to be heated in a beaker. Abida kept the beaker near the wick in the yellow part of the candle flame. Ramesh kept the beaker in the outermost part of the flame. Whose water will get heated in a shorter time?
    > **Answer:** Ramesh's water will get heated in a shorter time. Ramesh kept the beaker in the outermost part of the flame, which is the non-luminous zone. This zone is the hottest part of the flame. Abida kept the beaker in the yellow part, which is the luminous zone, a less hot part where incomplete combustion occurs. Therefore, the water placed in the hottest zone will heat up faster.

## Extended Learning - Activities and Projects

1.  Survey the availability of various fuels in your locality. Find out their cost per kg and prepare a tabular chart showing how many $\text{kJ}$ of various fuels you can get for every rupee.
2.  Find out the number, type and location of fire extinguishers available in your school, nearby shops and factories. Write a brief report about the preparedness of these establishments to fight fire.
3.  Survey 100 houses in your area. Find the percentage of households using LPG, kerosene, wood and cattle dung as fuel.
4.  Talk to people who use LPG at home. Find out what precautions they take in using LPG.
5.  Make a model of a fire extinguisher. Place a short candle and a slightly taller candle in a small dish filled with baking soda. Place the dish at the bottom of a large bowl. Light both the candles. Then pour vinegar into the dish of baking soda. Take care. Do not pour vinegar on the candles. Observe the foaming reaction. What happens to the candles? Why? In what order?
    > **Answer:** When vinegar is poured onto baking soda, a chemical reaction occurs producing carbon dioxide gas. Carbon dioxide is heavier than air and will fill the bowl from the bottom up, displacing the oxygen. The shorter candle, being lower in the bowl, will be extinguished first as the $\text{CO}_2$ level rises and cuts off its oxygen supply. The taller candle will be extinguished shortly after as the $\text{CO}_2$ level reaches its flame. This demonstrates how $\text{CO}_2$ can extinguish fire by cutting off the supply of air (oxygen).
