Here's a detailed description of the image, focusing on the scientific concept it conveys:

The image shows an experiment demonstrating the incomplete combustion of hydrocarbons, specifically paraffin wax from a candle. The setup consists of two glass slides held by clamps. In the first image (on the left), a lit candle is positioned beneath one of the glass slides. The flame's heat causes the paraffin wax to melt and vaporize, and these vapors rise. When the flame directly heats the glass slide, the glass intercepts and rapidly cools the uncombusted carbon particles in the flame (soot).
The second image (on the right) shows the resulting deposition of unburnt carbon particles (soot) on the glass slide. This dark deposit is evidence of incomplete combustion. Because the slide is not heated to the point of promoting combustion, these unburnt carbon particles instead form soot on the cold surface of the glass. This illustrates that incomplete combustion results in the formation of soot due to insufficient oxygen or a temperature too low for complete oxidation to CO2.
