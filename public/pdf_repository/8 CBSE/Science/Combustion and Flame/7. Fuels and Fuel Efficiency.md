# Fuels and Fuel Efficiency

## What is a Fuel?

Recall that the sources of heat energy for domestic and industrial purposes are mainly wood, charcoal, petrol, kerosene etc. These substances are called **fuels**. A good fuel is one which is readily available. It is cheap. It burns easily in air at a moderate rate. It produces a large amount of heat. It does not leave behind any undersirable substances.

There is probably no fuel that could be considered as an **ideal fuel**. We should look for a fuel which fulfils most of the requirements for a particular use.

Fuels differ in their cost. Some fuels are cheaper than others.

## Fuel Efficiency

Suppose you were asked to boil a given quantity of water using cow dung, coal and LPG as fuel. Which fuel would you prefer? Give your reason. You may take the help of your parents. Do these three fuels produce the same amount of heat? The amount of heat energy produced on complete combustion of 1 kg of a fuel is called its **calorific value**. The calorific value of a fuel is expressed in a unit called **kilojoule per kg** ($\text{kJ/kg}$). Calorific values of some fuels are given in Table 6.4.

> **Answer:** I would prefer LPG. This is because LPG has a much higher calorific value (55000 kJ/kg) compared to cow dung cake (6000-8000 kJ/kg) and coal (25000-33000 kJ/kg). This means that burning 1 kg of LPG produces significantly more heat energy than burning 1 kg of cow dung or coal, making it more efficient for boiling water. No, these three fuels do not produce the same amount of heat per kg.
