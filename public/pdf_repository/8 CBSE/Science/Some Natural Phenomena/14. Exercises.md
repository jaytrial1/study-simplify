# Exercises

Select the correct option in Questions 1 and 2.

1. Which of the following cannot be charged easily by friction?
(a) A plastic scale
(b) A copper rod
(c) An inflated balloon
(d) A woollen cloth.

> **Answer:** (b) A copper rod. Metals are good conductors and charge transferred by rubbing tends to dissipate through the hand holding it.

2. When a glass rod is rubbed with a piece of silk cloth the rod
(a) and the cloth both acquire positive charge.
(b) becomes positively charged while the cloth has a negative charge.
(c) and the cloth both acquire negative charge.
(d) becomes negatively charged while the cloth has a positive charge.

> **Answer:** (b) becomes positively charged while the cloth has a negative charge. The text states that it is a convention to call the charge acquired by a glass rod rubbed with silk as positive, implying the silk acquires the opposite charge (negative).

3. Write T against true and F against false in the following statements.
(a) Like charges attract each other (T/F)
(b) A charged glass rod attract a charged plastic straw (T/F)
(c) Lightning conductor cannot protect a building from lightning (T/F)
(d) Earthquakes can be predicted in advance (T/F)

> **Answer:**
> (a) F (Like charges repel each other)
> (b) T (Unlike charges attract, and the text states attraction between a charged glass rod and a charged plastic straw)
> (c) F (Lightning conductors are used to protect buildings)
> (d) F (It is not yet possible to predict earthquakes accurately)

4. Sometimes, a crackling sound is heard while taking off a sweater during winters. Explain.

> **Answer:** This crackling sound is heard due to the electric discharge that occurs when static electric charges built up on the sweater and your body (due to rubbing between the clothes and your body/hair) suddenly jump between the surfaces. You might also see sparks in the dark.

5. Explain why a charged body loses its charge if we touch it with our hand.

> **Answer:** When a charged body is touched by a person, the charge is transferred from the charged body to the earth through the person's body, as the human body is a conductor. This process is called earthing, and it neutralizes the charged body.

6. Name the scale on which the destructive energy of an earthquake is measured. An earthquake measures 3 on this scale. Would it be recorded by a seismograph? Is it likely to cause much damage?

> **Answer:** The destructive energy of an earthquake is measured on the **Richter scale**. An earthquake measuring 3 on the Richter scale would likely be recorded by a seismograph, as seismographs are sensitive instruments designed to detect tremors. However, the text states that really destructive earthquakes have magnitudes higher than 7. An earthquake of magnitude 3 is significantly less powerful (1000 times less destructive energy than magnitude 5, and 1,000,000 times less than magnitude 7) and is unlikely to cause much damage.

7. Suggest three measures to protect ourselves from lightning.

> **Answer:** Three measures to protect yourself from lightning are:
> 1. Rush to a safe place like a house or building when you hear thunder.
> 2. If outdoors and no shelter is available, squat low on the ground away from tall objects and metal.
> 3. Avoid contact with electrical wires, metal pipes, and telephone cords inside a house during a thunderstorm. Unplug electrical appliances.

8. Explain why a charged balloon is repelled by another charged balloon whereas an uncharged balloon is attracted by another charged balloon?

> **Answer:** A charged balloon is repelled by another charged balloon because they both carry the same type of electric charge (either both positive or both negative), and **like charges repel** each other. An uncharged balloon is attracted by a charged balloon due to the phenomenon of electrostatic induction or polarization, where the charged balloon induces opposite charges on the surface of the uncharged balloon closest to it, resulting in an attractive force between the unlike charges.

9. Describe with the help of a diagram an instrument which can be used to detect a charged body.

> **Answer:** The instrument used to detect a charged body is called an **electroscope**.
>
> **Diagram Description:** A simple electroscope consists of a glass jar with a metal rod inserted through a lid (often insulated). Two thin strips of metal foil (like aluminum foil) are attached to the bottom end of the metal rod inside the jar. The top end of the metal rod is often a knob or plate.
>
> **Working:** When an uncharged body is brought near or touches the metal knob, nothing happens. When a charged body touches the metal knob, charge is transferred to the metal rod and the foil strips. Since both foil strips receive the same type of charge, they repel each other and spread apart. The extent to which they spread indicates the presence and roughly the amount of charge.
>

11. Suppose you are outside your home and an earthquake strikes. What precaution would you take to protect yourself?

> **Answer:** If an earthquake strikes while you are outdoors, you should find a clear spot away from buildings, trees, and overhead power lines. Then, drop to the ground and squat low on the ground, placing your hands on your knees with your head between your hands. If you are in a car or bus, stay inside, ask the driver to drive slowly to a clear spot, and remain inside until the tremors stop.

12. The weather department has predicted that a thunderstorm is likely to occur on a certain day. Suppose you have to go out on that day. Would you carry an umbrella? Explain.

> **Answer:** No, it is not a good idea to carry an umbrella during a thunderstorm. An umbrella has a metal rod, which can act as a conductor and increase the risk of being struck by lightning, as lightning seeks a path of least resistance to the ground. It is safer to seek shelter in a building or vehicle.
