# Physical Properties of Metals and Non-metals

Have you ever seen a blacksmith beating an iron piece or an article made up of iron, like a spade, a shovel, an axe? Do you find a change in the shape of these articles on beating? Would you expect a similar change if we try to beat a piece of coal?

Let us find out.

You saw that the shape of the iron nail and the aluminium wire changed on beating. If they were beaten harder these could be changed into sheets. You might be familiar with silver foil used for decorating sweets. You must also be familiar with the aluminium foil used for wrapping food. The property of metals by which they can be beaten into thin sheets is called **malleability**. This is a characteristic property of metals. As you must have noticed, materials like coal and pencil lead do not show this property.

Would you expect a similar change if we try to beat a piece of coal?
> **Answer:** No, you would not expect a similar change. As observed in Activity 4.1, coal breaks into pieces when beaten, unlike iron or aluminium which flatten.

Can we call these metals?
> **Answer:** No, we cannot call materials like coal and pencil lead metals based on the property of malleability, as they break instead of flattening into sheets.
