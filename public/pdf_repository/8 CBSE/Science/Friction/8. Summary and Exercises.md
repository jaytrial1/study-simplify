## A riddle for you

Put some lubricant, and

> **Answer:** Friction

## WHAT YOU HAVE LEARNT

*   Friction opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It acts on both the surfaces.
*   Friction depends on the nature of surfaces in contact.
*   For a given pair of surfaces friction depends upon the state of smoothness of those surfaces.
*   Friction depends on how hard the two surfaces press together.
*   Static friction comes into play when we try to move an object at rest.
*   Sliding friction comes into play when an object is sliding over another.
*   Sliding friction is smaller than static friction.
*   Friction is important for many of our activities.
*   Friction can be increased by making a surface rough.
*   The sole of the shoes and the tyres of the vehicle are treaded to increase friction.
*   Friction is sometimes undesirable.
*   Friction can be reduced by using lubricants.
*   When one body rolls over another body, rolling friction comes into play. Rolling friction is smaller than sliding friction.
*   In many machines, friction is reduced by using ball bearings.
*   Fluid friction can be minimised by giving suitable shapes to bodies moving in fluids.

2. Four children were asked to arrange forces due to rolling, static and sliding frictions in a decreasing order. Their arrangements are given below. Choose the correct arrangement.
(a) rolling, static, sliding
(b) rolling, sliding, static
(c) static, sliding, rolling
(d) sliding, static, rolling
> **Answer:** (c) static, sliding, rolling

3. Alida runs her toy car on dry marble floor, wet marble floor, newspaper and towel spread on the floor. The force of friction acting on the car on different surfaces in increasing order will be
(a) wet marble floor, dry marble floor, newspaper and towel.
(b) newspaper, towel, dry marble floor, wet marble floor.
(c) towel, newspaper, dry marble floor, wet marble floor.
(d) wet marble floor, dry marble floor, towel, newspaper
> **Answer:** (a) wet marble floor, dry marble floor, newspaper and towel. (Note: Wet marble floor has very low friction, dry marble is smoother than newspaper or towel. A towel is likely the roughest surface listed).

4. Suppose your writing desk is tilted a little. A book kept on it starts sliding down. Show the direction of frictional force acting on it.
> **Answer:** The frictional force acts *up* the slope of the tilted desk, opposing the motion of the book sliding down.

5. You spill a bucket of soapy water on a marble floor accidently. Would it make it easier or more difficult for you to walk on the floor? Why?
> **Answer:** It would make it more difficult for you to walk on the floor. Soapy water acts as a lubricant, significantly reducing the friction between your shoes and the marble floor. Walking requires friction to provide grip and allow you to push off the surface. With reduced friction, your feet are more likely to slip.

6. Explain why sportsmen use shoes with spikes.
> **Answer:** Sportsmen use shoes with spikes to increase friction between their shoes and the ground. The spikes dig into the surface (like soil or grass), providing a much stronger grip. This increased friction allows them to start, stop, and change direction quickly and effectively without slipping, which is crucial in many sports.

7. Iqbal has to push a lighter box and Seema has to push a similar heavier box on the same floor. Who will have to apply a larger force and why?
> **Answer:** Seema will have to apply a larger force. Friction increases when the two surfaces are pressed harder together. The heavier box presses down on the floor with greater force than the lighter box. Therefore, the force of friction opposing Seema's push will be greater, requiring her to apply a larger force to move the box.

8. Explain why sliding friction is less than static friction.
> **Answer:** Sliding friction is less than static friction because when an object is already sliding, the contact points on its surface do not get enough time to fully lock into the irregularities on the surface it is sliding over. When the object is at rest (static), the irregularities have time to settle and interlock more completely, requiring a greater force to break this interlocking and start the motion.

9. Give examples to show that friction is both a friend and a foe.
> **Answer:**
> **Friend:**
> *   We can walk because of friction between our shoes and the ground.
> *   Vehicles can move, stop, and turn because of friction between tires and the road.
> *   We can write with a pen or pencil due to friction.
> *   Nails can be fixed in walls and knots can be tied due to friction.
>
> **Foe:**
> *   Friction causes wear and tear on surfaces, like shoe soles or machine parts.
> *   Friction produces heat in machines, leading to wasted energy.
> *   It makes moving heavy objects difficult.

10. Explain why objects moving in fluids must have special shapes.
> **Answer:** Objects moving in fluids (like air or water) experience fluid friction, also called drag. This friction opposes their motion and causes a loss of energy. To minimize this fluid friction and save energy, objects are given special shapes, often streamlined like birds or fish, which allow the fluid to flow around them more easily. Aeroplanes and ships are designed with such shapes.
