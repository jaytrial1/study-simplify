# Changes at Puberty

## Increase in Height

The most conspicuous change during puberty is the sudden increase in height. At this time the long bones, that is, the bones of the arms and the legs elongate and make a person tall.

> **Answer:** To calculate the probable full height, use the formula:
> Probable Full Height (cm) = (Present height (cm) / % of full height at this age) * 100.
> For example, if a boy is 9 years old and 120 cm tall, his probable full height is (120 / 75) * 100 cm = 160 cm. To find the tallest/shortest among friends, apply this calculation to each friend using their current age and height, then compare the calculated probable full heights.

## Calculation for full height (cm)

Present height (cm)
\% of full height at this age
(as given in the chart)
Example:
A boy is 9 years old and 120 cm tall. At the end of the growth period he is likely to be
$\frac{120}{75} \times 100 \mathrm{~cm}=160 \mathrm{~cm}$ tall

***Data Visualization Description**

The image is a line graph that displays the percentage of full height achieved by boys and girls as they age. The horizontal axis (X) represents age in years, ranging from 8 to 18. The vertical axis (Y) represents the percentage of full height, ranging from 65% to 100%. Two distinct lines are plotted: one representing boys, marked with squares, and another representing girls, marked with triangles.

**Key Insights and Patterns**

1.  **Growth Trends:** Both boys and girls experience a steady increase in the percentage of their full height as they age. This indicates continuous growth throughout adolescence.

2.  **Gender-Based Differences:** The growth patterns differ between boys and girls. The line representing girls' growth typically stays above the boys' line until age 14. Girls achieve a higher percentage of their adult height earlier than boys.

3.  **Growth Spurt:** The graph shows a noticeable steepening of the girls' growth curve between approximately 10 and 13 years, suggesting a growth spurt during this period. The boys' growth curve also shows a steepening, but it occurs slightly later.

4.  **Plateau:** Around the ages of 16, both lines show a flattening trend, indicating that growth slows down significantly. By 17 or 18, both genders are approaching 100% of their full height, indicating the end of the primary growth phase.

**Information Conveyed**

*   The rate of physical growth varies between boys and girls during adolescence.
*   Girls experience a growth spurt earlier than boys.
*   Both genders eventually attain their full height by the late teenage years.
*   The graph provides a visual representation of the typical growth patterns during adolescence, differentiating between boys and girls.*
