# Exercises

1.  List the different types of religious practice that you find in your neighbourhood. This could be different forms of prayer, worship of different gods, sacred sites, different kinds of religious music and singing etc. Does this indicate freedom of religious practice?

> **Answer:** This is an activity for the reader to observe their neighbourhood. Observing different forms of religious practice usually indicates freedom of religious practice, as people are able to follow their beliefs openly.

2.  Will the government intervene if some religious group says that their religion allows them to practise infanticide? Give reasons for your answer.

> **Answer:** Yes, the government will intervene. The Indian State can intervene in religious practices if they violate Fundamental Rights guaranteed by the Constitution. Practising infanticide violates the fundamental right to life, and the state is obligated to protect the rights of all citizens, regardless of religious claims.

4.  Look up the annual calendar of holidays of your school. How many of them pertain to different religions? What does this indicate?

> **Answer:** This is an activity for the reader to examine their school calendar. The presence of holidays for different religions indicates that the school and possibly the state acknowledge and respect the diversity of religions, reflecting the principle of treating all religions equally or with non-preference.

5.  Find out some examples of different views within the same religion.

> **Answer:** The chapter provides the example of untouchability within Hinduism, where differing views exist between those who support it and those who oppose it and seek reform. Other examples can often be found in interpretations of religious texts or practices within any major religion.

6.  The Indian State both keeps away from religion as well as intervenes in religion. This idea can be quite confusing. Discuss this once again in class using examples from the chapter as well as those that you might have come up with.

> **Answer:** This is a discussion prompt. Key points for discussion based on the chapter include:
> *   **Keeping Away (Distancing/Non-interference):** The state doesn't run on religious lines, doesn't promote one religion in government spaces (like schools), and makes exceptions for certain religious practices (like Sikhs wearing turbans instead of helmets).
> *   **Intervention:** The state can intervene in religious practices to abolish social evils (like untouchability) that violate fundamental rights or to ensure equal rights (like inheritance laws). Intervention is guided by constitutional ideals.

7.  This poster alongside highlights the need for 'Peace'. It says, "Peace is a never-ending process....It cannot ignore our differences or overlook our common interests." Write in your own words what you think the above sentences are trying to convey? How does it relate to the need for religious tolerance?

> **Answer:** The sentences suggest that achieving peace isn't a one-time event but requires continuous effort. It means accepting that people are different (have differences) but also finding things they share or care about together (common interests). This relates to religious tolerance because tolerance means accepting and respecting religious differences. To have peace in a diverse society, people must tolerate different religions and find common ground to live together, rather than letting differences lead to conflict.
