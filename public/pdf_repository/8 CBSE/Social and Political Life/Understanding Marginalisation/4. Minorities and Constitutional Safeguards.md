## Minorities and Marginalisation

The Constitution provides safeguards to religious and linguistic minorities as part of Fundamental Rights. The term **minority** usually refers to communities that are numerically small compared to the majority population. However, it also involves issues of power, access to resources, and social and cultural dimensions.

The Indian Constitution recognises that the majority's culture can influence society and government. In this situation, being small in number can be a disadvantage and lead to marginalisation. Therefore, safeguards are needed to:
*   Protect minority communities from cultural domination by the majority.
*   Protect them against discrimination and disadvantage.

Communities small in number may feel insecure about their lives, assets, and well-being, especially if relations with the majority are tense. The Constitution provides these safeguards to protect India's cultural diversity and promote equality and justice. The judiciary plays a key role in upholding these laws and enforcing Fundamental Rights; any citizen can approach the courts if their Fundamental Rights are violated.

Why do we need safeguards for minorities?
> **Answer:** Safeguards are needed for minorities to protect them from the possibility of being culturally dominated by the majority, to prevent discrimination and disadvantage they may face due to their smaller numbers, and to address their potential sense of insecurity.
