# Understanding Marginalisation

## What Does it Mean to be Socially Marginalised?

To be marginalised means being pushed to the sides or fringes, away from the centre. This can happen in small settings like a classroom or playground. If you are different from the majority in taste, accent, chattiness, sport preference, or dress, you might feel excluded or that you don't fit in.

Similarly, in society, groups or communities can experience exclusion. Their marginalisation can be due to:
*   Speaking a different language
*   Following different customs
*   Belonging to a different religious group
*   Being poor
*   Having a 'low' social status
*   Being viewed as less human or with hostility and fear

This difference and exclusion lead to marginalised communities having limited access to resources and opportunities and difficulty asserting their rights. They feel disadvantaged and powerless compared to dominant groups who are wealthy, educated, and politically strong. Marginalisation is often a result of economic, social, cultural, and political factors working together.

In this chapter, we will look at two communities considered socially marginalised in India: Adivasis and Muslims.

Explain at least three different reasons why groups may be marginalised.
> **Answer:** Groups may be marginalised because they speak a different language, follow different customs or religions, are poor, have a low social status, or are viewed with hostility and fear.

Why was Dadu forced to leave his village in Odisha?
> **Answer:** Dadu was forced to leave his village because powerful people, with the support of authorities, beat and threatened his community until they sold their land.
