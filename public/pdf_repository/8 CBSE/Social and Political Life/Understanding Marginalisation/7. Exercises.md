# Exercises

1.  Write in your own words two or more sentences of what you understand by the word 'marginalisation'.
    > **Answer:** Marginalisation means being pushed to the edge or side of society, away from the main group. It involves experiencing disadvantage, prejudice, and powerlessness, leading to limited access to resources and opportunities.
2.  List two reasons why Adivasis are becoming increasingly marginalised.
    > **Answer:** Two reasons Adivasis are becoming increasingly marginalised are:
    > *   Loss of their land and access to forests due to mining, industrial projects, dams, and forest policies.
    > *   Migration to cities for low-wage work, leading to poverty and deprivation, and loss of their traditional way of life.
3.  Write one reason why you think the Constitution's safeguards to protect minority communities are very important?
    > **Answer:** The Constitution's safeguards are important to protect minority communities from being culturally dominated by the majority and to ensure they do not face discrimination or disadvantage due to being smaller in number.
4.  Re-read the section on Minorities and Marginalisation. What do you understand by the term minority?
    > **Answer:** The term minority most commonly refers to communities that are numerically small compared to the rest of the population. However, it also includes aspects of their power, access to resources, and their social and cultural position in society.

6.  Imagine that you are watching the Republic Day parade on TV with a friend and she remarks, "Look at these tribals. They look so exotic. And they seem to be dancing all the time". List three things that you would tell her about the lives of Adivasis in India.

8.  Would you agree with the statement that economic marginalisation and social marginalisation are interlinked? Why?
    > **Answer:** Yes, economic marginalisation and social marginalisation are interlinked. The chapter shows that groups who are socially marginalised (like Adivasis or Muslims) often face economic disadvantage (e.g., loss of land, low wages, poverty).
