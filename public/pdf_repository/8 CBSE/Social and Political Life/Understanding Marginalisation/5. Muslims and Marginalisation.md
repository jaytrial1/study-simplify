This section examines the situation of the Muslim community in India, presenting data on their socio-economic status and discussing factors contributing to their marginalisation, including stereotypes and discrimination.

## Muslims and Marginalisation

According to the 2011 Census, Muslims make up 14.2% of India's population. They are considered a marginalised community partly because they have comparatively lower socio-economic development status. Data from various sources illustrate this situation regarding basic amenities, literacy, and public employment.

**I. Access to Basic Amenities, 2008-2009 (percentage)**

| Religious Community | Pucca House | Electricity | Tap Water |
| :-- | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Hindu | 65.4 | 75.2 | 43.7 |
| Muslim | 63.8 | 67.5 | 35.8 |
| Christian | 69.3 | 86.2 | 48.0 |
| Sikh | 91.3 | 96.0 | 49.3 |

Which of these communities have the most and the least access to basic amenities?
> **Answer:** Sikhs have the most access to pucca houses, electricity, and tap water among these communities. Muslims generally have the least access across these basic amenities, although Hindus have slightly less access to pucca houses than Muslims.

**II. Literacy Rate by Religion, 2011 (percentages)**

| All  | Hindus | Muslims | Christians | Sikhs | Buddhists | Jains |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| 74   | 63   | 57    | 74       | 67    | 71      | 86    |

Which of these communities have the highest and the lowest literacy rate?
> **Answer:** Jains have the highest literacy rate (86%), and Muslims have the lowest literacy rate (57%) among these communities.

**III. Public Employment of Muslims (percentages)**

| Population | IAS | IPS | IFS | Central Public <br> Sector Unit (PSU) | State PSU | Banks & RBI |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| 13.5 | 3 | 4 | 1.8 | 3.3 | 10.8 | 2.2 |

What do these figures convey?
> **Answer:** These figures convey that the representation of Muslims in various sectors of public employment (IAS, IPS, IFS, Central PSU, Banks & RBI) is significantly lower than their share in the total population (13.5%), indicating underrepresentation.

Recognising that Muslims were lagging in development, the government set up the **Sachar Committee** in 2005, chaired by Justice Rajindar Sachar. The committee studied the social, economic, and educational status of the Muslim community. Its report detailed their marginalisation, finding that their situation on various indicators was comparable to that of other marginalised groups like Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes. For instance, the report showed that the average years of schooling for Muslim children aged 7-16 was lower than other communities.

Economic and social marginalisation of Muslims has other aspects. Like other minorities, their customs and practices can be distinct from the mainstream. Certain visible markers, like wearing a burqa, sporting a long beard, or wearing a fez, can lead to identifying all Muslims differently and the perception that they are not like the 'rest of us'. This distinction is sometimes used as an excuse for different treatment and discrimination.

Read the data related to schooling provided by the Sachar Committee Report:
- 25 per cent of Muslim children in the 6-14 year age group have either never been enrolled in school or have dropped out. This percentage is much higher than that of any other socio-religious community (page 58).

Do you think special measures are required to address this situation?
> **Answer:** Yes, based on the data showing a high percentage of Muslim children out of school compared to other communities, special measures are likely required to improve enrollment and retention in schools for this community.

The Sachar Committee Report also corrected common myths. It's often believed Muslims prefer Madarsas for schooling, but the report showed only 4% of Muslim children attend Madarsas, while 66% attend government schools and 30% attend private schools.

Social marginalisation can lead to Muslims migrating from their homes, resulting in **ghettoisation** – communities grouping together in specific areas, partly for security, but potentially leading to alienation. Prejudice has also resulted in hatred and violence.

The experiences of Muslims, Adivasis, and women (as discussed in Class VII) show that marginalisation is complex. It requires various strategies, measures, and safeguards to address. Protecting the rights defined in the Constitution and the laws and policies to achieve these rights is crucial for everyone. This is necessary to protect India's diversity and uphold the state's commitment to equality.
