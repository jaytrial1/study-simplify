# Laws and Policies for the Marginalised

The government makes laws to protect its citizens, but it also takes action through specific laws and policies for the marginalised. Policies and schemes can emerge from committees or surveys. The government promotes these policies to provide opportunities to specific groups.

## Promoting Social Justice

To implement the Constitution, state and central governments create specific schemes for tribal areas or areas with high Dalit populations. For example, the government provides free or subsidised hostels for Dalit and Adivasi students to access education facilities not available locally.

The government also uses laws to end inequity. One significant and often debated policy is the **reservation policy**. Laws reserving seats in education and government employment for Dalits and Adivasis are based on the argument that in a society where sections of the population have been denied opportunities for centuries, a democratic government must step in to assist them.

### How the Reservation Policy Works

Governments in India have lists of Scheduled Castes (Dalits), Scheduled Tribes, and backward/most backward castes. Candidates applying to educational institutions or government jobs furnish proof of their caste or tribe status using certificates. If their caste or tribe is on the government list, they can avail of reservation benefits.

For college admissions, especially professional education like medical colleges, governments define **'cut-off' marks**. Only Dalit and tribal candidates who score above this cut-off qualify for admission. Governments also offer special scholarships to these students.

State one reason why you think reservations play an important role in providing social justice to Dalits and Adivasis.
> **Answer:** Reservations play an important role by providing access to education and employment opportunities that have historically been denied to Dalits and Adivasis due to discrimination and social exclusion. This helps level the playing field and promotes their upliftment and representation in society.
