# The Scheduled Castes and the Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989

Rathnam sought support from the law, filing his complaint under this Act to protest the domination and violence of the powerful castes.

This Act was framed in 1989 in response to demands by Dalits and others for the government to take seriously the ill-treatment and humiliation they faced daily. This treatment, long-standing, became more violent in the late 1970s and 1980s. During this time, assertive Dalit groups in southern India emerged, asserting their rights, refusing caste duties, and insisting on equal treatment. Like Rathnam, they rejected practices based on humiliation and exploitation. This led to violence from more powerful castes. Dalit groups demanded new laws listing various forms of violence against them and prescribing stringent punishment to indicate that untouchability was still practised in terrible ways.

Throughout the 1970s and 1980s, Adivasi people also organised successfully, demanding equal rights and the return of their land and resources. They too faced anger and violence from powerful social groups.

This Act contains a long list of crimes, some horrific. It not only describes terrible crimes but also reveals the dreadful deeds human beings are capable of. Such laws aim to both punish offenders and influence how we think and act.

The Act distinguishes several levels of crimes:

1.  **Modes of humiliation:** It lists physically horrific and morally reprehensible acts, punishing those who:
    *   Force a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe to drink or eat any inedible or obnoxious substance.
    *   Forcibly remove clothes from a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe, parade them naked or with painted face/body, or commit similar acts derogatory to human dignity.
2.  **Dispossession and forced labour:** It lists actions that dispossess Dalits and Adivasis of resources or force them into slave labour, punishing anyone who:
    *   Wrongfully occupies or cultivates land owned by or allotted to a member of a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe, or gets their allotted land transferred.
3.  **Crimes against women:** The Act recognises that crimes against Dalit and tribal women are specific and penalises anyone who:
    *   Assaults or uses force on any woman belonging to a Scheduled Caste or Scheduled Tribe with intent to dishonour her.
