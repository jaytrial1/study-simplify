# Introduction

In the previous chapter, we read about two different groups and their experiences of inequality and discrimination. Though powerless, such groups have fought, protested and struggled against being excluded or dominated by others. They have attempted to overcome their situation by adopting a range of strategies in their long history, including religious solace, armed struggle, self improvement, education, and economic uplift. The choice of struggle has depended on the specific circumstances faced by the marginalised groups.

In this chapter, we will read about some of the ways in which groups and individuals challenge existing inequalities. Adivasis, Dalits, Muslims, women, and other marginal groups argue that as citizens of a democratic country, they possess equal rights that must be respected. Many look to the Constitution to address their concerns. This chapter will explore why the Constitution of India is invoked by marginalised groups in their struggles, how rights are translated into laws to protect against exploitation, and the government's efforts to formulate policies promoting their access to development.
