# Water Supply to Chennai: Is it Available to All?

While public facilities should be available to all, in reality, there are often great shortages and inequalities. In Chennai, water supply is marked by shortages. Municipal supply meets only about half the city's needs on average. Some areas get water more regularly, particularly those close to storage points, while colonies further away receive less.

The burden of water shortages falls mostly on the poor. The middle class can cope through private means like digging borewells, buying water from tankers, and using bottled water.

Access to *safe* drinking water also depends on affordability. The wealthy have more choices with bottled water and purifiers. The poor are often left out. In reality, it seems access to water is often linked to having money, which is far from the goal of *universal access* to *sufficient and safe* water.

In rural areas, water is needed for both human use and cattle. Sources include wells, handpumps, ponds, and overhead tanks, many of which are privately owned. Compared to urban areas, there is an even greater shortage of public water supply in rural areas.

## Taking Water from Farmers

The water shortage has created opportunities for private companies. Many supply water to cities by buying it from surrounding areas. In Chennai, water is taken from nearby towns and villages using a large fleet of tankers. Water dealers pay farmers in advance for the right to exploit water sources on their land. This takes water away from agriculture and the drinking water supplies of villagers. As a result, groundwater levels have dropped drastically in these areas.
