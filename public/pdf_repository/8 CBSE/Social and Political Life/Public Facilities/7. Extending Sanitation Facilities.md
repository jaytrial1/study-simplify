Besides safe drinking water, sanitation is crucial for preventing water-borne diseases. However, sanitation coverage in India is even lower than water coverage. Official figures for 2011 showed 87% of households had access to drinking water, but only about 53% had access to sanitation (toilet facilities within their homes). Again, it is the poor in both rural and urban areas who lack access to sanitation.

Sulabh, a non-government organisation, has worked for nearly five decades to address sanitation problems for low-caste, low-income people in India. It has built over 8,500 community toilet blocks and 1.5 million household toilets, providing sanitation access to 20 million people. Most users of Sulabh facilities are from the poor working class.

Sulabh contracts with municipalities or local authorities to build toilet blocks using government funds. Local authorities provide land and funds for setup, while maintenance costs are sometimes covered by user charges (e.g., ₹2 for using city latrines).

Do you think that lack of access to proper sanitation facilities affects peoples' lives? How?
> **Answer:** Yes, lack of access to proper sanitation facilities significantly affects people's lives. It can lead to serious health problems and the spread of diseases like cholera, typhoid, and diarrhoea due to contaminated water sources and environment. It also impacts dignity, privacy, and safety, especially for women and children, and restricts daily routines and activities.

Why do you think that this would impact women and girls more acutely?
> **Answer:** Lack of sanitation impacts women and girls more acutely because they often have to wait for darkness to relieve themselves, making them vulnerable to harassment and danger. It also affects their privacy and dignity more severely. For girls, the lack of toilet facilities in schools is a major reason for dropping out. Managing menstruation without proper facilities is also a significant challenge.
