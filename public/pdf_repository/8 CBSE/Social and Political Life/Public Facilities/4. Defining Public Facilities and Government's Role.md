# Public Facilities and the Government's Role

Like water, other essential facilities need to be provided for everyone. These include healthcare, sanitation, electricity, public transport, schools, and colleges. These are known as *public facilities*.

The government needs to play an active role in providing adequate access to proper health facilities for the entire population, including eradicating preventable diseases like polio.

The important characteristic of a public facility is that once provided, its benefits can be shared by many people. A village school educates many children. Electricity supply benefits farmers running pumpsets, people opening workshops, students studying, and most villagers in various ways.

## The Government's Role

Given the importance of public facilities, someone must be responsible for providing them. This 'someone' is the government. One of the government's most crucial functions is ensuring these facilities are available to everyone.

We've seen that private companies operate for profit. Most public facilities offer no profit. For example, cleaning drains or running an anti-malaria campaign doesn't generate profit for a private company, so they likely won't undertake such work.

However, private companies may be interested in facilities like schools and hospitals, especially in large cities. Private companies also supply water through tankers or in bottles. When private companies provide public facilities, they do so at a price only some people can afford. This means the facility isn't available to all at an affordable rate. If access depended solely on ability to pay, many people would be deprived of a decent life.

This is undesirable. Public facilities relate to people's *basic needs*. A modern society requires these facilities to be provided so that everyone's basic needs are met. The *Right to Life* guaranteed by the Constitution is for all persons in the country. Therefore, the responsibility to provide public facilities must lie with the government.

Where does the government get money for public facilities?
Every year, the government budget is presented in Parliament. This details past expenses and planned spending. The budget also outlines how the government plans to meet these expenses. The main source of government revenue is taxes collected from the people. The government is empowered to collect these taxes and use them for public programmes. For example, supplying water involves costs for pumping, transport, distribution pipes, purification, and waste water treatment. The government covers these expenses partly from taxes and partly by charging a price for water. This price is set to allow most people to afford a minimum amount for daily use.

1. What are public facilities? Why should the government be responsible for providing public facilities?
> **Answer:** Public facilities are essential services like water, healthcare, sanitation, electricity, public transport, schools, and colleges that are necessary for people's basic needs and decent living. The government should be responsible for providing them because they are non-profitable or low-profit services that private companies are often not interested in providing universally and affordably. Ensuring access to these facilities for *all* citizens is considered part of the fundamental Right to Life guaranteed by the Constitution.

2. The government can get private companies to deliver some of the public facilities. For instance, contracts for building roads are given to private contractors. Distribution of electricity in Delhi is done by two private companies. However, the government must keep a close watch on these and ensure that they fulfil their commitment to reach these facilities to all people and at affordable prices.

Why do you think the government must assume the overall responsibility for public facilities even when it gets private companies to do part of the job?
> **Answer:** The government must retain overall responsibility even when using private companies because its primary goal is public welfare and ensuring universal, equitable access at affordable prices, not profit. Private companies are driven by profit, which can lead to services being unavailable or too expensive for the poor. The government's oversight ensures that the profit motive does not override the fundamental right of all citizens to access essential services.
