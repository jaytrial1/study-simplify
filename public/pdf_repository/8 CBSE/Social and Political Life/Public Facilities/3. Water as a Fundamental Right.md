This section explains why access to water is considered a basic need and a fundamental right under the Indian Constitution.

# Water as Part of the Fundamental Right to Life

Water is essential for life and good health. It is necessary for daily needs, and safe drinking water can prevent many water-related diseases. India has a large number of cases of diseases like diarrhoea, dysentery, and cholera, with many deaths, especially among young children, reportedly due to water-related illnesses. These deaths could be prevented with access to safe drinking water.

The Constitution of India recognises the *right to water* as being a part of the *Right to Life* under Article 21. This means that every person, regardless of wealth, has the right to sufficient water for daily needs at an affordable price. In essence, there should be *universal access* to water.

Several court cases, in both High Courts and the Supreme Court, have affirmed that the right to safe drinking water is a *Fundamental Right*. For example, in 2007, the Andhra Pradesh High Court reinforced this while hearing a case about contaminated drinking water in Mahbubnagar district. A textile company was discharging poisonous chemicals into a stream, contaminating groundwater used for irrigation and drinking. The judges directed the district collector to supply 25 litres of water to each person in the village.

"... right to water entitles everyone to sufficient, safe, acceptable, physically accessible and affordable water for personal and domestic use"
United Nations (2002)
