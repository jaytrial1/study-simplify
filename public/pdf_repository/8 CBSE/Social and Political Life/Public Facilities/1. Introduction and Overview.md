# Introduction and Overview

This unit discusses the role of the government in providing public facilities and implementing laws related to markets, factories, and working conditions. The aim is to help students understand how the government's role is connected to concerns addressed in our Fundamental Rights. This link to rights provides a connection to similar issues raised in earlier chapters. Discussions from previous textbooks on livelihoods and markets can also provide context for the topics covered.

Chapter 7 uses water as the primary example to discuss public facilities. It is important to understand clearly what public facilities are and why the government needs to play a crucial role in their provision and take overall responsibility. The idea of *equity*, or the equal availability, affordability, and quality of water for all, is a key issue related to public facilities highlighted in this chapter. It is crucial to separate the discussion on the importance of the government's role in providing public facilities from their current unequal provision. This means that unequal access to water does not negate the government's responsibility to provide it.

Chapter 8 discusses the central role of the government in regulating economic activities, largely through laws. The chapter focuses on the importance of implementing existing laws and making new ones to protect the rights of workers, consumers, and producers in the market. The Bhopal gas tragedy is used as an example of lax law enforcement. The Bhopal gas tragedy also highlighted how environmental issues are linked to economic laws. A key idea underlying this chapter is the accountability of manufacturers and the government to workers and citizens.
