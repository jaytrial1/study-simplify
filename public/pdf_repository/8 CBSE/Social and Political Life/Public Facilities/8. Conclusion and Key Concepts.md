This section summarizes the importance of public facilities, the government's role, and the challenges of unequal access, emphasizing the right of all citizens.

# Conclusion

Public facilities relate to our basic needs, and the Indian Constitution recognizes rights to water, health, education, etc., as part of the Right to Life. Therefore, a major role of the government is to ensure adequate public facilities for everyone.

However, progress has been far from satisfactory. There are shortages in supply and inequalities in distribution. Towns and villages are often less provided for than metros and large cities. Poorer localities are under-serviced compared to wealthy ones. Handing these facilities to private companies may not be the solution. Any solution must consider the important fact that every citizen has a right to these facilities, which should be provided equitably.

### Key Concepts

*   **Sanitation:** Provision of facilities for the safe disposal of human urine and faeces. This involves building toilets, sewage pipes, and waste water treatment to avoid contamination.
*   **Company:** A business setup by people or the government. Private companies are owned by individuals or groups (e.g., Tata Steel). Government-run companies are public sector undertakings (e.g., Indian Oil).
*   **Universal access:** Achieved when everyone can physically access a good or service and can also afford it. For example, a tap connection at home provides physical access to water, and low or free pricing makes it affordable for all.
*   **Basic needs:** Primary requirements like food, water, shelter, sanitation, healthcare, and education necessary for survival and a decent life.
