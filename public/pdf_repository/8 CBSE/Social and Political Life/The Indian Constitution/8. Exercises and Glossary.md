# Exercises and Glossary
This section contains exercises for students to test their understanding and a glossary of key terms used in the chapter.

## Exercises

1.  Why does a democratic country need a Constitution?
    > **Answer:** A democratic country needs a Constitution because it lays out the ideals the citizens aspire to live by, defines the nature of the political system, provides safeguards against the misuse of power by elected representatives, and ensures that a dominant group does not use its power against minorities.

2.  Look at the wordings of the two documents given below. The first column is from the 1990 Nepal Constitution. The second column is from the more recent Constitution of Nepal.

    | 1990 Constitution of Nepal <br> Part 7: Executive | 2015 Constitution of Nepal <br> Part 7: Federal Executive |
    | :-- | :-- |
    | Article 35: Executive Power: The executive <br> power of the Kingdom of Nepal shall be <br> vested in His Majesty and the Council of <br> Ministers. | Article 75: Executive Power: The executive power <br> of Nepal shall, pursuant to this Constitution and law, <br> be vested in the Council of Ministers. |

    What is the difference in who exercises 'Executive Power' in the above two Constitutions of Nepal?
    > **Answer:** In the 1990 Constitution of Nepal, the executive power was vested in both His Majesty (the King) and the Council of Ministers. In the 2015 Constitution of Nepal, the executive power is vested solely in the Council of Ministers, reflecting the change from a monarchy to a republic where the King no longer holds executive authority.

3.  What would happen if there were no restrictions on the power of elected representatives?
    > **Answer:** If there were no restrictions on the power of elected representatives, they could misuse their authority, potentially leading to gross injustice, favoring certain groups, or acting arbitrarily without accountability, which would harm the interests of the citizens and undermine democracy.

4.  In each of the following situations, identify the minority. Write one reason why you think it is important to respect the views of the minority in each of these situations.
    (a) In a school with 30 teachers, 20 of them are male.
    > **Answer:** The minority is the female teachers (10). It is important to respect their views because they represent a significant portion of the teaching staff, may have different perspectives or needs (e.g., related to gender-specific issues), and excluding their views would lead to an unfair or incomplete representation of the staff's opinions and experiences.
    (b) In a city, 5 per cent of the population are Buddhists.
    > **Answer:** The minority is the Buddhist population (5%). It is important to respect their views and rights to prevent the majority religion from dominating them, ensure their religious freedom, and allow them to practice their culture without discrimination, maintaining the principle of secularism and equality for all citizens.
    (c) In a factory mess for all employees, 80 per cent are vegetarians.
    > **Answer:** The minority is the non-vegetarian employees (20%). It is important to respect their views because they also need suitable food options in the mess, and excluding their dietary needs would be unfair and discriminatory, failing to cater to the diverse requirements of all employees.
    (d) In a class of 50 students, 40 belong to more well-off families.
    > **Answer:** The minority is the students from less well-off families (10). It is important to respect their views and needs because they might face different challenges (e.g., related to resources, access to technology, social dynamics) and ensuring their perspectives are considered helps create a more inclusive and equitable classroom environment that addresses the needs of all students, regardless of their socio-economic background.

## Glossary

*   **Arbitrary:** When nothing is fixed and is instead left to one's judgment or choice. This can refer to rules that are not fixed, or decisions that have no basis etc.
*   **Ideal:** A goal or a principle in its most excellent or perfect form.
*   **Indian national movement:** The Indian national movement emerged in nineteenth-century India and saw thousands of men and women coming together to fight British rule. This culminated in India's independence in 1947.
*   **Polity:** A society that has an organised political structure. India is a democratic polity.
*   **Sovereign:** In the context of this chapter it refers to an independent people.
*   **Human Trafficking:** The practice of the illegal buying and selling of different commodities across national borders. In the context of Fundamental Rights discussed in this chapter, it refers to illegal trade in human beings, particularly women and children.
*   **Tyranny:** The cruel and unjust use of power or authority.
