# Making of the Indian Constitution
This section discusses the historical context and the process by which the Indian Constitution was drafted, highlighting the challenges faced by the Constituent Assembly.

By the early twentieth century, the Indian national movement had been fighting for independence from British rule for decades. During this struggle, nationalists imagined and planned for a free India. Having experienced authoritarian rule under the British, they were determined that free India should be a democracy where everyone was treated equally and could participate in government.

The task of setting up a democratic government and defining its rules fell to the **Constituent Assembly**. This group of about 300 people, who became members in 1946, met periodically for three years to write India's Constitution.

The members of the Constituent Assembly faced significant challenges. India was diverse, with many different communities, languages, religions, and cultures. The country was also undergoing turmoil, with the imminent Partition into India and Pakistan, undecided Princely States, and widespread poverty. Despite these difficulties, the Assembly members created a visionary document that respected diversity while preserving national unity. They also focused on socio-economic reforms to eradicate poverty and emphasized the people's role in electing representatives.

As we look at the key features of the Indian Constitution, remember the concerns of diversity, unity, socio-economic reform, and representation that the framers were dealing with and how they tried to balance these to create a strong, democratic India.

**Baba Saheb Dr. Ambedkar** is known as the Father of the Indian Constitution. He believed his participation in the Constituent Assembly helped secure safeguards for Scheduled Castes in the draft constitution. However, he also recognized the potential challenges in the administration of these laws by existing officials and encouraged Scheduled Castes to join government and civil services.
