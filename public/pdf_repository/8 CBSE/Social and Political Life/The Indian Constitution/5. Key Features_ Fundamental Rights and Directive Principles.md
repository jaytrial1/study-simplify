# Key Features of the Indian Constitution: Rights and Directives
This section details the key features of the Indian Constitution related to the rights of citizens and guiding principles for the State: Fundamental Rights and Directive Principles of State Policy.

The Indian Constitution guarantees certain rights to its citizens and provides guidelines for the government.

**Fundamental Rights:**
Often called the 'conscience' of the Indian Constitution, Fundamental Rights protect citizens from the arbitrary and absolute exercise of power by the State. Nationalists, wary of State power after colonial rule, wanted written rights to prevent misuse of power in independent India. These rights protect individuals against the State and other individuals, and also guarantee the rights of minority communities against the majority.

The Fundamental Rights in the Indian Constitution include:

1.  **Right to Equality:** All persons are equal before the law and equally protected by it. No citizen can be discriminated against based on religion, caste, or sex. All public places are accessible to everyone. The State cannot discriminate in employment matters (with some exceptions). The practice of **untouchability** has been abolished.
2.  **Right to Freedom:** Includes freedom of speech and expression, the right to form associations, the right to move and reside anywhere in the country, and the right to practice any profession or business.
3.  **Right against Exploitation:** Prohibits human trafficking, forced labour, and the employment of children under 14 years old.
4.  **Right to Freedom of Religion:** Guarantees religious freedom to all citizens. Every person can practice, profess, and propagate their chosen religion.
5.  **Cultural and Educational Rights:** Allows religious and linguistic minorities to establish their own educational institutions to preserve and develop their culture.
6.  **Right to Constitutional Remedies:** Allows citizens to approach the courts if they believe their Fundamental Rights have been violated by the State.

According to Dr. Ambedkar, the objectives of Fundamental Rights are two-fold: enabling every citizen to claim these rights and making these rights binding upon every authority with law-making power.

**Directive Principles of State Policy:**
In addition to Fundamental Rights, the Constitution includes Directive Principles. These were designed by the Constituent Assembly to encourage greater social and economic reforms and guide the independent Indian State in making laws and policies to reduce poverty.

Which Fundamental Rights will the following situations violate:

*   If a 13-year old child is working in a factory manufacturing carpets.
    > **Answer:** This situation violates the **Right against Exploitation**.
*   If a politician in one state decides to not allow labourers from other states to work in his state.
    > **Answer:** This situation violates the **Right to Freedom**, specifically the right to move freely and reside in any part of the country, and potentially the right to practise any occupation or business.
*   If a group of people are not given permission to open a Telugu-medium school in Kerala.
    > **Answer:** This situation violates the **Cultural and Educational Rights** of minorities.
*   If the government decides not to promote an officer of the armed forces for being a woman.
    > **Answer:** This situation violates the **Right to Equality** by discriminating on the basis of gender in matters of employment.
