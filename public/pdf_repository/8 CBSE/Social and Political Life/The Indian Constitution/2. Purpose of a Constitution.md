# Purpose of a Constitution
This section explains the multiple reasons why countries need a constitution, including laying out ideals, defining the political system, preventing misuse of power, and protecting minorities.

Most countries today have a Constitution, especially democratic ones. However, having a Constitution does not automatically mean a country is democratic. A Constitution serves several important purposes:

*   **Laying out Ideals:** It defines the fundamental nature of a society and the ideals citizens aspire to live by. It provides a set of rules and principles that people agree upon for how the country should be governed, including the type of government and the ideals the country should uphold.
*   **Defining the Political System:** It describes the nature of a country's political system. For example, Nepal's earlier Constitution defined it as a monarchy, while its new Constitution established it as a democracy. In democracies, the Constitution provides guidelines for decision-making.
*   **Preventing Misuse of Power:** In a democracy, elected leaders exercise power on behalf of the people. The Constitution provides safeguards against leaders misusing their authority, which can lead to injustice. The Indian Constitution includes *Fundamental Rights* to protect citizens from such misuse.
*   **Preventing Majority Tyranny:** A Constitution helps ensure that a dominant group does not use its power to exclude or dominate less powerful people or groups (minorities). This protection applies to both *inter-community domination* (one community over another) and *intra-community domination* (within the same community).
*   **Saving Us From Ourselves:** Sometimes, individuals or groups might make decisions based on strong feelings that go against their own larger interests. The Constitution can help guard against these impulsive or detrimental choices.
