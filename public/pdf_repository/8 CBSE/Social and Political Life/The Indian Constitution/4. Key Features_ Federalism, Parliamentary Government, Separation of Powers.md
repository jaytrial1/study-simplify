# Key Features of the Indian Constitution: Structure of Government
This section details the first three key features of the Indian Constitution related to the structure and functioning of the government: Federalism, Parliamentary Form of Government, and Separation of Powers.

The Indian Constitution outlines several key features that define the country's governance.

1.  **Federalism:** This means there is more than one level of government in the country. In India, we have the central government and state governments. **Panchayati Raj** is the third tier at the local level. This system was devised because India has a vast number of communities, making it impractical for decisions to be made only from the capital. State governments can make decisions for their specific areas, while subjects of national concern are governed by central laws. The Constitution lists the subjects each tier can legislate on and specifies their sources of revenue. States are not just agents of the central government but draw their own authority from the Constitution. All persons in India are governed by laws and policies from each of these levels.

2.  **Parliamentary Form of Government:** The representatives in the different tiers of government are elected by the people. The Constitution guarantees **universal adult suffrage**, meaning all adult citizens have the right to vote. The members of the Constituent Assembly believed the freedom struggle had prepared people for this and that it would promote democracy and break down traditional hierarchies based on caste, class, and gender. Indian citizens directly elect their representatives, and any citizen, regardless of social background, can contest elections. These elected representatives are accountable to the people.

3.  **Separation of Powers:** The Constitution divides the government into three organs: the **legislature** (elected representatives who make laws), the **executive** (a smaller group responsible for implementing laws and running the government), and the **judiciary** (the system of courts). To prevent any one branch from misusing its power, the Constitution assigns different powers to each organ. Each organ acts as a check on the others, ensuring a balance of power.

**Understanding 'State' vs. 'Government'**
The term 'State' in this context does not refer to state governments. 'Government' is the body responsible for administering and enforcing laws, and it can change through elections. The 'State', on the other hand, is a political institution representing a sovereign people occupying a definite territory. We speak of the Indian State or the Nepali State. The government is one part of the State, which is a broader concept.
