This section outlines several important laws designed to protect the interests of different groups like workers, consumers, and the poor, and includes a table to summarize their purpose and beneficiaries.

Table 1 provides some important laws relating to the protection of these various interests. Columns (2) and (3) in Table 1 state why and for whom these laws are necessary. Based on discussions in the classroom, you have to complete the remaining entries in the table.

Table 1

| Law | Why is it necessary? | Whose interests does the law protect? |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Minimum Wages Act specifies that wages should not be below a specified minimum. | Many workers are denied fair wages by their employers. Because they badly need work, workers have no bargaining power and are paid low wages. | This law is meant to protect the interests of all workers; particularly, farm labourers, construction workers, factory workers, domestic workers, etc. |
| Law specifying that there be adequate safety measures in workplaces. For example, alarm system, emergency exits, properly functioning machinery. | **Answer:** To prevent injuries, accidents, and loss of life due to unsafe working conditions in factories, mines, and other workplaces. | **Answer:** Workers in hazardous environments, their families, and the community around the workplace. |
| Law requiring that the quality of goods meet certain prescribed standards. For example, electrical appliances have to meet safety standards. | Consumers might be put to risk by the poor quality of products such as electrical appliances, food, medicines. | **Answer:** Consumers who buy goods, ensuring their safety and value for money. |
| Law requiring that the prices of essential goods are not high For example, sugar, kerosene, foodgrains. | **Answer:** To ensure that basic necessities remain affordable and accessible, especially for lower-income families. | The interests of the poor who will otherwise be unable to afford these goods. |
| Law requiring that factories do not pollute air or water. | **Answer:** To protect the environment and the health of people and ecosystems from harmful industrial emissions and waste. | **Answer:** The general public, future generations, communities living near factories, and the environment itself. |
| Laws against child labour in workplaces. | **Answer:** To protect children from exploitation, ensure they receive education, and safeguard their health and development. | **Answer:** Children below a certain age, ensuring their right to education and a safe childhood, and protecting adolescents from hazardous work. |
| Law to form workers unions/associations | By organising themselves into unions, workers can use their combined power to demand fair wages and better working conditions. | **Answer:** Workers, by giving them collective bargaining power and a platform to voice grievances and improve their working lives. |
