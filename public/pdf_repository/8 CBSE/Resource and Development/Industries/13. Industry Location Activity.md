# Activity: Choosing an Industry Site
This activity requires analyzing factors and locations to determine the best site for an iron and steel plant based on provided information and a map.

How to identify a location for establishing an industry -
Divide your class into groups. Each group is a Board of Directors faced with the problem of choosing a suitable site for an iron and steel plant of Developen Dweep. A team of technical experts has submitted a report with notes and a map. The team considered access to iron ore, coal, water and limestone, as well as the main market, sources of labour and port facilities. The team has suggested two sites, X and Y. The Board of Directors has to take the final decision about where to locate the steel plant.

*   Read the report submitted by the team.
*   Study the map to find out the distances of the resources from each site.
*   Give each resource a 'weight' from 1 to 10, according to its importance. The greater the 'pull' of the factor on the industry the higher the weight from 1 to 10.
*   Complete the table below.
*   The site with the lowest total should be the most satisfactory site.
*   Remember each group of directors can decide differently.

### Report
Factors/Resources affecting the location of a proposed Iron and Steel Plant on Developen Dweep.

*   Iron ore: This is a very large deposit of low grade iron ore. Long distance transportation of the ore would be uneconomic. (This suggests Iron Ore location is very important - High Weight)
*   Coal: The only coalfield contains rich deposits of high grade coal. Transportation of the coal is by railway, which is relatively cheap. (Important, but transport is cheap - Moderate Weight)
*   Limestone: This is widely available over the island, but the purest deposits are in the Chuna Mountains. (Important, but less critical than ore/coal, purity matters - Moderate Weight)
*   Water: Both the tributaries of River Neel carry sufficient water to supply a large iron and steel plant in all seasons. The sea water because of its high salt content is unsuitable. (Available at both sites near rivers - Low Weight)
*   Market: It is expected that the chief market for the Plant's products will be the engineering works of Rajdhanipur. Transport costs for the products - mainly small steel bars and light steel plates would be relatively low. (Market is important, but product transport is cheap - Moderate Weight)
*   Labour supply: This will have to be recruited mainly from the unskilled workers in the 3 fishing villages of Hil, Rah and Sing. It is expected that most workers will commute daily from their present homes. (Access to labour needed, commuting expected - Moderate Weight)
*   Port facilities: These are at present minimal. There is a good, deep natural harbour at port Paschimpur developed to import metal alloys. (Important for imports, but not for main raw materials/exports based on the report - Moderate Weight, maybe slightly lower)

**Estimated Distances from Map (Relative):**
*   Site X is very close to Iron Ore.
*   Site Y is relatively close to Coal and Limestone.
*   Both sites are near rivers (Water).
*   Rajdhanipur (Market) is a significant distance inland from both, with existing rail to X.
*   Hil, Rah, Sing (Labour) are coastal, closer to Y with proposed rail.
*   Port Paschimpur is coastal, with existing rail connecting towards X/Rajdhanipur.

* the larger the pull, the higher the weighting

> **Answer:** Based on the estimated distances from the map and the assigned weights reflecting the importance described in the report, Site Y has a slightly lower total weighted distance (153) compared to Site X (162). This suggests that Site Y might be the more satisfactory location, primarily due to its closer proximity to Coal and Limestone, which are weighted moderately important, and labour supply, which is also moderately important. While Site X is very close to the highly-weighted Iron Ore, the combined transport costs for coal, limestone, and labour make Site Y appear slightly more favorable in this model. However, different weighting choices could lead to a different conclusion, as the activity notes.
