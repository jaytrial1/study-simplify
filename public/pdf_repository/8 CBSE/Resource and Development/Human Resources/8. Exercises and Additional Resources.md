# Exercises and Additional Resources

1. Answer the following questions.
(i) Why are people considered a resource?
> **Answer:** People are considered a resource because they are the ones who find things in nature useful and turn them into resources based on their demands and abilities. Healthy, educated, and motivated people develop resources.

(ii) What are the causes for the uneven distribution of population in the world?
> **Answer:** The uneven distribution of population is caused by geographical factors such as topography (plains preferred over mountains), climate (avoiding extremes), soil (fertile areas preferred), water availability (river valleys preferred), and minerals (areas with deposits attract people). Social, cultural, and economic factors like better housing, education, health facilities, places of cultural significance, and industrial areas also influence distribution.

(iii) The world population has grown very rapidly. Why?
> **Answer:** The world population has grown very rapidly mainly because of a rapid increase in the natural growth rate. This is due to better food supplies and medicine reducing death rates, while birth rates have remained fairly high.

(iv) Discuss the role of any two factors influencing population change.
> **Answer:** Two factors influencing population change are birth rate and death rate. A higher birth rate than death rate leads to population increase. Conversely, a higher death rate than birth rate leads to population decrease. Migration is another factor, where people moving into a country (immigration) increase the population, and people leaving a country (emigration) decrease it.

(v) What is meant by population composition?
> **Answer:** Population composition refers to the structure of the population, which includes characteristics such as the number of males and females, age groups, literacy levels, health conditions, occupations, and income levels.

(vi) What are population pyramids? How do they help in understanding about the population of a country?
> **Answer:** Population pyramids, also called age-sex pyramids, are graphical presentations of the age and sex composition of a population. They show the total population divided into age groups and the percentage of males and females in each group. They help in understanding the number of children, aged people, and dependents, and can indicate birth and death rates and the potential for future population growth or decline.

# 4. Activity

Discuss the characteristics of a society with 'too many under 15 s ' and one with 'too few under 15 s '.
Hint : need for schools; pension schemes, teachers, toys, wheel chairs, labour supply, hospitals.

> **Answer:**
> *   **Society with 'too many under 15s':** This society would have a large proportion of young dependents. Characteristics would include a high need for schools, teachers, and resources related to children like toys and healthcare (hospitals focusing on pediatric care). There would be a large potential future labour supply, but currently a smaller working-age population supporting the young. There would be less immediate concern for pension schemes or many wheelchairs compared to a society with more elderly.
> *   **Society with 'too few under 15s':** This society would have a smaller proportion of young people and likely a larger proportion of older people. Characteristics would include less need for schools and teachers, but a higher need for pension schemes, healthcare facilities for the elderly (hospitals focusing on geriatric care), and potentially more wheelchairs. The future labour supply might be smaller, and there could be a larger working-age population currently, but concerns about supporting an aging population in the future.
