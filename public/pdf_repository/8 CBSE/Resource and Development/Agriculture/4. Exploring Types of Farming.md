# Exploring Types of Farming

Farming practices vary globally based on geographical conditions, demand for produce, available labour, and level of technology. Farming can be classified into two main types:

1.  **Subsistence Farming**
2.  **Commercial Farming**

## Subsistence Farming

This type of farming is practiced primarily to meet the needs of the farmer's family. It traditionally uses low levels of technology and household labour to produce a small output.

Subsistence farming is further classified into:

*   **Intensive Subsistence Agriculture**
*   **Primitive Subsistence Farming**

### Intensive Subsistence Agriculture

In this system, the farmer cultivates a small plot of land using simple tools and more labour. Areas with many days of sunshine and fertile soils allow for growing more than one crop annually on the same plot. Rice is the main crop, while others include wheat, maize, pulses, and oilseeds. This type of agriculture is common in the thickly populated monsoon regions of south, southeast, and east Asia.

### Primitive Subsistence Farming

This includes **shifting cultivation** and **nomadic herding**.

**Shifting Cultivation:**

*   Practiced in thickly forested areas like the Amazon basin, tropical Africa, parts of southeast Asia, and Northeast India.
*   These areas have heavy rainfall and quick regeneration of vegetation.
*   A plot is cleared by felling and burning trees; ashes are mixed with soil.
*   Crops like maize, yam, potatoes, and cassava are grown.
*   Once soil fertility is lost, the land is abandoned, and the cultivator moves to a new plot.
*   Also known as **'slash and burn' agriculture**.

**Nomadic Herding:**

*   Practiced in semi-arid and arid regions like the Sahara, Central Asia, and parts of India (Rajasthan, Jammu and Kashmir).
*   Herdsmen move with their animals (sheep, camel, yak, goats) along defined routes in search of fodder and water.
*   Movement is dictated by climate and terrain.
*   Animals provide milk, meat, wool, hides, and other products to the herders and their families.

## Commercial Farming

In commercial farming, crops are grown and animals are reared for sale in the market. This type uses a large cultivated area and significant capital. Most of the work is done by machines.

Commercial farming includes:

*   **Commercial Grain Farming**
*   **Mixed Farming**
*   **Plantation Agriculture**

### Commercial Grain Farming

Crops are grown specifically for commercial sale. Wheat and maize are common examples. Major areas for this practice are the temperate grasslands of North America, Europe, and Asia. These regions are sparsely populated with large farms. Severe winters limit the growing season, allowing typically only a single crop per year.

### Mixed Farming

Land is used for both growing food and fodder crops and rearing livestock. This is practiced in Europe, eastern USA, Argentina, southeast Australia, New Zealand, and South Africa.

### Plantation Agriculture

Plantations are a type of commercial farming where a single crop like tea, coffee, sugarcane, cashew, rubber, banana, or cotton is grown on a large scale. They require a large amount of labour and capital. Produce is often processed on the farm or in nearby factories, making a developed transport network essential. Major plantations are found in tropical regions, such as rubber in Malaysia, coffee in Brazil, and tea in India and Sri Lanka.
