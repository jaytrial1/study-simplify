# Let's recall

1. What social ideas did the following people support.

*   Rammohun Roy
*   Dayanand Saraswati
*   Veerasalingam Pantulu
*   Jyotirao Phule
*   Pandita Ramabai
*   Periyar
*   Mumtaz Ali
*   Ishwarchandra Vidyasagar

> **Answer:**
> *   **Rammohun Roy:** Opposed Sati, advocated for widow remarriage, women's education, and greater freedom and equality for women.
> *   **Dayanand Saraswati:** Supported widow remarriage and attempted to reform Hinduism (founded Arya Samaj).
> *   **Veerasalingam Pantulu:** Formed an association for widow remarriage in the Madras Presidency.
> *   **Jyotirao Phule:** Opposed caste inequality, challenged Brahmanical supremacy, advocated for the unity of Shudras and Ati Shudras, supported education for girls and "low-caste" children (founded Satyashodhak Samaj).
> *   **Pandita Ramabai:** Felt Hinduism was oppressive to women, wrote about the lives of upper-caste Hindu women, founded a widows' home and training centre in Poona.
> *   **Periyar (E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker):** Led the Self Respect Movement against caste discrimination, criticised Hindu scriptures for upholding inequality, advocated for untouchables to free themselves from religion to achieve equality, and criticised the domination of men over women.
> *   **Mumtaz Ali:** Reinterpreted verses from the Koran to argue for women's education.
> *   **Ishwarchandra Vidyasagar:** Used ancient texts to support widow remarriage (led to the 1856 law) and set up schools for girls.

2. State whether true or false:
(a) When the British captured Bengal, they framed many new laws to regulate the rules regarding marriage, adoption, inheritance of property, etc.
(b) Social reformers had to discard the ancient texts in order to argue for reform in social practices.
(c) Reformers got full support from all sections of the people of the country.
(d) The Child Marriage Restraint Act was passed in 1829.

> **Answer:**
> (a) True
> (b) False (Many reformers used ancient texts to argue *for* reform, claiming current practices were against earlier traditions).
> (c) False (Reformers faced significant opposition from orthodox groups).
> (d) False (The Child Marriage Restraint Act was passed in 1929; Sati was banned in 1829).

# Let's Discuss

3. How did the knowledge of ancient texts help the reformers promote new laws?

> **Answer:** Reformers like Rammohun Roy and Ishwarchandra Vidyasagar used their knowledge of ancient texts to argue that the harmful practices they opposed, such as Sati or the ban on widow remarriage, were not actually part of the original or true interpretations of these texts. They claimed these practices were later additions or distortions. By citing verses or passages from ancient scriptures that supported their reformist views, they could persuade some people and also convince British officials that the reforms were not entirely new or alien ideas but were, in fact, restoring an older, purer tradition. This made it easier to gain support and pass new laws.

4. What were the different reasons people had for not sending girls to school?

> **Answer:** People had several reasons for not sending girls to school:
> *   Fear that schools would take girls away from home and prevent them from performing domestic duties.
> *   Belief that girls travelling through public places to reach school would have a "corrupting influence" on them.
> *   The idea that girls should stay away from public spaces.
> *   The superstition in some parts of the country that educating a girl would make her a widow.
> *   General conservative views that women's place was primarily in the home.

5. Why were Christian missionaries attacked by many people in the country? Would some people have supported them too? If so, for what reasons?

> **Answer:** Christian missionaries were attacked by many people because they were seen as representatives of the British rulers and were actively trying to convert Indians to Christianity. This was perceived as an attack on Indian religions and culture.
> However, some people would have supported them, particularly those from "low" castes and tribal groups. The missionaries often set up schools for these disadvantaged communities, offering them access to education and new opportunities that were previously denied by the traditional social structure. For these groups, the missionaries provided resources and a path towards upward mobility and escape from oppression.

6. In the British period, what new opportunities opened up for people who came from castes that were regarded as "low"?

> **Answer:** During the British period, new opportunities opened up for people from "low" castes, primarily through:
> *   Jobs in newly developing cities (factories, municipalities for sanitation work like digging drains, laying roads, cleaning).
> *   Work in plantations (in Assam, Mauritius, Trinidad, Indonesia).
> *   Jobs in the army (like the Mahar Regiment).
> *   Access to education through schools set up by Christian missionaries and later, reformers. These jobs and education provided a way to earn a living, gain some economic independence, and escape the oppressive control and daily humiliation faced in traditional village structures dominated by upper-caste landowners.

7. How did Jyotirao, and other reformer justify their criticism of caste inequality in society?

> **Answer:** Reformers like Jyotirao Phule justified their criticism of caste inequality in various ways:
> *   **Jyotirao Phule:** Argued that the caste system was not ancient Indian tradition but was imposed by invading Aryans who subjugated the indigenous population (the "low castes"). He claimed the land and power rightfully belonged to the indigenous people.
> *   **Shri Narayana Guru:** Proclaimed the unity of all humankind, stating there is "one caste, one religion, one god for humankind," directly contradicting the idea of inherent caste differences.
> *   **Haridas Thakur:** Questioned Brahmanical texts that supported the caste system.
> *   **E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker (Periyar):** Argued that religious authorities and scriptures (like the Codes of Manu, Bhagavad Gita, Ramayana) were used to establish and maintain the authority of Brahmans over lower castes and the domination of men over women. He felt that freeing oneself from these religious frameworks was necessary for social equality.
> *   **Prarthana Samaj:** Adhered to the Bhakti tradition, which believed in the spiritual equality of all castes.

8. Why did Phule dedicate his book Gulamgiri to the American movement to free slaves?

> **Answer:** Phule dedicated his book *Gulamgiri* (Slavery) to the American movement to free slaves because he saw a direct parallel between the condition of "lower" castes in India and that of black slaves in America. He viewed the caste system as a form of slavery, where certain groups were oppressed and denied basic rights and dignity, much like the enslaved people in America. By dedicating his book to the American abolitionists, he highlighted the universal nature of oppression and showed solidarity with the fight against slavery elsewhere in the world, framing the struggle against caste in India as part of a larger global fight for human freedom and equality.

9. What did Ambedkar want to achieve through the temple entry movement?

> **Answer:** Through the temple entry movement, B.R. Ambedkar wanted to achieve several things:
> *   To challenge the caste prejudices and the practice of untouchability directly in a highly symbolic public and religious space (temples).
> *   To assert the right of Dalits ("untouchables") to access public places and religious institutions, just like upper castes.
> *   To demonstrate that Dalits were also human beings and devotees, deserving of respect and equal treatment, as stated in his quote, "We now want to go to the Tank only to prove that like others, we are also human beings".
> *   To make the wider society, particularly the upper castes, confront the reality and injustice of caste discrimination.

10. Why were Jyotirao Phule and Ramaswamy Naicker critical of the national movement? Did their criticism help the national struggle in any way?

> **Answer:** Jyotirao Phule and E.V. Ramaswamy Naicker (Periyar) were critical of the national movement primarily because they felt it was dominated by upper-caste leaders who did not adequately address the issues of caste discrimination and inequality. Phule believed the unity called for by upper-caste nationalists was temporary and would not lead to true equality for "low castes" after independence. Periyar left the Congress because he saw caste distinctions being maintained even within nationalist gatherings. They felt that social reform and the abolition of caste were as crucial, if not more so, than political independence from the British, and that the national movement was failing to prioritize this.
> Their criticism likely helped the national struggle in some ways:
> *   It forced upper-caste nationalist leaders to confront the issue of caste inequality and prompted some self-reflection and efforts towards reform within the nationalist movement.
> *   It highlighted that "national unity" could not be achieved without addressing deep-seated social divisions and ensuring justice for all sections of society.
> *   It mobilized large numbers of "low-caste" people, adding another dimension to the overall struggle against oppression, even if their immediate focus was social rather than purely political.
