This section introduces two alternative revenue systems developed by the Company in different parts of India to address the shortcomings of the Permanent Settlement.

# New Revenue Systems: Mahalwari and Ryotwari

By the early nineteenth century, many Company officials realised the Permanent Settlement needed to be changed, especially as the Company's administrative and trade expenses increased, requiring more revenue.

### The Mahalwari System

In the North Western Provinces of the Bengal Presidency (now mostly Uttar Pradesh), **Holt Mackenzie** devised a new system that took effect in 1822. Mackenzie believed the village (*mahal*) was a crucial social unit in North India and should be preserved.

Under the Mahalwari system:

*   Collectors visited villages, inspecting land, measuring fields, and recording customs and rights of different groups.
*   The estimated revenue of each plot within a village was added up to calculate the revenue that each *mahal* (village or group of villages) had to pay.
*   This revenue demand was to be revised periodically, not fixed permanently.
*   The responsibility for collecting the revenue and paying it to the Company was given to the **village headman**, not the zamindar.

### The Munro System (Ryotwari)

In the British territories in South India, the idea of a Permanent Settlement was also abandoned. The new system developed there was known as the **Ryotwar** or **Ryotwari system**.

*   It was initially tested on a small scale by **Captain Alexander Read** in areas taken after wars with Tipu Sultan.
*   Subsequently developed by **Thomas Munro**, this system was gradually extended across South India.

Read and Munro argued that South India did not have traditional zamindars. Therefore, the settlement should be made directly with the cultivators (*ryots*) who had tilled the land for generations. Their fields were to be carefully surveyed before assessing the revenue. Munro believed the British should act as paternal figures, protecting the *ryots*.

*Mahal* - In British revenue records, mahal is a revenue estate which may be a village or a group of villages.
