This section explains the two primary systems used for indigo cultivation in India under British influence: *nij* and *ryoti*.

# Indigo Cultivation Methods in India

Facing rising demand, the Company in India sought to expand indigo cultivation. As the trade grew, Company officials and commercial agents invested in indigo production. Many officials even left their jobs to become planters. Attracted by high profits, numerous Scotsmen and Englishmen came to India to become planters. Those lacking funds could get loans from the Company and banks.

There were two main systems for cultivating indigo:

1.  **Nij Cultivation:**
    *   Under this system, the planter produced indigo on lands they directly controlled.
    *   Planters either bought the land or rented it from zamindars.
    *   Indigo was produced by directly employing hired labourers.

2.  **Ryoti Cultivation:**
    *   This system involved planters forcing *ryots* (cultivators) to sign a contract (*satta*).
    *   Sometimes, village headmen were pressured to sign contracts on behalf of the *ryots*.
    *   *Ryots* who signed received cash advances from planters at low interest rates to grow indigo.
    *   The contract obliged the *ryot* to cultivate indigo on at least 25 per cent of their landholding.
    *   The planter provided the seed and drill, while the cultivator prepared the soil, sowed the seed, and looked after the crop.

*Bigha* - A unit of measurement of land. Before British rule, the size of this area varied. In Bengal the British standardised it to about one-third of an acre.
