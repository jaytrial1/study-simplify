This section explains the term 'colonial' and describes the process of colonisation based on the British experience in India, highlighting the significant changes brought about by foreign rule.

# What is colonial?

The term **colonial** refers to the process and state of one country being subjugated by another, leading to political, economic, social, and cultural changes in the subjugated country.

In the context of British rule in India, colonisation involved:
*   The British conquering the country and establishing their rule.
*   Subjugating local rulers (**nawabs** and **rajas**).
*   Establishing control over the economy and society.
*   Collecting **revenue** to cover their expenses.
*   Buying goods at low prices.
*   Producing crops needed for export.
*   Bringing about changes in values, tastes, customs, and practices.

When one country's subjugation by another results in these kinds of political, economic, social, and cultural transformations, the process is called **colonisation**.

It is important to note that not all classes and groups within the colonised country experienced these changes in the same way. This diversity of experience is why the history is referred to in the plural, "Our Pasts."
