# Activities and Questions

## Let's imagine

Imagine that you are involved in the Indian national movement. Based on your reading of this chapter, briefly discuss your preferred methods of struggle and your vision of a free India.

> **Answer:** If I were involved in the Indian national movement, my preferred methods of struggle would likely be a combination of:
> *   **Mass mobilization and non-violent protest:** Inspired by Gandhi, I would advocate for large-scale demonstrations, boycotts, and civil disobedience (like breaking unjust laws) to pressure the British government. This method showed immense power in movements like the Swadeshi and Salt Satyagraha.
> *   **Constructive work:** Building self-reliance through promoting swadeshi goods, national education, and addressing social issues in villages would strengthen the movement from within and prepare people for self-governance.
> *   **Political negotiation:** While engaging in mass action, I would also support leaders who could negotiate with the British from a position of strength gained through popular support, aiming for a peaceful transfer of power.
>
> My vision of a free India would be:
> *   A **sovereign nation** where Indians make decisions about their own affairs.
> *   An **inclusive society** where all people, regardless of religion, caste, gender, or background, have equal rights and opportunities.
> *   An **economically self-reliant** country, free from exploitation, where resources benefit all its people.
> *   A nation based on the principles of **justice, freedom, and equality**.

## Let's recall

1.  Why were people dissatisfied with British rule in the 1870s and 1880s?
2.  Who did the Indian National Congress wish to speak for?
3.  What economic impact did the First World War have on India?
4.  What did the Muslim League resolution of 1940 ask for?

> **Answer to Question 1:** People were dissatisfied due to the British control over resources and lives, restrictive laws like the Arms Act and Vernacular Press Act, racial discrimination highlighted by events like the Ilbert Bill controversy, high land revenue, famines, and the economic drain of wealth to Britain.
>
> **Answer to Question 2:** The Indian National Congress wished to speak for all the people of India, irrespective of class, colour, caste, creed, language, or gender, aiming to represent the interests of the entire nation.
>
> **Answer to Question 3:** The First World War led to increased defence expenditure and taxes, causing hardship for common people. However, it also created demand for Indian industrial goods, reduced imports, and allowed Indian industries and business groups to expand and demand greater opportunities.
>
> **Answer to Question 4:** The Muslim League resolution of 1940 asked for "Independent States" for Muslims in the north-western and eastern areas of the country, based on the idea that Muslims constituted a separate nation.

## Let's discuss

5.  Who were the Moderates? How did they propose to struggle against British rule?
6.  How was the politics of the Radicals within the Congress different from that of the Moderates?
7.  Discuss the various forms that the Non-Cooperation Movement took in different parts of India. How did the people understand Gandhiji?
8.  Why did Gandhiji choose to break the salt law?
9.  Discuss those developments of the 1937-47 period that led to the creation of Pakistan.

> **Answer to Question 5:** The Moderates were the early leaders of the Indian National Congress (roughly 1885-1905). They proposed to struggle against British rule through constitutional means. Their methods included:
> *   Demanding reforms from the government.
> *   Holding meetings and passing resolutions.
> *   Publishing newspapers and writing articles to raise public awareness about British injustice and economic ruin.
> *   Sending representatives to different parts of the country to mobilise public opinion.
> *   Submitting petitions and sending representatives to England, believing the British had respect for freedom and justice and would accept just demands.
>
> **Answer to Question 6:** The politics of the Radicals (like Bepin Chandra Pal, Bal Gangadhar Tilak, Lala Lajpat Rai) differed from the Moderates in several ways:
> *   **Methods:** Radicals criticised the Moderates' "politics of prayers" and advocated for more direct action, including mass mobilisation and boycott of British institutions and goods.
> *   **Objectives:** While Moderates sought greater Indian representation and reforms within the British system, Radicals aimed for "swaraj" (self-rule) based on self-reliance, not relying on the government's good intentions.
> *   **Emphasis:** Radicals emphasized self-help, swadeshi enterprise, national education, and the strength of the people themselves, whereas Moderates focused more on appealing to the British sense of justice.
>
> **Answer to Question 7:** The Non-Cooperation Movement took various forms across India, often linked to local grievances:
> *   **General:** Students left government schools/colleges, lawyers gave up practices, titles were surrendered, legislatures boycotted, foreign cloth was burned.
> *   **Kheda (Gujarat):** Patidar peasants protested high land revenue through non-violent campaigns.
> *   **Coastal Andhra/Tamil Nadu:** Liquor shops were picketed.
> *   **Guntur (Andhra Pradesh):** Tribals/peasants staged "forest satyagrahas" against forest regulations.
> *   **Sind/Bengal:** The Khilafat-Non-Cooperation alliance fostered strong communal unity.
> *   **Punjab:** The Akali movement to remove corrupt mahants from gurdwaras aligned with Non-Cooperation.
> *   **Assam:** Tea garden labourers demanded wage increases and left plantations, invoking Gandhi's name.
> People often understood Gandhiji as a messiah or divine figure ("Mahatma," "sadhu," "deota," "Gandhi Raja") who could help them overcome local problems like illegal evictions and high taxes, sometimes attributing their own successes to him, and occasionally undertaking actions in his name that were not strictly non-violent.
>
> **Answer to Question 8:** Gandhiji chose to break the salt law because:
> *   The state's monopoly on salt manufacture and sale was seen as oppressive and sinful, as salt is an essential item for everyone.
> *   Taxing salt affected all people, rich and poor, making it a grievance that everyone could relate to and unite against.
> *   Breaking the salt law was a direct, symbolic act of defiance against British authority that could be performed by common people, making the struggle accessible and widespread.
>
> **Answer to Question 9:** Several developments from 1937-47 led to the creation of Pakistan:
> *   **Muslim League's "Two-Nation Theory":** From the late 1930s, the League increasingly viewed Muslims as a separate nation.
> *   **Experience of 1937 Elections:** The League's poor performance in "General" constituencies and the Congress's refusal to form joint governments in provinces like UP convinced the League that Muslims would be marginalized in a united India and might go unrepresented as a minority.
> *   **Failure to Mobilise Muslim Masses by Congress:** The Congress's inability to fully reach and assure the Muslim masses in the 1930s allowed the League to expand its support base, especially while Congress leaders were jailed during WWII.
> *   **Failure of Negotiations (1945-46):** Talks between the British, Congress, and League failed because the League insisted it was the sole representative of Muslims, which Congress could not accept.
> *   **1946 Election Results:** The League's spectacular success in reserved Muslim seats reinforced its claim and demand for Pakistan.
> *   **Failure of the Cabinet Mission (1946):** The mission's proposal for a united India with a loose confederation failed to gain agreement from both Congress and the League on details.
> *   **Direct Action Day (1946) and Communal Violence:** The League's call for mass agitation led to widespread riots and violence, particularly in Calcutta, which quickly spread, creating an atmosphere of fear and distrust that made the idea of a united India seem increasingly impossible to many. This escalating violence ultimately pushed the British and Congress towards accepting Partition as an unavoidable outcome.

## Let's do

10. Find out how the national movement was organised in your city, district, area or state. Who participated in it and who led it? What did the movement in your area achieve?
11. Find out more about the life and work of any two participants or leaders of the national movement and write a short essay about them. You may choose a person not mentioned in this chapter.

> **Answer to Question 10:** This activity requires local research specific to the student's area. The student should:
> *   Research local historical records, museums, or talk to older residents/local historians.
> *   Identify key events related to the national movement that happened locally (e.g., specific protests, Gandhi's visits, local leaders jailed, impact of movements like Non-Cooperation, Civil Disobedience, Quit India).
> *   Identify prominent local leaders and the groups of people who participated (e.g., peasants, students, women, specific communities).
> *   Determine the specific outcomes or achievements of the movement in their area (e.g., specific grievances addressed, contribution to the national struggle).
>
> **Answer to Question 11:** This activity requires independent research on two historical figures. The student should:
> *   Choose two participants/leaders (either from the chapter like Sardar Patel, Maulana Azad, or someone local/lesser-known but significant).
> *   Research their background, their role in the national movement, key events they were involved in, their methods of struggle, and their contributions.
> *   Write a short essay summarizing their life and work based on the research.
