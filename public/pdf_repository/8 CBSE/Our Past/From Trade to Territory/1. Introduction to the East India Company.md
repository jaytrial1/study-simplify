This section introduces the decline of the Mughal Empire after Aurangzeb and the emergence of regional kingdoms, setting the stage for a new power, the British East India Company, to rise.

# From Trade to Territory: The Company Establishes Power

Aurangzeb was the last of the powerful Mughal rulers. After his death in 1707, many Mughal governors (subadars) and big zamindars began asserting their authority and establishing regional kingdoms. As powerful regional kingdoms emerged, Delhi could no longer function as an effective centre.

By the second half of the eighteenth century, a new power was emerging: the British. Originally a small trading company, they were reluctant to acquire territories. This chapter explains how they became masters of a vast empire.

After Aurangzeb, there was no powerful Mughal ruler, but Mughal emperors continued to be symbolically important. When a massive rebellion against British rule broke out in 1857, Bahadur Shah Zafar was seen as the natural leader. After the revolt was put down, he was forced to leave the kingdom, and his sons were shot.
