# Let's discuss

4. What attracted European trading companies to India?

> **Answer:** European trading companies were attracted to India by the high demand and profitability of certain goods in Europe. These included fine qualities of cotton and silk produced in India, as well as spices like pepper, cloves, cardamom, and cinnamon. They sought to buy these goods at cheap prices in India and sell them at much higher prices in Europe.

5. What were the areas of conflict between the Bengal nawabs and the East India Company?

> **Answer:** The main areas of conflict were the Company's demands for trade concessions, its refusal to pay taxes (duties), its attempts to mint its own coins, and its efforts to extend fortifications around its settlements. The Nawabs accused the Company of depriving the government of revenue and undermining their authority, while the Company felt the Nawabs' demands ruined trade.

6. How did the assumption of Diwani benefit the East India Company?

> **Answer:** The assumption of Diwani in 1765 gave the East India Company the right to collect revenue from Bengal. This was a major benefit because it solved the problem of financing trade. Previously, the Company had to import gold and silver from Britain to buy goods in India. With the Diwani, revenues from Bengal could be used to purchase Indian textiles, maintain troops, and cover the costs of building forts and offices, stopping the outflow of gold from Britain.

7. Explain the system of "subsidiary alliance".

> **Answer:** The subsidiary alliance was a system imposed by the Company on Indian states. Under this system, Indian rulers were not allowed to have their own independent armed forces. They were to be protected by the Company's army, but they had to pay for the "subsidiary forces" maintained by the Company for this protection. If a ruler failed to make the payment, a portion of their territory would be taken away as a penalty.

8. In what way was the administration of the Company different from that of Indian rulers?

> **Answer:** The Company's administration introduced new concepts like dividing territories into Presidencies ruled by Governors, with a Governor-General as the supreme head. They established a formal system of justice with distinct civil (diwani adalat) and criminal (faujdari adalat) courts in each district, presided over by European collectors or supervised by them. The Collector became the central figure of authority in a district, focusing on revenue collection and law and order, a role that consolidated power differently than under many Indian rulers. They also attempted to codify laws based on interpretations of Indian legal texts.

9. Describe the changes that occurred in the composition of the Company's army.

> **Answer:** The Company's army, initially similar to the Mughal army with cavalry and infantry, began recruiting peasants and training them as professional soldiers, calling them sepoys. As warfare technology changed, particularly with the introduction of muskets and matchlocks, the importance of cavalry declined while infantry became more crucial. In the early 19th century, the British developed a uniform military culture, subjecting soldiers to European-style training, drill, and discipline, which sometimes conflicted with soldiers' caste and community feelings.
