Fig. 14 a, b, c show the expansion of British territorial power in India.

Compare these maps with a present-day political map of India. Identify the parts of India not under British rule in each map.

> **Answer:** Looking at the maps:
> *   **Fig 14 a (1797):** Large parts of northern, central, western, and southern India were not under British rule. Significant areas controlled by the Marathas, Mysore (before 1799), the Nizam of Hyderabad, the Sikh kingdom (Punjab), Rajput states, Awadh, and others were outside direct British control (shown in red).
> *   **Fig 14 b (1840):** British rule (yellow) has expanded significantly, especially in northern India (including Delhi and Agra), parts of central India, and along the coasts. Still, large independent or semi-independent areas remained, notably Punjab, Sind, Awadh, parts of central India, and many princely states (shown in red).
> *   **Fig 14 c (1857):** British direct rule (yellow) covers most of the subcontinent. Only a few large areas, like Kashmir, Rajputana, parts of Punjab (recently annexed), the Nizam's Hyderabad, Mysore (under subsidiary alliance), and various smaller princely states remained outside direct British administration (shown in red).
