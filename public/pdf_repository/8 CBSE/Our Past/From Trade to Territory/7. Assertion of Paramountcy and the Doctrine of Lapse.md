This section explains the Company's aggressive policy of "paramountcy" under Lord Hastings and the annexation policy known as the "Doctrine of Lapse" under Lord Dalhousie, along with instances of resistance.

# The claim to paramountcy

From the early nineteenth century, the Company pursued aggressive territorial expansion. Under Lord Hastings (1813-1823), a new policy of "paramountcy" was initiated. The Company claimed its authority was paramount or supreme, greater than that of Indian states. To protect its interests, it felt justified in annexing or threatening to annex any Indian kingdom. This policy guided later British actions.

This policy faced challenges. When the British tried to annex Kitoor (Karnataka), Rani Channamma led resistance. She was arrested in 1824 and died in prison in 1829. Rayanna, a poor chowkidar, continued the resistance, destroying British camps. He was caught and hanged in 1830.

In the late 1830s, the Company feared Russia might expand into India from the north-west. To secure the region, they fought a prolonged war with Afghanistan (1838-1842), establishing indirect Company rule. Sind was taken in 1843. Punjab was next. Maharaja Ranjit Singh held the Company back, but after his death in 1839, two wars were fought with the Sikh kingdom. Punjab was annexed in 1849.

# The Doctrine of Lapse

The final wave of annexations was under Lord Dalhousie (1848-1856). He devised the **Doctrine of Lapse**: if an Indian ruler died without a male heir, his kingdom would "lapse" (become part of Company territory). Kingdoms annexed by this doctrine included Satara (1848), Sambalpur (1850), Udaipur (1852), Nagpur (1853), and Jhansi (1854).

Finally, in 1856, the Company took over Awadh. Their justification was "duty" to free people from the Nawab's "misgovernment". Enraged by the Nawab's humiliating deposition, the people of Awadh joined the 1857 revolt.

### Activity

Imagine that you are a nawab's nephew and have been brought up thinking that you will one day be king. Now you find that this will not be allowed by the British because of the new Doctrine of Lapse. What will be your feelings? What will you plan to do so that you can inherit the crown?

> **Answer:** My feelings would be shock, anger, and betrayal. My birthright, my destiny, has been stolen by a foreign power using an unfair rule. I would feel humiliated and powerless.
> My plan would be to rally support among the people and nobles who are loyal to my family and resent the British interference. I would seek alliances with other rulers affected by this doctrine or the subsidiary alliance. I would secretly gather resources and perhaps seek help from powers hostile to the British. My goal would be to resist the annexation, perhaps through negotiation first, but ultimately through armed rebellion if necessary, to reclaim my rightful inheritance and protect my kingdom's honour.
