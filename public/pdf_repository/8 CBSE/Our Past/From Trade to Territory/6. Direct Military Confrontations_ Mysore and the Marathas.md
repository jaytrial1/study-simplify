This section describes the Company's direct military conflicts with powerful Indian states, specifically focusing on the wars against Mysore under Haidar Ali and Tipu Sultan, and against the Maratha confederacy.

# Tipu Sultan - The "Tiger of Mysore"

The Company resorted to direct military confrontation when its interests were threatened. This is seen with Mysore.

Mysore grew strong under Haidar Ali (1761-1782) and Tipu Sultan (1782-1799). Mysore controlled the profitable Malabar coast trade where the Company bought pepper and cardamom. In 1785, Tipu Sultan stopped this export through his ports and disallowed local merchants from trading with the Company. He also allied with the French and modernised his army.

The British saw Haidar and Tipu as dangerous rulers who needed to be controlled. Four wars were fought with Mysore (1767-69, 1780-84, 1790-92, 1799). The Company won only the last one, the Battle of Shrirangapatnam, in 1799. Tipu Sultan was killed defending his capital. Mysore was placed under the former Wodeyar dynasty, and a subsidiary alliance was imposed.

This mechanical toy shows a tiger mauling a European soldier. Turning a handle made the tiger roar and the soldier shriek. The British took it after Tipu's death in 1799. It is now in the Victoria and Albert Museum in London.

### The legend of Tipu

Legend says Tipu Sultan went hunting, faced a tiger when his gun failed, and battled it unarmed before killing it with a dagger. After this, he was known as the "Tiger of Mysore" and had a tiger image on his flag.

# War with the Marathas

From the late eighteenth century, the Company also aimed to destroy Maratha power. Their dream of ruling from Delhi was shattered after the Third Battle of Panipat in 1761. They were divided into states under chiefs (sardars) from dynasties like Sindhia, Holkar, Gaikwad, and Bhonsle. These chiefs formed a **confederacy** (alliance) under a Peshwa (Principal Minister) based in Pune, who was the military and administrative head. Mahadji Sindhia and Nana Phadnis were famous Maratha figures.

The Marathas were defeated in a series of wars:
*   **First War (ended 1782):** Treaty of Salbai, no clear victor.
*   **Second War (1803-05):** Fought on different fronts, British gained Orissa and territories north of the Yamuna (including Agra and Delhi).
*   **Third War (1817-19):** Maratha power ended. The Peshwa was removed and sent away to Bithur near Kanpur with a pension. The Company gained control over territories south of the Vindhyas.
