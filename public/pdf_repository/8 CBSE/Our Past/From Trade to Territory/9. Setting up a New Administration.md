This section details the administrative changes introduced by the Company, including the establishment of presidencies, a new justice system, and the role of the Collector.

# Setting up a New Administration

Warren Hastings (Governor-General from 1773 to 1785) was important in expanding Company power. By his time, the Company had power in Bengal, Bombay, and Madras. British territories were divided into **Presidencies**: Bengal, Madras, and Bombay. Each was ruled by a Governor. The Governor-General was the supreme head. Warren Hastings introduced administrative reforms, especially in justice.

From 1772, a new justice system was established. Each district had two courts:
*   **Criminal court (faujdari adalat):** Still under a qazi and a mufti but supervised by collectors.
*   **Civil court (diwani adalat):** European district collectors presided, advised by Maulvis and Hindu pandits who interpreted Indian laws.

**Qazi:** A judge
**Mufti:** A jurist who expounded the law the qazi would administer

A major problem was that Brahman pandits gave different interpretations of local laws based on various schools of **Dharmashastras** (Sanskrit texts prescribing social rules, composed from c. 500 BCE onwards). For uniformity, eleven pandits compiled a digest of Hindu laws in 1775, translated into English by N.B. Halhed. A code of Muslim laws was also compiled by 1778 for European judges.

Under the Regulating Act of 1773, a new Supreme Court was established. A court of appeal, the Sadar Nizamat Adalat, was also set up at Calcutta.

The principal figure in an Indian district was the **Collector**. His job was to collect revenue and taxes and maintain law and order with the help of judges, police officers, and darogas. His office, the Collectorate, became a new centre of power, replacing previous authorities.

When Warren Hastings returned to England in 1785, Edmund Burke accused him of misgovernment in Bengal, leading to a seven-year impeachment trial in the British Parliament.

Edmund Burke's opening speech during the impeachment: "I impeach him in the name of the people of India, whose rights he has trodden under his foot and whose country he has turned into a desert. Lastly in the name of human nature itself... I impeach the common enemy and oppressor of all."

**Impeachment:** A trial by the House of Lords in England for misconduct charges brought by the House of Commons.
