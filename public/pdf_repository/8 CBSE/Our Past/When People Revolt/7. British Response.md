# The Company Fights Back
Alarmed by the scale of the uprising, the British East India Company gathered its strength and launched a counter-offensive to regain control.

Unsettled by the massive revolt, the Company decided to crush it with full force. They brought in reinforcements from England, passed new laws to easily convict rebels, and moved into the main centres of the revolt.

Delhi was recaptured from the rebels in September 1857. The last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was tried, sentenced to life imprisonment, and sent to prison in Rangoon with his wife Begum Zinat Mahal in October 1858. Bahadur Shah Zafar died in Rangoon jail in November 1862.

However, recapturing Delhi did not end the uprising. People continued to resist. The British fought for two years to suppress the massive popular uprising.

Lucknow was taken in March 1858. Rani Lakshmibai was defeated and killed in June 1858. Rani Avantibai also died fighting after initial success. Tantia Tope escaped to the jungles of central India and continued a guerrilla war with support from tribal and peasant leaders until he was captured, tried, and killed in April 1859.

Just as rebel victories had encouraged the uprising, defeats led to desertions. The British also tried to win back loyalty. They offered rewards to loyal landholders, allowing them to keep their traditional land rights. Rebels who surrendered and had not killed any white people were promised safety and their land rights would not be denied. Despite this, hundreds of sepoys, rebels, nawabs, and rajas were tried and hanged.
