## Let's Recall
1. What was the demand of Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi that was refused by the British?
> **Answer:** Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi demanded that the Company recognise her adopted son as the heir to the kingdom after her husband's death.
2. What did the British do to protect the interests of those who converted to Christianity?
> **Answer:** The British passed a new law in 1850 that made conversion to Christianity easier. This law allowed an Indian who had converted to Christianity to inherit the property of his ancestors.
3. What objections did the sepoys have to the new cartridges that they were asked to use?
> **Answer:** The sepoys objected to the new cartridges because they were suspected of being coated with the fat of cows and pigs. Using these cartridges would offend the religious sentiments of both Hindus (cow fat) and Muslims (pig fat).
4. How did the last Mughal emperor live the last years of his life?
> **Answer:** The last Mughal emperor, Bahadur Shah Zafar, was tried in court, sentenced to life imprisonment, and sent to prison in Rangoon in October 1858 with his wife. He died in the Rangoon jail in November 1862.

> **Answer:** As a British officer, I would use trusted messengers who know the local language and terrain, send coded messages, avoid writing down sensitive information, hold meetings in secure locations away from public view, and perhaps spread misinformation among the local population to confuse the rebels. 5. What could be the reasons for the confidence of the British rulers about their position in India before May 1857?
> **Answer:** Before May 1857, the British rulers were likely confident due to their military superiority, their success in annexing territories and defeating Indian rulers over the past 100 years, their belief in their own administrative system and reforms, and possibly underestimating the level of widespread discontent among different sections of Indian society and the potential for them to unite.
6. What impact did Bahadur Shah Zafar's support to the rebellion have on the people and the ruling families?
> **Answer:** Bahadur Shah Zafar's support significantly boosted the morale of the rebels and gave the uprising legitimacy. It inspired people by offering a symbol of traditional authority and a potential alternative to British rule. For ruling families and chieftains who had lost power, his support offered the hope that they could regain control of their territories under Mughal authority, leading many to join the rebellion.
7. How did the British succeed in securing the submission of the rebel landowners of Awadh?
> **Answer:** The British secured the submission of rebel landowners by offering them rewards and assurances. They announced that loyal landholders would keep their traditional land rights. They also promised that rebels who surrendered and had not killed any white people would remain safe and their rights to land would not be denied.
8. In what ways did the British change their policies as a result of the rebellion of 1857?
> **Answer:** As a result of the 1857 rebellion, the British made several changes: they transferred power from the East India Company to the British Crown, assured Indian rulers that their territories would not be annexed in the future (allowing adoption but making them subordinates), reduced the proportion of Indian soldiers in the army while increasing European soldiers and changing recruitment practices, confiscated Muslim land and treated them with suspicion, decided to respect Indian religious and social practices, and protected the rights of landlords and zamindars over their lands.
