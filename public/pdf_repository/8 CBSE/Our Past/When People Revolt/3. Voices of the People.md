## Source 1: The list of eighty-four rules
Vishnubhatt Godse, a Brahman from Maharashtra, wrote in his book *Majha Pravaas* about meeting sepoys who warned him not to travel because a massive upheaval was imminent. According to the sepoys, the English were determined to destroy Hindu and Muslim religions. They claimed the English had announced eighty-four rules to kings and princes in Calcutta, which the rulers refused to accept, warning of consequences. The kings reportedly returned home angry and began planning. A date was fixed for a "war of religion," and a secret plan was circulated through letters from the Meerut cantonment.

## Source 2: "There was soon excitement in every regiment"
Subedar Sitaram Pande, who served the British for 48 years, wrote his memoirs *From Sepoy to Subedar*. He believed that the annexation of Awadh filled the sepoys with distrust. Agents of the Nawab of Awadh and the King of Delhi travelled across India, telling sepoys how the British had betrayed their kings and inventing stories to persuade them to mutiny and restore the Emperor of Delhi. They claimed this was possible if the soldiers acted together.

Sitaram Pande also wrote about the new rifle cartridges. Parties from each regiment were sent for training on the new rifle. A rumour spread that the cartridges were greased with the fat of cows and pigs, offending both Hindus and Muslims. Letters were sent between regiments, causing excitement. While some old soldiers felt the British had never insulted their religion before, the annexation of Awadh had already angered the sepoys. Interested parties quickly claimed the cartridge issue was a plot by the English to convert them all to Christianity.
