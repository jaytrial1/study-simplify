# When People Revolt: 1857 and After
This chapter explores the reasons behind the widespread revolt against the East India Company in 1857 and the changes that occurred afterward.

In previous chapters, we learned about the policies of the East India Company and how they affected various groups like kings, queens, peasants, landlords, tribals, and soldiers. We also saw how people reacted when these policies harmed their interests or went against their feelings.

## Policies and the People
The policies of the East India Company had diverse impacts on different sections of Indian society, leading to widespread discontent.

## Nawabs Lose Their Power
Since the mid-eighteenth century, Indian rulers like nawabs and rajas gradually lost their authority and respect. The British stationed Residents in their courts, reduced their freedom, disbanded their armies, and took away their territories and revenues step by step.

Many ruling families tried to negotiate with the Company to protect their interests. For example, Rani Lakshmibai of Jhansi wanted her adopted son recognised as the heir after her husband's death. Nana Saheb, the adopted son of Peshwa Baji Rao II, asked for his father's pension after his death. However, the Company, confident in its power, rejected these requests.

Awadh was one of the last territories the British took over. A subsidiary alliance was imposed in 1801, and it was annexed in 1856. Governor-General Dalhousie claimed the territory was being misgoverned and needed British rule for proper administration.

The Company also planned to end the Mughal dynasty. They removed the Mughal king's name from the coins they minted. In 1849, Governor-General Dalhousie announced that after Bahadur Shah Zafar's death, the royal family would be moved out of the Red Fort. In 1856, Governor-General Canning decided that Bahadur Shah Zafar would be the last Mughal king, and his descendants would only be called princes.
