# Aftermath and Changes in British Policy
Although the British suppressed the revolt by the end of 1859, the scale of the uprising forced them to significantly change their ruling policies in India.

By the end of 1859, the British had regained control, but they realised they could no longer rule India with the same policies.

Here are the important changes introduced by the British:

1.  **Transfer of Power:** The British Parliament passed a new Act in 1858, transferring the East India Company's powers to the British Crown. A Secretary of State for India was appointed in the British Cabinet, responsible for governing India, advised by the India Council. The Governor-General of India was given the title of Viceroy, acting as the Crown's personal representative. This meant the British government took direct responsibility for ruling India.
2.  **Treatment of Rulers:** All ruling chiefs were assured their territories would not be annexed in the future. They could pass their kingdoms to their heirs, including adopted sons. However, they had to accept the British Queen as their ultimate authority, becoming subordinates of the British Crown.
3.  **Military Reorganisation:** The proportion of Indian soldiers in the army was reduced, and the number of European soldiers was increased. Instead of recruiting from Awadh, Bihar, central India, and south India, more soldiers were recruited from groups like Gurkhas, Sikhs, and Pathans.
4.  **Treatment of Muslims:** The land and property of Muslims were widely confiscated, and they were treated with suspicion and hostility. The British believed Muslims were largely responsible for the uprising.
5.  **Respect for Customs:** The British decided to respect the traditional religious and social practices of people in India.
6.  **Protection for Landholders:** Policies were made to protect landlords and zamindars, giving them security of rights over their lands.

Thus, a new phase in Indian history began after 1857.
