# A Mutiny Becomes a Popular Uprising
Sometimes, struggles between rulers and the ruled can grow into widespread popular resistance that challenges state power. This happened in northern India in 1857.

A very large number of people began to believe they shared a common enemy and rose up together. For this to happen, people needed to organise, communicate, take initiative, and feel confident they could change the situation.

Such a situation developed in northern India in 1857. After a hundred years of control, the East India Company faced a massive uprising starting in May 1857 that threatened its presence in India. Sepoys mutinied in several places, beginning in Meerut, and many people from different parts of society joined the rebellion. Some consider it the biggest armed resistance to colonialism in the nineteenth century globally.

*Mutiny* - When soldiers as a group disobey their officers in the army

## Activity
1. What were the important concerns in the minds of the people according to Sitaram and according to Vishnubhatt?
> **Answer:** According to Vishnubhatt, the main concern was the English trying to destroy the religions of Hindus and Muslims by imposing new rules. According to Sitaram, the main concerns were the distrust caused by the annexation of Awadh and the fear that the new rifle cartridges, greased with animal fat, were an attempt to convert sepoys to Christianity.
2. What role did they think the rulers were playing? What role did the sepoys seem to play?
> **Answer:** Vishnubhatt's account suggests that Indian rulers were resisting the British rules and planning to fight back. Sitaram's account suggests that agents of the Indian rulers were actively trying to persuade the sepoys to mutiny and restore Mughal authority. The sepoys, in both accounts, are portrayed as being deeply affected by these issues and ready to rebel against the British.
