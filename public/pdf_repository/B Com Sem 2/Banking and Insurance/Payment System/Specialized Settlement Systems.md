Specialized Settlement Systems

# Forex Settlements

**Definition:**

*   Forex Settlement refers to the process of finalizing and transferring funds between parties involved in a foreign exchange (FX) transaction.
*   It involves the exchange of two different currencies at an agreed-upon exchange rate on a specified date (settlement date).
*   Settlement ensures that the contractual obligations between the buyer and seller of the currencies are fulfilled.
*   **Example:** Exchanging your local currency for a foreign currency when traveling involves a forex settlement.

**Key Concepts:**

1.  **Transaction Parties:** Buyer (purchases one currency) and Seller (sells the other currency).
2.  **Currency Pairs:** FX transactions always involve two currencies (e.g., USD/INR, EUR/USD). Settlement means delivering one and receiving the other.
3.  **Settlement Date:** The date currencies are exchanged.
    *   **Spot Settlement:** Typically two business days after the trade date (T+2).
    *   **Forward Settlement:** Agreed for a future date beyond T+2.
    *   **Same-Day (Cash) Settlement:** Occurs on the trade date (T+0).
    *   **Tom (Tomorrow) Settlement:** Occurs on the next business day (T+1).
4.  **Settlement Systems:** Facilitated via systems like SWIFT (Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication) and specialized clearinghouses/systems like CLS Bank.
5.  **Nostro and Vostro Accounts:**
    *   **Nostro Account:** A domestic bank's account holding foreign currency with a bank abroad ("Our account with you").
    *   **Vostro Account:** A foreign bank's account holding domestic currency with a domestic bank ("Your account with us"). These are crucial for interbank FX settlements.

**Types of Forex Settlement:**

1.  **Gross Settlement:** Each transaction is settled individually. High security, but requires more liquidity.
2.  **Net Settlement:** Multiple transactions between two parties are netted out, and only the net amount is settled. Reduces liquidity needs.
3.  **Payment-versus-Payment (PvP):** A mechanism where transfer of one currency happens only if the corresponding transfer of the other currency is confirmed simultaneously. Mitigates settlement risk. (e.g., CLS Bank).

**Settlement Risks in Forex:**

1.  **Settlement Risk (Herstatt Risk):** Risk that one party pays out its currency but does not receive the other currency in return (due to time zone differences, counterparty failure etc.). Named after the 1974 collapse of German Bankhaus Herstatt.
2.  **Credit Risk:** Risk that a counterparty defaults on its obligation.
3.  **Liquidity Risk:** Risk of not having sufficient funds (in the correct currency) to settle on time.
4.  **Operational Risk:** Risk of errors or failures in settlement processes, systems, or controls.

**Forex Settlement in India:**

*   Regulated by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI).
*   Uses domestic systems like RTGS/NEFT for the INR leg of transactions.
*   Involves banks, corporates, financial institutions.
*   Use of forward contracts and derivatives to hedge FX settlement risk is common.

**Importance of Forex Settlement:**

*   **Global Trade:** Essential for smooth functioning of international trade and investment.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Proper systems (like CLS, PvP) reduce counterparty and settlement risks.
*   **Financial Stability:** Efficient settlement contributes to global financial market stability.

## Role of CCIL in Forex Settlement

*   The Clearing Corporation of India Ltd. (CCIL) plays a crucial role in the Indian forex market.
*   **Introduction:** CCIL was set up in April 2001 for providing exclusive clearing and settlement for transactions in Money Market, Government Securities (G-Sec), and Foreign Exchange markets. Commenced operations on February 15, 2002.
*   **Forex Settlement:** CCIL commenced settlement of forex transactions from November 2002, initially covering inter-bank USD/INR spot and forward trades. From February 2004, Cash (T+0) and Tom (T+1) trades were also included for guaranteed settlement.
*   **Guaranteed Settlement:** CCIL guarantees settlement by becoming the central counterparty (CCP) to trades through novation.
*   **Cross-Currency Deals:** CCIL started the settlement of cross-currency deals through the CLS (Continuous Linked Settlement) Bank from April 6, 2005, further reducing settlement risk for eligible currencies.
*   **Trading Platform:** CCIL developed a forex trading platform "FX-CLEAR" which went live on August 7, 2003.

**Benefits of CCIL Forex Settlement:**

*   Mitigation of counterparty credit risk through CCP guarantee.
*   Operational efficiency and standardization.
*   Multilateral netting, reducing liquidity requirements.
*   Risk management framework.
*   The large value and volume of transactions settled by CCIL is testament to the value of the benefits provided.

# Securities Settlement

**Introduction:**

*   Securities settlement is the process of completing a transaction involving the sale or purchase of financial securities (like stocks, bonds, government securities).
*   It involves the transfer of securities from the seller to the buyer and the corresponding transfer of funds from the buyer to the seller.
*   **Example:** If you sell 50 shares, settlement ensures the buyer pays you, and ownership of the 50 shares is transferred to the buyer.

**Securities Settlement in India (Focus on Government Securities):**

*   Transactions relating to government securities are settled through member's SGL (Subsidiary General Ledger) accounts / current accounts maintained with the RBI.
*   Settlement of funds typically occurs on a net basis.
*   Settlement of securities occurs on a gross, trade-by-trade basis.

## Role of CCIL in Securities Settlement

*   CCIL acts as a central counterparty (CCP) for trades in Government Securities (including Treasury Bills) and other debt instruments.
*   **Novation:** CCIL guarantees settlement of trades on the settlement date by becoming the central counterparty to every trade through the process of novation (i.e., CCIL becomes the buyer to every seller and the seller to every buyer).
*   **Risk Management:** This mitigates counterparty risk for participants.
*   **Platforms:** CCIL developed and manages the NDS-OM (Negotiated Dealing System - Order Matching) and NDS-CALL electronic trading platforms for G-Secs and Call Money market respectively. It also developed the NDS-Auction module for RBI's Treasury Bill auctions.
*   **CBLO:** CCIL launched the Collateralised Borrowing and Lending Obligation (CBLO) in January 2003, a money market instrument collateralized by G-Secs, which it also clears and settles.

**Benefits of CCIL Securities Settlement:**

*   The key benefits for participants include:

| Benefit                                                | Description                                                              |
| :----------------------------------------------------- | :----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Counterparty Risk Mitigation**                       | Guaranteed settlement through CCP mechanism (Novation).                  |
| **Operational Efficiency**                             | Standardized processes, multilateral netting of funds.                   |
| **Liquidity Management / Efficient funds management**  | Net funds settlement reduces liquidity needs for participants.           |
| **Facilitates Market Mechanisms**                      | Supports features like short sale of securities and rollover of repo trades. |
