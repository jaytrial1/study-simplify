Overview of Payment Systems

# Global Payment Systems Landscape

The global payments landscape has been undergoing constant transformation ever since the first credit cards and ATMs came into existence in the 1950s and 1960s. While the pace of change was consistent in the latter half of the 20th century, there has been exponential growth in payments systems globally since the turn of the 21st century.

Traditionally, payments were seen as utility products managed by Central Banks, Commercial Banks, and a few global card schemes. However, advancements in technology, changing consumer expectations, urbanization, financial inclusion, and local considerations have sparked a revolution in global payments.

In the past, payments were cheque based, and therefore the cheques were physically (after receipt from payee) exchanged between the drawee bank and payee bank (the organised structure being through the clearing house), cheque getting debited to drawer's account (in his bank - called as drawee bank), suitable credit provided to drawee bank/branch through accounts of both of these banks mentioned with a common authority (central bank, clearing bank, or clearing house or similar). This is **Debit Clearing**.

Electronic conversion of cheque data, exchange of these electronic cheque electronic posting for debit to issuer, credit to payee, and thereafter bank-to-bank settlement happens electronically these days. Clearing initiated by presenting debit instruments to the payer (drawer of cheque) to his bank where his account is mentioned, is common in India. But Europe and US have more of outgoing credit advise or payment authorisation presented by the paying entity (i.e., the same accountholder drawer of the cheque in our scenario) to his bank, acting as the - the major initiation factor for clearing. Again, very often this payment authorisation is written by the payer electronically, obviating the cheque; the drawee bank effects a remittance based on that through the clearing house. This is **Credit Clearing**.

Other big and active payment systems also mostly have similar basic formats, apart from additional tiers for country-specific situations.

# Domestic Payment Systems Landscape (India)

The Reserve Bank of India, after establishing the Board for Payment and Settlement Systems in 2005, outlined a vision for creating an umbrella institution to oversee all retail payment systems in the country. The Payment and Settlement Act was passed in 2007, leading to the formation of the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI) in December 2008 as a non-profit company. Initially focusing on the National Financial Switch (NFS), NPCI gradually took control of various payment systems to unify and standardize business processes across the nation.

RBI's Vision 2012-2015 aimed to proactively encourage electronic payment systems, fostering a less-cash society in India. Traditionally a cash-based economy, RBI's initiatives over the past decade have modernized electronic payment systems. While cheques remain significant, their share in retail payments has reduced, and electronic payments have seen an uptick.

Cash still dominates as the primary payment mode in India, with a high currency-to-GDP ratio and a low number of non-cash transactions per citizen compared to other emerging economies. Despite substantial growth in debit, credit, and prepaid instruments, the acceptance infrastructure for electronic payments, including ATMs and Point of Sale (POS) terminals, lags behind. The POS infrastructure, with around 4.25 million terminals concentrated in metros and Tier 1 cities, is insufficient for a less-cash society. Innovations like micro ATMs and mobile POS offer potential solutions and require support from various stakeholders to expand the acceptance network in India.
