Risk Management and Frauds in Mobile Banking

# Types of Risk

1.  **Security Risks:** Vulnerabilities to hacking, data breaches, unauthorized access.
2.  **Operational Risks:** System failures, downtime, errors in transaction processing.
3.  **Fraud Risks:** Financial losses due to deceptive activities.

# Risk Management Process

1.  **Identification:** Recognizing potential risks associated with mobile banking.
2.  **Assessment:** Evaluating the likelihood and impact of identified risks.
3.  **Mitigation:** Implementing controls and measures to reduce risks (e.g., encryption, firewalls).
4.  **Monitoring:** Continuously observing the mobile banking environment for new threats and vulnerabilities.

# Types of Fraud

1.  **Phishing:** Deceptive attempts (emails, websites) to obtain sensitive information like usernames, passwords, and credit card details.
2.  **Malware Attacks:** Malicious software designed to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to a device or network. Banks and financial institutions are extremely vulnerable.
3.  **Identity Theft:** Stealing personal information to impersonate someone for financial gain.
4.  **Smishing (SMS Phishing):** Using deceptive text messages to lure victims into revealing personal information or clicking malicious links.
5.  **Insider Threat:** A current or a former employee responsible for a security breach.
6.  **Poor Defense:** Merely spending on cybersecurity products without a comprehensive strategy.

# Preventing Mobile Banking Frauds

1.  **Strong Authentication:** Multi-factor authentication (MFA), complex passwords, biometrics.
2.  **Regular Updates:** Keeping the mobile banking app and device operating system updated.
3.  **Awareness & Education:** Informing customers about common fraud tactics and safe practices.
4.  **Report Suspicious Activity:** Encouraging users to immediately report any unusual activity.

# Security Measures

-   Mobile banking, either by mobile banking apps, internet banking, USSD or SMS, is protected.
-   If you use the bank's website or mobile app to transfer funds, etc, there are protections like firewalls and SSL encryption that make sure your data is safe and cannot be used by anyone else.
-   Use a safe network connection (avoid public Wi-Fi for sensitive transactions).
-   Make sure you have anti-virus software installed in your device for an added layer of protection.
-   Subscribe for push message notification: If there are any unsuccessful login attempts to your internet banking account, your bank will send you a notification. In such cases, you can contact your bank and get your account secured.
-   Never click on URLs received in an SMS or promotional email.
