# INTRODUCTION
One of the important schools of management thought is known as 'Human Relations" approach or "Behavioural" Approach. This school has been developed by Elton Mayo, Rensis Likert, Abraham Maslow, Frederick Herzberg, Chris Argyris, Hugo Munsterbery, Douglas McGregor and Mary Parker Follett. According to them, human being and his behaviour are at the centre of whatever management does. Organisation behaviour is affected by informal groups formed in the formal organisation structure, the actions and reactions among them, decision making, leadership, motivation, individual goals and values etc. The efficiency or inefficiency of organisation depends on them. Nature of an individual is a very important determinant of his behaviour.

The way in which managers deal with their subordinates in order to achieve the objectives of the organisation depends primarily on their attitudes. If they treat their employees as those who do not like to work and are dishonest, then they will deal with them strictly. There are two such approaches of management. One is called the 'traditional' approach and the other is the 'modern' approach. The old approach looks at the workers with suspicion. It believes that man is lazy by nature and does not like to work. Hence, strict supervision and control are needed to get work done through, them. The modern belief is quite the opposite. It says that an average human being does not dislike work. He likes to undertake responsibility, but it requires favourable environment. If

he does not work, it is due to the wrong policies of management in respect of motivation.
