# THEORY Y
McGregor's approach about human behaviour is called theory Y. The main assumptions of $Y$ theory are as follows :
(i) Work is as natural as play or rest and work may be a source of satisfaction to him.
(ii) External control and threat of punishment are not the only. means for bringing about their best efforts. Man will exercise selfdirection and self-control in the service of objective, to which he is committed.
(iii) By efforts directed towards achieving organisational objective, the employees get satisfaction of ego and self-actualisation needs. This is their reward.
(iv) Under proper conditions, the average human being learns not only to accept but to seek responsibility. Avoidance of responsibility, lack of ambition and emphasis on security are generally the result of experience and are not inherent in human characteristics.
(v) The capacity to exercise a relatively high degree of imagination, ingenuity and creativity in the solution of organisation problems is widely, and not narrowly, distributed in the population.
(vi) Under the conditions of modern industrial life, the intelligence of the average human being is only partially utilised.

It is found, however, that majority of executives in work organisations practice traditional philosophy of management. They continue to deny their people true opportunities for assuming responsibilities and for growing in the organisation. It is reasonable to assume that as younger generations of managers enter the business world, they as younger generations of managers enter the business world, they will apply modern concepts of management. As McGregor remarks, 'Change in the direction of theory Y will be slow and it will require extensive modification of the attitudes of management and workers alike.' In this context, McGregor recommends a number of innovative ideas which are consistent with theory $Y$ and are being practised in American industry.

Policles and procedures adopted by Management : Gradually, the policies of Theory $Y$ are being adopted by management. These policies and procedures may be stated as follows :

(a) Decentralisation and delegation : Decentralisation consists in delegation of authorities to heads of departments and sections to take decisions: For this purpose, delegation of authority and responsibility is necessary. This will satisfy his ego needs and he will fulfil the responsibility given to him as best as he could.
(b) Job enlargement : The jobs should be so enlarged that they pose a challenge. The employees are prepared to face this challenge and thus, their social needs and ego needs are satisfied.
(c) Participation in management : Participation in management and consultation with the subordinates before taking decision would provide an opportunity to develop creativity and satisfy the sense of selfrealisation.
(d) Self evaluation : This consists of target setting for performance in consultation with the employee concerned. He will be asked to evaluate his own performance periodically. The supervisor has to play the role of a friendly guide, not that of a judge or inspector.

In his subsequent writing, McGregor has offered further clarifications. They are as follows :
(a) Theory $X$ and $Y$ are not the only two possible theories of management. They are just two examples among many theories of management. (b) The two theories are not managerial strategies. They are underlying beliefs about the nature of man that influence managers to adopt one strategy rather than another.
