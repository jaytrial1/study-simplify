# THEORY X

(1) Meaning : The old and traditional approach of management is that a worker does not like to work. Hence, they must be dealt with very strictly. McGregor called this approach as Theory X, which emphasises the negative aspect of employee's behaviour.
(2) Assumptions of Theory $X$ : The traditional belief which, McGregor calls theory X is based on the following assumptions :
(i) The average man is by nature indolent. He works as little as possible and will avoid it, if he can.
(ii) Because of this human characteristic of dislike of work, most people must be coerced, controlled, directed, threatened with punishment, to get them to put forth adequate effort towards the achievement of organisational objectives.
(iii) The average human being prefers to be directed, wishes to avoid responsibility, has relatively little ambition and wants security above all.
(3) Policy Employed by Management : These three points suggest the worker's characteristics. It also believed what management must do to get work done. According to theory X, only management can get the objectives of organisation achieved. High degrees of control and threats of punishment are the ways to manage such people. They believed that :

(ii) Without active intervention by the management, people would be passive to organisational needs. Therefore, they must be persuaded and controlled, rewarded and punished.
(iii) As an average human being is lazy, the management must fix up standard task, i. e. the quantity of work that an average worker should produce. Besides, strict control and supervision is necessary.
(iv) As he dislikes work, no amount of monetary incentive will motivate him. He has to be threatened of dismissal to get sufficient efforts.
(v) As an average human being has no ambition and likes to be directed, the system of method analysis has been developed. This will provide minute details about work methods and give him directions.

McGregor is of the opinion that assumptions about human nature under Theory X are not true. The behaviour of employees as assumed by Theory $X$ is due to environment. It is the responsibility of management to provide proper environment. Then employees would work enthusiastically. They would be prepared to take responsibility. Their needs must be satisfied to create such atmosphere.

In this connection McGregor has used Maslow's theory which is popularly known as the need theory of motivation. Maslow holds that man is a wanting animal with a hierarchy of needs. The human needs are divided into three groups.
(1) Primary Needs : Every employee has to satisfy his primary reeds of food, clothing and shelter. If he gets enough salary, he need not worry about these needs. Then he thinks of satisfying the higher reeds. But only after his primary needs are satisfied.
(2) Social Needs : A person's social needs include his status and group relations. Every human being aspires to get good position and status. This need can be satisfied by promoting employees in due course. Promotion should include higher position, salaries, and some other benefits also.
(3) Egoistic Needs : Every man wants to satisfy his ego; whether in his family, society or in business where he works. These needs are satisfied if responsibility' is given to him and if he is 'invited' to suggestions.

The main needs of man, according to Maslow are five : physiological needs, safety needs, social needs, esteem needs and selfrealisation needs.
(1) As examples of physiological needs, we may refer to food, drink, shelter, clothing, medical care etc. When his primary needs are not satisfied, his only need is to satisfy them. So far as a man his hungry, food is everything for him in the world. Man lives by bread alone, when there is no bread.
(2) The strange thing about human nature is that once his primary needs are satisfied, he feels that his higher needs are more important. He forgets that once he was suffering from hunger. A man is stimulated to work hard not due to his satisfied need, but because of those needs which are unsatisfied. Once his physiological needs are satisfied, his safety needs become important for him. They are concerned with security. Now, he.seeks job security, security against disease, age etc. Thus, they include both financial security and security against physical threat. These needs are satisfied by taking out insurance, provident fund. gratuity etc.
(3) Social needs include love, affection, sense of belonging to a group, etc. He desires to be loved by others, to be associated with others. He wants to be a member of a group, to be loved by the group. If his social needs of love and affection are not satisfied, he will become antagonistic and uncooperative.
(4) After satisfaction of social needs, he likes to satisfy his esteem needs or ego needs: He wants that he should be respected by others. Hence he wants status, prestige, appreciation for work etc. If these needs are not satisfied, he feels inferiority, helplessness etc.
(5) The final need is need for self-actualisation. These needs are for self-development. The man wants to achieve the highest position in which he has acquired skill, e.g. a painter would wish to draw best of pictures and get recognition as an outstanding painter in the society. Everyone wants to work in a cohesive group, to develop a sense of belonging, to win love and affection from others. The ego needs are reflecting in our desire for status and recognition and respect in the work group. The self-realisation or self-fulfilment or self-actualisation needs are those which, when satisfied, make the employee give up dependence on others. He becomes self-directed and creative. In an industrial organisation creativity of a man in producing new articles, in raising full satisfaction seldom occurs in their case.
