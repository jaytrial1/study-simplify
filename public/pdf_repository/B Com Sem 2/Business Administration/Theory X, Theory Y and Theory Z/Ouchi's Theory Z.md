# OUCHI'S THEORY Z

William Ouchi presented theory Z which explains what is Japanese managerial technique and how it can be utilised in the U.S.A. and other countries. Ouchi's theory Z has been widely utilised in industrial enterprises of America and Japan to motivate the human labour. In it comparative style of Japanese and American managerial system has been explained and recommendations have been made to utilise Japanese methods or techniques in America.

Ouchi mentioned four components or constituents for the construction of theory Z.
(A) Strong bond between organisation and employees.
(B) Employee involvement.
(C) No formal structure.

(D) Leader's role for co-ordination of human beings. and not as a technical factor.
(1) For a strong bond between organisation and employees Ouchi mentioned certain techniques. In these techniques life time employment is the main technique and it has been widely utilised by Japanese enterprise. This type of stability can be achieved through highly motivated work environment, work challenges and partnership in decision making.
(2) This principle's main factor is the employee's complete work involvement. This type of involvement can be achieved when they are given meaningful partnership. If any decision is related with the interest of employees, then they should be definitely made partner in the decision. If the situation is such that the management wants to take independent decisions individually, they can do so; but the employees should be informed speedily regarding such type of decisions, so that they can not feel that they are not considered or they are put into darkness.
(3) The third important factor of theory $Z$ is no formal structure. This theory gives importance to group work instead of formal structure. Group work is possible through distribution of information, distribution of research, joint plan formulation and feeling of complete co-operation. Achievement can be attained. For the simple understanding of this theory Ouchi gives the illustration of basket ball play. In the basket ball play. players can achieve success by doing their work without important position, work or interrelations. Ouchi argues that on work place employees should not be fixed constantly on a particular work but they should constantly transferred from one place to another. This type of transfer, every employee gets an idea about which type of effects are created on other works and individuals due to his work. Hence, group feeling will be created and it will lead the unit towards success.
(4) Lastly this theory puts stress on co-ordination of human beings. The role of leader is to integrate employees and not to achieve high productivity through technical pressure. This process will develop proper ability, new structure, incentives and new managerial styles. This constituent's basic intention is to increase employees' work involvement. to decrease self-centredness and to develop the feeling of co-operation more and more.

Ouchi's theory Z is quiet different from all the traditional concepts of motivation. With reference to India it is difficult to implement this theory because its first condition of life time employment is difficult in India. Even though Maruti Industry has implemented it up to certain extent, there is one canteen for every individual, in which all the employees and officers can take their food by sitting jointly. Secondly in India the concept of common culture and of classless employee group is not possible. In India one worker is from South India and another might be from North India. Everyone's habits, methods of living, the life-styles are different. Indian caste system is also an obstruction. Thirdly, in India it is not possible not to discharge employees, even though shareholders receive less profit or they bear some loss, because there is no effect of the concept of social responsibility. Fourthly, opposition of the concept of formal organisation is also not acceptable. In small units it might be possible, but in big companies, management is not possible without establishment of formal organisation.
