# CHARACTERISTICS OF CONTROL
Following characteristics of control can be derived from the above definitions :
(1) An End Function of Management : It is necessary that other functions of management precede controlling function. Control is a followup to the other functions of management. It begins only after a plan is formulated, an organisation structure is set up and employees are directed to do the work as planned. The main purpose of control is to ensure that actual work is proceeding in accordance with the plan.
(2) A Continuous Activity : Like planning, control is a continuous process. The process of controlling all employees, all activities and all things goes on every moment. In the words of Koontz and O'Donnell "the navigator continually takes readings to ascertain, where he is relative to a planned course, so should the business manager continually take reading to assure himself that his enterprise or department is on course:" Thus, like a radar controlling a plane, control is working non-stop.
(3) It is Exercised at all Leveis : Control is all pervasive in a business enterprise. Control is exercised at all levels of management. Control is not

a specific activity of a particular department only. Control is exercised to a more or less extent by all those superiors in the organisation who are required to get work done through subordinates. For instance, managing director controls the whole unit; a departmental head controls the activities of his department, the sectional head controls the activities of his section. Though, the scope of control differs for different authorities, there is no doubt that controlling is done at all levels of management.
(4) Dynamic Process : Control is a dynamic process. It is not rigid. It is flexible. It changes with the change in situation. It changes with the change in plans also. Billy Goetz writes. 'A complex programme of the managerial plans may fail in some particulars. The control system should report such failures and should contain sufficient elements of flexibility to maintain managerial control of operations despite such failures'.
(5) Constructive Activity : It is not true to say that freedom of managers or employees is curtailed because of control. In fact, control does not aim at interfering in anybody's activity, rather its aim is to lead the employees to a right path if they are going in wrong direction. In this sense, control is a constructive process. No one is allowed to exercise control according to his whims. Instead he has to evaluate the performance objectively on the basis of facts and figures. In this context, Mrs. Mary Follett has rightly remarked that a new conception of control is being evolved in modern business.
(6) Control is Forward Looking : Controlling is a future-oriented activity. Management has no power to change the past. Of course, they do review and assess the past events so that its benefits may be available in future. In fact, the main aim of controlling is to ensure that the mistakes of the past may not be repeated in future. Even if there is no deviation between actual performance and standards, controlling is necessary to improve upon the standards of performance. This again suggests that controlling is based on planning. Control is not possible without planning.
(7) Control is People-oriented : Control is exercised over people and through people. Of course, control is related to materials, process or even finance. But ultimately, some human being is responsible for anything going wrong with materials or finance. Hence, everything is controlled through people.
(8) Related to Planning : As a managerial function, control is closely linked with planning. Controlling is concerned with observing actual

performance in the light of planned performance. This implies that planning is a precondition of control.
(9) Control can be informal also : Generally, a definite system of control is implemented on the basis of the nature of organisation. This is a formal system of controlling. But informal control can give better results sometimes.
(10) Internal Activity : Controlling is confined to internal factors of the unit only. Obviously, management can have authority on the internal factors only. They do not have any command over the external factors, like govemment policy, fashions, customers' preferences, cyclical changes in business etc.
