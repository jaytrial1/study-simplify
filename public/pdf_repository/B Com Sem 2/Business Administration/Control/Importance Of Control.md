# IMPORTANCE OF CONTROL
Control is one of the most important functions of management. It has for its objectives pointing out weaknesses and errors. in order to rectify them and prevent their recurrence. Planning seeks to set goals, while control seeks to compel events to conform to plans. No business can exist without some form of planning and control. Control is necessary for the plan adopted to achieve the goals of business. Control is essential to see whether the executives and subordinates do their work efficiently.

It consists in finding out causes of deviation from standards and taking corrective actions. Decentralisation at the top level management is made possible due to control. Control also helps in achieving co-ordination. Everything in the enterprise-things, activities, functions, process and employees become effective on account of control. It is a tool of measuring the over-all efficiency of management.

Following points shed more light on the importance of control :
(1) Control helps in the attainment of goals : The plan is framed keeping in view the objectives of business enterprise. Other functions are also arranged according to this plan. Then these functions are brought under control. Thus, control helps in moving the enterprise towards its set goals.
(2) Control is the basis of planning : Planning cannot take concrete shape without control. Planning, however sound it may be, is meaningless without control. Whether the performance of the unit is in conformity with

the plan or not that can be judged through control only. Planning cannot proceed without control. As Mrs. Mary Cushing Niles observed, "whereas planning sets the course, control observes deviations from the course; and initiates action to return to the chosen course or to an appropriately changed one."
(3) Controlling is useful to organising also : Organisation structure is prepared keeping in view the objectives of the enterprise. In an organisation, interrelations are established by assigning authority, duties and responsibilities to definite individuals. If there is any error or defect in this, it will be brought to light through control activity. Thus, control can prevent maladjustments in the organisational set up. Hence, control is necessary on such organisational factors as delegation of authority and assignment of responsibilities.
(4) Directing made effective through control : Once planning and organising are completed, directing employees become necessary. Control helps directing by suggesting where, when and to whom direction or guidance is necessary.
(5) Control helps co-ordination : To realise the goals of business, it is necessary that all functions of management are well-co-ordinated. Control shows where there is lack of co-ordination among departments, functions of individuals. Koontz and O'Donnell believe that control is inevitable for the achievement and maintenance of co-ordination.
(6) Control is necessary for the delegation of authority : Delegation of authority is an important and complex problem is organising. If delegation of authority is a key to efficient management, controlling is a key to efficient organisation. Controlling is necessary to strike a balance between authority and responsibility.
(7) Control helps In removing mistakes, defects etc. : Control is exercised on all important aspects of a business unit such as policy, procedures, organisation, personnel, quality, production and budget. Errors and defects in any of these fields are detected and rectified through control. Unless, through control, performance is evaluated, errors and defects cannot be detected.
