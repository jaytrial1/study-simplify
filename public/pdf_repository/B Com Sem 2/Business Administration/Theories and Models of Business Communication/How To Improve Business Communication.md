# HOW TO IMPROVE BUSINESS COMMUNICATION
## (A) Improvement in Upward Communication.
(1) Lines of upward communication should be properly studied and then established. Listening to grievances is just half the job done; Managers must take concrete action to solve them as well.
(2) Similarly, suggestions for improvement, if found good enough, should be implemented else the junior staff will get the impression that the complaint-cum-suggetion boxes are just dummies.

(3) In order to enable the workers to overcome the awe of authority, the managers should encourage them to communicate freely. Workers will rarely take the initiative to talk; so if any initiative for constructive communication is to be taken, it has to be by the managers.
Keith Davis correctly points out,
"The way the open door can be most effective is for managers to walk through it and get among their people. The open door is for managers to walk through, not employees."
(4) Distortion by 'editing' can be avoided if the lines of communication are kept as short as possible.
## (B) Improvement in Downward Communication :
(A) Improvement
(1) If the managers are themselves well informed about the procedures, targets, work methodology and the nature of the job, they can pass on information to the juniors in a more effective manner. So, managers should keep themselves abreast of all information.
(2) Every organisation must have a distinet communication plan and managers must stick to it. What information, how much and why it is to be communicated should be decided upon beforehand. Looppholes and gaps in the information network must be anticipated and filled up before the information starts flowitg downward.
(3) The jurisdiction or area under a particular manager should be clearly defined so that for a local problem immediate decisions are taken and instructions are issued. Thus, over-concentration of authority at the top level can be avoided, and lines of communication will be shortened. Downward flow will gain momentum, delays will be eliminated and the loss of information and possibility of distortion will be minimised.
(4) While transmitting information downward, proper hierarchy must be maintained. Bypassing a superior person and communicating with a junior staff directly will create future problems.
