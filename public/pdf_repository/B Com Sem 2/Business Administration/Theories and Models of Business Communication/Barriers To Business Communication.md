# BARRIERS TO BUSINESS COMMUNICATION :

We have understood the process of Communication and its forms by now. On the face of it, communication appears to be fairly simple; but in spite of its seeming simplicity, every attempt at communication is not successful. There are several factors that can prevent the message from reaching the intended recipient or from having the desired effect on the recipient.

At times there may be some fault in the communication system which may prevent the message from reaching. Some of these defects are in the mechanical devices used for transmitting, that is, the medium. Some are in the tools we use for communicating, that is, language or other symbols used for encoding. Some are in the nature of the persons who are engaged in communication, that is the sender and the recipient.

In other words, barriers to communication can be of many kinds. As members in the Communication Process, we must learn to identify these barriers and also take steps to overcome them. If the barriers continue to flourish, the entire process will lose its significance and communication will yield no results at all.

## PHYSICAL BARRIERS 

### (i) Noise
Noise is the most common barrier to effective communication. At the workplace, oral communication becomes very difficult if there is shouting or loud conversation is being carried on. In factories, the sound of machines creates a hurdle to communication. Electronic noise like blaring often interferes in communication by telephone or the loudspeaker system. Companies, therefore, resort to making sound proof rooms by installing air conditioners and double window panes. Noise creates irritation to both - the speaker and the listener. Incomplete information may be transmitted and as a result, the feedback may be distorted or may not be received at all.

### (ii) Time and Distance
Time and distance act as barriers to the smooth flow of communication. Modern communication equipments like telephones, mobile phones, internet and teleconferencing have reduced the distance, yet breakdowns in these devices create barriers in communication. Physical distance between the Sender and the Receiver delays the message to be communicated. If the message is delayed, the feedback too is delayed or not obtained at all. Similarly, time too becomes a barrier. If a particular message passes through lot of via medium, a lot of time is wasted till it reaches the last recipient. Thus, a communication gap is created. Most of the factories run in shifts. There is a kind of communication gap between persons working in different shifts. This may at times even affect the work atmosphere, as workers of different shifts may not have friendly feelings towards each other.

### (iii) Defects in the Medium
We all know that for carrying out any kind of communication, a channel or a medium is needed. Talking about mechanical equipments for communication one can immediately think of a telephone, a public address system, fax, computer, e-mail or Internet, the postal system or the courier service. Defects in any of these can affect the communication system very negatively. Let us take a look how defects in these mechanical equipments can create barriers.

(a) Telephone : It is the most common and most frequently used device for communication. Poor quality of the telephone instrument or poor quality of available network can both hamper communication. Figures, names, data or any other vital information can be misunderstood or lost completely. Such a faulty equipment can become a dangerous tool for communication in emergency situations.

(b) Public-address system : The public-address system or a microphone is used in all formal meetings and large gatherings. Any defects in it will not only keep the communication incomplete but will also cause irritation, unnecessary delay, confusion and chaos. Now-a-days, the quality of such systems has vastly improved, yet defects can occur. These eupments run on electricity, so in case electricity fails, the equipment will not function properly. Railway stations, Airports and Bus depots use this system; defects therein will create obstacles in the communication process. A partial failure in such an equipment is more dangerous than a complete failure, because a partial failure carries an incomplete or distorted message, which might cause a wrong action to be taken.
The only way to overcome this barrier is to install a high quality equipment; and in case of defects, use an alternative system. At times written hoardings or placards are placed at public places to communicate when the public-address system fails.

(c) Fax : The fax machine is extremely useful for sending important documents instantly. But they too work on telephone lines or similar systems. Defects in the fax machine or the network provided can lead to an obstacle.

Though a fax machine is easy to handle, it should be used only by those who are familiar with it.

An alternative to the fax machine is now available: the scaning machine. But that too depends on internet availabilitywhile sending the scanned document. Hence, defects in such an equipment can become a barrier.

(d) Computers : The computer is a variable electronic instrument which can combine various media functions. The word processor helps in the preparation of letters, reports, meeting agenda and such documents. Programmes like PowerPoint guides through outlines of presentations. Spread sheets, tables, charts and other graphics can also be quickly prepared on the computer.

However, defects can be found in these equipments as well. All software programmes may not be compatible with every computer or laptop. There can be problems with Operating Systems, MS versions and editions, Random-access memory. Fonts, symbols and even command. It is possible that a word document prepared in MS Office 2007 can not be opened in another computer pre-installed with older version of MS Office.

Virus attacks on Computer files and documents are well known, and they can destroy the collected data completely. Hence, it is important to install anti-virus systems and take on back-ups of all important documents. Plus, it is a wise thing to carry one's own laptop or Compact Disc before giving a presentation.

(e) E-mail/Internet : The Internet has truly revolutionised our communication scenario. E-mail is the fastest means of conveying even complex information. But once again internet connectivity can become a major issue. Unless and until the recipient has a very advanced cell phone or tablet, he/she cannot be alerted about having received mails in the inbox. Thus, e-mail aleits if missed by the recipient, or if he/she is not a regular viewer of the inbox, then again information will not produce the desired result. E-mail hacking is another threat that can create hurdles in transmission of messages.
(f) Postal Services/Courier Services : Postal services in our country are in a good shape, yet they are largely outdated and inefficient. Vital documents, contracts, letters etc. run the risk of getting misplaced or completely lost. Moreover, they take a lot of time for delivery as well. If speedy deliveries are wanted, the cost becomes to high. So postal

services are largely used for routine, day-to-day documents only. Courier service can be described a shade better than the postal services, but they are decidedly costlier and they too take at least a day for delivery. Hence, in case of a situation where instant communication has to take place, both postal services and courier services fail to come up to the mark.

## SEMANTIC BARRIERS 

### (i) Misinterpretation of Words :
Language is our most important and powerful tool of communication. It is a tricky tool which requires skilful handling. Most of our communication takes place through words, spoken or written. But words can and do have multiple meanings attached, which may become very confusing. It is quite possible that the receiver of a message does not interpret the same meaning of a word used by the sender. Refer to any standard dictionary and you will find different meanings for common words we use in our everyday conversation. For example :

Similarly, words like "minute" and "wind" are pronounced in two different ways to give two entirely different meanings. "Minute" pronounced as refers to time; whereas "minute" pronounced as (4u6-42) refers to something small or tiny. Similarly, "wind," pronounced as means the air; whereas "wind," pronounced as (4u6-4) means to turn or twist in various ways. It will be very surprising to learn that the common and simple word "run" has 71 meanings as just a verb, 35 meanings as noun, and 4 as an adjective ! So the word 'run' when used in a sentence can have 100 different meanings and interpretations !

Similar sounding words like; "flower" and "flour"; "site" and "sight"; "birth" and "berth" can cause misunderstanding in speech. Even adjectives and adverbs like "far"; "fast"; "few"; "early"; "easy" do not convey definite or exact proportions in 'quality or quantity; so their meanings can be misinterpreted. A concrete noun like "table" may suggest a writing table or a dining table or a statistical table to different persons; likewise "chair"

could be a seat to sit upon, or a position to occupy.
Thus, we generally attach meanings to words as per our emotional and cultural attitudes and past experiences. So they can become a hurdle to communication. Interpretation of words also largely depends on social, economic, cultural and educational backgrounds.

### (ii) Technical Jargon :
Technical Jargon/terms can be a barrier to communication. Such terms are limited to the group of persons who work together, or work in the same kind of occupation; they need to use technical terms in their work. Often such words have other meanings in the day-to-day spoken language and are differently understood by people who do not belong to that occupational group. For an example, words used at the Stock Exchange have a different meaning as compared to a layman's understanding. A "Bull" or "Bear" do not mean the animals per se, but the brokers and the type of trading they indulge in. Similarly, words like "Forward Trading", "Hedging", "Primary or Secondary Market" are all technical but commonly used worts at the Stock Exchange. However, for someone who is not associated or familiar with the Stock Exchange, to understand such words will become a problem.

### (iii) Idioms and Phrases :
Idioms \& Phrases are not frequently used by people. The use of them definitely enriches the language but it also confuses the reades if they are not well versed. For an example, 'an apple of someone's eyes', 'born with silver spoon', 'to pull socks' are such idioms \& phrase which cant be understood by interpreting each word separately. A teaches says, 'you have to burn midnight oil to get first class' and the student plans to light the lamps while studying at night. This is how an idiom becomes a barrier. He failed to understand that 'burning midnight oil' means 'working hard. Thus, one should try to avoid using idioms \& phrases in regular communication, what if it goes inunanderstood?

## Personal Barriers : 

### (i) Status Block
Managers at times become very conscious of their status owing to their position. They, therefore, find it difficult to recéive favourably any

suggestions from their subordinates. People in senior positions often develop the feeling that they know everything about how to handle the business. They may, therefore, not agree to good ideas coming from the juniors. Many good ideas are thus wasted only because they come from junior employees, who are considered to be too young and inexperienced. The ideas of workers are most likely to go unheard because of the social distance between them and the managers. Social distance sometimes makes workers too shy, nervous or frightened of their senior bosses. This frequently becomes a difficult barrier to overcome.

Consciousness of one's status proves to be a very serious barrier in face-to-face communication. The subordinate feels jittery and nervous. fidgets about where he is standing, falters in his speech and fails in communicating what exactly he wanted to say. The officer, on the other hand, reveals impatience and starts giving comments or advice even before he has fully heard his subordinate. As a result, there is total failure of communication; the subordinate returns to his seat dissatisfied and angry, while the manager continues his work with the feeling that his employees have no consideration for the value of his time and keep on pestering him for nothing.

### (ii) State of Health
Physical condition affects the efficiency in all forms of communication. If one is in pain or any other type of physical discomfort, there is no inclination to engage in communication at all. Poor state of health reduces communicating ability because the mind is not sufficiently alert; there may occur gaps in attention while reading or listening; there may even be a lack of energy to think clearly and to find the right words. Perception is low when the state of health is poor. Emotions, which play an important part in successful communication, are easily disturbed. Poor health also makes both the sender and the receiver irritable and angry, which further results in misunderstanding and wrong communication. Poor state of health constantly bothers the sender and receiver, thus hampering communication. (iii) Group Identification

Each one of us belongs to a number of groups - family, relatives, neighbours, area, religion, language group, age group, nationality, economic

group, professional group and so on. Our values and opinions are influenced by various groups we belong to. In fact many of our ideas, attitudes and values are picked up from such groups.

When we begin to identify ourselves with a particular group, our obvious tendency is to reject any idea that goes against the interests of the group. For an example, very often parents and children do not agree on certain topics because both belong to different age groups. Similarly, men and women disagree because of different gender ideas; employees and the management cannot come to an agreement because the interests are different. It is difficult for persons of one group to understand how persons of another group think and feel. This becomes a barrier to communication.

### (iv) Poor Communication Skills
Lack of skill in writing and speaking prevents a person from encoding a message correctly. Oral communication can be handicapped by a number of problems : nervousness in facing an audience, fear of the receiver, little or no confidence about the matter to be communicated, discomfort with the surroundings and so on. Even excitement about an achievement or a new idea may create a barrier in understanding a person's speech. Haphazard tabling of ideas, non-chronological presentation of points, leaving out relevant details, giving importance to unnecessary information are all indicative of poor writing skills.

Poor reading and listening skills are more important requirements for successful communication. But in business training schools very little attention is paid to the development of these skills. Poor reading habits and faulty listening are psychological shortcomings and careful training is needed to overcome them.
