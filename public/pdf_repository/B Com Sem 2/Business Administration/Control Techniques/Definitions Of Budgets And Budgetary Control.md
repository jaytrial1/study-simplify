# DEFINITIONS OF BUDGETS AND BUDGETARY CONTROL
A budget is a forecast of future activities. It is a chart of future incomes and expenses. It is a co-ordinating financial plan for a business enterprise. It includes estimates of sales, production, purchases, labour cost, overheads and financial position.

(1) According to Shubin, "A budget is a comprehensive over-all plan in which management, on the basis of estimated sales volume and receipts, establishes cost and expense allowances for future operations, in this way effectively integrating and directing activities towards carefully determined goals."
(2) Institute of Cost and Management Accountants, England has given following definition of a budget :
"A Budget is a financial and/or quantitative statement, prepared and approved prior to a defined period of time, of the policy to be pursued during that period for the purpose of attaining objective. It may include income, expenditure and employment of capital."
(3) George R. Terry, has described the budget, "as an estimate of future needs, arranged according to an orderly basis, covering some or all of the activities of an enterprise for a definite period of time."
(4) According to Cecil Gillespie, "Budget is a plan of operation integrated and coordinated, comprising all phases of business activities and summarised to show, the financial results of carrying out the plans."

The above definitions make it clear that budgets are prepared to achieve objectives and targets of business. It is prepared for a definite period of future time. It expresses targets either in quantitative terms or in financial terms or in both.

Budgetary control is defined by various authorities as follows :
(1) According to the English Institute, "Budgetary control is the establishment of budgets relating to the responsibilities of executives to the requirements of a policy and the continuous comparison of actual with budgeted results either to secure by individual action, the objective of that policy or to provide a basis for its revision."
(2) Dr. G. R. Terry says, "Budgetary control is a process of finding out what is being done and comparing actual results with the corresponding budget data in order to approve accomplishments or to remedy differences by either adjusting the budget estimates or correct the cause of differences."

According to J. Betty, "Budgetary control is a system which uses budgets as a means of planning and controlling all aspects of producing and / or selling commodities or services."
(3) Glenn Welsch describes Budgetary control as 'the use of budgets and budgeting reports throughout the period to coordinate, evaluate and control day-to-day operations in accordance with the goals specified by the budget".

From the above definitions, it is clearly seen that the following steps, are necessary for budgetary control.
(1) Preparing departmental budgets.
(2) Co-ordinating all the budgets so that all the activities of business are: integrated.
(3) Comparing actual performance with budgeted figures so that the deviations can be located and necessary corrective steps may be taken.
The major purposes are threefold viz. planning, co-ordinating and controlling the activities of a business enterprise. It starts with stating the objectives in clear terms and is so useful in performing the functions of management that the resources are put to the best use. To put it more specifically, the purposes of budgetary control are as follows :
(1) To lay down the goals of business enterprise in clear and formal terms.
(2) To communicate these goals to all concerned with the management of business.
(3) To prepare a detailed programme of various activities.
(4) To co-ordinate the activities and efforts so as to put the resources to optimum use.
(5) To provide a tool for evaluating performance.

Advantages : An important tool for planning and control in the hands of management is budgeting. The management prepares itself for facing the challenges of future. It can evaluate the performance of various departments and employees on the basis of budgeting. To put them more specifically, the advantages of budgetary control are as follows :
(1) Planning : Budget is a short range planning for future. It makes management of business effective and efficient by forecasting what is likely to happen in future. It prepares management to face the oncoming eventualities.
(2) Co-ordination : Co-ordination of various activities and efforts is inade possible, as a master budget is prepared by co-ordinating various functional budgets. Budgeting makes it possible to divert efforts of all departments in the direction of achieving predetermined goals.
(3) Control : Budgeting provides a base for evaluating performance. By comparing budgeted figures with actual performance, management gets a good idea of what has been achieved. Corrective steps can be taken, if

possible. It is thus possible to check the different activities of business and efficiency of managers.
(4) Decentralisation : Budgeting makes it possible to decentralise the activities of business and to delegate responsibilities. The business is divided into various departments and budgets are prepared for each department. Responsibility of implementing the budget is entrusted to departmental heads and necessary authority is delegated to him.
(5) Improves Fificiency : Detailed and systematic analysis of all factors affecting business is made even before the actual budgeting begins. As a result, management gets an idea of where deficiencies exist and where corrective steps are needed. Steps are taken to remove these deficiencies. leading to overall improvement in efficiency.
(6) Co-operation : While preparing various budgets, the departmental heads meet together and discuss matters of mutual interest. They appreciate the difficulties of one another, which reduce the possibility of conflicts, and they work in close co-operation.
(7) Improves Communication : Budgeting leads to efficient communication. Business goals, plans, authority and responsibility assigned to each manager etc. are clearly written and communicated to all concerned. This leaves no doubt about who has to do what, how and within what time period.
(8) Optimum Use of Resources : Optimum use of firm's resources is made possible through budgeting. Efforts of all are directed in desired channels. Thus, use of physical and human resources becomes econon cal. leading to realising the goal of maximisation of profits.
(9) Higher Morale and Productivity : It raises morale and productivity of employees, as they participate in formulation of plans. It leads to -harmony between individual goals and enterprise objectives. Employecs participate with zeal in implementing the plan, leading to increase in productivity.
(10) Management by Exception : Detailed programmes for various departments are formulated and the responsibility of efficiently implementing them is assigned to departmental managers. Corresponding authority in given to them. Thus, the burden of top management is reduced. Now, they are required to pay attention to only those matters which deviate significantly from planned course. Tial is, it leads to ininagelnent by oxdipition.
(11) Facilitates getting Bank Loan : Banks are ready to provide loans to firms where system of budgetary control is in operation. This is because

the bank feels that the management has arranged for systematic control of various activities for the smooth operation of business. Hence, the bank will get interest regularly and the principal amount on time.
(12) Develops Cost-consciousness : It leads to cost-consciousness among officers. Every manager gets used to think about the cost of various proposals put before them. It inculcates a habit of doing everything with a calculative mind. It creates an atmosphere of profit mindedness and cost consciousness.

Limitations :* The benefits of budgeting appear very attractive. However, budgeting would fail if following limitations are not kept in view :
(1) Success depends on Estimates : The success of budgetary control system depends on accuracy of estimates. Budgeting will succeed only to the extent to which the estimates arc accurate. Hence, it would fail if budget figures are not carefully and judiciously prepared.
(2) Rigidity : Changes must be made in budget estimates according to the changing circumstances. If this is not done, it leads to lack of initiative and enthusiasm on the part of employees. It may become rigid and restrictive. Thus employees may not be able to meet the performance goals, if they do not have free hand.
(3) Success depends on Cooperation : It is not easy to implement the budget. It can be done only by co-ordinated efforts of all executives. All of them must make efforts continuously to realise the targets. Each of the managers must realise that if they do not achieve the targets fixed for him. It will have adverse impact on other departments. Finally, without the co-operation of top manager, budgeting cannot succeed.
(4) It is only a Tool : Budget is a tool of management and not management itself. Management has to play an active role. Budget is not the master, but is a servant of management. It is the best tool for the company to develop its various activities and for every individual to develop his activities in various circles. But it should never be taken that any budget is complete.
(5) Expensive : Preparing budgets and arranging for its implementation requires huge expenses. Small firms cannot afford to implement them. If it is not used carefully and with calculative mind, its cost will outweigh its benefits.

(6) Success in Long run : Perfect budgetary control system is not evolved within a short time. If too much is expected of a system of budgeting within a short period, it may disappoint the manager. It takes quite a long time to develop procedures and programmes suited to the particular business enterprise. Once a perfect system is developed after trial and error, after considerable experience, it may give encouraging results only in the long run.
(7) Too high Targets Disappointing : The targets set must be realistic, capable of being achieved by the personnel available. If they are too high or unrealistic, the workers will fail to achieve them. This will result in disappointment and lower morale of workers, bringing down the over all efficiency of the business unit. It may create conflict between workers and management.
(8) Lack of Proper Evaluation : Success of budgeting presupposes system of proper evaluation of actual performance. Lack of such a system will hide inefficiencies instead of revealing them. And the whole system of budgetary control will be futile.
