# ZERO-BASED BUDGETING
The need for an effective budgeting technique is increasingly felt in the constantly changing environment of modem times. The traditional budgeting has not measured upto this expectation. The traditional budgeting process

takes up the previous allocation for various activities as the base in formulating the new budget. On this basis, the new allocations are made with a few changes here and there. e.g. Rs. 50,000 were spent during the previous year on advertisement. On this basis the fresh allocation may be made at say Rs. 60,000 for next year and this may be included in the new budget. The zero - based budgeting does not take any previous allocation as the base. If rather subjects all activities and expenditure current as well as new to fresh scrutiny. Its cost - benefit is analysed and is included in the new budget only if justified but such analysis. This shows that the allocation to any activity during the previous year is not accepted at the base. The allocation for all activities is treated as zero. All alternatives are thoroughly examined and evaluated. If on evaluation, the benefits exceed the cost, then only it would be included in the new proposals.

Zero-based budgeting is thus a process of reviewing, analysing and evaluating each demand to be incorporated into the budget, so that its justification for including it in or excluding it from the budget can be determined.

The concept of zero-base budgeting was put forward as far back as in 1924 by a budget expert of England Mr. Hilton Young. He initiated a method of reviewing each budget proposal afresh. However it was presented in its modern form and with a modern nomenclature by Mr. Peter Pyrr in 1970. He has successfully utilised it in his company the Texas Instruments Inc. of the U.S.A. The current interest in this method is attributable, in large measure to Ex-President Carter of the U.S.A. who as Governor of Georgia promoted the concept into the states budget in 1970 and who had vowed to bring it infederal budget for fiscal year 1979. More than a dozen States of the U.S.A. adopted the zero-base budgeting. A number of companies have applied it either fully or in segments.

ADVANTAGES / BENEFITS OF ZERO-BASED BUDGETING :1
The being a new and modern technique, the following benefits are claimed for it :
(1) Every proposal is considered every year afresh and is evaluated on the basis of benefit analysis. All alternatives are considered and only the most profitable one is selected. This help eliminating wastes, unnecessary expenditure etc. and improves overall efficiency of the business.
(2) The validity of traditional belief that the current programmes are profitable is challenged. Under the traditional budgeting just increases and

decreases from the current operations are made and budget formulated. Rethinking of each proposal promotes more effective allocation of resources.
(3) It is an educational process that can promote development of the management teams. Management at all levels take part in preparation of budgets. They are thus trained in the mentioned of cost benefit analysis and can evaluate proposals independently. Their sense of ego is satisfied, as they feel that they have contributed to the formulation of budgets. This leads to job satisfaction.
(4) The new technique leads to improvements in financial planning and management information system, which results into improved efficiency of the business as a whole.
(5) Top management has greater flexibility in reallocating resources and allowing greater budget shifts among organisation.
(6) Duplication of efforts among organisational limits will be identified. This will result in eliminating or centralizing these functions.
(7) High priority new programmes can be funded totally or in part by rejecting or reducing current activities.

## LIMITATIONS OF ZERO-BASED BUDGETING
The opponents of this method believe that the benefits mentioned above are exaggerated and the method suffers from a number of limitations.
(1) It is not correct to say that the method is a new one. The current practices also make use of such techniques of evaluation of various alternatives, cost - benefit analysis etc. Hence, it cannot be labelled as a neo-modern method.
(2) The method cannot be used in all types of organisations or for every manager. It cannot claim to be universally applicable. Even in the U.S.A. hardly 100-150 companies have adopted it. Managers at all levels of organisation may not be competent to undertake cost benefit analysis. Nor do they have enough time to give ranks to hundreds and thousands of proposals. This work has to be entrusted to the expert. It will take a heavy toll of top management's time, if they are required to evaluate all the proposals.
(3) It is not practicable to evaluate each proposal on the basis of cost benefit. e.g. the Govemment is required to accept many proposals in respect of which financial considerations have to be ignored. Such proposals have to be included in the budget from the view point of moral obligation or social obligation.

The cost benefit analysis involves the consideration of social costs, too. This would make the calculations of cost very complicated. On the other hand, certain benefits cannot be measured in terms of money, e.g. how shall we measure the benefits accruing from expenditure on recreational activities of the employees?
(4) Managers are required to evaluate various decision packages and rank them. Some objective basis is required for ranking the proposals. The measuring rod for the production or result achieved in respect of different proposals has to be decided. It is not easy to have such a measuring rod.
(5) As far as govemment budgets are concerned, all the proposals to be included cannot be evaluated on the basis of cost benefit analysis. Political or social objectives are sometimes more important than financial considerations in case of certain proposals. In addition, in countries like India, most of the members of the parliament and legislative assemblies do not understand the technique of cost - benefit analysis. Even when the members are enlightened and educated, they are not prepared to give up certain proposals of their choice, which cannot stand the test of cost benefit analysis.
(6) The psychological effects can also not be ignored. It holds out high hopes as modern technique, claiming to raise the profitability and efficiency of the business. When however the ultimate results are found to be not so encouraging, the managers are frustrated.
(7) The bureaucrats in government will oppose it tooth and nail. The government officers always seek safety and they like to prepare budgets on the basis of past budget proposals only. They are not prepared to undertake any risk of new proposals. Hence, they would make every effort to denounce it and see that the new methods is not adopted.
(8) The method is undoubtedly expensive. It invites evaluation of hundred of decision packages, comparing various alternative proposals and ranking them on the basis of cost benefit analysis. This demand appointment of experts, creating the whole organisation, buying costly electronic machines etc. They heavy expenses involved in these are beyond the capacity of majority of the business firms. It is the experience of even government departments in the U.S.A. that the benefit available is negligible as compared to cost involved in adopting the system.
(9) The method suffers from the defect of rigidity. It is not flexible. Once budget is finalised on the basis of evaluation of alternative proposals with the help of cost - benefit analysis. it becomes difficult to make

adjustments demanded by the sudden changes in circumstances. Again all alternatives have to be re-evaluated and ranking has to be revised. This makes implementation of budget proposals difficult.

In India, the method is hardly useful for government budgeting as most of the proposals have to be included in the budget on political and social considerations. Even in business field too, its use is bound to be limited, looking to the fact that it involves heavy expenditure which very few firms can afford.

The method is still in this infancy and is yet to be tested. Enough time has not passed to share the experience of all those business enterprise who have switched to this new method. However, majority of them believe that is worth giving a trial.
