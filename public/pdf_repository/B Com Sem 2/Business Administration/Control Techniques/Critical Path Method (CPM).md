# CRITICAL PATH METHOD (CPM)

The purpose of both PERT and CPM is to determine the estimated time of each of the activities and on that basis to determine the minimum time to complete a Project. For the purpose, we must know the longest path of completing the project. This longest path will be the critical path. The Expected time to complete the project is determined on the basis of the time required by the critical path. If the total time of completing a project is to be reduced, then the critical path must be shortened. Let us take an illustration to make this point clear.

Suppose, in order to manufacturer an article $X$ two parts $A$ and $B$ are to be produced and assembled. Now, for part A the activities to be performed are (1) to requisition it from stores (2) to put it on lathe machine for processing (3) to colour it and (4) to assemble it.

For Part B the activities are (1) to requisition it from stores (2) to put it on shaper machine (3) to polish it (4) to assemble it.

Suppose timings are as follows.:

| Activity | Description | Time (in hours) |
| :--: | :--: | :--: |
| A | To requisition Part A | 1 |
| B | To put it on lathe | 4 |
| C | To get it coloured | 3 |
| D | To assemble it | 2 |
| E | To requisition Part B | 1 |
| F | To put it on shaper | 7 |
| G | To get it polished | 5 |
| H | To assemble it | 2 |


It is clear from the above chart that $A$ takes $1+4+3=8$ hours to come to the assembly and Part B takes $1+7+5=13$ hours to come to the assembly. In the assembly division, the work of assembling takes 2 hours more. Thus, the maximum time taken to complete a product $X$ is $13+2=15$ hours.

Here, the longest path is that of $B$ which is 13 hours. In the above diagram it has been shown with a thick line. This path is called the Critical Path. If the total time of completing article $X$ is to be reduced, then the time of $B$ must be reduced. Suppose, the time of doing lathe work on Part A is reduced from 4 hours to 3 hours; even then the total time of 15 hours will remain the same, because the time of $B$ is not reduced which is 13 hours. Unless that time is reduced, it is not possible to reduce the total time. The project will be delayed as much as the activities on the critical path are delayed. Thus it is necessary to complete all activities on the critical path in targeted time. All the activities on this path must be received from time to time. If these activities are not proceeding as planned, then corrective step must be taken.

Slack or Float : We have seen above that part A takes 8 hours to reach the assembly, while part B takes 13 hours. Hence, there is a surplus time of 5 hours in activities relating to part A. If therefore, any of these activities is delayed by 5 hours, it will not affect the completion time of the whole project. Leisure time of 5 hours is available for completing lathe work or colour work on A. Such information is helpful to production manager in scheduling, as he has some latitude in determining the schedule of this path. Remember that there is no slack or leisure time on the critical path.
