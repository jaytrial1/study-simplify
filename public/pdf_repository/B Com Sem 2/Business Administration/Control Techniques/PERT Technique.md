# PERT TECHNIQUE
In industry and business, there are a number of projects which are planned and executed. For example building a bridge, introducing a new product in the market, sending a space craft on to the Moon, preparing a missile etc. are all called 'Projects'. All these projects involve huge amount of money and time. They must therefore be completed in minimum of time and money. For his purpose there must be careful study and analysis of the whole project. The techniques used for the purpose of planning and control of such projects are known as PERT and CPM. PERT is the short form of Programme Evaluation and Review Technique and full form of CPM is Critical Path Method. Both the methods are systems of determining the minimum time within which a programme or a project can be completed systematically. Both were developed almost at the same time i.e. in 1957 and 1958.

CPM was developed by Walker and Kelly in the well-known Do Pont Company. while PERT was developed in 1958 by the US Navy in planning. scheduling and controlling Polaris Missile System. Thereafter the use of PERT was so fast that it has become compulsory to use it for implementing any programme in US military. Though, both the system, were developed at the same time independent of each other, the basic techniques are the same.

All these activities are shown in an order and a chart is prepared. Each activity proceeds in particular direction and is shown by arrow. That is why the diagram is called Arrow Diagram. The whole diagram looks like a net work and so it is also called Net Work Analysis.

Let us takes an illustration form industry. Suppose, a company has a project of preparing a new product. This can be prepared by assembling two components $A$ and $B$. First both of them are to be put on a lathe machine. Then part B is to be polished. while A does not require polishing. The whole project can thus be divided into various Activities and Events as follows :

(1) Starting the work
(2) Receiving materials for A from stores
(3) Receiving materials for B from stores
(4) Processing A on lathe machine
(5) Processing B on lathe machine
(6) Polishing B
(7) Assembling A and B
(8) Completing the work

Here, the events of starting work and completing work do not consume any time. But doing activities takes time. For an example, receiving material for part A takes time. When this activity is completed, the event of 'Materials received' takes place, which does not take any time.

## CHARACTERISTICS OF PERT 

From the above two illustrations, we can derive the following characteristics of PERT :
(1) Planning and Control : It is a technique of planning and control. A project is planned and its performance is controlled, so that it is completed in the shortest possible time. A project may be to construct a building, a bridge, a missile, a ship, manufacturing a generator or any other machine or installing a computer system, repair of a ship etc.
(2) Division into Activities : The whole project is divided into various activities or jobs that would be necessary to complete a project. Each activity is an independent job in the whole project and is represented by an arrow.
(3) Event : At the end of the activity some accomplishment occurs. This is called an event. It is a point at which some activity is completed and another activity starts. It is represented by a circle. Every activity has an antecedent event. It will also have a subsequent event.
(4) Mutually Related : The activitios or jobs divided as above are all related to one another in a particular order. On the basis of that order, a chart is prepared.

(5) Expected Time : PERT is basically a system of planning and controlling, so that all the activities and projects are completed in time period assigned. Time estimates are made for each activity by persons who are familiar with activities.
(6) Related to Time : Originally PERT (known as PERT/time) was developed with a view to completing the project in the minimum possible time. Thereafter of course PERT/cost was also developed whose basic objective is to reduce the cost by reducing the time involved. But even today the main objective is to complete the project in the minimum time.
(7) Presentation by a Chart : In PERT the whole project along with its activities and events are presented in the form of a chart showing the expected time for each activity, the events and critical Path. This diagram is known as arrow diagram and also known as Project Diagram or a Network Diagram.
(8) Critical Path : When the Pert Chart shows various activities and events in a particular order, it also shows the longest path from the first event sill the last event. This route is known as Critical Path. If the project is to be completed in less time, then the time of only the critical path is to be reduced.

## PERT CHART : 

We now will try to see how a PERT chart can be drawn by taking an illustration from industry.

Suppose, a large manufacturing company wants to prepare a budget for the next year. Here, preparing a budget is a project. Let as divide this project into its component activities. The managing director asks the salesman to provide sales estimated for the period. The salesmen provide these estimates to both the sales manager and the production manager. The sales manager estimates the sale prices on the forecast and sends it to the finance manager. The production manager must make production schedules. On the basis of production schedules, the accountant prepares the cost of production and sends it to the finance manager, who is also the Budget Officer. Now the finance manager using the information provided by the sales manager and the accountant, prepares the final budget and submits it to the managing director.

## Advantages
(1) Planning : Basically, this method is developed for planning and control of the project or programmes. In preparing a chart under this method, the whole project is divided into number of activities. Start time and finish time of each activity are planned in advance. Thus, all the activities are systematically planned.
(2) Control : As we have just seen, starting time and finishing time are determined for each activity. On the basis of these timings, progress of these activities are checked. Checks and safeguards are provided against activities going astray. If needed, corrective steps are taken. Thus, PERT is a tool of controlling performance.

(3) Management by Exception : The PERT chart shows the Critical Path and this Path decides the time by which the whole project would be completed. It means that the management have to concentrate their attention on activities lying on this path. Less attention may be devoted to those activities which have slack. Thus, management by exception is used.
(4) Utilisation of Resources : The concept of slack is useful in the optimum utilisation of resources. If the activities on critical path are not proceeding as planned, employees and other resources are diverted from activities having slack and the project can be completed in targeted time.
(5) Estimated Time : The estimated time of completing each of the activities depends on three basis : optimistic time, most likely time and pessimistic time. Proper weightage is allotted to all three and the estimated time is worked out. This is the most probable time and the project is most likely to be completed within this time.
(6) Balance : All the activities of a project are represented in the chart in proper sequence and the inter-relationship between various activities is made clear. Hence, and imbalance between these activities can be corrected.
(7) Savings in Time : The purpose of both PERT and CPM is to see that the whole project is completed within the least possible time. In most of the cases, the savings in time is so significant that it saves huge amount of money, e.g. the programme of developing Polaris missiles by American Navy could be completed two years ahead by the use of this method, while in case of Du Pont company, the time of maintenance of machines could be reduced from 125 hours to 93 hours with the help of CPM.
(8) Flexibility : An important feature of PERT is its flexibility, which allows the planner any degree of refinement he feels necessary and the use of costing procedures.
(9) Manpower Planning : Time for each activity is determined and the manpower requirement is estimated. A separate chart is prepared for each type of employee required during every week. Thus, PERT assists in manpower planning also.

## Limitations

(1) Difficulty in Estimating : In case, the project is not very clear and it is difficult to divide it into various activities and determine their timings, PERT cannot be used.
(2) Repetitive Activities : In large scale industries, where most of the activities are of repetitive nature, PERT is of little us. The definite

sequence and timings of each of the activities in such cases, are fixed and no strict control is called for.
(3) Cost Control : The basic purpose of this method is to control the timings of the project. Hence, it is useful in planning and control of such projects where time is of utmost importance. However, it is not of much use where the question of cost control is important. Of course, now the PERT/cost system has been developed for this purpose.
(4) Support of Top Management : This system would bear fruits only where the top management takes interest and gives its support. In the absence of such support the system will not be of much avail.
(5) Complications : This system of planning and control is very complicated. Even the top management do not understand it properly. They have to depend more on statistical experts for this purpose.
(6) Estimates : Number of factors are to be estimated. The estimates are made mostly by sophisticated statistical or mathematical techniques, even then there are possibilities that they prove to be incorrect. To that extent, the usefulness of this method is limited.
