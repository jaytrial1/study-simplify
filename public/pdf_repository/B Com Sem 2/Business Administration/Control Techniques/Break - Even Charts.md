# BREAK - EVEN CHARTS
In order to find out break-even point and profit (or loss) at different levels of production, a break - even chart is prepared. A break - even chart is graphic presentation of relationship between total cost, sales and profit or cost-volume-profit relationship. It is a device to show relationship between sales volume-marginal cost and fixed cost and profit or loss at different levels of production. Three lines are drawn in this chart; (1) Fixed cost line, (2) Total cost line, and (3) Total sales line. The point of intersection of total cost line and total sales line is called the break - even point. As this chart shows the break-even point, it is popularly known as the Break-Even Chart.

The break-even chart will be drawn as follows. (1) Firstly, a fixed cost line will be drawn. This will run parallel to the $x$-axis, as fixed expenses do not vary according to the changes in output. (2) Total cost line will then be drawn. (3) Lastly, the sales line will be drawn which will be at $45^{\circ}$ angle to the $x$ and $y$-axis. The break-even point is located where these two lines intersect. Below that point, the gap between sales line and total cost line shows loss, because Total cost line is above Sales line. i.e. Total Cost is more than sales revenue. But after the break-even point, Sales line is above the total cost line. It means that gap between two lines shows profit.

## Illustration 1 : 
Variable expenses per unit of a factory is Rs. 25. Total fixed cost is Rs. 60,000. If the selling price is Rs. 40, then find BEP by drawing Break-even chart.

### Solution :

Before drawing a break-even chart the following two tables are prepared.
(A) Total Cost Table (Variable cost per unit Rs. 25 + Fixed cost Rs. 60,000).

| Units | 0 | 2,000 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Total cost (Rs.) | 60,000 | 1,10,000 | 1,60,000 | 2,10,000 | 2,60,000 |

(B) Revenue Table (selling price per unit Rs. 40)

| Units | 0 | 2,000 | 4,000 | 6,000 | 8,000 |
| :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: | :--: |
| Revenue (Rs.) | 0 | 80,000 | 1,60,000 | 2,40,000 | 3,20,000 |


## UTILITY OF BREAK - EVEN ANALYSIS
The break - even analysis is useful to management in solving some of the important problems they face :
(I) To find out the sales required to earn the profit necessary for reasonable return on investment, paying reasonable dividend on preference and equity shares after transferring a reasonable amount to reserve.
(2) To fix up the selling price so as to reach a desired level of break even point and that of profits.
(3) To determine with the help of break-even chart, variable cost per unit of output.
(4) To ascertain the cost and revenue at different levels of production, so that figures are available for preparation of Sales Budget.
