# BREAK EVEN ANALYSIS
Management of every business enterprise is interested in knowing the level of sales at which the cost and revenue will be equal. This helps the management in determining the amount of sales to be achieved for desired level of profit. Break-even analysis is useful for this purpose.

The purpose of break-even analysis is to determine that level of sales at which total income and expenses are equal and neither profit is made nor loss is incurred. When the business achieves this level, it is said to have reached the break-even point. Thus, the break-even point is that level of sales at which neither profit nor loss is made. If the sale exceeds this level, the business will make profit; and if the sale goes below this level, it will incur loss. Break - even analysis is a technique which helps management in knowing the position of no profit-no loss. Similarly, it is possible to determine profit or loss at different levels of sales.

For the purpose of break-even analysis, all expenses are divided into two parts : Fixed costs and variable costs. Those expenses which do not vary in proportion to output. i.e. which remain fixed whether the output increases or decreases, are called Fixed Expenses or Fixed Costs. e.g.

manager`s salary, rent, rates etc. Those expenses which vary with output i.e. which increase when output increases and decrease when output is reduced, are called Variable Expenses or Variable Costs. e.g. raw materials, direct wages, motive power for running machinery etc. Besides, some expenses change with change in output but not in that proportion are called Semi-variable expenses.

Following are the assumptions on which the theory of break-even analysis is based: [Assumptions of Break Even Analysis]: 1. The changes in the level of various revenue and costs arise only because of the changes in the number of product (or service) units produced and sold, e.g., the number of television sets produced and sold by Sigma Corporation. The number of output (units) to be sold is the only revenue and cost driver. Just as a cost driver is any factor that affects costs, a revenue driver is any factor that affects revenue. 2. Total costs can be divided into a fixed component and a component that is variable with respect to the level of output, variable costs include the following: - Direct materials - Direct labour - Direct chargeable expenses Variable overheads include the following: - Variable part of factory overheads. - Administration overheads. - Selling and distribution overheads 3. There is linear relationship between revenue and cost. 4. When put in a graph, the behaviour of total revenue and cost is linear (straight line), i.e., $Y=m x+C$ holds goods which is the equation of a straight line. 5. The unit selling price, unit variable costs and fixed costs are constant. 6. The theory of CVP is based upon the production of a single product. However, of late, management accountants are functioning to give a theoretical and a practical approach to multi-product CVP analysis. 7. The analysis either covers a single product or assumes that the sales mix sold in case of multiple products will remain constant as the level of total units sold changes. 8. All revenue and cost can be added and compared without taking into account the time value of money. 9. The theory of CVP is based on the technology that remains constant. 10. The theory of price elasticity is not taken into consideration.

In break - even analysis, cost per unit is first found out without taking into account the fixed cost and this variable cost (or marginal cost) is deducted from selling price. The resultant figure is calld contribution. When fixed costs are exactly recovered from this contribution, a point of no profit, no loss reached. This is the break-even point.
(1) Contribution :* The difference between sales and variable cost is termed as "Contribution."

Contribution = Sales - Variable Cost
In short period, fixed costs are constant and more the amount of contribution the more will be the ultimate profit. Contribution shows what each unit contributes towards fixed costs and profit. When contribution is more than fixed cost, the profit is earned. Conversely, if contribution is less than fixed cost, loss is incurred.
(2) Profit : The excess of contribution over the fixed cost will be the profit.

Profit = Contribution - Fixed Cost 

For an example, total fixed expenses are Rs. 80,000 and contribution from sale of one unit is Rs. 80, then the total amount of fixed expenses are recovered, if 1,000 units are sold. If one more unit is sold, i.e. if sales are of 1,001 units, then the contribution of the last unit sold viz. Rs. 80 is the profit.

This equation can be explained as under :

Profit = Selling Price - Total Cost

$\therefore$ Profit = Selling Price - (Variable Cost + Fixed Cost)
$\therefore$ Profit = Contribution - Fixed Cost
From the above equation, the following can also be derived :
Contribution = Fixed Cost + Profit
(3) Break Even Point (BEP) : It is that volume of output at which neither a profit is made nor a loss in incurred, i.e. at this point, contribution just covers the fixed cost, i.e. it is break-even point.

Contribution = Fixed Cost.
A business is said to break-even when total cost equals total revenue in a period of time. The break-even point indicates the level of operation at which the firm makes no profit, nor incurs any loss.

Break - even point may be presented in two ways :
(A) The number of units that should be sold so that expenses and incomes. are equal.
(B) The amount of sales value in rupees so that incomes and expenses are equal.
(4) Calculation of BEP [Formula of BEP]* :

The break-even point can be calculated either in terms of units or in terms of sales value by applying the following formulas :
(i) BEP (Units) $=\frac{F}{S-V}$
(ii) BEP (in Rs.) $=\frac{F}{1-\frac{V}{S}}$

For an example, variable expenses in a business are Rs. 4 per unit, the selling price is Rs. 10 per unit and total fixed expenses are Rs. 60,000, then the break-even point in units will be as follows :

$$
\text { B.E.P. (in units) }=\frac{F}{S-V}=\frac{\text { Rs. } 60,000}{10-4}=\frac{\text { Rs. } 60,000}{\text { Rs. } 6}=10,000 \text { units }
$$

If break - even point is to be expressed in sales value, then its formula will be as shown in (ii) above.

For an example, according to figures shown above, the break - even point in sales value will be as under :

$$
\begin{aligned}
& =\frac{F}{1-\frac{V}{S}}=\frac{\text { Rs. } 60,000}{1-\frac{4}{10}}=\frac{60,000}{10} \\
& =\frac{60,000 \times 10}{6}=\text { Rs. } 1,00,000
\end{aligned}
$$

This shows that when sales are Rs. 1,00,000, expenses and revenue are equal.
