# MASLOW'S THEORY OF HIERARCHY OF NEEDS

We discussed various classifications of human needs. But it is not clarified which needs are important at a particular point of time and place. Psychologists hold that needs have a certain priority. If basic needs are not satisfied, they claim priority over other needs. As the basic needs are satisfied, a person seeks to satisfy, higher needs.

Shri. A. H. Maslow presented his theory of hierarchy of needs in his article 'A Theory of Human Motivation' published in 1943. He presented a need priority of five levels. These needs are as follows :
(1) Physiological Needs
(2) Safety Needs
(3) Social Needs (Love Needs)
(4) Esteem Needs
(5) Need for self actualization.

Let us examine Maslow's views as regards each of these need levels.
(1) Physiological Needs : The physiological needs such as hunger, thirst, sex, sleep, rest etc. are basic needs. The priority of basic needs is obvious. If these needs are not satisfied his most powerful need will

be to eat and drink. When a man is extremely hungry, he thinks about food, he perceives only food everywhere and he wants food only. He feels that once his need to eat is satisfied, he does not require anything else in the world. Man lives for bread-alone, when there is no bread.
(2) Safety Needs : When physiological needs are fairly well satisfied, needs at the next higher level begin to dominate. The needs that next tend to dominate are safety and security. This need is concerned with protection against danger. It is a need for self preservation. As protection is afforded by the state in the modern times, he wants security of job. He wants economical security such as workmen's compensation programmes and pension scheme. This security provides for future to satisfy physiological needs when he is injured out of work or otherwise unable to provide for himself. He would like to provide for a rainy day.
(3) Social Needs : When the two needs stated above are satisfied, the social needs become important. They are concerned with love and affection, sense of belonging, association and friendship. He feels that he must be loved and liked by some people. He would obtain satisfaction by becoming the member of a group and would desire to be loved, protected and advised by the group.
(4) Esteem Needs : These are the needs for achievement and appreciation. Man desires that he should be an important entity in his field and that his importance must be recognised. If these needs are not satisfied, he feels inferiority and helplessness. However, most of the business organisations do not offer much opportunity for the satisfaction of such needs.
(5) Need for Self-actualization : There are the needs for realising one's own potentialities, for continued self-development and for being creative in the widest sense. A painter has the ambition to be known the world over as a great artist. A man interested in writing poetry wishes to be a world famous poet and so on.

The first two of the above needs are lower level needs, or they are essential needs. They are aggressive and must be satisfied immediately. Industrial peace is disturbed when these needs are not satisfied. The remaining three needs are higher level needs. This will be clear from the following chart.


Maslow did not intend that his need hierarchy theory should be directly applied to motivation. It was McGregor who popularized the Maslow theory in management literature. Thereafter the need hierarchy theory had a tremendous impact on the modern management approach to motivation.

Managers have now conceded that the lower level needs can be met through monetary motivators. Therefore, monetary incentives will be quite effective so far as these needs are concerned. But when it is a question of higher level needs, monetary rewards will not do. Some other incentives would need to be applied.

Some attempts have been made to test Maslow's theory, but the results have not lent support to his theory. The most recent and comprehensive study by Wahba and Birdwell also does not support Maslow's model. They reviewed 22 studies and found only two primary groups of needs instead of five and they found no support for the contention that higher level needs arise only after the fulfilment of lower level needs. Maslow therefore modified his position by saying that higher needs may also emerge when lower needs are suppressed for long, and not after the satisfaction of lower needs.

The intensity of needs are conditioned by the level of development of the country. In countries like the U.S.A., for an example, the lower level needs are almost always found to be satisfied and therefore the need hierarchy seems to have no relevance. In countries like India, on the other hand, the lower level needs are most pressing and therefore managers have to d'vise measures to satisfy these needs only.

Criticism of Maslow's Theory :*
The following criticisms are levelled against Maslow's presentation :
(1) Artificial Classification : In order to explain the motivation principle, Maslow has divided needs into five categories. This classification of needs seems to be artificial. The problem here is why only five needs. Even in actual situation such classification of needs is not seen. It is not supported even by studies made. In certain research studies the needs are classified into only two groups : (i) Higher level needs (ii) Lower level needs.
(2) Order of Needs is not always maintained : Maslow has stated in his approach that needs have a definite order of priority. Needs proceed in this order and then they are satisfied. According to him firstly only physical needs must be satisfied. The Safety Needs arise only after physical needs are satisfied. But the hiearchy of needs as stated by Maslow is not always maintained in real life situation. The intensity of needs of a person depends on the circumstances of development of a country and the person concerned; see the following figures. In figure 1, in the Maslow's hierarchy, the physical needs and safety needs are on a higher level, while love needs, esteem needs and self-actualisation needs are at lower level.

In figure 2, the social needs or love needs are the most important ones and physiological and self actualisation needs of less importance.

Figure 3, is almost reverse of figure 1: Here, the self-actualisation needs and esteem needs are the most important and the physiological and safety needs are less important.

From comparative analysis of all the three figures, it can be said that high level needs arise only after lower level needs are satisfied is not true. Any need may be more or less important for any person at any time.

(3) It is difficult to distinguish between Needs : Maslow has presented that human needs are different and also shows separate remedies for each of them. But that is also not supported by experience. One author has said that money is perhaps the best means to satisfy all types of needs. Money is able to satisfy all needs of a human being. As a result, there is no distinction between different needs.
(4) Concept of Unsatisfied Needs : According to Maslow unsatisfied needs, and not satisfied needs, play an important role in moulding the behaviour of human being. But even this is not true. According to Maslow, for a person, one need is more intense at one time and another need arises only after that need is satisfied. But some times, for a person more than one need may be having equal intensity at the same time. Thus, the Maslow's contention of hiearchy of needs is not true, as a man may experience more than one need at any time.
(5) Concept of Higher Needs Satisfied Last is False : Maslow 'propounded that the first two needs are primary needs and are aggressive, so that they must be satisfied first. The last three needs are of higher level and are of social nature and may be satisfied later. But this is not supported by findings of the study. In fact, satisfied needs often recur i.e. they may arise again.
(6) Some Needs are always Unsatisfied : While explaining his theory Maslow stated that once physiological needs are satisfied, then only other needs arise. But physiological needs always recur. Though once it is satisfied, it arises frequently. Such needs are unsatisfied needs. He has to satisfy these needs till he lives to maintain life.

Whatever may be the result of the studies, Maslow's model has made a significant contribution in making management aware of the variety of the needs of the people at work. The number and levels of needs are not important. What is important is that employees have different motives and management must try to satisfy them.
