# HERZBERG'S TWO FACTOR THEORY OF MOTIVATION
In the 1950's, Herzberg and associates conducted a motivational study on about 200 accountants and engineers employed by firms in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. They conducted this study with the prior approval of employers assuring them that the answer of the employees would not be disclosed. The employees were given following directions by interviewers :
"Think of a time when you felt exceptionally good or exceptionally bad about your job, either your present job or any other job you have had."

Herzberg's aim was to know which factors are responsible for dissatisfaction among the workers and which are the motivating factors. Herzberg concluded that there are two types of motivational factors : Hygienic Factors and Motivators. Taken together they become known as Two-factor Theory of Motivation.
(1) Hygienle Factors : Wages, security of job and work conditions are called hygienic factors. If wages are high, job is secured and working conditions are satisfactory, employees do not feel dissatisfied. Their morale and efficiency do not weaken. But these factors do not motivate the employees. They lead to dissatisfaction in their absence. But their presence does not motivate them to work more. They are only preventive. They are environmental in nature and they are roughly equivalent to Maslow's lower level needs. Thus, these factors only prevent dissatisfaction. They do not motivate. They bring motivation upto a theoretical zero level and are a necessary floor to prevent dissatisfaction. They serve as a take-off for motivation. They represent a situation akin to 'no-debt, no-saving, situation of an individual.

For a long time, management had concentrated on the hygienic factors taking them as the motivational factors. In fact these factors only prevent dissatisfaction. They do not motivate. These factors are also known as maintenance factors in the job, because they maintain a reasonable level of satisfaction in employees. They are known as hygienic factors because they support employee's mental health. Herzberg has listed ten hygienic factors. They are as follows :
(1) Company policy and Administration.
(2) Technical Supervision
(3) Interpersonal relations with Supervisors
(4) Interpersonal relations with Colleagues
(5) Interpersonal relations with Subordinates
(6) Salary
(7) Job Security
(8) Personal Life
(9) Working conditions
(10) Status
(2) Motivators : These are the factors which build strong motivation. An example of an accounting supervisor may be given in this respect, who felt good about being given a job of installing new computer equipment. He took pride and was gratified in knowing that the new equipment made a big difference in the overall functioning of his department. Thus when an employee is entrusted with some challenging responsibility, he is motivated to accept the challenge. Herzberg has listed six motivational factors. They are as follows :
(1) Achievement
(2) Recognition
(3) Advancement
(4) Work itself
(5) Possibility of Growth
(6) Responsibility

For many years management had been wondering why their policies to pay high wages and grant fringe benefits were not increasing employee motivation on the job. Herzberg's theory helped to answer their question. High wages and fringe benefits were only maintenance factors. They only maintain satisfaction, they do not motivate. They are necessary in maintaining satisfaction. But once they are fully provided, paying any more will not motivate employees. Only a challenging job which has the opportunities for achievement, recognition, responsibility, advancement and growth will motivate them.

Various studies conducted under different conditions have to a large extent supported Herzberg's Motivation-Maintenance Model.

It is worth noting that employees attribute their dissatisfaction to hygienic factors such as inadequate wages and unfavorable working conditions. But when they feel good, they attribute this feeling to some of their achievements. Thus the former is related to job context, while the latter is related to job content.

(2) Nathan King has criticised Herzberg's theory on the ground that his theory is. based on the hypothesis which itself is not tested scientifically. A follow-up study of female office workers that specifically tested all five theories did not confirm Herzberg's contention.
(3) Other research studies have shown that there is not always a clear distinction between motivational and maintenance factors. The distinction that Herzberg has created is illusory.
(4) Herzberg's theory does not apply to all categories of employees. He conducted research on high level 200 professionals for whom salaries and work conditions were maintenance factors only. However, these factors are not maintenance factors for the low grade employees. These are motivational factors for them. A study conducted by Malinovsky and Barry in 1965 reveled that bluecollar workers give much more emphasis to maintenance factors. This is true also in case of office employees of the lower categories.
(5) It is difficult to measure satisfaction. Herzberg has presented maintenance factors as satisfying factors. But in real life, it is very difficult to measure satisfaction of a person when an employee is satisfied, he considers it due to his work, skill and capacity, and where he is dissatisfied, he considers circumstances around him to be responsible.
(6) Even some Hygienic Factors provide motivation to a person. According to Herzberg, hygienic factors simply maintain employees in the job and even if they are increased, they do not motivate employees. For motivating them, separate motivating factors have to be provided. But in practice, this is not true. Even by improving hygienic factors like wages, status, physical facilities etc. also the employees are motivated.
(7) Concept that Job Satisfaction increases production is also not true. Herzberg stated that due to job satisfaction, the productivity of a worker increases. This concept is partly true but is a narrow concept. Productivity depends on so many other factors also.

In spite of his criticism, there is no doubt that Herzberg has contributed substantially to work motivation. He drew attention to the importance of job content factors in work motivation which previously
