# INGENTIVES
The study of incentives is essential to increase production and to make the employer-employee relationship cordial. Incentive is an outside factor that induces or encourages persons to show better performance.

Incentives are of two types :
(1) Financial Incentives (2) Non-financial Incentives
(A) Financial Incentives : Financial incentives are monetary rewards or rewards measurable in monetary worth, which are provided by employer to employees for better perfomance. Money is of course most important as a motivator. It does not mean that non-financial Incentives are of no importance. But money is such a thing that satisfies most needs of human beings.

The financial Incentives may take various forms : (1) Wage system may be so designed that payment is made according to work done. (2) An element may be introduced in the wage system, so that a worker is induced to show better performance, e.g. incentive wage system may be introduced; (3) Employees may be motivated by means of profit

sharing scheme or by stock option scheme, in which employees are made shareholders. But these group incentive schemes are not good motivators. (4) If bonus is paid to employees according to his performance, it works as a good motivator. (5) Employees may be given prizes for their good suggestions. (6) Some companies give reward for extreme regularity and for cost reduction suggestions. (7) The steps of social security like sickness benefits, accident compensation, provident fund scheme etc. are good motivators and induce workers to continue in the service for a long time. (8) Similarly, job security is a powerful financial incentive, as a worker does not worry about his living expenses when he is assured of security of service.

Even today, money is considered as a powepul financial incentive, because of following reasons :
(1) Money indicates the status and position of a person. Sometimes, the money compensation indicates the level of achivement of an individual. It means when a person is getting high monetary reward, (salary) he considers it as a status symbol or as proof of his achievement. Hence, money as a motivator is still used on large scale.
(2) Money links the performance with the compensation. Monetary -compensation is a proof that shows that he is getting reward in proportion to his performance. When an employee gets compensation in proportion to his performance, he is greatly satified. and it induces him to continue his better performance. Particularly in countries like India, this is true even today. As Giles and Barett have said, "money may potentially be an effective motivator, regardless of the level one has attained or the amount of money he is earning." Another author has remarked, "pay in one form or another is certainly one of the mainsprings of motivation in our society."
(3) Economists and most of management experts consider money to be a very powerful tool of motivation. How a worker would work and how much will depend on the financial return that he gets.
(4) Money is a sound proof of compensation. When a worker gets compensation according to the work done by him, he gets satisfied. Other forms of compensation are not very tangible, hence they are notso effective as money.
(5) Money as a tool is not useful. But it is a good means by which a person is able to buy all things he needs and satisfies them. Now.

the thing that becomes a tool to satisfy the need would definitely act as an incentive.
(6) Money is such a means, the form of which can be changed and the desired result can be obtained.
(B) Non-financial Incentives : Money is a tool to satisfy the basic needs of a peson. But he is not satisfied with fulfilling his basic needs. Besides money the appreciation, self respect, sense of pride, status, position etc. also work as good motivators. They are all non-financial.
(1) Appreciation : If good work done is appreciated by management, the worker willingly come forward to do work better and to show good perfomance. For this, the management has not to take any great trouble. But the result will be amazing.
(2) Responsibility : When some responsibility is delegated to a person, his sense of pride is satisfied and he comes forward to fulfil this responsibility in a better way. He feels that his ability has been recognised by management.
(3) Welfare Measures : Employers try to win over the loyalty of workers by introducing certain welfare measures. e.g. housing facilities, canteen, transportation facitities, medical services etc. Some companies run libraries; gymkhanas, sports complex etc.
(4) Besides the positive incentives we have discussed so far, there are negative incentives too, which have to be used in exceptional circumstances, e.g. dismissal or fear of losing the job, wage-cut, penalty, etc. are powerful negative incentives. But they have to be used very carefully and cautiously. In the atmosphere of uncertainty and fear, a worker is not able to work peacefully. He remains mentally disturbed.

Some Opinious about Money as a Motivator : So far it is believed that money is the most powerful and important incentive. But for last several years, it has been argued that money is not as important motivator as it is made out to be. Particularly after the presentation of Maslow's and Herzberg's theories and after some of research studies made, people believe that importance of money as an incentive is there, but not as a very powerful incentive. Some of the opinions are as follows :
(1) Maslow has remarked that money is a means of satisfying lower needs of human beings and these lower needs are not so important in

modern abvanced countries of the world. Even Herzberg observed that money works as a hygienic factor. He has found that money does not motivate personnel, but it acts only as a maintenance factor. It is not an incentive. Thus, in the theories of motivation that have been presented during last 4 to 5 decades suggest that money is not a powerful incentive.
(2) Money is most important to people who are younger and are raising a family. But those who have already settled, money needs are not as urgent as he is getting regular income.
(3) As Gellerman has observed, "in most kinds of business organisations, money is actually used as a means for keeping an organisation adequately stuffed and not as a motivator. In developed countries, employes are maintained by giving good salaries, so that they may not go away to other companies.
(4) Money as a motivator is diluted somewhat because of the practice of giving equitable salaries to managers of the same level. This is because people do not tolerate that a person in a comparable position gets higher compensation. Thus, in the words of Herzberg money only works as a maintenance factor and not as an incentive.
(5) As Gellerman has said, money can motivate only when the payment of incentive is large relative to a person's income. But the trouble is that most wage and salary increases or even bonus payments are not large enough to motivate the receivers. They only keep individual from being dissatsfied and from looking for another job. Unless they are large enough to be 'felt' and unless they are linked with performance, they are not likely to be a strong motivator.
(6) In some researches carried out by some professors of American Universities that only $10 \%$ of the workers engaged in production could be motivated with money, while $90 \%$ of workers depended on other factors for motivation. The researchers have found that fixing of quota for production becomes an aim of workers and that would increase production. Even the workers would try to increase production to attract the attention of his superior. Thus, they work regularly with enthusiasm and that leads to increased production. The reseach studies have proved that money is not very important as an incentive. But that is so in developed countries like the U.S.A. and not in India, where the standard of living is low.
