# BARRIERS TO EFFECTIVE COMMUNICATION
If we wish to make communication effective, we should know the barriers to communication. They are as follows :
(1) Badly Expressed Message : When the sender shows carelessness in expression of the message, choose wrong words, constructs awkward sentences, omits some important point and creates confusions in drafting, then it leads to costly errors. Similarly, erroneous translation of the message also distorts the message. If message is worded in highly technical terms, its interpretation creates difficulties.
(2) Change of Meaning during Transmission : The meaning of the message may change as it passes from one individual to another. It is believed that about 30 per cent of information gets evaporated in such transmission process. In fact, it is almost impossible to transfer oral message correctly from one level to another in a large-sized industrial unit. The capacity of the receiver to grasp the content of the message is also limited. Even if a message is received in its original form, it is possible that it may not be interpreted correctly.
(3) Lack of Attention : Many of the employees do not care to pay attention to the bulletin, notices, circulars and reports published by the company, and hence, they do not have important information concerning their firm. Similarly, managers at the higher level also remain indifferent to the reports received from the lower level.
(4) Unclarified Assumption : Sometimes, the senders and receivers both assume away certain things, which may not in fact be contained in the message. For instance, suppose a customer informs the manager that he is to visit the company at definite time on a particular day. Here, the customer assumes that the company would receive him, provide him transport facility and would be ready to welcome him. On the other hand, the company manager may assume that the customer is coming to the town for some other personal reason and will make a routine visit to the company. In this case, communication would fail due to assumptions which were not clarified.
(5) Insufficient Adjustment Period to Change : Sometimes, managers propose important changes affecting employees significantly. But if such changes are implemented without giving sufficient time to the person

concemed to whom message is communicated, it will fail. Thus, it is necessary that changes should not be forced before people can adjust to implications.
(6) Lack of Trust : Sometimes, supervisors represent messages received from the top managers in a distorted form. Due to such off-repeated experience, employees do not take message seriously, show no interest in it and make delay in its implementation.
(7) Overloaded Communication : If the same employee has to send so many different kinds of information, he feels overloaded. He tends to give more importance to the quick disposal of work at the cost of accuracy of information. Thus, overloading of information reduces the effectiveness of communication. Similarly, the capacity of different media to bear information is also limited. If a medium is overloaded with information, 'ts effectiveness is reduced.
(8) Neglect of the Proper Ordering of Information : Information should be arranged in order according to its importance. If information not ordered correctly and is cut short, follow-up action cannot be taken on time and the employees who are excluded from the communication channel feel displeased. Hence, it is becomes difficult to take decisions and implement them on a right time.
(9) Selection of Improper Medium : If wrong or improper medium is chosen for conveying a message, it fails to reach the persons concerned.
(10) Sentimental : Some sentimental persons give precedence to emotions over the content of messages. Consequently, correct information cannot be obtained.
(11) Fear : Often, the managers rely on their assistants for gathering information. They receive routine reports and special reports from their assistants. But the assistants do not always convey true or complete information in the reports. This may be due to fear that true information would harm their interests, as it may displease the superiors.
(12) Failure in Giving Information : Managers cannot give all information to the employees. Hence, they have to decide which information to convey to them. Sometimes, they fall to give even important information because of carelessness.
(13) Premature Evaluation : Evaluation of what the other person has to say play an important role in making communication! more effective. But evaluation must be made after carefully listening to the speaker and judged from what is being said, but the hidden meaning must be understood.
