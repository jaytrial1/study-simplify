# TYPES (KINDS) OF COMMUNICATION
The various types of communication used in business management are as follows :
(i) Formal and Informal Communication
(ii) Verbal and Written Communication
(iii) Upward, Downward and Horizontal Communication

Let us describe them in some detail.
(i) Formal and Informal Communication : Formal communication implies the flow of information along the lines of hierarchy according to. the procedures specifically set-up in the organisation. Thus, when a manager asks his assistant to do something by virtue of his position, it is a formal communication. Usually, formal communication is written.

Informal communication is based on the informal relationship that spontaneously grows up in the organisation. It is not official or authoritaive. Informal communication is generally conveyed by a simple glance, gesture or smile. For an example, a manager may take casual round in the factory and make some suggestions to the workers in a frank and friendly manner, it is informal communication. Being spontaneous, informal communication is likely to be more effective. But it has the weakness that it may convey distorted information.

(ii) Verbal and Written Communication : Written communication means transmission of the contents by written words in the form of letters, memos, circulars, manuals, boards, reports, instruction cards, publications etc. According to Dr. Terry, four 'Cs' are essential to make written communication effective. What he intends to suggest is that written communication must be complete, clear, concise and correct.

If the message to be conveyed is lengthy and needs a thorough clarification, written communication would be more suitable. Moreover. written communication provides a permanent record and can be referred to as evidence. It must be remembered that decorative language reduces the effectiveness of communication and makes the message less intelligible. Hence, it may be worded in simple and clear language. Verbal communication means conveyance of messages through spoken words, visual media, gestures etc. In the case of verbal communication, everything is oral. The examples of verbal communications are orders, face-to-face discussions, telephonic talk, lectures, conferences, interviews, public speeches, slides and movies, broadcast etc. Verbal communication is comparatively more effective because there is a personal touch in it. If any doubts creep in the minds of any party, they can be immediately removed. Thus, there is easy understandability in the case of verbal communication.
(iii) Upward, Downward and Horizontal Communication : Downward communication is that which flows from a superior to a subordinate e. g. a managing director may issue orders to sales manager. Orders, instructions, circulars and bulletins etc. come under downward communication. Communication is upward when it flows from the lower levels of organisation to the top levels of authority, e. g. departmental heads may report to the general manager. Examples of upward communication are activity reports, suggestions, grievances, opinions etc. Horizontal communication refers to interchange of ideas among the officers and other personnel refers to interchange of ideas among the officers and other personnel of the same rank. Consultation between departmental managers and meetings of workers of the same class to discuss a problem belong to this mode of communication.
